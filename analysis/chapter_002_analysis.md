# Chapter 2 Analysis: Swimmer's Body Illusion

## Bias Name and Description

**Swimmer's Body Illusion** (also known as the "Result Illusion") — confusing selection factors (traits that allow someone to be selected for an activity) with results (outcomes produced by that activity). We mistake cause for effect.

## Key Examples from the Chapter

### 1. Professional Swimmers
Swimmers have perfect bodies not because swimming created them, but because their body type made them good swimmers.

### 2. Models and Cosmetics
Models advertise cosmetics, but they were selected for their existing beauty, not made beautiful by the products.

### 3. Harvard University
Does Harvard make you smarter, or does it simply recruit the brightest students? The success of graduates may stem from selection, not education.

### 4. MBA Programs
MBA tuition buys access to a selective network, not necessarily a guaranteed income boost. The income correlation is driven by who enters the program.

### 5. Self-Help Books
Happy people's advice may not apply to others because they were born with a happy disposition.

## Actionable Steps

1. **Reverse the causal arrow**: Ask which came first — the trait or the activity?
2. **Examine selection mechanisms**: Investigate how participants are selected
3. **Look for missing data**: Who tried this and failed?
4. **Disaggregate correlation from causation**: Test whether removing the activity removes the trait
5. **Set realistic goals**: Based on what you can actually change, not genetic baselines
6. **Question marketing claims**: Are they showing survivors?
7. **Take further study for intrinsic reasons**: Not just for a bigger paycheck

## Best Practices

- ✅ Pause before emulating success stories
- ✅ Investigate the baseline population
- ✅ Distinguish nature from nurture
- ✅ Seek out failure data
- ✅ Beware of self-help generalities
- ✅ Audit institutional claims

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Assuming correlation implies causation
  **Solution:** Test whether the activity actually creates the trait
- **Problem:** Ignoring invisible non-participants
  **Solution:** Look at who tried and failed
- **Problem:** Overestimating nurture over nature
  **Solution:** Accept genetic baselines and set realistic goals
