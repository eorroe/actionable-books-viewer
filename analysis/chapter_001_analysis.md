# Chapter 1 Analysis: Survivorship Bias

## Bias Name and Description

**Survivorship Bias** — systematically overestimating success chances because failures are invisible. We only see the successes (survivors) and not the failures, leading to distorted probability assessments.

## Key Examples from the Chapter

### 1. Music Industry
Rick sees rock stars everywhere and starts a band. The probability of making it big is near zero, but the graveyard of failed musicians (10,000 times more than successful ones) is invisible.

### 2. Publishing
Behind every published author are 100 unpublished writers. We only see the successful authors.

### 3. Startups
We hear about successful startups but not the 90% that fail.

### 4. Stock Indices
Companies listed on stock indices are the survivors. Failed companies are delisted and forgotten.

### 5. Scientific Studies
Published studies show positive results, but negative results are often not published.

## Actionable Steps

1. **Seek out failure data**: Actively look for information about failures, not just successes.
2. **Adjust probability estimates**: Remember that visible successes are a tiny fraction of all attempts.
3. **Question success stories**: Ask "What about the people who tried and failed?"
4. **Use base rates**: Look at overall statistics, not just individual success stories.
5. **Avoid emulation without data**: Don't start a band, business, or career based solely on visible success stories.
6. **Consider the cemetery**: Always think about the invisible failures.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Look for base rate data before making decisions
- ✅ Seek out negative results and failures
- ✅ Remember that "visible success" ≠ "typical outcome"
- ✅ Adjust your probability estimates for survivorship bias

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Starting a business because you see successful entrepreneurs
  **Solution:** Research business failure rates (90%+ fail within 10 years)
- **Problem:** Emulating a successful person's habits without understanding selection bias
  **Solution:** Ask whether the habits were causes or correlations of success
