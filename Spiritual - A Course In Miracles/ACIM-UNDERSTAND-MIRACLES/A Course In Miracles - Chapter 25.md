# Chapter 25: The Justice of God

## A Course in Miracles - Text Analysis

**Source Pages:** 875-876, 1059-1091 (PDF pages; chapter content spans non-contiguous pages)

---

## Overview

Chapter 25, "The Justice of God," explores the nature of divine justice as fundamentally different from the world's concept of justice as vengeance and punishment. The chapter teaches that God's justice is rooted in love, equality, and the recognition that no one can lose for another to gain. It examines how perception shapes reality, the special function each person has in salvation, and the state of sinlessness as the natural condition of the Son of God. The chapter culminates in the understanding that true justice means everyone benefits, no one is sacrificed, and miracles are the natural expression of this divine justice.

## Lesson 1: Christ Inhabits Not a Body, Yet He Is In You

**Source:** Section 1 - Introduction

### Key Principles
- The Christ in you inhabits not a body, yet He is in you.
- What is within you cannot be outside.
- You cannot be apart from what is at the very center of your life.
- What gives you life cannot be housed in death.
- The body needs no healing; the mind that thinks it is a body is sick.
- Christ's purpose folds the body in His light and fills it with Holiness.
- Nothing the body says or does but makes Christ manifest.
- Your brother has a mission for you, and your mission is for him.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that Christ is within you, not within your body.
2. Understand that what is within you cannot be outside — your true nature extends beyond the body.
3. Accept that the body itself does not need healing; it is the mind's belief that it is a body that requires correction.
4. Allow Christ's purpose to fold the body in His light, using it as a vessel for gentleness and love.
5. See your brother as carrying the same Christ within, and recognize that his mission is yours, and yours is his.

### Best Practices
- Look past the body to the Christ within when you observe yourself or others.
- Use the body as a frame for holiness rather than a prison for the mind.
- See every interaction as an opportunity to manifest Christ's gentleness and love.
- Remember that your brother's mission for you is inseparable from your mission for him.

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing that the body is the primary reality or that healing must occur at the physical level first.
- Identifying yourself as a body and thus limiting your perception of who you are.
- Overlooking the Christ in others because you see only their physical form.
- Thinking your spiritual mission is separate from or more important than your brother's.

## Lesson 2: Perception Is a Choice — You See What You Want to See

**Source:** Sections 2 and 4 - The Link to Truth; Perception and Choice

### Key Principles
- Perception is a choice of what you want yourself to be; the world you want to live in.
- Perception has one fundamental law: You see what you believe is there, and you believe it there because you want it there.
- To the extent you value guilt, you perceive a world where attack is justified.
- To the extent you recognize guilt is meaningless, you perceive attack cannot be justified.
- The Holy Spirit links the part that wants to be separate to the Christ, making oneness clear.
- What is the same cannot be different; what is one cannot have separate parts.
- There is another Maker of the world who corrects the mad belief that anything could exist without some link to God's laws.
- Sin is the fixed belief that perception cannot change; to forgive sin is to change its state from error into truth.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Notice what you are perceiving in any given situation.
2. Ask yourself: "What do I want this to be?" or "What do I want to see here?"
3. Recognize that your perception is faithful to your purpose — it shows you what you want to see.
4. If you see attack, fear, or guilt, recognize these as reflections of your chosen purpose.
5. Choose again: decide what you want to see, and perception will shift to reflect that choice.
6. When you perceive error in yourself or others, remember it is a misperception, not a sin — and therefore forgivable and changeable.

### Best Practices
- Regularly examine your perceptions and trace them back to your underlying desires and purposes.
- Use the Holy Spirit's perception as your model: see only what justifies forgiveness and reveals sinlessness.
- Treat every perception as a choice point where you can choose again.
- Remember that what you see in your brother is a reflection of what you have chosen to see.

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing that perception is objective or that what you see is "just the way things are."
- Thinking that some perceptions are beyond change or that certain people are irredeemably "bad."
- Using perception to confirm your judgments rather than to reveal truth.
- Forgetting that perception serves your purpose, whether that purpose is love or attack.

## Lesson 3: See the Masterpiece, Not the Frame

**Source:** Section 3 - The Savior from the Dark

### Key Principles
- The body's eyes perceive fills you with fear; despair always results from seeking satisfaction in the world.
- The past gave no rewards you would want to keep — learn this and be willing to relinquish it.
- Take not the form for content; the frame is but a means for content.
- The Holy Spirit is the frame God set around the part of Him you would see as separate.
- God's masterpiece is set within a frame that will endure forever; the picture is never destroyed.
- Your brother is the frame in which your holiness is set.
- Who hangs an empty frame upon a wall and stands before it, deep in reverence? Yet if you see your brother as a body, it is but this you do.
- Forgive your brother, and you cannot separate yourself from him nor from his Father.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. When you look at your brother, notice whether you see the body (the frame) or the Christ within (the masterpiece).
2. If you see only the body, recognize that you are looking at an empty frame.
3. Ask the Holy Spirit to show you the masterpiece God has placed within that frame.
4. Look past the body to the holiness that shines from within.
5. Forgive your brother for any perceived imperfections of the frame.
6. See that what God has created needs no frame, for what He has created He supports and frames within Himself.

### Best Practices
- Practice looking past physical appearance to the divine essence within.
- When you judge someone based on their body or behavior, pause and look for the masterpiece.
- Use the body as a temporary frame, not the permanent reality.
- See your brother as your savior from the dark, just as he is yours.

### Common Pitfalls
- Worshiping the frame (the body, achievements, persona) instead of the masterpiece (the Christ within).
- Believing that the body or external form defines a person's worth or identity.
- Overlooking the masterpiece because you are fixated on the frame.
- Thinking that forgiveness means ignoring the frame rather than looking past it to the truth.

## Lesson 4: The State of Sinlessness — No Desire to Attack

**Source:** Section 6 - The State of Sinlessness

### Key Principles
- The state of sinlessness is merely this: the whole desire to attack is gone.
- Attack and sin are bound as one illusion, each the cause and aim and justifier of the other.
- Attack makes Christ your enemy, and God along with Him.
- Who would attack whatever he perceives as wholly innocent?
- The Son of God asks only this: that you return to him what is his due.
- Alone does neither have it; together, it gives to each equal strength to save the other.
- Forgiven by you, your savior offers you salvation. Condemned by you, he offers death to you.
- In everyone you see but the reflection of what you choose to have him be to you.
- The role you give to him is given you, and you will walk the way you pointed out to him.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Examine your mind for any desire to attack — whether in thought, word, or deed.
2. Recognize that attack and sin are the same illusion, each justifying the other.
3. When you feel the urge to attack, pause and remember: you are attacking your own salvation.
4. See your brother as wholly innocent, for in his innocence you see your own.
5. Forgive your brother by returning to him what is his due: his sinlessness.
6. Recognize that you and your brother save each other together; alone, neither has the gift.

### Best Practices
- Make the renunciation of attack your primary spiritual goal.
- Practice seeing innocence in everyone, especially those who trigger defensive or angry responses.
- Remember that your brother's role in your life is what you have assigned to him; choose wisely.
- See forgiveness as the means by which you give your brother his due, and receive your own.

### Common Pitfalls
- Thinking that sinlessness means never making mistakes, rather than the absence of the desire to attack.
- Believing that some people are beyond forgiveness or that certain attacks are justified.
- Forgetting that you see your own reflection in everyone you look upon.
- Trying to achieve sinlessness alone, rather than through joining with your brother.

## Lesson 5: The Special Function — Your Unique Role in Salvation

**Source:** Section 7 - The Special Function

### Key Principles
- The grace of God rests gently on forgiving eyes.
- Eyes become used to darkness, and the light of brilliant day seems painful to eyes grown long accustomed to dimness.
- The wish to see calls down the grace of God upon your eyes.
- The Holy Spirit uses what you made to heal instead of harm — this is His kind perception of specialness.
- To each He gives a special function in salvation he alone can fill.
- Forgiveness is the only function meaningful in time.
- Salvation is no more than a reminder this world is not your home.
- His special sin was made his special grace; his special hate became his special love.
- The Holy Spirit needs your special function.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that you have a special function in God's plan that only you can fulfill.
2. Do not believe you lack a special value here — you wanted it, and it is given you.
3. Look with forgiving eyes, for grace rests upon them.
4. When you wish to see truth, ask for the grace of God to be upon your sight.
5. Use what you have made (your experiences, relationships, skills) for healing rather than harm.
6. Practice forgiveness as your special function, for it is the only meaningful use of time.
7. Remember that your special sin has been transformed into your special grace.

### Best Practices
- Ask the Holy Spirit to reveal your special function to you.
- Do not compare your function to others'; each role is unique and necessary.
- Practice seeing every encounter as an opportunity to exercise your special function.
- Trust that everything in your life — even what seems like a mistake — can be used for salvation.

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing you have no special purpose or value in God's plan.
- Trying to determine your special function through ego-based analysis or comparison.
- Using your specialness to elevate yourself above others rather than to serve them.
- Thinking your function is to judge rather than to forgive.

## Lesson 6: The Rock of Salvation — No One Can Lose for Another to Gain

**Source:** Section 8 - The Rock of Salvation

### Key Principles
- If the Holy Spirit can commute each sentence you laid upon yourself into a blessing, then it cannot be a sin.
- Sin is the only thing in all the world that cannot change — and on its changelessness the world depends.
- It must be so that either God is mad, or this world is a place of madness.
- The Holy Spirit can change the whole foundation of the world to something else — a basis not insane, on which a sane perception can be based.
- Your special function is the special form in which the fact that God is not insane appears most sensible to you.
- Salvation is rebirth of the idea no one can lose for anyone to gain.
- Everyone must gain, if anyone would be a gainer.
- Remember all temptation is but this: a mad belief that God's insanity would make you sane.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. When you feel condemned or believe you have sinned, remember: the Holy Spirit can commute any sentence into a blessing.
2. Test every belief against this one requirement: does it uphold the idea that no one loses for another to gain?
3. Recognize that the world's justice (vengeance, punishment, sacrifice) is insanity.
4. Accept your special function as the form in which God's sanity appears most sensibly to you.
5. When tempted to believe in sin, remember: it is a mad belief that God must be insane.
6. Affirm: "No one can lose for me to gain. If I gain, everyone gains with me."

### Best Practices
- Hold fast to the principle that no one can lose as the rock of your salvation.
- Question every value the world upholds: does it support sacrifice and loss, or universal gain?
- See your special function as proof that God is not insane — He has a plan that makes sense.
- Use temptation as a reminder to return to sanity: God is not insane, and neither are you.

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing that sin is real and immutable, and therefore some people are beyond redemption.
- Accepting the world's justice system (punishment, vengeance, sacrifice) as sane.
- Thinking that your salvation requires someone else's loss.
- Believing that God's justice is like human justice — punitive and vengeful.

## Lesson 7: Justice Returned to Love — The Principle of No Loss

**Source:** Section 9 - Justice Returned to Love

### Key Principles
- The Holy Spirit can use all that you give Him for your salvation, but He cannot use what you withhold.
- Here is the only principle salvation needs: no one can lose for you to gain.
- To the world, justice and vengeance are the same; sinners see justice only as punishment.
- Justice looks on all in the same way; it is not just that one should lack for what another has.
- Justice demands no sacrifice, for any sacrifice is made to preserve sin.
- Love and justice are not different; because they are the same, mercy stands at God's right Hand.
- To him who merits everything, how can it be that anything be kept from him?
- Your special function shows you nothing else but perfect justice can prevail for you.
- Let love decide, and never fear that you will deprive yourself of what God's justice has allotted you.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Give freely to the Holy Spirit everything you are ready to release — do not withhold anything.
2. Understand the core principle: no one can lose for you to gain. This is the only principle salvation needs.
3. Release your belief that justice means punishment; see that true justice looks on all equally.
4. When you feel someone else has what you deserve, remember: God's justice means nothing is kept from you.
5. Let love, not vengeance, be your guide in every situation.
6. Accept that love and justice are one — what love demands, justice provides.
7. Trust that God's justice will not deprive you, even when you feel deprived.

### Best Practices
- Practice giving without hesitation — what you give is not lost but preserved in Heaven.
- See every situation through the lens of "no one loses" rather than "someone must lose."
- When you feel wronged, ask: "How can justice be served without vengeance?"
- Allow love to be your arbiter, not the world's standards of fairness.

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing that justice requires punishment, sacrifice, or someone paying for another's gain.
- Withholding forgiveness or love because you think someone "deserves" punishment.
- Thinking that God's justice is like human justice — retaliatory and conditional.
- Believing that you must sacrifice your own good for another's, or take from another for your own good.

## Lesson 8: The Justice of Heaven — Miracles and Universal Salvation

**Source:** Section 10 - The Justice of Heaven

### Key Principles
- What can it be but arrogance to think your little errors cannot be undone by Heaven's justice?
- God's justice warrants gratitude, not fear.
- The Holy Spirit's problem solving ends the problem because it has been met with justice.
- The principle that justice means no one can lose is crucial to this course; miracles depend on it.
- No one deserves to lose; a miracle is justice.
- Unless you think all your brothers have an equal right to miracles with you, you will not claim your right to them.
- The miracle that you receive, you give. Each one illustrates the law on which salvation rests: justice must be done to all if anyone is to be healed.
- No one can lose, and everyone must benefit.
- What is God's belongs to everyone, and is his due.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Release the arrogant belief that your errors are beyond redemption or that you deserve punishment.
2. Replace fear of God's justice with gratitude, for His justice means no one loses.
3. Bring every problem to the Holy Spirit, trusting that His solution involves no loss to anyone.
4. Accept that miracles are justice — they ensure no one loses while everyone gains.
5. Recognize your equal right to miracles alongside every other Son of God.
6. When you receive a miracle (healing, forgiveness, peace), give it freely to all.
7. Affirm: "What is God's belongs to everyone, and is his due."

### Best Practices
- Approach every problem with the certainty that a just solution exists where no one loses.
- Give miracles freely, knowing that what you give is not diminished.
- See every healing as evidence that justice has been done — no one was sacrificed.
- Practice equal rights to miracles: if your brother can be healed, so can you, and vice versa.

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing you are too sinful to be healed or that some errors are unforgivable.
- Thinking miracles are special gifts for a select few rather than universal rights.
- Keeping your healings and insights secret, thereby withholding them from others.
- Believing that justice requires some to suffer so others can be saved.
- Thinking that your own salvation is separate from or more important than your brother's.

## Lesson 9: The Light You Bring — Extending Heaven to Others

**Source:** Section 5 - The Light You Bring

### Key Principles
- Minds that are joined and recognize it feel no guilt, for they cannot attack.
- Everyone seeks what will bring him joy as he defines it.
- Perception's basic law: You will rejoice at what you see because you see it to rejoice.
- You maker of a world that is not so, take rest and comfort in another world where peace abides.
- From you can come their rest; from you can rise a world they will rejoice to look upon.
- In you is all of Heaven — every leaf, bird, and flower lives in you.
- How better could your own mistakes be brought to truth than by your willingness to bring the light of Heaven with you?

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that your mind has the power to bring light or darkness to any situation.
2. Choose to bring the light of Heaven with you as you walk through the world.
3. When you encounter those who are weary, offer them the rest you have found.
4. Extend the vision that sees only holiness and innocence in your brother.
5. Allow your own "evil" thoughts to be pushed away by the rising sun of truth within you.
6. Stand in quiet, in innocence and wholly unafraid, and let your peace extend to all.

### Best Practices
- Practice seeing the world as you would have it be — a world of light, peace, and joy.
- Be the light-bearer for those who are still in darkness.
- Allow Heaven to enter the world through you, not by changing the world, but by changing your perception of it.
- Extend peace to everyone, for those who offer peace have found a home in Heaven.

### Common Pitfalls
- Looking to the world for happiness and then feeling disappointed when it fails to deliver.
- Thinking that bringing light requires changing external circumstances rather than internal perception.
- Withholding your peace from others because you believe they do not deserve it.
- Forgetting that you carry all of Heaven within you, and therefore have everything to give.

## Lesson 10: Join Minds and Release Guilt Through Oneness

**Source:** Sections 1, 2, and 7 - Introduction; The Link to Truth; The Special Function

### Key Principles
- Christ is within a frame of Holiness whose purpose is that He may be made manifest to those who know Him not.
- No one who carries Christ in him can fail to recognize Him everywhere except in bodies.
- As long as he believes he is in a body, where he thinks he is Christ cannot be.
- The mind is Christ's, and so it must be yours.
- The Holy Spirit links the tiny, mad desire to be separate to the Christ, to make the oneness clear.
- It is the Holy Spirit's function to teach you how oneness is experienced.
- Minds that are joined and recognize it feel no guilt.
- The Holy Spirit's special function is to release the holy Son of God from the imprisonment he made to keep himself from justice.
- Your function and the Holy Spirit's are not apart; they are one.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that Christ is in you, even though you may believe you are in a body.
2. Understand that the body does not define you; your mind does, and your mind is Christ's.
3. Allow the Holy Spirit to link your desire for separation to the Christ, making oneness clear.
4. Practice joining minds with your brother, for in that joining guilt cannot exist.
5. Accept that your mission and your brother's are the same: to manifest Christ to those who know Him not.
6. Do not keep Christ unknowing by hiding Him behind the body's identity.
7. Participate in the Holy Spirit's function of releasing the Sonship from its self-imposed prison.

### Best Practices
- Look for the Christ in everyone you meet, especially those who seem most separated from Him.
- Practice mind-joining rather than body-identifying in your relationships.
- Remember that you carry Christ unknowingly when you identify with the body; choose to make Him manifest.
- See every encounter as an opportunity for both you and your brother to recognize Christ in each other.

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing that you are a body and therefore separate from others and from Christ.
- Keeping Christ hidden by over-identifying with physical form, personality, or specialness.
- Thinking that your spiritual path is individual rather than shared with all the Sonship.
- Forgetting that the Holy Spirit's function and yours are one — your joining is essential to salvation.

## Summary of Core Principles

1. **God's justice is not vengeance** — it is rooted in love, equality, and universal salvation.
2. **No one can lose for another to gain** — this is the rock on which salvation rests.
3. **Perception is choice** — you see what you want to see, and what you see reflects your purpose.
4. **The body is a frame, not the picture** — look past form to the masterpiece of Christ within.
5. **Sinlessness is the absence of the desire to attack** — it is a state of mind, not a record of deeds.
6. **Each person has a special function** — what you made to hurt yourself, God appointed to save you.
7. **Forgiveness is the only meaningful function in time** — it translates specialness from sin into salvation.
8. **Miracles are justice** — they ensure no one loses while everyone gains.
9. **What is God's belongs to everyone** — you cannot have what belongs to another, nor can they have what is yours.
10. **Heaven is restored through relationship** — your joining with your brother is the means of salvation for both.
11. **The state of sinlessness restores your awareness of your true nature** — you are not a body, but the Christ within.
12. **Justice and love are the same** — they cannot be separated; mercy is love expressed through justice.
