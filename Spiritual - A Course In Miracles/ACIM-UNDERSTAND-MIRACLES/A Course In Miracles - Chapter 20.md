# Chapter 20: The Vision of Holiness - Analysis

## Chapter Overview

**Title:** The Vision of Holiness  
**Section 1:** Holy Week  
**Source Pages:** 864-865  

This chapter introduces Holy Week as a spiritual journey from the celebration of victory (Palm Sunday) to the gift of lilies (Easter), emphasizing the resurrection over crucifixion, forgiveness over fear, and the recognition of the Son of God's innocence.

---

## Lesson 1: Holy Week as a Symbol of the Spiritual Journey

### Lesson Title
Holy Week Represents the Complete Journey of the Son of God

### Key Principles
- Holy Week is not merely a historical event but a symbol of the entire spiritual journey the Son of God undertakes
- The week begins with palms (victory) and ends with lilies (innocence)
- The purpose is celebration of life, not mourning of death

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that Holy Week symbolizes your own spiritual journey
2. Begin with the acceptance of victory and the promise of resurrection
3. Avoid wandering into the temptation of crucifixion and delaying there
4. Go in peace beyond crucifixion with the light of your own innocence
5. Allow the whiteness of your gift of lilies to speed you on to resurrection

### Best Practices
- Spend Holy Week celebrating release rather than brooding on crucifixion
- Honor the perfect purity of the Son of God, not his sins
- Keep the dark sign of crucifixion from intervening between the journey and its purpose

### Common Pitfalls
- Focusing on the crucifixion of God's Son rather than his release
- Delaying spiritual progress by lingering on thoughts of pain and death
- Allowing the symbol of suffering to overshadow the symbol of resurrection

---

## Lesson 2: The Risen Christ as the True Symbol

### Lesson Title
A Risen Christ Becomes the Symbol of Forgiveness

### Key Principles
- A slain Christ has no meaning in spiritual truth
- A risen Christ symbolizes the Son of God's forgiveness on himself
- The risen Christ represents healing and wholeness

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Release the concept of a slain Christ from your mind
2. See the risen Christ as the true symbol of spiritual truth
3. Recognize that resurrection signifies the Son of God's self-forgiveness
4. Look upon yourself as healed and whole through this symbol

### Best Practices
- Let Easter be the sign of peace, not pain
- Use the risen Christ as a symbol of your own healing
- Focus on the meaning of resurrection rather than crucifixion

### Common Pitfalls
- Clinging to images of a suffering or slain Christ
- Associating spiritual truth with pain and sacrifice
- Missing the message of healing by focusing on death

---

## Lesson 3: The Gift of Lilies vs. The Crown of Thorns

### Lesson Title
Choose the Gift of Lilies Over the Crown of Thorns

### Key Principles
- You stand beside your brother with both thorns and lilies in your hands
- The choice between offering love or fear is always present
- Lilies represent the white and holy sign of the Son of God's innocence
- Thorns represent crucifixion, fear, and guilt

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that you have both thorns and lilies to offer
2. Join with the Holy Spirit to throw away the thorns
3. Offer lilies to replace the thorns
4. Give your brother the gift of love, not the "gift" of fear
5. Offer forgiveness as your gift to Christ

### Best Practices
- Consciously choose lilies over thorns in every interaction
- Offer the gift of love rather than the gift of fear
- Join with the spiritual guide to release the thorns
- Speed your brother's redemption with the whiteness of your gift

### Common Pitfalls
- Giving the crown of thorns instead of lilies
- Offering fear disguised as a gift
- Remaining uncertain about which to give
- Holding onto thorns and nails when redemption is near

---

## Lesson 4: Forgiveness as the Path to Unity and Redemption

### Lesson Title
Forgiveness Unites Us and Completes the Resurrection

### Key Principles
- We cannot be united in crucifixion and death
- The resurrection is not complete until forgiveness rests on Christ
- Your forgiveness offered to others returns to you
- In forgiving a stranger, you find your ancient Friend

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Offer forgiveness to your brother as your Easter gift
2. Recognize that this forgiveness will be returned to you
3. Understand that your redemption is linked with your brother's
4. See beyond the stranger to the ancient Friend within
5. Allow your forgiveness to rest on Christ along with others' forgiveness

### Best Practices
- Make forgiveness the central gift of your spiritual practice
- Recognize the interconnection of your redemption with others'
- Look for the face of Christ behind the veil of appearances
- Celebrate the holiness of your risen Friend

### Common Pitfalls
- Trying to achieve unity through shared suffering
- Delaying the resurrection by withholding forgiveness
- Seeing others as strangers rather than ancient Friends
- Forgetting that forgiveness is a gift that returns to the giver

---

## Lesson 5: Seeing the Face of Christ Through the Gift of Lilies

### Lesson Title
The Gift of Lilies Reveals Your Brother's True Face

### Key Principles
- Glimpses of the face of Christ can be seen behind the veil
- The snow-white petals of lilies you have received and given are the medium through which you behold your brother's face
- Recognizing your brother's face leads to knowing Christ
- Forgiveness of the stranger/alien reveals him as your ancient Friend

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Receive and give lilies as your spiritual gift
2. Look between the snow-white petals for glimpses of Christ's face
3. Behold your brother's face through this gift
4. Recognize him as your ancient Friend
5. Forgive this stranger who is alien to you yet known to you
6. Know Christ through your gift of lilies and forgiveness

### Best Practices
- Look for Christ's face in the medium of your gifts
- Use the symbol of lilies to see beyond appearances
- Practice forgiveness as the way to recognize your ancient Friend
- Allow your gift to reveal what is hidden behind the veil

### Common Pitfalls
- Looking directly at the veil instead of through the lilies
- Failing to recognize the stranger as your ancient Friend
- Keeping the veil in place by withholding forgiveness
- Missing the revelation because you do not offer the gift

## Lesson 6: Easter as the Time of Salvation

### Lesson Title
Easter Is the Time of Your Salvation

### Key Principles
- The time of Easter is a time of joy, not mourning
- Easter is the time of your salvation along with others'
- You are called to look on your risen Friend and celebrate his holiness
- Resurrection is the sign of peace and release

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize Easter as a time of joy rather than mourning
2. Look on your risen Friend
3. Celebrate his holiness along with spiritual guidance
4. Accept that this is the time of your salvation
5. Join with others in the celebration of resurrection

### Best Practices
- Maintain joy as the dominant tone of Holy Week
- Celebrate life rather than death
- Honor the holiness of others
- See Easter as personal and collective salvation

### Common Pitfalls
- Mourning instead of celebrating
- Focusing on death rather than resurrection
- Forgetting that Easter is your time of salvation
- Celebrating alone rather than joining with others

## Key Themes and Principles

### 1. Symbolism Over Literalism
Holy Week is understood as a symbol of the spiritual journey, not just a historical event. The symbols of palms, lilies, crucifixion, and resurrection carry deeper spiritual meanings about the journey from guilt to innocence, from fear to love.

### 2. The Centrality of Forgiveness
Forgiveness is the mechanism through which redemption is accomplished. Your forgiveness offered to others returns to you, and your salvation is linked with the salvation of others.

### 3. Choice Between Love and Fear
At every moment, you stand with both thorns (fear, crucifixion) and lilies (love, resurrection) in your hands. The spiritual path involves consciously choosing lilies over thorns.

### 4. Recognition of Innocence
The vision of holiness involves seeing the innocence of the Son of God in yourself and others. This requires looking beyond appearances and the veil of separation.

### 5. Joy Over Mourning
The appropriate response to spiritual truth is joy, not mourning. Easter signifies peace, not pain; life, not death.

### 6. The Risen Christ as Symbol
The risen Christ, not the crucified Christ, is the meaningful symbol of spiritual truth. Resurrection represents healing, wholeness, and self-forgiveness.

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **Practice choosing lilies over thorns** in every interaction today
2. **Offer forgiveness** as your primary spiritual gift to others
3. **Celebrate life** rather than ruminating on pain or death
4. **Look for the face of Christ** in those you perceive as strangers
5. **Maintain the vision of innocence** in yourself and others
6. **Use Easter (or any holy time)** as a reminder of joy and resurrection
7. **Join with others** in the celebration of holiness rather than separating in crucifixion
8. **Speed others on their way** with the gift of love rather than holding them back with thorns

---

## Conclusion

Chapter 20 establishes the "Vision of Holiness" as the ability to see the innocence of the Son of God in oneself and others. This vision is achieved through forgiveness, the choice of love over fear, and the celebration of resurrection over crucifixion. Holy Week serves as the symbol of the complete spiritual journey, reminding us that we stand always with the choice between thorns and lilies, and that our redemption is found in offering the gift of love and forgiveness to our brothers.
