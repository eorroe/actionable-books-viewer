# Chapter 22: Salvation and the Holy Relationship
## Section 1: Introduction
**Source:** A Course in Miracles - Text Chapter 22 (Pages 868-869)

---

## Lesson 1: The Shared Nature of Sin

**Overview:** Sin is not an individual perception but a shared one. No two people can look on sin together because they cannot see it in the same place and time.

### Key Principles
- Sin is perceived in the other but believed to exist within oneself
- Each person seems to make a different error, but it is fundamentally the same error made by the same maker
- The error is forgiven for its maker in the same way it was made

### Best Practices
- When you perceive sin in another, recognize that you are also perceiving it in yourself
- Understand that what appears as different errors are variations of the same fundamental mistake
- Extend forgiveness knowing that the maker of the error is the same in all cases

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing that your error is unique or different from others
- Thinking that sin can be truly individual or private
- Failing to see the connection between your perceptions of others and your own self-perception

## Lesson 2: The Holy Relationship as Forgiveness

**Overview:** The holiness of a relationship actively forgives both parties, undoing the effects of what both believed and saw. With the undoing of these beliefs, the need for sin disappears.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that the relationship has a holiness that can undo past beliefs
2. Allow the relationship to forgive both you and your brother
3. Release the effects of what both of you believed and saw
4. Notice that as these effects go, the need for sin goes with them

### Key Principles
- The holiness inherent in a true relationship is redemptive
- Forgiveness operates on both parties simultaneously
- The need for sin is dissolved when its perceived purpose is removed

### Best Practices
- Cultivate awareness of the holy nature of your relationships
- Allow forgiveness to flow naturally within the relationship
- Do not cling to grievances that reinforce the need for sin

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing that forgiveness must come from one party only
- Trying to undo sin through individual effort rather than through the relationship
- Holding onto the belief that sin has an independent reality

---

## Lesson 3: Who Needs Sin and Why

**Overview:** Only the lonely and alone need sin. The perceived need for sin arises from seeing brothers as different from ourselves—a difference that is seen but not real.

### Key Principles
- Sin serves the lonely and isolated mind
- The need for sin is created by the illusion of separation from others
- Differences that are "seen but not real" justify the need for sin
- This need is itself not real, even though it seems justified

### Best Practices
- Examine your sense of isolation when you feel the pull toward sin
- Question the reality of the differences you perceive between yourself and others
- Recognize that the need for sin is a symptom of loneliness, not a solution

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing that sin is a legitimate response to real differences
- Accepting loneliness as a natural or inevitable state
- Using sin to cope with the pain of perceived separation

---

## Lesson 4: The Nature of the Unholy Relationship

**Overview:** The unholy relationship is based on perceived differences where each partner believes the other possesses what they lack. It is fundamentally exploitative and ends in emptiness.

### Key Principles
- The unholy relationship is transactional—each comes to complete themselves by taking from the other
- Partners rob each other until they believe there is nothing left to steal
- They then move on to new relationships
- They live as strangers under a common roof, in the same room yet a world apart

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize when a relationship is based on what you can get from the other
2. Notice when you see the other as having what you lack
3. Observe the emptiness that follows exploitation
4. See the loneliness of two people living together yet apart

### Best Practices
- Examine your motivations for entering relationships
- Notice when you are focused on taking rather than giving
- Recognize the futility of trying to complete yourself through others

### Common Pitfalls
- Mistaking the intensity of need for love
- Believing that acquiring what the other has will bring fulfillment
- Staying in exploitative relationships hoping for a different outcome

## Lesson 5: The Foundation of the Holy Relationship

**Overview:** The holy relationship begins from a fundamentally different premise—each person has looked within and seen no lack. Accepting their own completeness, they extend it by joining with another who is whole as they are.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Look within and recognize that you lack nothing
2. Accept your own completeness and wholeness
3. Extend this wholeness by joining with another who is also whole
4. See no differences between yourself and the other (differences are only of the body)
5. Look on nothing that you would take from the other
6. Deny not your own reality, for it is the truth

### Key Principles
- The holy relationship starts from inner completeness, not from lack
- Joining occurs between equals who are both whole
- Differences are recognized as superficial (pertaining only to the body)
- There is nothing to gain by taking from the other
- Your reality cannot be denied without denying the truth itself

### Best Practices
- Begin each day by affirming your own completeness
- Look beyond physical appearances to the shared essence
- Practice extending rather than taking in all interactions
- Affirm the reality of your being and that of others

### Common Pitfalls
- Entering relationships to fill a perceived inner void
- Focusing on physical or superficial differences
- Trying to take from others what you believe you lack
- Denying your own truth to accommodate illusions

---

## Lesson 6: The Position of the Holy Relationship

**Overview:** The holy relationship stands just under Heaven, close enough not to return to earth. It carries Heaven's holiness and is therefore far from the illusions of the world.

### Key Principles
- The holy relationship exists in a state just below Heaven
- It is close enough to truth that it does not return to illusion
- It possesses Heaven's holiness
- It is as far from the world of separation as Heaven itself

### Best Practices
- Honor the holy relationships in your life as near-Heavenly experiences
- Do not drag holy relationships back into worldly concerns
- Protect the sanctity of holy relationships by keeping them focused on truth

### Common Pitfalls
- Treating holy relationships as ordinary or mundane
- Bringing worldly problems and concerns into holy relationships
- Failing to recognize the sacred nature of true holy relationships

---

## Lesson 7: What the Holy Relationship Teaches

**Overview:** The holy relationship is a profound teacher. It undoes belief in differences, shifts faith from separation to sameness, and leads to the recognition of the Son of God.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Allow the relationship to undo your belief in differences
2. Shift your faith from differences to sameness
3. Let reason lead you and your brother to the logical conclusion of your union
4. Extend the relationship beyond itself as you extended beyond the body
5. Allow the sameness beneath all differences to become apparent
6. Enter the golden circle where you recognize the Son of God

### Key Principles
- The holy relationship teaches by undoing illusions
- It transforms faith from separation to unity
- Reason supports the logical conclusion of union
- The relationship must extend beyond itself to fulfill its purpose
- Sameness lies beneath all perceived differences
- Recognition of the Son of God occurs within this relationship
- What is born into a holy relationship is eternal and can never end

### Best Practices
- Study the teachings of your holy relationships
- Practice reasoning from sameness rather than difference
- Allow the relationship to grow and extend beyond its current limits
- Look for the Son of God in every relationship

### Common Pitfalls
- Resisting the undoing of differences
- Clinging to the uniqueness of errors
- Failing to extend the relationship's reach
- Not recognizing the eternal nature of what holy relationships produce

---

## Summary

Chapter 22: Salvation and the Holy Relationship introduces the concept that salvation comes through holy relationships where perceived differences are undone. The unholy relationship is based on lack and exploitation, while the holy relationship begins from inner completeness and leads to the recognition of the Son of God. Through holy relationships, sin is forgiven, the need for sin disappears, and we move closer to Heaven.
