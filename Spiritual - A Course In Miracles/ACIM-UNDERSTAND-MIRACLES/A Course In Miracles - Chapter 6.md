## Lesson 1: Understanding the Relationship Between Anger, Fear, and Attack

### Lesson Title
The Hidden Connection Between Anger and Fear

### Key Principles
- The relationship of anger to attack is obvious, but the relationship of anger to fear is not always so apparent.
- Anger always involves projection of separation.
- Separation must ultimately be accepted as one's own responsibility, rather than being blamed on others.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize when anger arises in your experience.
2. Look beyond the obvious connection to attack and examine the hidden connection to fear.
3. Identify the projection of separation within the anger.
4. Accept responsibility for this projection rather than blaming others.

### Best Practices
- Practice self-reflection when experiencing strong emotions.
- Distinguish between surface-level reactions (attack) and underlying causes (fear/separation).
- Take ownership of your mental projections.

### Common Pitfalls
- Blaming others for your feelings of separation.
- Failing to recognize that anger is a projection rather than a response to external events.
- Assuming anger is always justified by external circumstances.

## Lesson 2: The Three Irrational Premises of Anger

### Lesson Title
Deconstructing the False Foundations of Anger

### Key Principles
- Anger cannot occur unless you believe three wholly irrational premises:
  1. You have been attacked
  2. Your attack is justified in return
  3. You are in no way responsible for it
- Given these premises, the equally irrational conclusion follows: a brother is worthy of attack rather than of love.
- Insane premises produce insane conclusions.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. When anger arises, examine the three underlying beliefs:
   - Do I believe I have been attacked?
   - Do I believe my retaliation is justified?
   - Do I believe I bear no responsibility?
2. Recognize these as irrational premises.
3. Undo the insane conclusion by reconsidering the sanity of the premises.
4. Replace irrational premises with truth: You cannot be attacked, attack has no justification, and you are responsible for what you believe.

### Best Practices
- Develop the habit of examining beliefs before reacting.
- Question the validity of premises that lead to anger.
- Practice replacing false beliefs with their truthful counterparts.

### Common Pitfalls
- Accepting premises without examination.
- Believing that feeling angry means the premises are valid.
- Trying to change conclusions without addressing underlying premises.

## Lesson 3: The Three Fundamental Truths That Undo Anger

### Lesson Title
The Foundation of Sanity: Three Unshakable Truths

### Key Principles
1. **You cannot be attacked** - No true assault upon the self is possible.
2. **Attack has no justification** - No circumstance makes attack reasonable or valid.
3. **You are responsible for what you believe** - Your perceptions and interpretations are your own responsibility.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Memorize or internalize the three truths: You cannot be attacked, attack has no justification, you are responsible for what you believe.
2. When you notice anger, apply these truths as a mental filter.
3. Ask yourself: "Given these truths, does my anger make sense?"
4. Let these truths replace the irrational premises that fueled the anger.

### Best Practices
- Regular meditation or reflection on these three truths.
- Use these as affirmations during moments of emotional disturbance.
- Apply them systematically to thought patterns and relationships.

### Common Pitfalls
- Treating these as abstract concepts rather than lived realities.
- Forgetting to apply them in the heat of the moment.
- Believing that external circumstances justify abandoning these truths.

## Lesson 4: The Responsibility to Teach Through Example

### Lesson Title
You Are Always Teaching: The Power of Example and Thought Systems

### Key Principles
- Everyone teaches all the time, whether consciously or unconsciously.
- This responsibility is inevitable the moment you accept any premise at all.
- No one can organize their life without some thought system.
- Once you develop a thought system, you live by it and teach it.
- Your capacity for allegiance to a thought system may be misplaced, but it is still a form of faith.
- Faith in a thought system can be redirected.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that you are always teaching through your thoughts, words, and actions.
2. Examine your current thought system: What beliefs do you live by?
3. Assess whether your thought system is aligned with love or fear.
4. If misplaced, consciously redirect your faith toward a thought system of love and truth.
5. Use your life as a classroom for others by embodying the principles you wish to see in the world.

### Best Practices
- Regularly audit your thought system for alignment with love.
- Choose carefully what you give your allegiance and faith to.
- Recognize that your influence extends beyond conscious intention.
- Seek to be a conscious teacher of peace rather than an unconscious teacher of fear.

### Common Pitfalls
- Believing you are not influencing others.
- Assuming teaching requires verbal instruction.
- Remaining attached to thought systems that no longer serve you.
- Underestimating the power of example over words.
