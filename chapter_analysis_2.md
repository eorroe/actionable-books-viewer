The path to understanding others leads through understanding yourself, and the way out of suffering is through coming home to your own breath and body.

## Actionable Lessons

### 1. Stop and Communicate with Yourself
- When you feel disconnected from yourself, stop whatever you are doing, sit down, and connect with yourself
- This practice is called mindful awareness
- In just a few seconds of sitting and breathing, you can connect with your body, feelings, emotions, and perceptions

### 2. Write with Understanding and Compassion
- When you cannot communicate well in person, write a letter or email full of understanding and compassion
- During the time of writing, you nourish yourself even before the other person receives it
- Especially at the beginning of your practice, mindful communication in writing may be easier than in person

### 3. Pause Before Sending or Speaking
- Before pressing send on a text or email, or before picking up the phone, take at least one in-breath and out-breath
- This pause increases the chance of putting more compassionate communication out into the world

### 4. Go Home to Yourself Through Mindful Breathing
- Home is inside you; going home requires only sitting down and being with yourself, accepting the situation as it is
- Even if it is messy inside, accept it because you have been away for a long time
- Begin to tidy up your home with your in-breath and out-breath

### 5. Practice Mindful Breathing as Communication
- Mindful breathing promotes communication between the mind and the body
- Follow your in-breath and out-breath all the way through
- If your in-breath lasts four seconds, allow your attention to rest entirely on it without interruption
- During breathing out, focus entirely on your out-breath

### 6. Use the Breathing Gatha
- Silently say to yourself while breathing:
  - "Breathing in, I know I'm breathing in."
  - "Breathing out, I know I'm breathing out."
- Follow the air entering and leaving your body throughout the entire breath

### 7. Turn Off Your Phone for Self-Communication
- To communicate with yourself, leave your telephone behind
- Turn off your phone when attending a meeting or event, and do the same when communicating with yourself
- Communication with yourself is not possible with the phone

### 8. Practice Nonthinking and Nontalking
- Happiness is possible when you are in communication with yourself, which requires leaving your telephone behind
- Cut off the paths of talking and thinking, as the Zen tradition teaches
- When you stop thinking and talking and listen mindfully to yourself, you notice your greater capacity for joy
- You also notice suffering present in your life that you had hidden under talking and texting

### 9. Don't Run Away from Suffering
- When feelings of suffering come up, don't try to run away from them
- Continue to follow your in-breath and out-breath
- Don't tell fear or anger to go away; recognize them, acknowledge them
- Treat feelings like a small child tugging at your sleeves: pick them up and hold them tenderly
- Acknowledging feelings without judging them or pushing them away is an act of homecoming

### 10. Generate Enough Mindfulness Energy
- Practice mindful breathing and walking to generate enough energy of mindfulness to recognize and take care of suffering
- You are not afraid of being overwhelmed because you know how to breathe and walk to generate mindfulness energy
- If you have enough mindfulness, you are no longer afraid to be with yourself

### 11. Make Time to Sit Daily
- Make time to sit down and do nothing, even if only for a few minutes a day
- Sitting is a pleasure and nurtures the habit of practice
- When you are restless and don't know what to do, that is a good time to sit down
- When you are peaceful, sitting is also good as a way of nurturing the practice

### 12. Practice Communicating with Your Body
- When you sit and breathe mindfully, your mind and body finally get to communicate and come together
- Say to yourself:
  - "Breathing in, I'm aware of my body."
  - "Breathing out, I release all the tension in my body."
- Take breaks and return to your body before it becomes too achy, stiff, or tense to ignore

### 13. Practice Mindful Walking
- When you walk mindfully, integrate your breath with each step and focus on your foot connecting with the ground
- Be aware that you are making a step, and stop thinking altogether
- Focus your attention on making the step; in that time your mind is only with the step, and you are free
- While walking, you can say to yourself: "I have arrived. I am home."
- These words are a realization, not just an affirmation
- If you haven't arrived one hundred percent, stop where you are and breathe until you are sure you have arrived, then continue

### 14. Walk to Heal Alienation
- When you take a step with full awareness that you are stepping on the ground and the earth, there is no distinction between body and mind
- Every step brings you home to the here and now
- When you walk mindfully, you don't need a device to tell you how many friends you have or how many steps you have walked
- Walk from the parking lot to your office going home in each step; recover yourself and connect with yourself during every step

### 15. Listen Deeply to Your Own Suffering
- Suffering has been trying to communicate with you; listen to it
- When you feel loneliness, sadness, fear, or anxiety, continue following your in-breath and out-breath
- Don't push these feelings away; embrace them with mindfulness
- If you understand the nature and roots of your suffering, the path leading to its cessation will appear

### 16. Understand Ancestral Suffering
- Understand that suffering inside you contains the suffering of your fathers, mothers, and ancestors
- If you transform your suffering, you heal your parents and ancestors as well as yourself
- Getting in touch with suffering passed down to you helps you understand your own suffering

### 17. Practice Loving Speech to Transform Relationships
- If you can see your own suffering and your responsibility in creating it, you can see the suffering in the other person
- Use loving speech: acknowledge your part, express that you didn't understand, and ask to understand their difficulties
- This kind of communication can transform relationships filled with resentment and arguments

### 18. Cultivate Self-Understanding Before Understanding Others
- Ask yourself: "Do I understand myself enough? Do I understand my suffering and its roots?"
- If you can't accept yourself, if you hate yourself and get angry with yourself, you cannot love another person
- Once you understand your own suffering, you suffer less and can more easily understand another person's suffering

## Key Concepts and Principles

1. **Mindful Awareness**: Full awareness of the present moment. It is the foundation of self-communication. You don't need a device; you just need to sit down and breathe in and out.

2. **Home Is Inside You**: Home is the place where loneliness disappears. Going home requires only sitting down, being with yourself, and accepting the situation as it is.

3. **Mindful Breathing as Communication**: Mindful breathing is a means of communication between the mind and the body, just like a phone. It helps us know what we are feeling.

4. **Nonthinking and Nontalking**: To communicate with ourselves, we need to practice nonthinking and nontalking. The paths of talking and thinking should be cut off, as in the Zen tradition. This creates space to truly listen to ourselves.

5. **Suffering as Signal**: Suffering has been trying to communicate with us. When we stop running and listen, we can understand its nature and transform it.

6. **Ancestral Suffering**: The suffering inside us contains the suffering of our ancestors. Transforming our suffering heals them as well.

7. **Listening Deeply**: Avoiding suffering through consumption is common, but listening deeply to our own suffering brings about the birth of compassion and love.

8. **Suffering and Happiness Are Interconnected**: Understanding suffering brings compassion and understanding happiness. A lotus needs mud to grow; compassion is born from understanding suffering.

9. **Self-Understanding Before Other-Understanding**: If we haven't understood our own suffering and perceptions, we cannot understand another person's suffering. Self-understanding is crucial for understanding others.

10. **Self-Love as Basis for Loving Others**: Self-love is crucial for loving others. When you understand your own suffering, you suffer less and can more easily understand another person's suffering.

11. **The Way In Is the Way Out**: Once you can communicate with yourself, you will be able to communicate outwardly with more clarity. Internal communication is the foundation for external communication.

12. **Freedom Through Breath**: Freedom is available with each conscious breath. It is the foundation of happiness. Real freedom comes when we release our suffering and come home.

## Examples Used in the Chapter

1. **The Disconnected Life (PDF page 9)**: We walk but don't know we are walking. We are here but don't know we are here. We are alive but don't know we are alive. This illustrates how disconnected we are from ourselves.

2. **Writing a Compassionate Letter (PDF pages 9-10)**: When you write a letter full of understanding and compassion, you nourish yourself during the time of writing. The other person hasn't received it yet, but you are already nourished.

3. **The Phone as Communication Tool (PDF page 10)**: Next time you hold your phone, remember its purpose is to help you communicate with compassion, not to rush mindless messages.

4. **Home as Inside Us (PDF page 10)**: Home is the place where loneliness disappears. We have gone away from our homes for a long time, but the path back is not long because home is inside us.

5. **Breath as a Means of Communication (PDF page 10)**: Mindful breathing promotes communication between the mind and the body, just like a phone.

6. **Nelson Mandela's Wish (PDF page 13)**: When Nelson Mandela was freed from prison and came to France, a journalist asked what he would most like to do. He said, "Sit down and do nothing." This illustrates the value of simply sitting.

7. **The Body-Mind Reunion (PDF page 14)**: When you breathe in mindfully, there is a happy reunion between body and mind. Usually the mind is in one place and the body in another, but mindful breathing brings them together.

8. **The Step to Freedom (PDF page 15)**: Every mindful step brings you home to the here and now. You take one step, and you are free. You don't need a device to tell you whether you have arrived; you will know because you are comfortable being.

9. **The Woman from Washington, D.C. (PDF pages 18-19)**: A woman planned to commit suicide because of suffering in her relationship. After listening to a talk on deep listening and loving speech, she attended a retreat, learned to listen to her own suffering, discovered her own responsibility, and used loving speech to transform her marriage.

10. **Lotus and Mud (PDF page 18)**: If a lotus is to grow, it needs to be rooted in the mud. Compassion is born from understanding suffering. This illustrates that suffering is necessary for the growth of happiness and compassion.

## Best Practices Mentioned

1. **Stop and breathe before communicating**: Take at least one in-breath and out-breath before picking up the phone or pressing send

2. **Practice mindful awareness daily**: Sit down and connect with yourself for just a few seconds or minutes

3. **Write mindfully**: Use emails, texts, and letters as opportunities for nourishing communication

4. **Turn off your phone for inner communication**: Don't try to communicate with yourself through a device

5. **Practice mindful breathing throughout the day**: Follow your in-breath and out-breath; use the gatha to remind yourself

6. **Make time to sit**: Even a few minutes a day of sitting and doing nothing nurtures the practice

7. **Practice mindful walking**: Integrate breath with each step, stop thinking, and say "I have arrived. I am home."

8. **Don't run from suffering**: When suffering arises, stay with your breath and acknowledge it without judgment

9. **Hold suffering tenderly**: Treat feelings like a small child tugging at your sleeves; pick them up and embrace them

10. **Generate mindfulness energy**: Practice breathing and walking to generate enough energy to be with your suffering

11. **Listen deeply to your own suffering before trying to help others**: Understanding your own suffering is the prerequisite for understanding others

12. **Communicate with your body**: Pause to release tension and bring mind and body together

13. **Connect with the earth while walking**: Feel the ground beneath your feet as part of healing alienation

14. **Use loving speech**: When transforming a relationship, acknowledge your own responsibility and ask to understand the other's suffering

15. **Don't be too sure you understand others**: Always check whether you understand yourself first

## Common Pitfalls Mentioned

1. **Living disconnected from yourself**: Walking, being here, and being alive without knowing it; losing yourself throughout the day

2. **Rushing communication**: Sending emails and texts immediately after writing them without taking a mindful breath

3. **Using technology for self-communication**: Believing you can communicate with yourself through a phone or device

4. **Overthinking and overtalking**: Dwelling on the past, trying to control the future, generating misperceptions, and worrying about what others are thinking

5. **Speaking or acting on misperceptions**: A misperception can happen in a flash, and anything said or done based on it can be dangerous

6. **Covering up suffering**: Using consumption, eating when not hungry, watching bad TV, and constantly checking devices to avoid encountering inner suffering

7. **Running from suffering**: Trying to get away from or avoid suffering instead of listening to and understanding it

8. **Judging or pushing away feelings**: Telling fear or anger to go away instead of acknowledging them

9. **Being overwhelmed by suffering**: Fearing that if you encounter suffering, you will be overwhelmed, when in fact mindfulness protects you

10. **Neglecting the body**: Spending hours at the computer and forgetting you have a body until it becomes too painful to ignore

11. **Thinking while walking**: If you think while you walk, you get lost in thinking and don't know what is going on in your body, feelings, or the world

12. **Believing you understand others without self-understanding**: Thinking you know and understand loved ones without having understood your own suffering and perceptions

13. **Hating yourself**: If you cannot accept yourself, you cannot love another person

14. **Trying to prove yourself right or feel better**: Communication based on these desires rather than understanding causes more suffering

15. **Isolation from earth and body**: Living in a way that alienates you from the earth and your own body
