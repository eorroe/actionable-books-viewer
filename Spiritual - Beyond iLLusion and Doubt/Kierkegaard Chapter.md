# BID 7: Kierkegaard — Actionable Lessons Analysis
*Source: Beyond Illusion & Doubt (Bhaktivedanta Vedabase)*

---

## Lesson 1: Accept a Bona Fide Spiritual Master to Understand God

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that religious truth is not innate within man—it must be received from God through an authorized teacher.
2. Understand that God comes as His own servant in human form (the spiritual master) because people would be overawed if God came directly as He is.
3. Approach a bona fide guru who is fully trained in the ocean of spiritual knowledge (Vedic knowledge) and is no longer interested in material things.
4. Surrender unto the spiritual master, serve him, and inquire from him about God and your relationship with God.
5. Accept the spiritual master as God's representative—not as someone who simply juggles words for money or fame.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Guru** — a bona fide spiritual master who is God's representative and fully trained in spiritual knowledge
- **Bona fide spiritual master** — one who has no material desires and has taken shelter of the Supreme Lord
- **Brahma-jijñāsā** — the inquisitiveness to understand the Absolute, possible only in the human form
- **Descending path** — receiving knowledge from God through the spiritual master
- **Authorized teacher** — one who is fully trained in Vedic knowledge and acts as God's servant
- **God's representative** — the spiritual master who reveals God's will and instructions

## Lesson 2: Advance Through the Three Stages of Life to the Religious Platform

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize the three basic stages of life: the aesthetic stage, the ethical stage, and the religious stage.
2. Understand that the aesthetic stage involves hedonism (sensual pleasure) or philosophical speculation—both are uncommitted and lack an ultimate goal.
3. Recognize that despair is the first stepping-stone toward self-realization—when the aesthetic life ends in hopelessness, abandon it for the next stage.
4. On the ethical stage, make active choices rather than aimlessly seeking pleasure; do good deeds and act piously.
5. Understand that mere decision-making without a basis in truth has no real value—you must know the proper decision to make.
6. Advance to the religious stage by submitting yourself to God and obeying Him totally—this is the topmost stage of life (Kṛṣṇa consciousness).

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Aesthetic stage** — seeking sensual pleasure or philosophical speculation without commitment or ultimate goal
- **Ethical stage** — active moral decision-making and humanitarian deeds
- **Religious stage** — complete surrender to God and total obedience; the highest stage of life
- **Despair** — the result of an empty, goal-less life; a catalyst for seeking self-realization
- **Brahma-jijñāsā** — the natural inquiry into the ultimate truth that arises from despair
- **Kṛṣṇa consciousness** — the topmost stage of life, complete surrender to God

## Lesson 3: Confront Despair and Use It as a Catalyst for Spiritual Advancement

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that despair arises from impersonalism and voidism—when one has no goal in life, disappointment and despair follow.
2. Recognize that despair can be a springboard to higher consciousness—when you realize the material world offers no lasting happiness, turn to spiritual life.
3. Understand that Krishna sometimes creates situations of material loss to remove impediments to devotional service.
4. When you despair of all material happiness, you are relieved of responsibility and can fully engage in Krishna's devotional service.
5. Accept that despair of material life is actually a great happiness (nirāśaṁ paramaṁ sukham) because it frees you to seek the Supreme.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Despair** — hopelessness arising from a life without spiritual goals
- **Nirāśaṁ paramaṁ sukham** — "When one despairs, that is a great happiness"
- **Impersonalism** — the philosophy that God has no personal form, leading to despair
- **Voidism** — the philosophy that ultimate reality is void or nothingness
- **Material impediments** — attachments that Krishna removes to facilitate surrender
- **Springboard to higher consciousness** — using despair as motivation for spiritual life

## Lesson 4: Make Decisions Based on Eternal Truth, Not Whim or Relative Ethics

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that the basis of your decision is more important than the decision itself—without knowing the aim of life, you cannot make the right decision.
2. Recognize that "choosing yourself" has no value unless you know your real self—you must know yourself before you can choose yourself.
3. Inquire from a bona fide spiritual master who knows the self and the Supreme Self perfectly.
4. Do not decide by whim, inward passion, or relative ethics—what one person considers good, another may consider bad.
5. Anticipate death in every act—consider each act as your last—so that you will naturally make the right decisions.
6. Decide to put a permanent end to suffering—birth, old age, disease, and death—rather than making trivial decisions about temporary matters.
7. Make your decisions on the basis of the soul, not the temporary material body.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Whim** — arbitrary, unmotivated decision-making without knowledge of the goal
- **Relative ethics** — moral standards that change according to personal opinion
- **Anticipate death** — considering every act as one's last to ensure proper decisions
- **Aim of life** — the ultimate goal of returning to Godhead; necessary for right decision-making
- **Self-knowledge** — understanding one's real identity as spirit soul, not body
- **Soul-based decisions** — making choices based on eternal spiritual identity, not temporary bodily concerns

## Lesson 5: Submit to God Totally and Obey Him Completely

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that the religious stage of life means submitting yourself to God and obeying Him totally.
2. Recognize that Krishna demands full surrender: abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Him (sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja).
3. Accept that Krishna will deliver you from all sinful reactions and guide you back to Godhead when you surrender completely.
4. Give up all your own plans and the plans of others, and accept God's plan instead.
5. Obey the orders of Krishna absolutely—this is your only real choice: either abide by Krishna's will or defy it.
6. After you decide to obey Krishna, He or His representative makes all other decisions.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Total surrender** — complete submission to God's will, abandoning all other duties
- **Sarva-dharmān parityajya** — "Abandon all varieties of religion" (Bhagavad-gītā 18.66)
- **Obedience** — carrying out God's orders absolutely without deviation
- **God's plan** — accepting Krishna's arrangement instead of one's own plans
- **Krishna's will** — the supreme will of the Lord that must be obeyed
- **Religious stage** — the highest platform of life, characterized by total surrender to God

## Lesson 6: Understand That True Religious Life Does Not Require Intense Suffering

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Reject the misconception that the deeply religious life must involve intense suffering comparable to Job's suffering.
2. Understand that suffering arises from identifying with the material body—when you know you are not the body, you do not suffer when the body is injured or dies.
3. Recognize that for one who is fully Krishna conscious, there is no suffering—only transcendental bliss—even if bodily pain is present.
4. Understand that penance and austerity do not require suffering for those advanced in knowledge—they simply do their duty and chant Hare Krishna.
5. Do not equate material renunciation with suffering—Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmīs appeared to suffer materially but were actually merged in transcendental bliss.
6. Avoid the Christian ascetic misconception that abandoning worldly life means abandoning pleasure and taking on suffering.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Intense suffering** — Kierkegaard's idea that religious life requires suffering; rejected as wrong theory
- **Body identification** — the cause of suffering; when one identifies with matter, one suffers
- **Transcendental bliss** — the constant happiness of a Krishna conscious person beyond bodily pain
- **Material renunciation** — giving up material comforts without suffering when done with knowledge
- **Ignorance** — the root cause of suffering; knowledge of the self eliminates suffering
- **Christian asceticism** — the misconception that spirituality requires bodily suffering

## Lesson 7: Learn to Love God Unconditionally, Without Ulterior Motives

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that love of God must be unconditional—love God in unconditional obedience and adoration, not for any personal gain.
2. Recognize that material "love" is actually lust—everyone in the material world is interested only in their own sense gratification.
3. Understand that pure love means not thinking of one's own sense gratification but of the sense gratification of the beloved (God).
4. Accept that love of God is the decisive factor in spiritual life, and from it stems love of all living entities.
5. When you love God above all else, you naturally love your neighbor and every living being—because everything is God's expansion.
6. Recognize that the test of a first-class religion is whether its followers have learned to love God.
7. Express love for God through admiration, service, friendship, parenthood, or conjugal relationship—there are five basic relationships of pure love for God.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Unconditional love** — loving God without any desire for personal return or benefit
- **Pure love (prema)** — love of God free from all ulterior motives; anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam
- **Lust** — material "love" based on sense gratification; the opposite of pure love
- **Sense gratification** — seeking one's own pleasure rather than the pleasure of the beloved
- **Five relationships with God** — śānta (neutral admiration), dāsya (service), sakhya (friendship), vātsalya (parental affection), and mādhurya (conjugal love)
- **Love of neighbor** — extending God's love to all living entities because everything is God's expansion

## Lesson 8: Practice Prayer to Understand God's Will and Receive His Directions

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that the true success in prayer is not when God hears what is prayed for, but when the person praying continues to pray until he hears what God wills.
2. Recognize that through prayer one becomes qualified to understand God, talk with God, and receive His directions.
3. Engage in the nine processes of devotional service (śravaṇaṁ, kīrtanaṁ, smaraṇam, pāda-sevanam, arcanaṁ, vandanaṁ, dāsyaṁ, sakhyam, ātma-nivedanam) to become purified.
4. Understand that God gives buddhi-yoga (divine understanding) to those who are constantly devoted to serving Him with love.
5. Accept that prayer is just one form of devotional service—any one of the nine processes can help you progress in spiritual life.
6. When you offer God sincere service, He takes charge and gives you directions by which you can swiftly approach Him.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Prayer** — communicating with God to understand His will and receive His directions
- **Nine processes of devotional service** — hearing, chanting, remembering, serving the feet, worshiping, praying, becoming servant, becoming friend, and surrendering
- **Buddhi-yoga** — divine understanding given by God to His devotees
- **God's will** — the supreme direction of the Lord that the devotee seeks through prayer
- **Purification** — the result of offering service to God, enabling one to see and talk with Him personally
- **Śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ** — hearing and chanting about Lord Vishnu (Krishna)

## Lesson 9: Stand Transparent Before God Through Engaged Devotional Service

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that standing transparent before God means engaging in His service—this is true self-realization.
2. Recognize that every living entity is meant to engage in the service of God, just as each part of the body serves the entire body.
3. Understand that as soon as you engage in Krishna's service, you are self-realized and liberated from the miseries of material life.
4. Avoid the mistake of thinking that self-realization comes from mental speculation, fruitive work, or yoga without serving the Supreme Self (Krishna).
5. Accept that your only real decision is to serve Krishna—all other decisions come from Him or His representative after you surrender.
6. Engage in practical service to Krishna as the means to stand transparent before Him.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Standing transparent before God** — engaging in pure devotional service without material motives
- **Self-realization** — understanding one's real identity as servant of God and engaging in His service
- **Service to God** — the natural function of every living entity; the essence of spiritual life
- **Mukti** — liberation from material miseries achieved through engagement in Krishna's service
- **Part and parcel of God** — the living entity's eternal position as a dependent servant of the Supreme
- **Supreme Self** — Krishna, the ultimate object of service and the only true self

## Lesson 10: Surrender Fully to Krishna—He Reciprocates According to Your Surrender

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that full will means surrendering to Krishna fully—to obey the orders of the Supreme absolutely.
2. Recognize that the only real choice you have is to abide by Krishna's will or to defy it—once you decide to obey Krishna, He or His representative makes all other decisions.
3. Accept that God reciprocates with His devotees according to how they surrender—He responds and cooperates accordingly (Bhagavad-gītā 4.11: "As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly").
4. Become an instrument of God—it is in your interest to become His instrument, even though He can accomplish His will without your help.
5. Accept that no one can check what God decides—His will is supreme and unstoppable.
6. After surrendering to Krishna, accept that He will guide you back to Him and deliver you from all sinful reactions.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Full surrender** — complete submission to Krishna's will, giving up all personal plans
- **Instrument of God** — becoming a tool in God's hands for executing His will
- **Reciprocation** — God's response to the devotee's surrender
- **Bhagavad-gītā 4.11** — "As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly"
- **God's will** — the supreme determination of the Lord that is unstoppable
- **Krishna's guidance** — the personal direction given by the Lord to His surrendered devotees

## Lesson 11: Despair of Material Happiness to Strengthen Your Faith in God

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that to strengthen your faith in God, you must give up all hope of happiness in this material life.
2. Recognize that Krishna sometimes creates situations of material loss to remove impediments to your devotional service.
3. When Krishna smashes your material resources, do not think He is being unkind—realize this is His mercy, removing all obstacles so you can fully and absolutely surrender.
4. When you despair of material happiness, you become relieved of all responsibility and can turn fully to spiritual life.
5. Accept that despair of material life is actually a great happiness because it frees you from the illusion of material enjoyment.
6. Use the example of Indra, who had to be forced into despair (losing his hog family) before he would return to the heavenly kingdom—sometimes Krishna creates desperate situations to bring us back to Him.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Despair of material happiness** — giving up all hope of finding lasting pleasure in this world
- **Krishna's mercy** — material setbacks that force the living entity to turn to God
- **Material impediments** — attachments that block devotional service
- **Nirāśaṁ paramaṁ sukham** — "When one despairs, that is a great happiness"
- **Faith in God** — strengthened by rejecting material hopes and turning to the Supreme
- **Indra's example** — the Lord of heaven who had to lose everything as a hog before returning to God

## Lesson 12: Follow the Trio — Saintly Persons, Scriptures, and the Spiritual Master

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that to know God's will, you need more than scripture and conscience—you must also associate with saintly persons and follow the instructions of a bona fide spiritual master.
2. Accept the threefold process: sādhu (saintly persons), śāstra (Vedic scriptures), and guru (spiritual master).
3. Understand that these three corroborate one another: a sādhu talks and acts in terms of the scriptures; the guru is a sādhu who personally teaches according to the scriptures.
4. Recognize that a guru cannot manufacture words that are not in the scriptures—he simply presents what God has spoken.
5. Do not accept just anyone's proclamations about God—statements must be corroborated by the standard scriptures.
6. When you receive instructions from all three (sādhu, śāstra, guru), you can progress perfectly in understanding the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Sādhu** — a saintly person who talks and acts in terms of the scriptures
- **Śāstra** — Vedic scriptures that contain God's instructions and the standard for truth
- **Guru** — a sādhu who personally teaches disciples according to the scriptures
- **Sādhu-śāstra-guru** — the three authorities that corroborate one another in revealing God's will
- **Scriptural corroboration** — the requirement that spiritual teachings align with revealed scripture
- **Tattva-darśī guru** — a spiritual master who has seen the truth and teaches accordingly

## Lesson 13: Renounce Everything as an Act of Worship to God

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that worship begins with the renunciation of ulterior motives—do not love God or serve Him for personal gain.
2. Recognize that your only business is to love God, and a first-class religious system teaches its followers to love God without ulterior motive.
3. Renounce all attachment to material possessions, status, and relationships as an offering to God.
4. Accept that such worship cannot be checked by material considerations—in any condition you can love God, and He will help you to love Him.
5. Do not worship God for material benefits—worship Him purely because He is the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
6. Accept that when you renounce everything for God, He takes charge of your life and provides everything you need.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Renunciation** — giving up material possessions and motives as an offering to God
- **Ulterior motives** — desires for personal gain hidden behind religious or spiritual activity
- **First-class religion** — a system that teaches love of God without material motives
- **Worship** — serving and loving God purely, without expectation of material return
- **Material considerations** — external conditions that should not check one's devotional service
- **Renunciation of everything** — giving up all attachments to serve God exclusively

## Lesson 14: Approach God Immediately Through the Bona Fide Spiritual Master

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that you do not need to pass through lower stages of life (aesthetic or ethical) to reach God—you can approach Him directly through the spiritual master.
2. Accept the process described in Bhagavad-gītā 4.34: approach a spiritual master, inquire from him submissively, and render service unto him.
3. Understand that the self-realized soul can impart knowledge to you because he has seen the truth.
4. Do not continue on your own way, hoping to find the right path through experience—if you do not know the direction, your endeavors will be frustrated.
5. Accept the spiritual master as the captain of your ship in the ocean of material existence—he gives the direction you need.
6. If you accept Christ, Krishna, or any genuine representative of God, you are accepting some guidance—take that guidance seriously and follow it.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Direct approach** — going straight to God through the spiritual master without passing through preliminary stages
- **Bhagavad-gītā 4.34** — "Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto Him."
- **Tad vijñānārthaṁ sa gurum evābhigacchet** — "To learn the truth, one must approach a bona fide spiritual master"
- **Self-realized soul** — a tattva-darśī who has seen the truth and can impart it to others
- **Captain of the ship** — the spiritual master who gives direction in the ocean of material existence
- **Frustrated endeavors** — wasted effort when one tries to advance without proper guidance

## Lesson 15: Recognize That Truth Is Absolute, Not Relative or Subjective

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that truth is truth, and it is absolute—there is one Absolute Truth, not many relative truths.
2. Recognize that emphasizing relative truths without knowing the Absolute Truth leads to being misled.
3. Do not accept Kierkegaard's idea that truth is relative and subjective, discovered only through personal inward passion.
4. Understand that passion (activity) without knowing the ultimate goal is useless—you may drive your car, but if you do not know where to go, you simply waste your energy.
5. Accept that the Absolute Truth is Krishna (mattaḥ parataraṁ nānyat kiñcid asti dhanañjaya—"There is no truth superior to Me").
6. Know the goal before you act—unless you know the fixed point (God), your action and energy may be misdirected.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Absolute Truth** — the one supreme reality, Krishna, not subject to relativity
- **Relative truth** — temporary, changing truths that have no lasting value without reference to the Absolute
- **Inward passion** — Kierkegaard's method of discovering truth through personal reflection; insufficient without knowledge of the Absolute
- **Goal of life** — Krishna consciousness; necessary for proper action
- **Fixed point** — God as the unchanging goal that gives direction to all activity
- **Bhagavad-gītā 7.7** — "There is no truth superior to Me. Everything rests upon Me, as pearls are strung on a thread."

## Lesson 16: Practice Proper Action, Not Mere Speculation

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that spiritual life means proper action, not mere speculative thought or intellectual games.
2. Recognize that when you attain the perfectional stage, you do not become inactive—the soul is never inactive.
3. Understand that inactivity is recommended only when one cannot speak or act intelligently—this is not the same as spiritual perfection.
4. Reject the impersonalist (Māyāvādī) idea that when one becomes spiritually "full" one becomes silent or inactive like a full jug that makes no sound.
5. Engage in practical devotional service to Krishna—this is the proper action of spiritual life.
6. Do not think that philosophy is only an intellectual or academic affair—it must be a way of life based on direct touch with the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Proper action** — practical devotional service to Krishna, the essence of spiritual life
- **Speculation** — mental gymnastics without practical application; useless for understanding God
- **Impersonalism (Māyāvāda)** — the philosophy that the soul merges into Brahman and becomes inactive
- **Jug analogy** — the Māyāvādī idea that a full jug makes no sound; equated with spiritual inactivity
- **Philosophy as way of life** — living according to spiritual principles, not mere intellectual study
- **Active spiritual life** — constant engagement in Krishna's service, not passive contemplation

## Lesson 17: Understand Your Individuality as an Eternal Part of God

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that God is the origin and wellspring of all individuality—your individuality is a gift from God.
2. Understand that qualitatively you are the same as God (both are eternal conscious living beings), but quantitatively you are different (God is infinite, you are infinitesimal).
3. Accept that your natural position is to serve God and love Him, just as each part of the body serves the entire body.
4. Do not identify yourself with your body, nation, family, or community—these are false identifications.
5. Understand that you are an eternal individual part of God (Krishna), meant to engage in His service.
6. Recognize that you are in a constant state of becoming—but the becoming process ends when you reach Krishna; then you simply enjoy spiritual variety.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Individuality** — the unique, eternal identity of the living entity as a part of God
- **Part and parcel of God** — the living entity's constitutional position as a dependent servant of Krishna
- **Qualitatively same, quantitatively different** — the soul is like God in quality (spirit) but tiny in quantity (infinitesimal)
- **Infinitesimal** — the living entity is a tiny part of the infinite God
- **False identification** — identifying with body, nation, family, or community instead of recognizing oneself as servant of God
- **Kaṭha Upaniṣad 2.2.13** — "God is the eternal living being among all living beings; He is the conscious being among all conscious beings"

## Lesson 18: Love God and, as a Result, Love Everyone

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that God is the center of all love—when you love God, you automatically love everyone because everything is God's expansion.
2. Recognize that the test of a first-class religion is whether its followers have learned to love God.
3. Do not discriminate in your love—do not think only human beings should be loved and served; love all living entities regardless of their form.
4. Apply the analogy of watering the root of a tree: when you nourish the root, you nourish the trunk, branches, twigs, and leaves.
5. Accept that when you give food to the stomach, you satisfy the entire body—similarly, when you love God, you love everyone and everything.
6. Teach the principle of love of God to the whole world—this is the basis of the Krishna consciousness movement.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Love of God** — the central principle that automatically generates love for all living entities
- **God's expansion** — everything in the universe is a manifestation of God's energy
- **Watering the root** — serving God benefits all living beings, just as watering a tree's root nourishes the whole tree
- **Test of religion** — whether followers have learned to love God
- **Non-discrimination** — loving all living entities regardless of species or form
- **Krishna consciousness movement** — teaching the principle of love of God to the entire world

## Lesson 21: Understand That the Becoming Process Ends at Krishna

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that you are in a constant state of becoming—but the goal of all becoming is Krishna.
2. Understand that Krishna is the ultimate truth and the supreme goal (mattaḥ parataraṁ nānyat kiñcid asti dhanañjaya).
3. Accept that when you reach Krishna consciousness, the becoming process ends—you are no longer striving to become something else.
4. Understand that in the spiritual world, there are spiritual varieties, but everything is complete in itself—there is no lack or becoming.
5. Do not confuse material variety with spiritual variety—material variety is like a plastic flower (artificial, unsatisfying), while spiritual variety is like a real flower (natural, fulfilling).
6. Recognize that completeness means coming to Krishna consciousness and enjoying variety in His service without the anxiety of becoming.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Becoming process** — the endless striving of material life; ends when one reaches Krishna
- **Krishna as supreme goal** — the ultimate end of all becoming; the only truth superior to all others
- **Completeness** — the state of Krishna consciousness where all striving ceases and pure enjoyment begins
- **Spiritual variety** — natural, fulfilling variety in Krishna's service, unlike artificial material variety
- **Material variety** — temporary, unsatisfying variety in the material world; compared to a plastic flower
- **Bhagavad-gītā 7.7** — "There is no truth superior to Me. Everything rests upon Me, as pearls are strung on a thread."

## Lesson 22: Make the Leap of Faith Toward God

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Understand that anxiety and uncertainty arise from not knowing life's aim—the person who does not know the goal is always in anxiety.
2. Recognize that Kierkegaard's "leap of faith" is necessary to dispel anxiety—but the leap must be toward the proper goal.
3. Know the goal before you leap—unless you know the fixed point (God), your action and energy may be misdirected.
4. Accept that the goal is God—after passing through the aesthetic and ethical stages, use all your energy to reach God.
5. Approach God through the bona fide spiritual master—this is the proper direction for your leap of faith.
6. Do not continue on your own way, hoping to take the right path through experience—if you do not know the direction, your endeavors will be frustrated.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Leap of faith** — Kierkegaard's concept of making a decisive commitment to the religious life
- **Anxiety** — the uncertainty that arises from not knowing life's ultimate aim
- **Fixed point** — God as the unchanging goal that gives direction to spiritual life
- **Proper goal** — Krishna as the only worthy object of faith and commitment
- **Spiritual master** — the guide who gives the correct direction for the leap of faith
- **Frustrated endeavors** — wasted effort when one tries to advance without knowing the goal

## Lesson 23: Understand That Suffering Is Due to Ignorance, Not the Path to God

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Recognize that suffering arises from identifying with the material body—when you know you are not the body, you do not suffer when the body is injured or dies.
2. Understand that the Kṛṣṇa conscious person is always in full knowledge and transcendental to the material world—he never suffers, even if bodily pain is present.
3. Accept that suffering is due to ignorance—when you understand your real identity as the eternal soul, suffering ceases.
4. Do not adopt the philosophy that religious commitment requires intense suffering—this is a wrong theory.
5. Understand that for those advanced in knowledge, there is no suffering—they simply do their duty and chant Hare Krishna.
6. Reject the Christian ascetic misconception that abandoning worldly life means taking on suffering—Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmīs were not suffering despite their apparent material austerities.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Ignorance** — the root cause of suffering; identifying with the material body
- **Body identification** — mistaking the temporary material body for the eternal soul
- **Transcendental bliss** — the constant happiness of a self-realized person beyond bodily pain
- **Suffering as wrong theory** — rejecting Kierkegaard's idea that religious life requires intense suffering
- **Knowledge of the self** — understanding that one is not the body but the eternal soul
- **Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmīs** — examples of advanced devotees who appeared to suffer materially but were actually in transcendental bliss

## Lesson 24: Love God Above All Else and Love Your Neighbor Through That Love

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Make God the center of your love—love Him above all else, with unconditional obedience and adoration.
2. Understand that if you love God above all else, you will also love your neighbor and every human being.
3. Accept that to help another person love God is truly to love that person—this is the highest expression of love for others.
4. Recognize that pure love means not thinking of your own sense gratification but of the sense gratification of the beloved (God).
5. Understand that material "love" is actually lust—both parties are interested only in their own sense gratification.
6. Accept that real love applies only to God—Krishna is the supreme object of love, and this love can be expressed through admiration, service, friendship, parental affection, or conjugal relationship.
7. Teach the principle of love of God to the whole world—this is the basis of the Krishna consciousness movement.

### Key Terms and Keywords
- **Love of God above all else** — the highest duty, from which all other love flows naturally
- **Unconditional love** — loving God without any desire for personal return or benefit
- **Pure love (prema)** — love of God free from all ulterior motives; anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam
- **Lust vs. love** — material "love" is lust based on sense gratification; real love is service to God
- **Five relationships with God** — śānta, dāsya, sakhya, vātsalya, and mādhurya
- **Test of religion** — whether followers have learned to love God
