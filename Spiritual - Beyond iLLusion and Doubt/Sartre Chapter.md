# BID 14: Sartre — Actionable Lessons

## Chapter Overview
This chapter examines Jean-Paul Sartre’s existentialism through a Vedic lens. Śrīla Prabhupāda systematically challenges Sartre’s atheism, his notion of “useless passion,” and his claim that existence precedes essence, while presenting the Vedic alternative of Krishna consciousness.

---

## Lesson 1: Challenge Atheistic Claims with Sound Logic

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. When someone denies God’s existence, demand that the denial be based on logic or reason.
2. Point out the inconsistency of mentioning “God” while simultaneously claiming He does not exist.
3. Argue that if human reality exists, there is no rational basis to deny the existence of a Supreme Reality.
4. Note that the very act of denying God implies He is being considered, which contradicts the denial.

**Key terms and keywords:** Atheism, logical consistency, denial of God, existence, rationality, God’s existence

---

## Lesson 2: Accept the Existence of a Supreme Being

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. Observe that in the world there are beings of varying degrees of intelligence, power, and perfection.
2. Reason that if degrees of perfection exist, a superlative being must also exist.
3. Accept that God is the ultimate intelligence and the origin of everything—mind, intelligence, body, and circumstance.
4. Recognize that just as material nature is accepted as mother, a father (God) must also be accepted.

**Key terms and keywords:** Supreme Being, superlative, God as origin, material nature, father and mother, cause of all causes, intelligence, perfection

---

## Lesson 3: Understand That Responsibility Is Owed to God

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. Recognize that true responsibility implies someone to whom one is responsible.
2. Accept that God gives every living entity specific duties to discharge.
3. Understand that rejecting God leads to social chaos, while fulfilling one’s duty to God establishes proper order.
4. Realize that responsibility is not accidental—it is given by a higher authority.

**Key terms and keywords:** Responsibility, duty, God as Supreme Person, social order, chaos, duty to God, regulation

---

## Lesson 4: Reject Demoniac Philosophy

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. Identify philosophies that claim the world is unreal, without foundation, and without God.
2. Recognize that such philosophies assert everything is accidental—produced by sex desire alone.
3. Understand that this view is called “demoniac” because it is rooted in darkness and ignorance.
4. Reject the idea that life has no essential purpose or that existence is meaningless.

**Key terms and keywords:** Demoniac philosophy, Bhagavad-gītā 16.8, accidentalism, meaninglessness, ignorance, darkness, purpose

---

## Lesson 5: Approach a Bona Fide Spiritual Master

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. Understand that to learn transcendental science, one must humbly approach a spiritual master.
2. Seek a spiritual master who is learned in the Vedas and firmly devoted to the Absolute Truth.
3. Accept instructions from such a master rather than relying solely on personal speculation.
4. Recognize that without a qualified guru, one remains bewildered.

**Key terms and keywords:** Spiritual master (guru), Vedas, transcendental science, humility, Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.2.12, bona fide, Absolute Truth

---

## Lesson 6: Avoid the Futile Attempt to Become God

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. Understand that a conditioned soul is part and parcel of God and thus desires to be united with Him.
2. Recognize that trying to become God through mystic practice is impossible and frustrated.
3. Accept that one can become godly (servant of God) but cannot become God.
4. Decide to serve God properly rather than remain a servant of māyā (illusion).

**Key terms and keywords:** Become God, godly, servant of God, māyā, mystic practice, frustration, Acyuta, conditioned soul

---

## Lesson 7: Choose Krishna Consciousness Over Despair

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. Recognize that Sartre’s philosophy is a philosophy of despair born of poor knowledge.
2. Understand that the desire to exist eternally is natural because the soul is eternal.
3. Instead of concluding that one’s nature is “nothingness,” create one’s nature as something through Krishna consciousness.
4. Transfer yourself to a safe place free from anxiety (Vaikuṇṭha) by serving Krishna.

**Key terms and keywords:** Krishna consciousness, Vaikuṇṭha, despair, nothingness, eternal soul, liberation, anxiety, safe place

---

## Lesson 8: Make Decisions Under the Guidance of Superior Authority

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. Avoid “bad faith,” which is the self-deception of drifting without making responsible decisions.
2. Make solid, courageous choices, but do not rely solely on personal whim.
3. Accept guidance from a higher authority (God, scripture, spiritual master) rather than making arbitrary decisions.
4. Understand that without discrimination and higher authority, freedom is blind and decisions are worthless.

**Key terms and keywords:** Bad faith, self-deception, decision-making, higher authority, discrimination, good faith, guidance

---

## Lesson 9: Recognize That Nothing Is Accidental

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. Understand that changes in life take place under regulations (karma), not by accident.
2. Accept that being “thrown into the world” implies a thrower—God.
3. Recognize that freedom is conditioned by material nature when one is placed under its control.
4. Understand that one’s duty is to get liberated from material entanglement.

**Key terms and keywords:** Accident, karma, material nature, regulation, conditioned soul, liberation, prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni, Bhagavad-gītā 3.27

---

## Lesson 10: Understand the Difference Between Being God and Being Godly

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. See that Sartre contradicts himself: he denies God’s existence while simultaneously claiming man desires to be God.
2. Understand that the soul’s desire to exist eternally is natural and can be fulfilled by returning to God.
3. Accept that becoming Krishna conscious is godly, while attempting to become Krishna is demoniac.
4. Realize that the “useless passion” label applies only to those who try to become God; the attempt to be Krishna conscious is supremely useful.

**Key terms and keywords:** Desire to be God, being-for-itself, being-in-itself, useless passion, demoniac, godly, Krishna conscious

---

## Lesson 11: Serve God Personally in the Eternal Spiritual Realm

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. Understand that by fulfilling one’s duty to God, one makes spiritual progress.
2. Recognize that the ultimate goal is to live with God personally in Vaikuṇṭha.
3. Accept that religion means meeting one’s responsibility to God by fulfilling His orders.
4. Realize that this is the highest stage of life—complete surrender and obedience to God.

**Key terms and keywords:** Vaikuṇṭha, spiritual progress, personal relationship with God, religion, obedience, surrender, duty fulfillment

---

## Lesson 12: Do Not Try to Be a Hero or Coward Under Material Conditioning

**Step-by-step instructions from the text:**
1. Recognize that if one is placed under the control of a superior power (material nature), one cannot truly become a hero by one’s own effort.
2. Understand that heroism and cowardice are mental concoctions when one is under material control.
3. See that a real hero is one who is fully empowered or protected by Krishna—a devotee.
4. Accept that saving the temporary material body is not true heroism; true heroism lies in Krishna consciousness.

**Key terms and keywords:** Hero, coward, material conditioning, devotee, Krishna consciousness, protection, mental concoction, superior power
