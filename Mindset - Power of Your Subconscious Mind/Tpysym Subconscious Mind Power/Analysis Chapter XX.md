Chapter XX: How To Stay Young In Spirit Forever
From: The Power of Your Subconscious Mind by Dr. Joseph Murphy

## ACTIONABLE LESSONS WITH STEP-BY-STEP INSTRUCTIONS

1. Recognize that your subconscious mind is timeless, ageless, and endless. Accept that fatigue and old age cannot be predicated on any spiritual quality or power.

2. Continually generate and express qualities that never grow old: patience, kindness, veracity (truthfulness), humility, good will, peace, harmony, and brotherly love. By doing so, you will always remain young in spirit.

3. Release the belief that years alone cause degenerative disorders. Understand that it is the neurotic fear of time, not time itself, that causes premature aging. Let go of fear-based thinking about aging.

4. Reframe your understanding of age: Age is not the flight of years but the dawn of wisdom. Believe this, expect it, and your subconscious mind will bring it to pass.

5. Welcome advancing years joyfully and gladly. View aging as change and a step forward on a path with no end. Accept your age gracefully.

6. Maintain the perspective that life is endless and eternal. Man's life is spiritual and eternal. Accept that what men call death is but a journey to a new city in another dimension of life.

7. Stop using age-based language that reinforces limitation. Instead of saying "I am old," say "I am wise in the way of the divine life."

8. Reject societal propaganda that holds up pictures of old age as declining years, decrepitude, senility, and uselessness. Refuse to be hypnotized by such false imagery.

9. Cultivate a vision of yourself as happy, radiant, successful, serene, and powerful. Your subconscious mind will manifest this vision regardless of chronological age.

10. Accept that your gray hairs are an asset, not a liability. You are not selling your age or gray hair; you are selling your talents, experience, and wisdom gathered through years in the marketplace of life.

11. Understand that diet, exercises, and games alone will not keep you young. Your subconscious mind is conditioned by your thoughts. If your thoughts are constantly on the beautiful, noble, and good, you will remain young regardless of chronological years.

12. Combat the fear of old age by releasing anticipation of mental and physical deterioration. What you think and feel comes to pass. Focus on vitality and new truths instead.

13. Continue to learn and grow. Learn something new every moment of the day. Keep your mind open to new ideas, new interests, and let in the sunshine and inspiration of new truths. This keeps you young and vital.

14. Recognize that you have much to give regardless of age. You can stabilize, advise, and direct younger generations. Give the benefit of your knowledge, experience, and wisdom.

15. Always look ahead, for at all times you are gazing into infinite life. Never cease to unveil the glories and wonders of life.

16. Keep your mind from retiring. Your mind must be like a parachute - it is no good unless it opens up. Be open and receptive to new ideas throughout life.

17. Reframe retirement as a new venture, new challenge, new path, and the beginning of the fulfillment of a long dream. Shift your focus from making a living to living life.

18. View retirement as "promotion from kindergarten to the first grade." Take up new studies and new interests you were too busy to pursue while making a living.

19. Become enthusiastic over new creative ideas. Make spiritual progress. Continue to learn and to grow. In this manner, you remain young in heart.

20. Be a producer and not a prisoner of society. Do not let society compel you to idleness. Don't hide your light under a bushel.

21. Feel the miraculous, healing, self-renewing power of your subconscious mind moving through your whole being. Know and feel that you are inspired, lifted up, rejuvenated, revitalized, and recharged spiritually.

22. Affirm the presence of your good regardless of appearances. Walk by the guidance of your subconscious mind, knowing that the dawn appears and the shadows flee away.

23. Pray and meditate on truths of life. The text recommends praying: "As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O God." (Psalm 42:1)

24. Go into the silence of your soul and commune with the divine presence within to find peace, which is the power at the heart of God.

25. Realize that you are on an endless journey, a series of important steps in the ceaseless, tireless, endless ocean of life.

## KEY PRINCIPLES AND CONCEPTS

1. The subconscious mind is part of the universal mind of God - never born, never dying, timeless, ageless, and endless.

2. Spiritual qualities (patience, kindness, love, good will, joy, happiness, wisdom, understanding) never grow old or die.

3. Age is not measured in years but in wisdom and spiritual awareness.

4. The most productive years of life can be from 65 to 95, or even beyond.

5. Life is self-renewing, eternal, indestructible, and is the reality of all men.

6. Evidence for survival after death is overwhelming (referencing psychical research societies and scientific findings over 75+ years).

7. Man has powers and senses that transcend his bodily powers and five physical senses.

8. The mind is the master weaver, the architect, the designer, and the sculptor. Your character, quality of mind, faith, and convictions are not subject to decay.

9. "As a man thinketh in his heart, so is he." (Proverbs 23:7) - Your subconscious mind is conditioned by your thoughts.

10. "Man is as strong as he thinks he is, and as valuable as he thinks he is."

11. "You are as young as you think you are. You are as strong as you think you are. You are as useful as you think you are. You are as young as your thoughts."

12. God is life, and life is eternal. We live forever because our life is God's life.

13. What men call death is but a journey to a new city in another dimension of life.

14. The secret of youth is love, joy, inner peace, and laughter. "In Him there is fullness of joy. In Him there is no darkness at all."

15. The fruits of the spirit are: love, joy, peace, patience, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, and temperance. Against such there is no law. (Galatians 5:22-23)

## BEST PRACTICES MENTIONED

1. Welcome the change of aging joyfully and gladly.

2. Accept old age gracefully, recognizing it has its own glory, beauty, and wisdom.

3. Reject societal propaganda and negative statistics about aging.

4. Continuously cultivate and express spiritual qualities that never age.

5. Study new subjects and investigate new ideas throughout life.

6. Learn something new every moment of every day.

7. Take up new creative pursuits after retirement (photography, travel, lecturing, etc.).

8. Give attention to living life rather than merely making a living.

9. Stabilize, advise, and direct younger generations with your accumulated wisdom.

10. Become a producer rather than a consumer/prisoner of society.

11. Open your mind to new ideas and let in inspiration and new truths.

12. Look ahead constantly, remembering you are gazing into infinite life.

13. Take courses and pursue interests you've always wanted to explore.

14. Share your talents, abilities, and wisdom rather than focusing on your chronological age.

15. Ground yourself in the knowledge that life is eternal and your true nature is spiritual.

## COMMON PITFALLS MENTIONED

1. Falling into the mental attitude of futility and worthlessness - believing "We are born, grow up, become old, good for nothing, and that's the end."

2. Anticipating senescence and nothingness - looking forward only to decline and extinction.

3. Fear of old age and the effects of time - the neurotic fear that causes premature aging.

4. Losing interest in life and ceasing to dream.

5. Stopping the hunger for new truths and the search for new worlds to conquer.

6. Becoming irritable, crotchety, petulant, and cantankerous.

7. Retiring mentally and spiritually - allowing the mind to stagnate.

8. Surrendering to advancing years and accepting limitations imposed by age.

9. Becoming stagnant and dying while still physically alive by accepting "I am retired; I am old; I am finished."

10. Trying to keep up physically with younger people through exercise alone, rather than understanding that age is a mental and spiritual condition.

11. Accepting shallow societal reasoning that values only youth (under 35) and dismisses experience and wisdom.

12. Burying talents and hiding light under a bushel due to age discrimination.

13. Allowing criticism, condemnation, hatred, resentment, ill will, and bitterness to age the mind and spirit.

14. Focusing on appearances rather than affirming the presence of good.

15. Concentrating only on making a living rather than living life.

16. Believing that 65, 75, or 85 years of age is synonymous with the end.

## TECHNIQUES AND METHODS DESCRIBED

1. Affirmation Practice:
   - Replace negative age-related statements with positive affirmations.
   - Example: Instead of "I am old," affirm "I am wise in the way of the divine life."
   - Affirm life, not death.
   - Believe and expect that age can be the beginning of a glorious, fruitful, active life.

2. Mental Reconditioning:
   - Reject false pictures of old age (senility, uselessness, decline).
   - Refuse to be hypnotized by propaganda and statistics about aging.
   - Reject thoughts of futility and worthlessness.
   - Condition your subconscious mind by focusing thoughts on the beautiful, noble, and good.

3. Visualization/Visioning:
   - Get a vision of yourself as happy, radiant, successful, serene, and powerful.
   - Feel the miraculous, healing, self-renewing power of your subconscious mind moving through your whole being.
   - Know and feel that you are inspired, lifted up, rejuvenated, revitalized, and recharged spiritually.

4. Meditation and Silence:
   - Go into the silence of your soul and commune with the divine presence within.
   - This leads to finding peace, which is the power at the heart of God.

5. Continuous Learning:
   - Learn something new every moment of the day.
   - Study new subjects and investigate new ideas.
   - Take up new studies, courses, and interests.

6. Creative Engagement:
   - Become enthusiastic over new creative ideas.
   - Make spiritual progress.
   - Engage in activities like photography, lecturing, writing, travel.
   - Continue to learn and grow.

7. Service to Others:
   - Stabilize, advise, and direct younger generations.
   - Give the benefit of your knowledge, experience, and wisdom.
   - Help others as a way of staying engaged and vital.

8. Biblical Meditation and Prayer:
   - Pray: "As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O God." (Psalm 42:1)
   - Meditate on: "His flesh shall be fresher than a child's; he shall return to the days of his youth." (Job 33:25)
   - Meditate on: "They shall bring forth fruit in old age; they shall be fat and flourishing." (Psalm 92:14)
   - Reflect on: "And this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God." (John 17:3)
   - Study: "As a man thinketh in his heart, so is he." (Proverbs 23:7)

9. Spiritual Practice:
   - Recognize that the candle shining upon your head is divine intelligence.
   - Enable yourself to affirm the presence of your good regardless of appearances.
   - Walk by the guidance of your subconscious mind.

10. Reframing Retirement:
    - View retirement as "promotion from kindergarten to the first grade."
    - See it as a step forward on the ladder of life and wisdom.
    - Take up new creative pursuits and interests.
    - Shift focus from making a living to living life.

## EXAMPLES AND EVIDENCE CITED

1. Medical research from De Courcy Clinic, Cincinnati, Ohio: Years alone are not responsible for degenerative disorders; fear of time is the harmful aging factor.

2. An 84-year-old surgeon who operates every morning, visits patients in the afternoon, and writes for medical journals in the evening.

3. George Bernard Shaw - active at age 90 with no relaxation of artistic quality.

4. Former President Herbert Hoover - active at 88, writing books, handling correspondence, with full mental acumen.

5. The author's father - learned French at 65, Gaelic at over 60, taught until passing at 99 with a mind as clear at 99 as at 20, with improved handwriting and reasoning powers.

6. Marcus Porcius Cato - learned Greek at 80.

7. Madame Ernestine Schumann-Heink - reached pinnacle of musical success after becoming a grandmother.

8. Socrates - learned to play musical instruments at 80.

9. Michelangelo - painting greatest canvases at 80.

10. Cios Simonides - won prize for poetry at 80.

11. Johann von Goethe - finished Faust (exact age not specified, but noted as accomplished later in life).

12. Leopold von Ranke - commenced History of the World at age unspecified, finished at 92.

13. Alfred Tennyson - wrote "Crossing the Bar" at 83.

14. Isaac Newton - working hard close to 85.

15. John Wesley - directing, preaching, and guiding Methodism at 88.

16. Several men of 95 in the author's lectures who reported better health than at 20.

17. A 110-year-old man in Bombay, India, with a beautiful, transfigured face and clear mind.

18. An executive who viewed forced retirement at 65 as "promotion from kindergarten to the first grade" and went on to travel, lecture, and pursue photography.
