# Analysis: Chapter XV — Your Subconscious Mind and Happiness

## Chapter Title and Number

**Chapter XV: Your Subconscious Mind and Happiness**

## Actionable Lessons with Step-by-Step Instructions

### 1. Start Each Morning With a Happiness Affirmation
When you open your eyes in the morning, say to yourself:
> "Divine order takes charge of my life today and every day. All things work together for good for me today. This is a new and wonderful day for me. There will never be another day like this one. I am divinely guided all day long and whatever I do will prosper. Divine love surrounds me, enfolds me, and enwraps me, and I go forth in peace. Whenever my attention wanders away from that which is good and constructive, I will immediately bring it back to the contemplation of that which is lovely and of good report. I am a spiritual and mental magnet attracting to myself all things which bless and prosper me. I am going to be a wonderful success in all my undertakings today. I am definitely going to be happy all day long."

- Say this deliberately and with feeling.
- Make it a consistent morning practice.

---

### 2. Make Happiness a Daily Habit Through Blessing Practice
Adopt the habit of blessing key areas of your life twice daily:
- **Upon waking:** Bless your family, your work/crops/business, and express gratitude.
- **Before sleeping:** Bless your family, your work/crops/business, and express gratitude.
- Give thanks specifically for the harvest, provision, or positive outcomes you desire.

> "Every morning when I awaken and every night before I go to sleep, I bless my family, the crops, the cattle and I thank God for the wonderful harvest."

- Practice this consistently over time (the example cites over forty years) so that these thoughts sink into the subconscious mind and become habitual.

---

### 3. Sincerely Desire Happiness
- Examine your inner desire: you must genuinely want to be happy.
- If you have grown accustomed to depression or unhappiness, recognize that you may feel uncomfortable with happiness.
- Consciously choose to release attachment to old mental patterns of misery.
- Nothing is accomplished without desire. Desire is a wish with wings of imagination and faith.

---

### 4. Think on Good Things — Philippians 4:8 Practice
Deliberately discipline your thoughts by focusing only on:
- Whatever things are **true**
- Whatever things are **honest**
- Whatever things are **just**
- Whatever things are **pure**
- Whatever things are **lovely**
- Whatever things are of **good report**
- Whatever is **virtuous**
- Whatever is **praiseworthy**

- Whenever your attention wanders away from good and constructive thoughts, **immediately bring it back** to the contemplation of that which is lovely and of good report.
- Pondering these things often is described as a good habit.

---

### 5. Begin Each Day by Choosing Happiness, Success, Right Action, Love, and Peace
Upon waking, affirm the following (or similar):
> "I choose happiness today. I choose success today. I choose right action today. I choose love and good will for all today. I choose peace today."

- Pour life, love, and interest into this affirmation.
- You have the freedom to choose happiness — this choice is available to you each day.

---

### 6. Practice Gratitude Multiple Times Per Day
- Give thanks for all your blessings several times throughout the day.
- Gratitude reinforces the mental state of happiness and aligns the subconscious mind with abundance.

---

### 7. Pray for the Happiness and Prosperity of Others
- Include prayers for the peace, happiness, and prosperity of:
  - All members of your family
  - Your associates
  - All people everywhere
- This expands your awareness beyond the self and strengthens your connection to divine order.

---

### 8. Imagine and Feel the Fulfillment of Your Desire
- Desire is defined as "a wish with wings of imagination and faith."
- Imagine the fulfillment of your desire.
- Feel its reality as if it is already accomplished.
- Happiness comes in answered prayer — which is the conscious alignment of your mind with the desired outcome.

---

### 9. Interrupt Negative Thoughts Immediately
- When fearful or angry thoughts arise, say to yourself:
  > "Peace, harmony and poise govern my mind at all times."
- Repeat this until the harmful thoughts cease to come.
- Do not dwell on fear, worry, anger, hate, or thoughts of failure — doing so leads directly to depression and unhappiness.

---

### 10. Affirm Positively About Others and Your Work Environment
When dealing with workplace conflict, resentment, or difficult relationships, repeat a structured affirmation such as the following (repeat slowly **three times in the morning**, feeling the truth of each word):
> "All those working in our corporation are honest, sincere, cooperative, faithful and full of good will to all. They are mental and spiritual links in the chain of this corporation's growth, welfare and prosperity. I radiate love, peace and good will in my thoughts, words and deeds to my two associates and to all those in the company. The president and the vice president of our company are divinely guided in all their undertakings. The Infinite Intelligence of my subconscious mind makes all decisions through me. There is only right action in all our business transactions and in our relationship with each other. I send the messengers of peace, love and good will before me to the office. Peace and harmony reign supreme in the minds and hearts of all those in the company including myself. I now go forth into a new day, full of faith, confidence and trust."

- Continue disciplining your mind in this manner until harmful thoughts cease and peace takes their place.

---

### 11. Face and Remove Mental Blocks (The Stump Principle)
- Identify the mental "stump" — a memory of failure, fear, or past hurt — that is holding you back.
- "Dig it up" by consciously replacing it with faith in success, achievement, and victory over all problems.
- Do not whine or complain about past failures — instead, learn from them and affirm new, positive outcomes.
- Example affirmation for overcoming failure:
  > "I made mistakes. I've learned a lot. I am going back into [my endeavor] and I will be a tremendous success."

## Key Principles and Concepts

1. **Happiness is a state of mind, not dependent on external circumstances.** Wealth, marriage, children, career success, and material possessions do not guarantee lasting happiness. External experiences are transitory.

2. **You have the freedom to choose happiness.** This is presented as a daily, conscious decision available to every person.

3. **The subconscious mind is the source of all power for happiness, healing, and prosperity.** Touched by faith, it can overcome any problem in the world.

4. **"Whosoever trusteth in the Lord (the power and wisdom of your subconscious mind) to lead, guide, govern and direct all your ways, you will become poised, serene and relaxed."**

5. **Happiness is a habit.** Just as negative mental patterns can become habitual, happiness can be cultivated through repeated, systematic thought.

6. **Your thoughts determine your world.** "A man's life is what his thoughts make of it" (Marcus Aurelius). "A man is what he thinks all day long" (Emerson). Your habitual thoughts tend to actualize themselves in physical conditions.

7. **Thought is cause; external things are effects.** There is no block to your happiness except in your own thought life and mental imagery. External things are not causative — they are effects. New thoughts (causes) produce new effects.

8. **Happiness and virtue complement each other.** The happiest person is he who constantly brings forth and practices what is best in him. Expressing more of God's love, light, truth, and beauty produces happiness.

9. **The kingdom of happiness is in your thought and feeling.** It is not purchased through money, possessions, or status.

10. **Happiness is the harvest of a quiet mind.** Anchor your thoughts on peace, poise, security, and divine guidance, and your mind will be productive of happiness.

11. **External events are not the cause of your experience.** Your experience of the world is entirely determined by what goes on in your own mind.

## Best Practices Mentioned

- Begin each morning with deliberate, heartfelt affirmations before engaging with the day.
- Make a habit of blessing your family, work, and circumstances both morning and evening.
- Practice gratitude several times throughout the day.
- Continuously monitor your thoughts and immediately redirect attention away from negativity.
- Anchor your mind on qualities like peace, poise, security, and divine guidance.
- Radiate love, peace, and goodwill to all people through your thoughts, words, and deeds.
- Use repetition to embed positive thoughts into the subconscious mind.
- Treat happiness as a practice — not a passive experience that happens to you.
- Commit your decisions and actions to Infinite Intelligence (the subconscious) rather than relying solely on conscious reasoning.
- Pray for the well-being of others as a daily spiritual discipline.

---

## Common Pitfalls Mentioned

- **Choosing unhappiness by entertaining negative anticipations:** Thoughts like "Today is a black day," "I am not going to succeed," "Everyone is against me," "I never get the breaks" attract exactly those experiences.
- **Being so accustomed to depression that happiness feels foreign:** Some people have been unhappy so long that they long for their former depressed state and feel it is "wrong to be so happy."
- **Clinging to misery for attention or secondary gain:** The example of the woman with rheumatism who enjoyed her "misery" because it drew attention from family and neighbors — she did not truly want to be happy.
- **Attempting to buy happiness through material acquisition:** Radios, televisions, automobiles, homes, yachts, and swimming pools cannot purchase happiness.
- **Believing that status or achievement will bring happiness:** Promotions, elected office, and organizational positions do not necessarily bequeath happiness.
- **Indulging in negative, defeatist, or depressing thoughts:** These thoughts, if entertained habitually, actualize themselves into physical conditions of unhappiness.
- **Attributing blocks to external circumstances:** Believing that other people, economic conditions, or physical situations are the cause of unhappiness — rather than recognizing that thought is the true cause.
- **Failing to sincerely desire happiness:** Without genuine desire, no change in mental state is possible.
