# Chapter 28 Analysis: Wealth File #16

## Core Intent (1 sentence)
The chapter teaches that successful people take action despite fear, doubt, and discomfort, while unsuccessful people let these feelings stop them, and provides practical strategies to train the mind to act regardless of internal resistance.

## Actionable Lessons
### Lesson 1: Act in Spite of Fear
- **Step 1:** List your three greatest worries, concerns, or fears regarding money and wealth.
- **Step 2:** For each fear, write down what you would do if the situation actually happened and whether you could survive and make a comeback.
- **Step 3:** Decide to quit worrying and start taking action toward wealth.
- **Best Practices:** 
  - Do not wait for fear to subside before acting—most people wait forever.
  - Act in spite of fear, doubt, worry, inconvenience, discomfort, and not being in the mood.
  - Commit 100 percent to your actions; hesitation and halfhearted effort will fail.
- **Common Pitfalls:** 
  - Waiting for fear to disappear before taking action.
  - Allowing convenience or a three-hour drive/flight/trek to stop you from doing what you need to do.
  - Walking into challenges slowly or halfheartedly instead than with full commitment.

### Lesson 2: Expand Your Comfort Zone
- **Step 1:** Intentionally make decisions that are uncomfortable for you (speak to people you normally wouldn't, ask for a raise, raise your prices, wake up earlier, walk in the woods at night).
- **Step 2:** Embrace discomfort as a sign of growth.
- **Step 3:** Live at the edge of your comfort zone rather than retreating to safety.
- **Best Practices:** 
  - Remember the equation: CZ = WZ (Comfort Zone equals Wealth Zone).
  - When you feel uncomfortable, pat yourself on the back and say, "I must be growing."
  - If your goal is to be comfortable, you will never be rich or truly happy.
- **Common Pitfalls:** 
  - Making comfort your highest priority.
  - Retreating back into your old comfort zone when things get uncomfortable.
  - Thinking that being comfortable leads to happiness or success.

### Lesson 3: Practice Power Thinking
- **Step 1:** Observe yourself and your thought patterns.
- **Step 2:** Divide thoughts into two categories—empowering or disempowering.
- **Step 3:** When a nonsupportive thought bubbles up, say "Cancel" or "Thank you for sharing" and replace it with a supportive thought.
- **Step 4:** Make a pact with yourself that whenever the internal voice tries to stop you from doing something supportive to your success, you will do it anyway.
- **Best Practices:** 
  - Understand that no thought lives in your head rent-free—you will pay for negative thoughts in money, energy, time, health, and happiness.
  - Recognize that thoughts are not true; they are stories you make up. Since you are making up a story anyway, make up one that supports you.
  - Be the captain of your mind, not the other way around.
- **Common Pitfalls:** 
  - Believing negative thoughts are true and letting them stop you.
  - Allowing the internal voice that says "I can't" or "I don't feel like it" to get the better of you.
  - Using "positive thinking" to pretend everything is rosy when you believe it is not.

### Lesson 4: Train and Manage Your Mind
- **Step 1:** Notice how your mind consistently produces thoughts that are not supportive to your wealth and happiness.
- **Step 2:** As you identify those thoughts, consciously replace them with empowering ones from the declarations in this book.
- **Step 3:** Decide right now that your thoughts do not run you—you run your thoughts.
- **Best Practices:** 
  - Adopt the empowering ways of thinking, being, and attitudes described in the book as your own.
  - Practice canceling negative thoughts and installing self-empowering thoughts at any time.
- **Common Pitfalls:** 
  - Letting your mind be the captain of the ship.
  - Thinking you must eliminate fear before you can succeed.

## Key Examples
- The arrow-breaking exercise: Volunteers break a wooden arrow with their throat to practice acting despite fear and discomfort. Those who commit 100 percent break it; those who hesitate do not. Participants report it was much easier physically than their minds made it out to be.
- The Seattle seminar attendee: A man who said all his family and friends had benefited from the Millionaire Mind Intensive seminar, and he would attend if it were in Seattle, but he let a three-hour drive to Vancouver stop him. The author pointed out that if a three-hour drive stops him from doing something he needs and wants, anything will stop him.
- The author's own story: Harv Eker went from broke to multimillionaire by modeling rich people's thinking and acting despite fear, doubt, and discomfort, eventually opening ten fitness stores in two and a half years and selling half the company to a Fortune 500 company for $1.6 million.
- Susan Jeffers' book "Feel the Fear and Do It Anyway": Cited as a fantastic book about acting despite fear.
- Mark Twain's quote: "I've had thousands of problems in my life, most of which never actually happened." Used to illustrate how the mind creates worst-case scenarios that rarely occur.
- Robert Allen's quote: "No thought lives in your head rent-free." Used to illustrate the cost of negative thinking.

## Notes
- **Security/Safety Note:** The arrow-break exercise is explicitly warned against attempting at home. It must be set up in a specific way with protective equipment; otherwise, it could cause serious injury to yourself or others.
- **Declaration to practice:** "I act in spite of fear. I act in spite of doubt. I act in spite of worry. I act in spite of inconvenience. I act in spite of discomfort. I act when I'm not in the mood." Followed by touching the head and saying, "I have a millionaire mind!"
- **Wealth Principles summarized:** 
  - Action is the "bridge" between the inner world and the outer world.
  - A true warrior can "tame the cobra of fear."
  - It is not necessary to try to get rid of fear in order to succeed.
  - If you are willing to do only what's easy, life will be hard. But if you are willing to do what's hard, life will be easy.
  - The only time you are actually growing is when you are uncomfortable.
  - Training and managing your own mind is the most important skill you could ever own, in terms of both happiness and success.
  - Comfort kills—it has killed more ideas, opportunities, actions, and growth than everything else combined.
