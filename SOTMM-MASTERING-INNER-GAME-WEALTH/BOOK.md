# Secrets of the Millionaire Mind: Mastering the Inner Game of Wealth

## Overview

This book teaches that your internal "money blueprint"—formed by childhood conditioning—is the primary determinant of your financial destiny, not external strategies or opportunities. It provides a structured framework to identify, disassociate from, and reprogram limiting subconscious beliefs about wealth, replacing them with the 17 Wealth Files that characterize rich people's thinking. The book exists to help readers achieve lasting financial transformation by changing their inner world first, using declarations, exercises, and actionable principles that align their mindset with abundance, success, and permanent wealth creation.

## When to Follow Book Teachings

- When you want to understand why your current financial strategies haven't produced lasting results
- When you are ready to change your relationship with money at the subconscious level
- When you need practical steps to build wealth that goes beyond surface-level tactics
- When you are preparing for major financial decisions or transitions
- When you want to develop the mindset needed to create and sustain lasting wealth

## Lessons From Book

### Lesson 1: Understanding Your Money Blueprint (Chapters 1–12)

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Financial Thermostat

A person earning $50,000/year will consistently earn or retain $50,000/year regardless of external events such as bonuses or windfalls; a bonus is like opening a window—the thermostat always brings it back to the set point. A salesperson who made $90,000 one year had a crummy follow-up year to bring him back to his $50,000 blueprint. Donald Trump's financial thermostat is set for billions, not millions, explaining why he rebuilt his wealth after losing it all.

##### Example 2: Lottery Winners

Lottery winners return to their original financial state regardless of prize size, proving an unchanged internal blueprint causes wealth to be short-lived. Self-made millionaires like Donald Trump regain wealth quickly after losing it because they never lose their "millionaire mind"—their internal blueprint remains intact.

##### Example 3: The Author's Fitness Store

The author borrowed $2,000, committed to staying until he became a millionaire, challenged negative thoughts, modeled rich-people strategies, opened ten stores in 2.5 years, and sold half the company to an unnamed Fortune 500 company for $1.6 million.

##### Example 4: Josey the Nurse

At age 11 she watched her father die of a heart attack after a money argument, linking money with pain; as an adult she subconsciously spent all her income to avoid that pain until she revised her blueprint at the Millionaire Mind Intensive Seminar, then became a financial planner.

##### Example 5: The Typo on the Printed Letter

Erasing a typo on a printed letter without changing the computer program is futile; similarly, changing physical results without changing the inner program is ineffective. The author describes trying to erase a typo by rubbing the paper, then using a bigger eraser, then studying a manual on effective erasing, only to find that the same typo appeared again on the printed page.

##### Example 6: The Cliff-Hanger Parable

A man hanging from a ledge hesitates to let go and trust even when a better path is offered, illustrating the difficulty of surrendering old thinking. The man asks if there's anyone else to help rather than taking the leap of faith.

##### Example 7: The Author's Twenties

The author started multiple businesses with obsession for success but saw results go "from dismal to worse," working hard yet never seeing profit; he experienced a pattern where profit remained elusive despite his efforts.

##### Example 8: The Seminar Observation

Two people in the same room learning the same principles, yet one skyrocketed to success while the other saw little change—revealing that the principles and exercises taught in Secrets of the Millionaire Mind are useless if your internal "toolbox" (mindset) has a leak.

#### Step 1: Accept that your financial outcomes are caused by your internal money blueprint

Accept that your financial outcomes are caused by your internal money blueprint, not external circumstances such as the economy or luck. Look beyond surface-level explanations (bad luck, poor partners) when evaluating financial struggles; dig into the internal causes.

#### Step 2: Identify your personal "financial thermostat"

Identify your personal "financial thermostat"—the level of wealth you are internally set to earn and hold. Compare your current results against this thermostat to see whether you are consistently below, at, or above your set point. Use the thermostat analogy to self-assess: if you earn more than your set point, you will likely lose it.

#### Step 3: Write down your childhood verbal programming about money

Write down every statement you heard about money, wealth, and rich people when you were growing up. Write down how you believe each of these statements has affected your financial life so far. Be thorough and honest; include phrases like "money is the root of all evil," "rich people are greedy," "we can't afford it," "money doesn't grow on trees."

#### Step 4: Identify your money modeling patterns

Consider the ways of being and habits each of your parents or guardians had around money and wealth (spender vs. saver, risk taker vs. conservative, investor vs. non-investor). Write down how you may be identical or opposite to either of them in the arena of money, and write down the effect this modeling has had on your financial life.

#### Step 5: Identify and reprogram a specific childhood money incident

Consider a specific emotional incident you experienced around money when you were young. Write down how this incident may have affected your current financial life. Recognize that this way of being is only what you learned and isn't you; acknowledge that you have a choice in the present moment to be different. Place your hand on your heart and say: "I release my nonsupportive money experiences from the past and create a new and rich future." Touch your head and say: "I have a millionaire mind!"

#### Step 6: Perform declarations daily

Place your hand over your heart and say: "What I heard about money isn't necessarily true. I choose to adopt new ways of thinking that support my happiness and success." Touch your head and say: "I have a millionaire mind." Repeat declarations aloud each morning and evening while looking into a mirror to accelerate the reprogramming of your money blueprint.

#### Step 7: Assess your current money blueprint

Look at your bank account, income, net worth, investment success, and business success. Observe whether you are a spender or saver, manage money well or mismanage it, are consistent or inconsistent. Notice if money is a struggle or comes to you easily, and whether you jump between jobs or stick with one. Identify if you are programmed for a specific income range ($20K–$30K, $40K–$60K, $75K–$100K, or $150K–$250K). Use your current results as the clearest indicator of your blueprint; be brutally honest in your assessment.

#### Step 8: Develop consciousness

Put yourself under a microscope—watch your thoughts, fears, beliefs, habits, actions, and inactions. Recognize that for the most part, you run on automatic, ruled by past conditioning. Understand that you are the recorder, not the recording; the hardware, not the software. See your programming as information you received in the past, not who you are today.

#### Step 9: Choose empowering thoughts

Remember that no thought lives in your head rent-free—each thought is either an investment or a cost. Choose thoughts that move you toward happiness and success rather than away from it. Release beliefs that are not supportive to wealth and replace them with ones that are. Don't believe every thought you think; entertain only thoughts that empower you.

#### Best Practices

- Focus on internal change before chasing external strategies; treat mindset as a prerequisite, not an afterthought.
- Use the thermostat analogy to self-assess: if you earn more than your set point, you will likely lose it; if below, you will struggle to reach higher levels without reconditioning.
- Treat problems as signals that the inner program needs revision, not as isolated external issues.
- Repeat declarations aloud each morning and evening while looking into a mirror; touch your body to reinforce the words through physical sensation and bodily awareness.
- Use the four-step change framework: Awareness, Understanding, Disassociation, and Declaration for all conditioning influences.
- Use the computer/printout analogy to remind yourself that erasing the physical printout without fixing the program will always reproduce the same result.
- Treat outer circumstances as feedback signals about your inner state.

#### Keep In Mind

- Your money blueprint affects more than just money—it influences relationship choices (attracting partners who validate your financial comfort zone) and business results.
- The only way to permanently change your financial temperature is to reset the thermostat.
- You are the recorder, not the recording; the hardware, not the software.
- Wealth is framed as a result of character, thinking, beliefs, habits, and the ability to act despite fear, worry, inconvenience, and discomfort—not merely a function of opportunity or intelligence.
- The physical world is merely a "printout" produced by the mental, emotional, and spiritual realms, not the source of results.
- Suffering is framed as the natural consequence of neglecting your inner world—your thoughts, beliefs, and subconscious programming—which violates the foundational principle that internal change must precede external results.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Chasing get-rich schemes, business opportunities, or investment strategies while ignoring underlying self-defeating thoughts and habits.
  **Solution:** Focus on internal change before chasing external strategies; treat mindset as a prerequisite, not an afterthought.

- **Problem:** Blaming external factors for financial failure; assuming more knowledge or better strategies alone will fix money problems.
  **Solution:** Look beyond surface-level explanations (bad luck, poor partners) when evaluating financial struggles; dig into the internal causes.

- **Problem:** Assuming that a sudden windfall (e.g., lottery winnings) will permanently change your financial life without first changing your internal blueprint.
  **Solution:** Use the thermostat analogy to understand why you gravitate back to your accustomed financial level.

- **Problem:** Living strictly in the visible world—working and thinking only about surface-level results without addressing root causes.
  **Solution:** Direct attention and effort toward changing the inner program (thoughts, emotions, spirit) instead of trying to fix outer symptoms.

- **Problem:** Believing that more effort, knowledge, or tools at the physical level will solve problems that originate in the mental, emotional, or spiritual realms.
  **Solution:** Commit to changing your inner world first as the only reliable way to change your outer world.

- **Problem:** Dismissing the childhood money programming exercise as unimportant, forgetting painful or repeated statements, or rationalizing that childhood words no longer matter.
  **Solution:** Be thorough and honest in your written exercises; do them without judgment.

- **Problem:** Believing the old beliefs are absolute truth, feeling guilty for questioning parental or cultural conditioning, or thinking you cannot change deeply held beliefs.
  **Solution:** View old programming as "files" stored in your mind that may no longer hold truth or value; observe them objectively rather than fighting them.

- **Problem:** Assuming you are completely different from your parents without deeper inspection; overlooking that being angry or rebellious about your parents' financial situation can still link money to negative emotions.
  **Solution:** Be honest about both conscious and unconscious patterns; recognize that rebellion against a parent's money habits is still a form of being controlled by those habits.

- **Problem:** Believing your thoughts are true; being run by old habits without awareness.
  **Solution:** Observe your thoughts and actions so you can live from true choice in the present moment rather than reacting to past programming.

### Lesson 2: The Wealth Files—17 Ways Rich People Think and Act Differently (Chapters 13–29)

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Three Victim Clues

Poor people spend a large share of their income on lottery tickets, believing wealth will land on them by chance, whereas rich people view the lottery as entertainment, not a wealth strategy. The author confronts seminar participants who claim money isn't important by pointing out they are broke, demonstrating that this belief directly correlates with their financial reality. Complaining focuses energy on what is wrong, which generates additional negative experiences and obstacles in your financial life.

##### Example 2: The Sports Analogy

Playing any game strictly on defense virtually guarantees you will not win. Poor people play defensively with survival or comfort as goals, while rich people play to win by aiming for massive wealth.

##### Example 3: The Three Levels of Wanting

"I want to be rich" (passive desire that produces no action and generates no results), "I choose to be rich" (a conscious decision that is stronger than wanting, but still not the most powerful level of commitment), and "I commit to being rich" (warrior's way, unreserved). The author's personal story: modeling rich people's thinking and committing unreservedly to building a fitness store led to rapid success (ten stores in 2.5 years, sold half the company for $1.6 million).

##### Example 4: Mother Butler's Pie Shop

Harv wanted to learn the dessert business so he got a job as a busboy rather than continuing to research from the outside; he learned by sweeping floors, clearing dishes, talking with the manager about revenues and profits, and helping the baker at 4:00 a.m.; this inside access eventually led to an unexpected opportunity in exercise equipment.

##### Example 5: The Jaguar Experience

When driving a clunker, people let him merge; when driving a new black Jaguar, he was cut off, given the finger, and had beer cans thrown into his sunroof with people screaming "You rich bastard!" The car was keyed in a lower-end neighborhood within ten minutes, but when he rented a Ford Escort instead, he had no problems.

##### Example 6: The Ham-Cutting Story

A woman cuts off both ends of a ham because that is how her mother cooked it, and her mother did it because her pan was too small—illustrating how people blindly replicate observed behaviors without understanding the original reason.

##### Example 7: The Level-Scale Analogy

A level 2 person sees a level 5 problem as huge; a level 8 person sees it as manageable; a level 10 person doesn't even register it as a problem. The author lost his first million because his internal "toolbox" wasn't yet big enough to hold that level of wealth, but after reconditioning his mind, he made it back and continued building wealth.

##### Example 8: The Ice Cream Cone Parable

A child who cannot hold a single scoop is not given a triple scoop—illustrating the principle that you must demonstrate you can handle what you have before the universe gives you more. Emma started with $1/month divided into accounts, doubled her allocation monthly until managing $2,048/month, then received a $10,000 bonus check and could save it entirely because her other needs were covered.

##### Example 9: The Country Club Example

Wealthy people at country clubs discuss net worth (e.g., "Paul's company just went public; he's worth eight million") rather than working income (e.g., "Joe got a two percent cost-of-living raise").

##### Example 10: The $5 Circulation Example

The same $5 bill can be passed between five people, creating $5 worth of value for each person and $25 total for the group, proving money is not depleted but multiplies in value as it circulates.

##### Example 11: The Arrow-Breaking Exercise

Volunteers break a wooden arrow with their throat to practice acting despite fear and discomfort. Volunteers who fully commit to the action without hesitation break the wooden arrow; those who hesitate or withdraw their commitment do not break it. Participants report it was much easier physically than their minds made it out to be.

##### Example 12: The Bus Analogy

Driving a bus on one wheel (focusing only on working income) results in a slow, bumpy, circular ride, whereas driving on all four wheels (income, savings, investments, simplification) creates a fast, smooth, direct journey to wealth.

##### Example 13: The Trainer's Net Worth Growth

A trainer increased net worth from $250,000 to over $600 million in only three years by beginning to think big and serving thousands of people.

##### Example 14: The Orlando Seminar Attendee

After decades of trying every strategy and earning an MBA, he discovered his financial struggles were due to modeling his Depression-era father's pattern of consistently coming home empty-handed; once he released this blueprint, he accumulated more wealth in 18 months than in the previous 18 years.

#### Step 1: Recognize and stop the three victim clues

Monitor your thoughts and speech for the three victim clues: blaming others or circumstances for your lack of wealth, justifying your situation by saying money isn't important, and complaining about what is wrong in your life. When you catch yourself engaging in any of these behaviors, stop immediately and remind yourself that you are creating your life and attracting either success or negativity at every moment. Use a physical trigger (sliding your index finger across your neck) each time you catch yourself blaming, justifying, or complaining to reinforce the awareness that these behaviors are damaging your financial future.

#### Step 2: Write down two "play to win" financial objectives

Write down two financial objectives that demonstrate your intention to create abundance, not mediocrity or poverty. Write "play to win" goals for your annual income and net worth. Make these goals achievable with a realistic time frame, while still "shooting for the stars." Frame goals around massive wealth and abundance rather than survival or comfort.

#### Step 3: Eliminate mixed messages about wealth

List all negative beliefs you have about being rich (e.g., taxes, responsibility, danger, people wanting handouts, losing health or friends). Recognize that these mixed messages confuse the universe (Law of Attraction) and block wealth creation because you send contradictory signals. Replace mixed messages with clarity by affirming your commitment to wealth.

#### Step 4: Commit unreservedly to being rich

Acknowledge that "wanting" alone is useless and leads to more wanting. Recognize the three levels: "I want to be rich" (passive), "I choose to be rich" (deciding), and "I commit to being rich" (unreserved devotion). Make the decision to commit unreservedly to being rich—do whatever it takes for as long as it takes, with no excuses, ifs, buts, or maybes.

#### Step 5: Increase the quantity of value you deliver

Recognize that your income is directly proportional to the value you deliver in the marketplace, determined by supply, demand, quality, and quantity. Focus on the quantity factor—how many people you actually serve or affect. Identify opportunities to expand your reach to serve more people. Brainstorm at least three different strategies for expanding your impact. Make a declaration to think big.

#### Step 6: Adopt an opportunity-focused mindset and take action

Shift from fear-based scanning ("What if it doesn't work?" or "It won't work") to a confident, responsible stance: "It will work because I'll make it work." Conduct research and due diligence quickly—don't take forever to get educated. Make an informed decision, then act. Get ready as best you can in as short a time as possible, then take action immediately. Begin taking action now from wherever you are with whatever resources you currently have.

#### Step 7: Reframe your perception of rich people

Recognize when you think or speak negatively about rich people. Immediately neutralize the resentment by saying out loud, "Cancel, cancel, thank you for sharing." Reframe the thought into admiration and blessing (e.g., "Way to go! You deserve it."). When you see a beautiful home, car, business, or success, consciously bless it and its owner.

#### Step 8: Model successful people and curate your environment

Read biographies of extremely rich and successful people for inspiration, specific success strategies, and to copy their mindset. Join a high-end club or visit upscale venues to get comfortable in that atmosphere. Identify a situation or person who is a downer in your life and remove yourself from that situation or association. Stop watching trash TV and bad news, and avoid arguing, gossiping, and backstabbing.

#### Step 9: Believe in your value and promote it with integrity

Rate the product or service you offer on a 1–10 scale based on how strongly you believe in its value. If the rating is 7–9, revise the product or service to increase its value and your belief in it. If the rating is 6 or below, stop offering it and begin representing something you truly believe in. Commit to learning marketing and sales by reading books, listening to audio programs, and taking courses.

#### Step 10: Reframe problems as opportunities to grow

When you feel upset about a "big" problem, point to yourself and say, "Mini me, mini me." Take a deep breath and declare, "I can handle this. I am bigger than any problem." Write down a specific problem you are currently facing, list ten specific actions you can take to resolve or improve the situation, and take action on the solutions rather than ruminating on the problem.

#### Step 11: Dismantle the "unworthy" story and practice receiving

Recognize that feelings of unworthiness are a made-up story from past conditioning, not objective truth. Decide that you are worthy; understand that worthiness is a perspective you choose, not a stamp given by others. Reject the adage "It's better to give than to receive" as a falsehood. When receiving a compliment, say only "Thank you." Celebrate any money found or received by declaring out loud, "I am a money magnet. Thank you, thank you, thank you." Pamper yourself at least once per month with nurturing experiences.

#### Step 12: Shift from time-based to results-based compensation

If employed, create a compensation plan proposal for your employer that pays you at least partly based on individual and company results rather than only hours worked. If self-employed, redesign employee or supplier compensation to reward results and company performance. If in a personal service business, build systems or teams that allow you to deliver results without personally performing every hour of service.

#### Step 13: Replace either/or thinking with "both" thinking

When confronted with an either/or alternative, pause and ask yourself the quintessential question: "How can I have both?" Train yourself to approach every situation with the explicit intention of creating an outcome where all parties win and you get what you truly want. Consciously acknowledge that spending money goes through hundreds of people and creates value for all of them.

#### Step 14: Focus on net worth rather than working income

Create a net worth statement by adding the current dollar value of everything you own (assets) and subtracting everything you owe (liabilities). Set a specific net worth objective. Make it a policy to know your net worth to the penny. Update and track your net worth every ninety days using a simple chart. Work with a financial planner who works with an array of financial products rather than just one specialty.

#### Step 15: Adopt the Money Management Allocation System

Open a Financial Freedom bank account and deposit 10% of every dollar received (after taxes) into it. Open a Play account or jar and deposit 10% of all income, spending the full amount monthly on extravagant self-reward. Open four additional accounts: Long-Term Savings for Spending (10%), Education (10%), Necessities (50%), and Give (10%). Divide every dollar received according to these percentages and track it consistently. Start with any amount, even $1/month, and double your managed amount each month.

#### Step 16: Build passive income streams and long-term investments

Shift focus from active to passive income by identifying business or career directions where generating passive income is natural. Learn about various investment vehicles: real estate, mortgages, stocks, funds, bonds, currency exchange, and other financial instruments. Save consistently and invest rather than spending all earned money. Purchase real estate as soon as you can afford it, preferably property that produces positive cash flow, and hold it long-term rather than selling in a down market.

#### Step 17: Act in spite of fear, doubt, and discomfort

List your three greatest worries, concerns, or fears regarding money and wealth. For each fear, write down what you would do if the situation actually happened and whether you could survive and make a comeback. Decide to quit worrying and start taking action toward wealth. Intentionally make decisions that are uncomfortable for you. Remember the equation: CZ = WZ (Comfort Zone equals Wealth Zone). When you feel uncomfortable, pat yourself on the back and say, "I must be growing."

#### Step 18: Commit to continuous learning and personal growth

Acknowledge that if you are not already rich and happy, there are still things you need to learn about money, success, and life. Shift your identity from a "know-it-all" to a "learn-it-all." Read at least one book per month, listen to one educational tape or CD per month, or attend a seminar on money, business, or personal development. Set aside 10 percent of your income into a dedicated Education Fund. Identify people who have already achieved the results you want and study those who have real-world results, not just those who claim to be experts.
