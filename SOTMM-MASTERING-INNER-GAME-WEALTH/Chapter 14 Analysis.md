# Chapter 14 Analysis: Wealth File #2

## Core Intent (1 sentence)
Rich people set aggressive, abundance-focused financial goals and play the money game to win by aiming for massive wealth, while poor and middle-class people play defensively with survival, security, or mere comfort as their goals, which directly limits their results to exactly what they intend.

## Actionable Lessons
### Lesson 1: Write Down Two "Play to Win" Financial Objectives
- **Step 1:** Write down two financial objectives that demonstrate your intention to create abundance, not mediocrity or poverty.
- **Step 2:** Write "play to win" goals for your annual income and net worth.
- **Step 3:** Make these goals achievable with a realistic time frame, while still "shooting for the stars."
- **Best Practices:** Frame goals around massive wealth and abundance rather than survival or comfort; align them with genuine intention to win.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Setting goals only to "pay the bills" or to be "comfortable"; allowing the goal to be merely survival or security, which guarantees you will get exactly that and nothing more.

### Lesson 2: Practice Abundance by Ordering Without Looking at Price
- **Step 1:** Go to an upscale restaurant.
- **Step 2:** Order a meal at "market price" without asking how much it costs.
- **Step 3:** If funds are tight, sharing is acceptable.
- **Best Practices:** Use this as a tangible practice to reinforce an abundance mindset and break the habit of making decisions based on price.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Asking the price before ordering; defaulting to cheaper items like chicken out of habit; avoiding "market price" items entirely due to fear or embarrassment.

## Key Examples
- The author being extremely broke and having to borrow a dollar for gas in four quarters, feeling humiliated.
- Being "comfortable" and only ordering chicken because of menu prices, never daring to look at the bottom of the menu.
- Being rich and not having to look at prices anymore, eating exactly what is wanted regardless of cost.
- The sports analogy: playing any game strictly on defense virtually guarantees you will not win.
- The wealth principle: "If your goal is to be comfortable, chances are you'll never get rich. But if your goal is to be rich, chances are you'll end up mighty comfortable."

## Notes
- The chapter emphasizes the power of intention: you get exactly what you truly intend to get.
- The author draws from personal experience across all three financial states (broke, comfortable, rich) to illustrate the mindset differences.
- The "shoot for the stars, you'll at least hit the moon" principle is presented as a key motivator for setting high goals.
- A declaration is included: "My goal is to become a millionaire and more!" and "I have a millionaire mind!"
- Poor people's goal is framed as "having enough to pay the bills... and on time would be a miracle," while middle-class people settle for being "comfortable," neither of which leads to true wealth.
