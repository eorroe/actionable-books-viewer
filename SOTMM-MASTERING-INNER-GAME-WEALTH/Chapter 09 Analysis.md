# Chapter 9 Analysis: The First Influence: Verbal Programming

## Core Intent (1 sentence)
To demonstrate how childhood verbal conditioning creates subconscious money blueprints that drive financial outcomes, and to provide a four-step process for identifying, disassociating from, and reprogramming these limiting beliefs through awareness and intentional declarations.

## Actionable Lessons
### Lesson 1: Identify Your Childhood Money Programming
- **Step 1:** Write down every statement you heard about money, wealth, and rich people when you were growing up.
- **Step 2:** Write down how you believe each of these statements has affected your financial life so far.
- **Best Practices:** Be thorough and honest; include phrases like "money is the root of all evil," "rich people are greedy," "we can't afford it," "money doesn't grow on trees," etc.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Dismissing the exercise as unimportant, forgetting painful or repeated statements, or rationalizing that childhood words no longer matter.

### Lesson 2: Disassociate From Limiting Beliefs
- **Step 1:** Recognize that the statements you wrote down represent only what you learned from others—they are not part of your anatomy and not who you are.
- **Step 2:** Acknowledge that you have a choice in the present moment to be different from those conditioned beliefs.
- **Best Practices:** View old programming as "files" stored in your mind that may no longer hold truth or value; observe them objectively rather than fighting them.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Believing the old beliefs are absolute truth, feeling guilty for questioning parental or cultural conditioning, or thinking you cannot change deeply held beliefs.

### Lesson 3: Reprogram With Declarations
- **Step 1:** Place your hand over your heart and say: "What I heard about money isn't necessarily true. I choose to adopt new ways of thinking that support my happiness and success."
- **Step 2:** Touch your head and say: "I have a millionaire mind."
- **Best Practices:** Repeat declarations aloud each morning and evening while looking into a mirror; touch your body to anchor the vibrational energy of the words.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Skipping the physical gesture, only thinking declarations silently, or dismissing the practice as "hokey" without testing it.

### Lesson 4: Understand the Emotional Power of Subconscious Conditioning
- **Step 1:** Recognize that when the subconscious mind must choose between deeply rooted emotions and logic, emotions will almost always win.
- **Step 2:** Identify nonsupportive emotional links in your money blueprint (e.g., rich = greedy, stocks = dangerous).
- **Best Practices:** Use experiential techniques to change the blueprint rather than relying solely on intellectual understanding; address the emotional charge, not just the thought.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Assuming logic alone will override conditioning, trying to "think away" emotional associations, or ignoring the need for ongoing frequency and support to maintain change.

## Key Examples
- **Stephen's Story:** A seminar participant earning $800,000/year who remained broke because his mother repeatedly told him "Rich people are greedy... You should have just enough to get by. After that you're a pig," linking wealth with being bad; after reconditioning, he went from broke to millionaire in two years by investing in a Maui condo for his mom, resolving the approval conflict.
- **Author's Stock Market Failure:** The author couldn't make money in stocks because his father daily ranted "Those stinkin' stocks!" after work, subconsciously programming him to pick the wrong stocks at the wrong time; after reconditioning, his stock choices immediately began to boom.
- **Josey's Story (referenced from Chapter 11 but relevant):** An operating-room nurse who spent all her money because at age eleven she watched her father die of a heart attack during a money argument, linking money with pain.

## Notes
- The four elements of change—awareness, understanding, disassociation, and reconditioning—are the foundational framework used throughout the book for all three conditioning influences (verbal, modeling, and incidents).
- Subconscious conditioning operates automatically: people subconsciously validate their money blueprint by making choices that produce results matching their programming, even when those choices appear irrational on the surface.
- Declarations are distinguished from affirmations: a declaration states an official intention to undertake a course of action, whereas an affirmation asserts something is already true; declarations are more believable to the subconscious because they do not require believing something unreal in the present moment.
- Ongoing support through resources like the "Millionaire Mind thought of the week" is recommended to reinforce new programming and ensure lasting change.
- The chapter emphasizes that changing the "software" (programming) is the only way to permanently change results; trying to change the physical world directly is ineffective because the outer world is merely a printout of the inner world.
