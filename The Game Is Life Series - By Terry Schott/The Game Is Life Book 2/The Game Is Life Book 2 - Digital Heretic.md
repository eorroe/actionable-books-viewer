# Digital Heretic

## Overview

Digital Heretic by Terry Schott is the second novel in The Game Is Life series, and Digital Heretic continues two storylines after the avatar Trew Radfield dies inside the Game at age 40: Danielle Radfield's storyline inside Earth, and Zack's storyline on Tygon, where Zack renames himself Trew. Digital Heretic converts a disbelieved claim — Earth is a computer simulation, Tygon may also be a simulation, and a human being can learn to fly — into a discipline with four parts: the reader states the claim in testable words, the reader tests the claim with a witness and a recorded result, the reader shares the tested claim after ridicule begins, and the reader keeps living, training, and protecting other people instead of surrendering the reader's remaining lifetime to grief, vengeance, or spectatorship. Digital Heretic also warns the reader that every miracle in Digital Heretic carries a named price: Zack returns to life on Tygon because Brandon Strayne agrees to surrender Brandon Strayne's own life, Danielle Radfield gains years to hunt Carl while Gamers die in the Culling, and Shane releases thirteen Zoo subjects while retaining an infinity-symbol mark that lets Shane find each released subject at any time.

## When to Follow Book Teachings

- When a reader holds a claim that named listeners reject as absurd, and the reader needs the Heretic test in Lesson 1: state the claim, test the claim, record the result, and share the recorded result after ridicule begins
- When a reader loses a spouse, a partner, a parent, a child, or a close friend, and grief pushes the reader toward reckless activity that could kill the reader, as cliff diving and shark swimming push Danielle Radfield toward death on PDF (Portable Document Format) pages 6-8
- When a reader leads a public movement, a public campaign, or a public community, and public visibility makes members of the movement, campaign, or community easy for a hostile actor to find, as public Gamer meetings make Gamers easy for Carl to find on PDF pages 105-107
- When a reader experiences an event that the reader cannot explain — a floating body, a voice that answers prayer, a coincidence with a measurable result — and the reader needs a witness, a demonstration, and a practice record before trusting the event, as Melissa requires Sam as a witness on PDF pages 13-14
- When protectors, employers, doctors, or friends withhold a material fact from the reader, and the reader must decide what question to ask, what evidence to demand, and what cost the answer will impose, as Eva's word "severed" forces Danielle Radfield to demand the Sever Spike account from Raphael on PDF pages 28-32
- When a reader's single goal becomes revenge against one named person, and the revenge goal consumes years that the reader could spend on family, work, health, and protection of other people, as Danielle Radfield's hunt for Carl consumes Danielle Radfield's activity from age 41 to age 49 on PDF pages 35 and 52-53
- When an authority announces a purge, a layoff, a closure, or a cull with a number attached, and the reader must protect named people instead of debating the authority's stated reason, as Gabriel and Daniel agree to eject 500 million players on PDF pages 98-99
- When a reader lives or works under a controller who renames the reader, schedules the reader's waking state, and punishes questions, as Shane renames Miranda Stewart "Three" and controls Miranda Stewart's waking state with a forehead tap on PDF pages 20-23 and 65-68
- When a reader spends the reader's evenings watching another person's recorded life, channel, or feed, and the reader produces no record of the reader's own action, as the Game fan in Chapter 67 watches a waiter's entire play on PDF pages 246-247
- When power, communication, or transport systems fail without warning, and the reader needs a pre-arranged group, a pre-arranged meeting method, and supplies that do not depend on a network, as the Game feeds and Earth's power fail together on PDF pages 253-259
- When a person offers the reader a rescue, a cure, a release, or a second chance, and the offer carries a price that the offerer states only after the reader accepts, as Cooper states Brandon Strayne's price only after Brandon Strayne collapses on PDF pages 255-256

## Lessons From Book

### Lesson 1: Be the Heretic Who Believes, Tests, and Keeps Sharing

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Heretic Passage Defines the Pattern Before Chapter 1

The passage printed before Chapter 1 on PDF page 5 defines a Heretic as a person who holds a belief that the masses reject. The passage states the sequence in order: people scorn the Heretic, mock the Heretic, laugh at the Heretic, challenge the Heretic, and dismiss the Heretic as absurd, and the Heretic continues to believe after each response. The passage states the result in order: one or two people join the Heretic first, more people join after the first one or two people join, and after a period of time that the passage does not number in days, months, or years, the Heretic receives a new public name — visionary thinker — because the Heretic persisted in the belief and persuaded other people to share the belief.

##### Example 2: Trew States on Tygon That Tygon Citizens Live in a Simulation

In Chapter 46 on PDF page 170, Lisa Rohansen asks Trew a direct question during a morning interview: does Trew believe that the people of Tygon live in an elaborate and complex reality simulation, as players in a computer game? Trew answers with one word first — "Yes" — and Trew adds that millions of other people share the same belief. Trew does not withdraw the claim when Lisa Rohansen states that the claim makes Lisa Rohansen dizzy, and Trew closes the interview with a commitment: "I'm here to hold us all steady."

##### Example 3: Millions of Tygon Citizens Form Gamer Groups Underground

In Chapter 44 on PDF page 163, a Channel 1 Game News report states that millions of people on Tygon follow the teachings of Trew, call themselves Gamers, and meet to discuss Trew's messages and philosophy. The report states two facts that confirm the Heretic sequence from PDF page 5 on Tygon: exact numbers of Tygon Gamers are difficult to estimate because Tygon Gamers follow Danielle Radfield's advice from inside the Game, remain underground, avoid large meetings, and hide communication trails; and Tygon, a world that previously had no large religion, has a large religion again.

#### Step 1: Write the Rejected Claim as One Sentence With a Testable Result

The reader writes one sentence that names the claim, names the actor, and names the result that a witness can observe. A valid sentence reads: the reader claims that the reader can float six inches above the floor for five seconds while a named witness watches, as Melissa demonstrates floating to Sam on PDF pages 13-14. A sentence that names no observable result fails Step 1. The reader knows Step 1 worked when a second person reads the sentence and states, without asking the reader a question, what event the second person must watch for.

#### Step 2: Run the Test in Front of a Named Witness and Record the Result

The reader invites one named witness, performs the test once under the conditions written in Step 1, and records three items within one hour: the date, the witness's name, and the observed result in measurable units (inches, seconds, metres, or a count). Melissa follows Step 2 when Melissa floats in Sam's apartment on PDF pages 13-14 and Sam measures the height with Sam's hands held about six inches apart. The reader knows Step 2 worked when the reader holds a dated record that a named witness signed or verbally confirmed on the day of the test.

#### Step 3: Share the Recorded Result With a Small Audience Before a Large Audience

The reader shares the Step 2 record with two named people first, following the Heretic sequence on PDF page 5, in which one or two people join before more people join. The reader states the claim, states the test conditions, states the recorded result, and invites each listener to attend a repeated test. The reader does not open the claim to a public audience until two named people have seen the record and one named person has attended a repeated test. The reader knows Step 3 worked when the reader can name the first two people who accepted the record and the date on which each person accepted the record.

#### Step 4: Continue the Test-and-Share Cycle After Ridicule Begins

When a listener mocks the claim, the reader records the mockery in one factual sentence, repeats the Step 2 test on the next scheduled test date, and shares the new recorded result with the Step 3 audience. The reader does not answer mockery with a threat, a Sever Spike, or a public insult. Trew follows Step 4 on PDF page 170: Lisa Rohansen reports dizziness at the simulation claim, and Trew answers the simulation claim question again without withdrawing the claim. The reader knows Step 4 worked when the reader's record contains three test results dated after the first recorded act of mockery.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ State the claim in one sentence with an observable result, as Trew answers Lisa Rohansen's simulation question with "Yes" on PDF page 170 before Trew adds explanation
- ✅ Recruit one witness before recruiting an audience, as Melissa shows Sam the floating demonstration on PDF pages 13-14 before Melissa approaches Danielle Radfield
- ✅ Let the first one or two supporters arrive before pursuing a crowd, following the Heretic sequence on PDF page 5
- ❌ Do not present a claim to a crowd before a witness records a test result; the crowd receives a slogan, and the reader receives ridicule without evidence
- ❌ Do not withdraw a tested claim because a listener reports discomfort; Lisa Rohansen reports dizziness on PDF page 170, and discomfort is not a test result

#### Keep In Mind

- The Heretic passage on PDF page 5 gives the visionary name only after persistence and persuasion both occur; persistence without persuasion leaves the Heretic alone, and persuasion without a test leaves the Heretic with followers who hold no evidence
- A belief becomes a power source inside Digital Heretic: the voice that holds Zack in Chapter 5 on PDF pages 17-19 states that the voice draws power from followers and has been asleep for a long period because followers were absent; Lesson 1 therefore treats an audience as a resource with obligations, not as decoration
- Trew's simulation claim about Tygon on PDF page 170 remains a claim inside Digital Heretic; Digital Heretic ends on PDF page 259 without a scene in which a character proves the Tygon simulation claim to a sceptical witness with a repeated test

#### Security & Safety Notes

- A reader must not test a physical claim in a place where a failed test injures the reader or a bystander; Melissa's first floating demonstration occurs indoors on PDF pages 13-14, and Danielle Radfield's cliff diving on PDF pages 6-7 is a warning case in Lesson 2, not a test method
- A reader must protect the identity of a witness who fears retaliation; Melissa hides from groups that hunt people with Melissa's ability for seven years on PDF page 38, and a reader must obtain a witness's written consent before publishing the witness's name
- A reader must not use a Heretic claim to demand money, obedience, or medical decisions from followers; Daniel and Gabriel use authority over believers to schedule the Culling on PDF pages 98-99, and Lesson 7 sets the reader's duty when an authority schedules harm

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** The reader repeats a claim for months and produces no dated test record, so listeners must choose between blind belief and blind rejection
  **Solution:** The reader completes Step 1 and Step 2 within seven days of first stating the claim, and the reader shows the dated record at every later sharing
- **Problem:** The reader treats every critic as proof that the reader is a Heretic, so failed tests accumulate without revision
  **Solution:** The reader revises the Step 1 sentence after two consecutive failed tests under identical conditions, and the reader records the revision date beside the original sentence
- **Problem:** The reader gathers followers faster than the reader gathers evidence, and followers act on an untested claim at scale
  **Solution:** The reader applies the Step 3 gate: no public sharing until two named people accept the record and one named person attends a repeated test

### Lesson 2: Choose to Live Long Instead of Turning Grief Into a Death Wish

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Stephanie Names Danielle Radfield's Death Wish on the Cliff

Chapter 1 opens on PDF pages 6-7 with Stephanie and Raphael watching Danielle Radfield from a rock about 80 feet above a beach. Stephanie states the diagnosis in one sentence: "She has a death wish." Danielle Radfield has spent the morning diving off different cliffs into ocean waves on a day Stephanie judges unsuitable for diving, and Raphael reports that Raphael thought Raphael saw sharks in the water a few minutes earlier. Stephanie corrects the wording without softening the diagnosis: Danielle Radfield calls the behaviour a wish to experience life more fully, and Stephanie and Raphael agree that Danielle Radfield will die in an accident during thrill-seeking activity if the behaviour continues.

##### Example 2: Danielle Radfield Tells Trew That Death Will Reunite Danielle Radfield With Trew Sooner

In the interior address to Trew on PDF pages 7-8, Danielle Radfield states the belief that drives the risk: Danielle Radfield believes Danielle Radfield and Trew will be together again after Danielle Radfield dies, and Danielle Radfield states, "if I die in the process? Big deal. I'll see you sooner rather than later." Danielle Radfield reports swimming with sharks in the ocean on the same morning, reports that the sharks swam farther away as Danielle Radfield tried to approach the sharks, and states that Danielle Radfield is "just getting started."

##### Example 3: Danielle Radfield Accepts a Long Life and a Clean Mark Instead of a Short Life

In Chapter 2 on PDF pages 10-12, Raphael tells Danielle Radfield that Danielle Radfield carries a Clean Mark: an invisible mark that Eternals and Infernals can see, that orders Eternals and Infernals to leave Danielle Radfield alone, and that kills an Eternal or an Infernal who harms Danielle Radfield while the Clean Mark is present. Raphael states that Raphael stopped assassins from killing Danielle Radfield when Danielle Radfield was a child and arranged the Clean Mark at a meeting with the assassins' side. Danielle Radfield then states a lifespan decision in the same conversation sequence: Danielle Radfield will live for another hundred years, matching an agreement Danielle Radfield and Trew made on Trew's birthday, and Danielle Radfield confirms the lifespan decision with credits inside the Game; Brandon Strayne reports the purchase on PDF page 71, stating that Danielle Radfield spent credits to live to age 140.

#### Step 1: Name the Risk Behaviour and the Belief That Excuses the Risk Behaviour

The reader writes two sentences. Sentence one names the risk behaviour with a date, a place, and a measurable exposure: Danielle Radfield's sentence one names cliff diving into ocean waves and swimming toward sharks on PDF pages 6-8. Sentence two names the belief that makes the risk behaviour appear harmless: Danielle Radfield's sentence two names the belief that death returns Danielle Radfield to Trew sooner. The reader knows Step 1 worked when a named friend reads both sentences and identifies the behaviour and the belief without asking the reader to explain either sentence.

#### Step 2: Accept a Named Protector's Observation as Data on the Day the Protector Speaks

The reader selects one named person who has watched the reader's behaviour for at least thirty days, asks the named person one question — "What behaviour of mine most concerns you?" — and writes the named person's answer word for word. Stephanie performs the protector role on PDF pages 6-7, and Raphael confirms Stephanie's observation in the same scene. The reader does not argue during the writing. The reader knows Step 2 worked when the reader holds a written answer that names a behaviour, a frequency, and a predicted result.

#### Step 3: Set a Lifespan Goal in Years and Remove One Risk Behaviour for Thirty Days

The reader writes a lifespan goal as a number of years, following Danielle Radfield's hundred-year decision on PDF pages 10-12 and Danielle Radfield's credit purchase of a life to age 140 reported on PDF page 71. The reader then selects the single risk behaviour from Step 1 with the highest probability of death and stops the selected behaviour for thirty consecutive days. The reader replaces the stopped behaviour with a scheduled activity that produces exertion without a lethal failure mode: a timed run on a measured course, a supervised swim in a pool, or a martial-arts class with an instructor present. The reader knows Step 3 worked when a calendar shows thirty consecutive days without the selected behaviour and at least twelve replacement activities completed.

#### Step 4: Tell Two Named People the Lifespan Goal and the Stopped Behaviour

The reader tells two named people three items in one conversation with each person: the lifespan goal from Step 3, the stopped behaviour from Step 3, and the date on which the thirty-day period ends. Danielle Radfield states the lifespan decision to Raphael and Stephanie, the two protectors who raised the concern, on PDF pages 10-12. The reader knows Step 4 worked when each of the two named people can repeat the lifespan goal and the stopped behaviour to the reader seven days after the conversation.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Treat a protector's blunt sentence as a measurement, as Stephanie's sentence "She has a death wish" on PDF page 6 names Danielle Radfield's pattern before an accident occurs
- ✅ Replace a lethal thrill with a demanding activity that has a non-lethal failure mode, as Danielle Radfield later channels exertion into martial-arts training and an Olympic gold medal project reported on PDF page 52
- ✅ State a lifespan goal as a number, as Danielle Radfield states another hundred years on PDF pages 10-12
- ❌ Do not use an afterlife belief to discount a present risk; Danielle Radfield's reunion belief on PDF pages 7-8 increases Danielle Radfield's exposure to sharks and cliffs instead of decreasing the exposure
- ❌ Do not wait for an accident to confirm a protector's observation; Stephanie and Raphael state the predicted accident on PDF pages 6-7 while Danielle Radfield remains able to change the behaviour

#### Keep In Mind

- Danielle Radfield does not take Danielle Radfield's own life in Chapter 1; Stephanie states on PDF pages 6-7 that Danielle Radfield will not quit the Game directly, and that the danger operates through escalating accidents, so a reader must watch accident patterns, not only explicit statements of intent
- A Clean Mark protects Danielle Radfield from Eternals and Infernals only; Raphael states on PDF pages 10-12 that the largest remaining threat to Danielle Radfield is other humans — people Raphael calls crazies and zealots from other religions — so one protection never covers every threat class
- Grief remains present after the lifespan decision; Danielle Radfield still hunts Carl years later on PDF page 35, and Lesson 6 governs the revenge goal that grief creates after the death wish recedes

#### Security & Safety Notes

- A reader who has a plan to end the reader's own life, or who has taken an action intended to end the reader's own life, must contact emergency services in the reader's country and one named person from Step 4 immediately; Digital Heretic is a novel, and no character in Digital Heretic provides a clinical intervention
- A reader must not perform cliff diving, shark swimming, free climbing, or vehicle stunts as a grief response; PDF pages 6-8 present Danielle Radfield's cliff diving and shark swimming as the behaviour Stephanie and Raphael identify as a path to an accidental death
- A reader must tell a named protector before entering a high-risk environment during the thirty-day period in Step 3, and the named protector must hold the reader's location and return time in writing

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** The reader renames a death wish as a wish to experience life fully, so friends hesitate to object
  **Solution:** The reader applies Stephanie's test from PDF pages 6-7: the reader asks whether the behaviour's failure mode is death, and the reader classifies the behaviour by the failure mode, not by the reader's preferred name for the behaviour
- **Problem:** The reader stops one risk behaviour and substitutes a second risk behaviour with an equal failure mode
  **Solution:** The reader lists the failure mode of the replacement activity in Step 3 and rejects a replacement activity whose failure mode is death or permanent injury
- **Problem:** The reader makes a lifespan decision alone and tells no person, so no person can challenge a relapse
  **Solution:** The reader completes Step 4 within forty-eight hours of Step 3 and schedules a review conversation with both named people on day thirty

### Lesson 3: Disband the Public Movement and Rebuild the Movement as Small Cells

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Danielle Radfield Orders the Gamers to Disband and Disappear

At the end of Chapter 2 on PDF page 12, Danielle Radfield sits at a desk, confirms that Danielle Radfield is in charge of the Gamers, and issues one order: "In order to become the biggest movement the world has ever seen… we're going to disband and disappear." The order follows the report in Chapter 2 on PDF page 10 that the Gamers have taken no collective action and have received no clear direction in the months after Trew's assassination, strong in belief and leaderless in practice. Danielle Radfield chooses disappearance as the growth method at the moment when commentators expect Danielle Radfield to step forward, lead publicly, and grow the Gamers at a phenomenal rate.

##### Example 2: Tygon Gamers Follow the Cell Method and Become Uncountable

In Chapter 44 on PDF page 163, the Channel 1 Game News report states that millions of Tygon Gamers remain underground, avoid large meetings, and hide communication trails, following Danielle Radfield's advice inside the Game. The report states the operational result directly: exact numbers of Tygon Gamers are difficult to estimate. A movement that an enemy cannot count is a movement that an enemy cannot target with a complete list.

##### Example 3: Danielle Radfield as Fallen Rallies Cells That Survive the Final Outage

In Chapter 65 on PDF page 242, a report states that Danielle Radfield has increased Danielle Radfield's persona of "Fallen," rallying cells of Gamers all over the globe, and conducting meetings and setting up methods for communicating and assembling in times of crisis. In the Epilogue on PDF pages 258-259, after Earth's power and wireless signal fail across the entire planet, Stephanie states the payoff in one sentence: the Gamers are all prepared for this type of scenario, everyone will lay low and live separate lives, and the small cells will continue to band together and meet, helping each other against trouble.

#### Step 1: List Every Public Surface That Lets a Hostile Actor Find Members

The reader writes an inventory with four columns: public surface (a meeting, a mailing list, a social-media account, a published address), members exposed by the surface, information the surface reveals, and the date the reader will close or restrict the surface. Danielle Radfield's inventory inside Digital Heretic includes the large Gamer gatherings that Taylor Jenkins addresses on PDF pages 105-106, where one hostile man locks the exit doors of a single meeting room. The reader knows Step 1 worked when the inventory contains every recurring public meeting and every published member list, verified against the reader's calendar for the previous ninety days.

#### Step 2: Divide Members Into Cells With a Named Leader and a Maximum Cell Size

The reader divides the membership into cells, assigns one named leader to each cell, and caps each cell at a size the named leader can contact within one hour without a network service: the reader writes the cap as a number before assigning members, and a cap of twelve members per cell satisfies Step 2 for a group whose leader uses telephone calls or in-person visits. Stephanie's Epilogue statement on PDF pages 258-259 describes the end state: small cells band together and meet, and each cell helps the members of the cell against trouble. The reader knows Step 2 worked when every member appears on exactly one cell roster and every cell roster names one leader and one deputy leader.

#### Step 3: Give Each Cell a Communication Method and an Assembly Method That Work Without a Public Announcement

The reader writes, for each cell, one communication method (a telephone tree, a scheduled in-person check-in, a coded message drop at a named location) and one assembly method (a named meeting place, a trigger event, and a maximum travel time). Danielle Radfield sets up methods for communicating and assembling in times of crisis on PDF page 242 before the crisis arrives. The reader knows Step 3 worked when the reader runs one drill in which every cell leader confirms receipt of a test message within one hour and confirms that the cell can assemble at the named meeting place within the maximum travel time the reader wrote for the cell.

#### Step 4: Keep a Public Message and Remove the Public Target

The reader continues publishing the movement's message through channels that expose no member roster, no meeting address, and no leader's home location. Trew follows Step 4 on Tygon: Trew speaks on the "Trew live" channel on PDF pages 100 and 123-125 while Tygon Gamers meet underground on PDF page 163. The reader knows Step 4 worked when a hostile reader of the public channel can state the movement's message and cannot state the name, address, or meeting place of any cell member from the public channel alone.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Announce the disappearance as a deliberate order from the named leader, as Danielle Radfield announces the disband order on PDF page 12, so members treat disappearance as discipline rather than defeat
- ✅ Build the cell communication and assembly methods before a crisis, as Danielle Radfield builds crisis methods on PDF page 242 and the Epilogue outage arrives on PDF pages 253-259
- ✅ Keep the message public and the membership private, following the Tygon pattern on PDF page 163
- ❌ Do not hold recurring large meetings with published times after a hostile actor has demonstrated surveillance; Taylor Jenkins's meeting on PDF pages 105-107 ends with locked doors and a massacre of every attendee except ten named survivors
- ❌ Do not keep a central member list in one location; Carl's Culling assignment on PDF page 122 requires Carl to find Gamers who joined secretly and as small underground cells, and a central list hands Carl the completed assignment

#### Keep In Mind

- Disappearance does not end growth inside Digital Heretic: the Gamers reach about 70 million members on PDF page 53, pass 100 million members on PDF page 110, and add members during the Culling, because Daniel complains to Carl on PDF page 122 that the Gamers "have added to their ranks despite this mass annihilation"
- A cell structure protects members from a list-based purge and does not protect a member who attends a public meeting; Lesson 7 governs the reader's response when a purge begins despite the cell structure
- Danielle Radfield's disband order on PDF page 12 precedes Danielle Radfield's refusal to lead the Gamers publicly for years, and Nadine reports on PDF page 35 that the Gamers look for a real leader while Danielle Radfield hunts Carl; a reader who disbands a public structure must still name who leads the cells, or the cells inherit the condition reported on PDF page 10: no collective action taken and no clear direction given

#### Security & Safety Notes

- A reader must store each cell roster with access limited to the cell leader and the deputy leader named in Step 2, and a reader must never store every cell roster in one file, one account, or one building
- A reader must not use a cell structure to plan violence; Digital Heretic presents cells as a protection and mutual-aid structure on PDF pages 258-259, and Daniel's Infernal organisation on PDF page 122 is the counterexample of a secret structure built to kill
- A reader must give each cell member a lawful emergency contact method (a telephone number for emergency services in the member's country) that does not depend on the cell leader's availability

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** The reader disbands the public movement and provides no cell assignment, so members drift, duplicate effort, and re-form public groups without protection
  **Solution:** The reader completes Step 2 before announcing the disband order, and the announcement message contains each recipient's cell leader name and first check-in date
- **Problem:** The reader builds cells and continues publishing meeting photographs with faces, name badges, and location signs
  **Solution:** The reader applies the Step 4 test to every publication: a publication that reveals a member name, a member face with a location, or a meeting address fails Step 4 and the reader withdraws the publication
- **Problem:** The reader treats secrecy as a substitute for a crisis plan, and cells cannot assemble when networks fail
  **Solution:** The reader completes the Step 3 drill before declaring the cell structure ready, and the reader repeats the drill after every change of cell leader

### Lesson 4: Test an Extraordinary Ability With a Witness, a Demonstration, and Years of Practice

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Melissa Floats Six Inches Above Sam's Floor and Sam Measures the Height

In Chapter 3 on PDF pages 13-14, Melissa arrives at Sam's apartment, states that Melissa needs Sam's trust, recalls Trew's last public talk about flight and the power of the human mind, closes Melissa's eyes, and feels a gold, glowing feeling spread over Melissa's body. Sam gasps, swears, and holds Sam's hands about six inches apart to show Melissa the height at which Melissa floated. Melissa asks, "How high?" and Sam confirms the measurement with a nod and a whisper. The demonstration has three parts that Lesson 4 requires: a witness (Sam), a measurable result (about six inches), and a witness reaction recorded in the scene (Sam covers Sam's mouth and whispers the confirmation).

##### Example 2: Melissa Flies 25 Feet Above a Field in Front of Danielle Radfield, Raphael, and Stephanie

In Chapter 14 on PDF pages 54-56, Melissa meets Danielle Radfield in a secluded field, states that Melissa needs protection, and demonstrates flight on Raphael's instruction. Melissa pushes upward, floats about 25 feet above the group, flies one circle in about 30 seconds, flies a second circle in less than five seconds, drops, stops about one foot from the ground, and floats the remaining distance down. Danielle Radfield, Raphael, and Stephanie watch the entire demonstration. Danielle Radfield responds with an offer that converts the demonstration into training: Melissa will live with Danielle Radfield and teach Danielle Radfield the flight ability.

##### Example 3: Andrew Young States the Flight Method as a Present-Tense Identity and a Clean Body

In Chapter 58 on PDF page 219, Andrew Young, identified in the interview heading as Tygon's first flying citizen, states the mental rule for flight in two sentences: a person must not think "Yes, I can do it" and must think "Yes, I do fly. I flew earlier and I'll fly later as well." Andrew Young states the reason in one sentence: a person who tells the person that the person will fly soon will never fly, because the person never lives in "soon." Andrew Young adds a physical rule in the same interview: birds have hollow bones, humans fill human bodies with poisons, and one sip of a product containing aspartame makes Andrew Young sink to the ground; Andrew Young therefore treats flight as a mind component and a body component, and Andrew Young credits Melissa as Andrew Young's teacher and Trew as Melissa's source.

#### Step 1: Demonstrate the Ability Once in Front of One Named Witness in a Controlled Place

The reader selects an indoor or secluded place, one named witness, and one measurable target (a height in inches, a duration in seconds, or a distance in metres). The reader performs the demonstration once and asks the witness to state the observed measurement aloud immediately after the demonstration. Melissa performs Step 1 in Sam's apartment on PDF pages 13-14. The reader knows Step 1 worked when the witness states a measurement that the reader writes down with the date and the place within one hour.

#### Step 2: Record the Practice Price in Hours, Weeks, and Months Before Promising a Result

The reader writes a practice plan that states the daily practice duration, the weekly practice frequency, and the total practice period in months. Melissa states the price to Danielle Radfield on PDF page 108 in exact terms: Melissa spent hours, weeks, and months learning to meditate and channel the Glow, and only after Melissa could summon the golden tingly feeling on command did Melissa begin to experiment with flight. Melissa also reports on PDF page 38 that seven years passed between Melissa's first float in Sam's apartment and Melissa's meeting with Danielle Radfield, and that control of the ability took years because the ability activated at unsuitable moments in front of watchers. The reader knows Step 2 worked when the practice plan contains a total period measured in months and a daily duration measured in minutes, and the reader has logged fourteen consecutive practice days.

#### Step 3: Teach the Ability to One Named Student and Use the Student's Result as the Second Test

The reader selects one named student, teaches the student the Step 2 practice plan without adding a new technique, and records the date on which the student first produces the measurable result from Step 1. Danielle Radfield becomes Melissa's student on PDF page 56, and Melissa teaches Danielle Radfield in scheduled sessions on PDF page 108 ("Same time tomorrow?"). Andrew Young becomes the Tygon confirmation in Chapter 58 on PDF page 219: Andrew Young learned from Melissa, and Andrew Young states that Andrew Young would not have achieved flight without Melissa. The reader knows Step 3 worked when a named student produces the measurable result in front of a second named witness and the reader records the student's name, the date, and the measurement.

#### Step 4: State the Ability in the Present Tense and Audit the Body Inputs That Block the Ability

The reader writes the ability statement in the present tense, following Andrew Young's rule on PDF page 219: the reader writes "I do" followed by the ability verb — Andrew Young's own form for flight is "I do fly" — and writes the past-tense form as "I" followed by the past tense of the ability verb, the word "on," and the calendar date of the reader's most recent demonstration, instead of a future-tense form such as "I will fly soon." The reader then lists every food, drink, drug, and sleep pattern that the reader consumed or followed during the seven days before a failed demonstration, and the reader removes one listed input for fourteen days and repeats the demonstration. Andrew Young names aspartame as the input that makes Andrew Young sink on PDF page 219. The reader knows Step 4 worked when the reader holds a written input list, a fourteen-day removal record, and a repeated demonstration result dated after the removal period.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Use a secluded demonstration place, as Melissa uses Sam's apartment on PDF pages 13-14 and a secluded field on PDF pages 54-56, so the reader controls the witness list
- ✅ State the practice price before stating the promise, as Melissa states hours, weeks, and months on PDF page 108 before Danielle Radfield begins training
- ✅ Convert a successful demonstration into a teaching relationship, as Danielle Radfield offers Melissa housing and protection in exchange for flight lessons on PDF page 56
- ❌ Do not claim mastery after a first uncontrolled activation; Melissa reports on PDF page 38 that the ability activated at the worst moments for years before Melissa controlled the ability
- ❌ Do not state an ability goal in future-tense language during practice; Andrew Young identifies future-tense language as the failure mechanism on PDF page 219

#### Keep In Mind

The reader knows Step 4 worked when the trigger exists in writing, the named reviewer has accepted the trigger in writing, and the reader's calendar or monitoring tool can detect the threshold without a human remembering to check.

  **Solution:** The reader performs Step 1 in both time units that the system touches and reports the larger growth figure as the audit figure

- A reader must include medication, infant supplies, and mobility requirements for each roster member in the Step 3 supply list, with quantities calculated from each member's daily requirement multiplied by the stated number of days

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** The reader accepts a benefit, celebrates the benefit, and learns the price from a bystander after the payer collapses
  **Solution:** The reader applies Step 1 and Step 2 as a gate: no written payer confirmation, no acceptance, and the reader records declined offers with the missing confirmation named as the reason for decline
- **Problem:** The reader prepares for a short outage because an official source predicts a short outage, and supplies run out while the outage continues
  **Solution:** The reader sizes Step 3 supplies by the roster's daily requirements for the number of days the reader writes in the fallback plan, and the reader treats an official duration prediction as unproven until service actually returns
- **Problem:** The reader's fallback group has a roster and no rehearsal, and members travel to different places during the first crisis day
  **Solution:** The reader runs the Step 3 drill within thirty days of writing the fallback plan and repeats the drill after any roster change, so the meeting place and the travel route are memories, not documents
