# Book Title: Principles: Life and Work

## Overview

This book teaches a framework for life and management based on radical truth and radical transparency. It provides actionable principles for making better decisions, building effective organizations, and learning from mistakes. The goal is to help readers develop their own principles for achieving goals through systematic thinking and continuous improvement.

## When to Follow Book Teachings

- When you need to design or improve an organization
- When making important life or business decisions
- When working with teams and need clearer responsibilities
- When learning from mistakes and want systematic improvement
- When building a culture of truth and accountability

## Lessons From Book

### Lesson 1: Design Organizations With Clear Structure And Limited Layers

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Department Structure

Combining client agreements and counterparty agreements under one manager would be a mistake because the skills required are very different. Each kind calls for specific knowledge and skills.

##### Example 2: Manager Ratios

The ratio of senior managers to junior managers should generally not be more than 1:10, and preferably closer to 1:5, to preserve quality communication.

#### Step 1: Limit Manager Ratios

Maintain a ratio of senior managers to direct reports that preserves communication quality. Generally not more than 1:10, preferably closer to 1:5.

#### Step 2: Minimize Hierarchy Layers

Create an organizational chart that looks like a pyramid with straight lines down that don't cross. Limit the number of layers to minimize hierarchy while maintaining effective span of control.

#### Step 3: Assign By Ability Not Title

Assign responsibilities based on workflow design and people's abilities, not job titles. Just because someone has "human resources" in their title doesn't mean they should determine hiring and benefits.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do build the organization around the work that needs to be done
- ✅ Do maintain clear reporting lines without dual reporting
- ✅ Do appoint co-heads when complementary strengths are needed
- ❌ Don't build the organization to fit the people
- ❌ Don't let support departments determine vision
- ❌ Don't allow dual reporting without proper coordination

#### Keep In Mind

- The larger the organization, the more important information technology expertise and cross-department communication become
- Jobs are created based on work that needs to be done, not what people want to do

#### Security & Safety Notes

- People doing auditing should report to people outside the department being audited
- Auditing procedures should not be made known to those being audited

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Department slip occurs when support departments mistake their responsibility to provide support with determining how things should be done.
**Solution:** Clarify that support departments provide feedback and know goals, but do not determine the vision.

### Lesson 2: Create Leverage Through Technology And Smart People

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Training Leverage

Document common questions and answers through audio, video, or written guidelines and assign someone to regularly organize them into a manual.

##### Example 2: Manager Leverage

A master manager doesn't have to "do" practically anything. A "supreme master" manager can hire people to hire and oversee, achieving such leverage that things run effortlessly.

#### Step 1: Think Creatively About Tools

Constantly think about how to produce leverage using technology. Design tools that provide leverage for you and your team.

#### Step 2: Delegate Details Away

You should be able to delegate the details away. If you can't, you either have managing/training problems or the wrong people doing the job.

#### Step 3: Hire Smart People With Great Technology

It is far better to find a few smart people and give them the best technology than to have many ordinary, less-equipped people. Smart people with great technology in a streamlined organization is optimal.

#### Step 4: Use Leveragers

Use "leveragers" who are capable of implementing concepts. Conceptualizing and managing take only about 10% of the time needed for implementing, so good leveragers allow you to accomplish much more.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do hire a few smart people and equip them with great technology
- ✅ Do document processes for easy leverage
- ✅ Do delegate details to capable people
- ❌ Don't get involved in nitty-gritty details yourself
- ❌ Don't use consultants for work that should be done by employees

#### Keep In Mind

- Great people and great technology are almost always a great value
- Fragmented understanding and control create inefficiencies

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Not applicable to this lesson

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Consultant addiction - chronic use of consultants for work that should be done by employees.
**Solution:** Build internal capability and use consultants only for truly specialized needs.

### Lesson 3: Use Systems And Tools For Quality Control

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Procedures Manual

Maintain a procedures manual describing how all pieces of your machine work with enough specificity for operators to refer to it. Include output from the issues log so mistakes aren't repeated.

##### Example 2: Checklists

When people are assigned tasks, capture them on checklists so they can check off each item. Crossing items off serves as a task reminder and confirmation.

##### Example 3: Double-Do

Use "double-do" rather than "double-check" for mission-critical tasks. Two parties independently do the same task and results are compared, catching more errors than double-checking.

#### Step 1: Maintain A Procedures Manual

Create a living document describing how all pieces of your machine work. Include output from the issues log so identified mistakes aren't repeated.

#### Step 2: Implement Checklists

Capture assigned tasks on checklists so people can check off each item as done. This serves as reminder and confirmation.

#### Step 3: Use Double-Do For Critical Tasks

For mission-critical tasks, use "double-do" where two different people independently do the same task and results are compared, rather than one person double-checking another's work.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do maintain a living procedures manual
- ✅ Do use checklists for task tracking
- ✅ Do use double-do for critical tasks
- ❌ Don't confuse checklists with personal responsibility
- ❌ Don't think systematic must mean computerized

#### Keep In Mind

- "Systematic" doesn't necessarily mean computerized - it can mean people doing specified tasks and indicating completion
- Checklists remind people of agreed tasks and confirm completion

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Have good controls so you are not exposed to dishonesty
- Security controls are necessary tools, not personal affronts

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Job slip - a job changes without being explicitly thought through and agreed to.
**Solution:** Think clearly how things should go, and when they aren't going that way, acknowledge it and investigate before the job changes unintentionally.

### Lesson 4: Make Decisions Logically Using Expected Value

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Risk/Reward Bets

All decisions are risk/reward bets. Pursue opportunities with fabulous risk/reward ratios that have huge upside and very little downside, even if probability is small.

##### Example 2: Raising Probability

Raising the probability of being right from 51% to 85% can be worth more than going from probably wrong (45%) to probably being right (51%) because we are all playing probabilities.

#### Step 1: Make Decisions As Expected Value Calculations

Considering both probabilities and payoffs, make sure the probability of unacceptable outcomes (risk of ruin) is nil.

#### Step 2: Diversify Your Bets

Don't bet too much on anything. Make 15 or more good, uncorrelated bets to spread risk.

#### Step 3: Recognize What You Don't Know

Your goal is to come up with the best answer, not give the best one you have. The answer doesn't have to be in your head - you can look outside yourself.

#### Step 4: Triangulate Your View

Never make important decisions without asking at least three believable people. Understand their reasoning, don't just get their conclusions.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do consider both probabilities and payoffs
- ✅ Do triangulate with believable people
- ✅ Do recognize what you don't know
- ❌ Don't bet too much on any one outcome
- ❌ Don't give opinions as soon as you have them

#### Keep In Mind

- The cost of a bad decision is equal to or greater than the reward of a good decision
- Almost anything is possible - all possibilities must be looked at in terms of likelihoods and prioritized

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Not applicable to this lesson

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Arguing against something because you can find something wrong without weighing pros against cons.
**Solution:** Remember best choices have more pros than cons, not those without any cons. Watch for people who argue without proper weighing.

### Lesson 5: Apply The 80/20 Rule And Prioritize Effectively

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Important Things First

Distinguish the important things from the unimportant things and deal with the important things first.

##### Example 2: Perfectionism Trap

Perfectionists often spend too much time on little differences at the margins at the expense of other big, important things. Be an effective imperfectionist.

#### Step 1: Distinguish Important From Unimportant

Identify what matters most. Since 80% of the juice can be gotten with the first 20% of squeezing, there are relatively few important things to consider.

#### Step 2: Avoid Perfectionism

Don't be a perfectionist. Solutions that broadly work well are generally better than highly specialized solutions, especially in early stages.

#### Step 3: Prioritize Must-Dos

Make sure all "must do's" are above the bar before anything else. Distinguish between "must do's" and "like to do's". Get "must do's" excellent before turning to others.

#### Step 4: Time Decisions Appropriately

Think about the appropriate time to make a decision in light of marginal gains of additional information versus marginal costs of postponement.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do distinguish important from unimportant things
- ✅ Do deal with important things first
- ✅ Do get must-dos excellent before nice-to-dos
- ❌ Don't be a perfectionist
- ❌ Don't mistake small things for unimportant things
- ❌ Don't worry inappropriately about unimportant details

#### Keep In Mind

- 80% of the juice can be gotten with the first 20% of squeezing
- There are typically less than five important things to consider in making a decision

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Not applicable to this lesson

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Detail anxiety - worrying inappropriately about unimportant, small things.
**Solution:** Watch out for detail anxiety and focus on the few important things that matter most.

### Lesson 6: Synthesize To Understand How Your Machine Works

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Connecting Dots

It takes diagnosing a few dots of the same type to get at the true root cause. Understand Harry handling orders by looking objectively at outcomes and getting in synch, especially about bad outcomes.

##### Example 2: Seeing Through Time

To understand how your machine is working, you must perceive change over time, charting improvement vs. deterioration. Synthesize understanding into a picture of how your machine should be improved.

#### Step 1: Diagnose Multiple Instances

Diagnose at least a few occurrences of the same type to get at the true root cause. Categorize outcomes by type and quality.

#### Step 2: Examine Bad Outcomes

Examine bad outcomes as they occur to understand what they are symptomatic of. Don't pass problems by without discussing reasons.

#### Step 3: Perceive Change Over Time

Collect, analyze, and sort outcomes over time. Chart improvement vs. deterioration to understand your machine's trajectory.

#### Step 4: Synthesize Understanding

Synthesize your understanding into a picture of how your machine is working and how it should be improved. People who do this well are rare and essential.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do diagnose multiple dots of the same type
- ✅ Do examine bad outcomes as they occur
- ✅ Do synthesize understanding over time
- ❌ Don't accept solutions below the bar
- ❌ Don't compromise on uncompromisable things

#### Keep In Mind

- It is the level and not the rate of change that matters most
- Everything important must be on a trajectory to be "above the bar" and headed for "excellent"
- Synthesizing well is partially innate and partially learned through practice

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Not applicable to this lesson

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Accepting your best solution when it isn't good enough.
**Solution:** If your best solution isn't good enough, think harder or escalate that you can't produce a solution that is good enough.

### Lesson 7: Embrace Reality And Learn From Mistakes

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Facing Harsh Realities

Recognizing harsh realities is both very painful and very productive. If you can stare hard at your problems, they almost always shrink or disappear.

##### Example 2: Learning From Failure

If you recognize short-term failure as a step toward long-term success, you won't be afraid of it and will approach all experiences as learning experiences.

#### Step 1: Embrace Pain And Reality

Accept that recognizing harsh realities can be extremely painful but is productive. Stare hard at your problems, especially your mistakes and weaknesses.

#### Step 2: Reflect Deeply

Use your power of reflectiveness to think deeply about your problems. Ask other believable people with opposing views about root causes to enhance your reflections.

#### Step 3: Learn From Mistakes

Recognize that mistakes are essential for learning. School overrates intellectualized learning; hands-on experience is particularly valuable.

#### Step 4: Push Through

When faced with the choice between plodding along normally or pushing through to achieve the goal, choose to push through. MAKE great things happen.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do face reality and your problems head on
- ✅ Do reflect deeply with others who have opposing views
- ✅ Do learn from mistakes and failures
- ✅ Do push through to achieve goals
- ❌ Don't let ego prevent acknowledging weaknesses
- ❌ Don't avoid pain that is consistent with achieving objectives

#### Keep In Mind

- The more difficult the problem, the more important it is to think hard about it and deal with it
- Ego often stands in the way of acknowledging weaknesses
- There are many paths to happiness - what happens to you doesn't determine happiness

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Not applicable to this lesson

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Mistakephobia - fear of not knowing and making mistakes.
**Solution:** Recognize that learning from mistakes is critical to real learning. Don't let embarrassment about not knowing prevent you from learning.

### Lesson 8: Build A Culture Of Truth And Accountability

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Radical Honesty

What you say should match your thoughts. Integrity means being the same inside and out.

##### Example 2: Merit-Based Decision Making

Involve believable people in challenging and probing to assess quality of work over time, not to second-guess every decision.

#### Step 1: Practice Radical Truth

Tell people what you really think. What you do say should match your thoughts. Encourage open-minded exploration of disagreements.

#### Step 2: Create Clear Responsibilities

Clearly designate responsibilities to enhance feedback and learning. The connection between actions and results should be clear.

#### Step 3: Maintain High Standards

Fight to maintain standards. Hire, train, and sort out people so responsibilities are in the hands of those who can be trusted to do excellent work.

#### Step 4: Use Believable People

Involve believable people in decision-making. Challenge and probe to assess quality, not to second-guess. Those with expertise have the right to opinions.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do say what you really think
- ✅ Do create clearly designated responsibilities
- ✅ Do maintain high standards
- ✅ Do involve believable people in decisions
- ❌ Don't compromise on standards
- ❌ Don't give responsibilities to people without required qualities

#### Keep In Mind

- The clearer the relationship between cause and effect, the better it is for learning
- Constantly worry about what you are missing
- Successful people ask for criticism of others and consider its merit

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Trustworthiness is a quality that should be appreciated
- Even the best controls will never be foolproof

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Allowing great people to avoid exploring mistakes and weaknesses because they find it painful.
**Solution:** Don't compromise on this - the process of exploration is healthy for the organization, the individual, and key to culture.
