# How To Get Rich

## Overview

"How To Get Rich" by Donald J. Trump is a concise guide to business success, leadership, deal-making, and personal development distilled from nearly thirty years of experience at the top of real estate and business. The book offers actionable advice on management, negotiation, hiring, branding, investing, and maintaining momentum, all framed through Trump's own career stories and business rules. It exists to provide practical, no-nonsense wisdom for anyone with big dreams who wants to understand what it takes to succeed in business and life.

## When to Follow Book Teachings

- When you need guidance on leadership and managing a team effectively
- When working on deal-making and negotiation strategies
- When building or scaling a business and need to maintain momentum
- When hiring or evaluating employees and want to avoid costly mistakes
- When preparing for job interviews or salary negotiations
- When developing a personal or company brand and learning to promote it
- When making financial or investment decisions
- When navigating career changes or considering entrepreneurship

## Lessons From Book

### Lesson 1: Be a General — Lead Like a Commander

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Running The Trump Organization

Trump describes running a 20,000-person organization like being a general. He relies on key people like Mark Brown (CEO of casinos), Laura Cordovano (sales at Trump Park Avenue), and Allen Weisselberg (CFO) who give him fast, informed answers. He emphasizes that good people and good management must work together.

##### Example 2: Motivating Through Challenge

When an employee's project lacked inspiration, Trump politely challenged his creative ego by suggesting he might not be genuinely interested. The employee went ballistic, but the revised work was terrific. The lesson: tailor your motivation method to the personality.

#### Step 1: Build a Trusted Inner Circle

Surround yourself with a few key people who keep you informed and whom you trust. Their competence and loyalty make management easier and decisions faster.

#### Step 2: Hire for Attitude and Drive

Choose people with creative drive and an inherent work ethic. Creative people rarely need external motivation — they have inner drive that refuses to be bored or complacent.

#### Step 3: Inspire and Motivate Tailored to Each Person

Generals motivate their soldiers and inspire their officers. Learn how to tailor your method to the personalities you manage. Keep the big picture in mind while attending to daily details.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Hire people who suit your business style
- ✅ Rely on key people for fast, informed answers
- ✅ Challenge creative egos to produce better work
- ✅ Choose attitude and drive over experience when needed
- ❌ Don't hire mediocre people — they will drive away good performers
- ❌ Don't ignore the need for both good management and good people

#### Keep In Mind

- Every decision is important whether you have twenty thousand employees or one
- Bad strategy can affect many people
- The good managers will eventually leave if surrounded by mediocre people, leaving you with a team that gets along because they're all mediocre

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Protect your organization by selecting trustworthy leadership — a breach at the top can cascade across thousands of employees

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Hiring people who look good on paper but lack the inner drive or cultural fit
  **Solution:** Look for a sense of responsibility that goes beyond the bare minimum; test their reaction to challenges during the interview process
- **Problem:** Mediocre management driving away top performers
  **Solution:** Fix management first — excellent people will not stay in bad management environments

### Lesson 2: Stay Focused — Avoid Distraction at All Costs

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Late 1980s Real Estate Crash

In the 1980s, Trump lost focus — flying to Europe for fashion shows and not paying attention to his business. When the real estate market crashed, he owed $9.2 billion. At the low point, he walked fifteen blocks in freezing rain to Citibank at 3 A.M. to negotiate with banks. He personally guaranteed $1 billion of the debt because he hadn't followed his own advice from The Art of the Deal.

##### Example 2: Recovery Through Focus

After surviving the crash, Trump worked as hard in the 1990s as he did in the 1970s as a young developer. He learned his lesson: don't make the mistake he did — stay focused.

#### Step 1: Recognize When You Are Losing Focus

If you find yourself chasing distractions (fashion shows, side projects, non-core activities) while your core business suffers, you are losing focus.

#### Step 2: Work Harder Than Anyone Else

Trump works seven days a week and loves almost every minute of it. He began working at a young age and never stopped.

#### Step 3: Never Personal Guarantee More Than You Can Afford

From his own mistake: never personally guarantee large amounts unless you are certain you can deliver. Protect your personal assets.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Stay focused on your core business every day
- ✅ Work as hard today as you did when you were starting out
- ✅ Keep your eye on the details of your field even when successful
- ❌ Don't get distracted by side interests when your business needs you
- ❌ Don't personally guarantee more than you can handle

#### Keep In Mind

- Even the best need to be retrained regularly to stay current
- You can't glide by on experience or smarts alone
- Momentum must be maintained through constant attention

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing your own hype and thinking success is easy
  **Solution:** Treat every day as if you are still earning your place; stay vigilant about market changes
- **Problem:** Personal guarantees that exceed your risk tolerance
  **Solution:** Follow your own advice: never personally guarantee anything you cannot comfortably cover

### Lesson 3: Maintain Your Momentum — Never Stop Moving

#### Examples

##### Example 1: William Levitt's Downfall

William Levitt, master builder of Levittown, sold his company in 1956 for $100 million, retired, married the wrong woman, and moved to the French Riviera. When ITT called him back because they couldn't zone the land, he went back into business and went bankrupt. At a cocktail party in 1994, two weeks before he died, he told Trump: "I lost my momentum. I was out of the world for twenty years, I came back, and I wasn't the same."

#### Step 1: Keep Your Hand on Every Detail

No detail is too small. Levitt used to personally collect stray nails and extra wood chips from building sites. You must remain vigilant about the details of your field at all times.

#### Step 2: Never Assume You Can Glide By

Don't assume experience alone will keep you successful. The best surgeons need regular retraining. You need to stay current on the latest research and procedures in your industry.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Treat every success as the beginning of the next one
- ✅ Keep learning every single day
- ✅ Stay physically and mentally engaged in your business
- ❌ Don't retire mentally even if you retire physically
- ❌ Don't assume past success guarantees future success

#### Keep In Mind

- Momentum is something you have to work at to maintain
- Growth is an indication of life — keep your organization moving forward

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Taking extended breaks that disconnect you from the business
  **Solution:** Stay involved daily; even brief disconnections can cost you critical market awareness
- **Problem:** Believing you have "made it" and can relax
  **Solution:** Each success is a new beginning, not an ending

### Lesson 4: Get a Great Assistant — Your Right Hand

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Norma Foerderer

Norma has been with Trump for over twenty years. She handles him, the office, and several hundred calls a week. She is tough, smart, gracious, and indefatigable. Trump's two executive assistants handle more than 1,250 calls a week on average.

##### Example 2: Testing a New Employee's Speed

To test how fast a new employee could work, Trump told him he was leaving in fifteen minutes and needed something done within that time. He wasn't actually going anywhere, but he had what he needed in fifteen minutes.

#### Step 1: Find Someone You Can Trust Implicitly

Surround yourself with people you can trust. Ask God for a great assistant — a great one can make your life a whole lot easier.

#### Step 2: Demand Competence and Work Ethic

You don't have to be beautiful to work for Trump — just be good at your job. He needs competent people with an inherent work ethic who can move forward quickly.

#### Step 3: Find a Receptionist Who Speaks English

Trump learned this from experience: a European beauty who couldn't recognize well-known Americans let their calls fall through. Practical communication skills matter more than appearance.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Hire assistants with speed, efficiency, and a pleasant demeanor
- ✅ Test new hires with time constraints to see what they're capable of
- ✅ Value competence over credentials
- ❌ Don't hire complacent staff — they will slow you down
- ❌ Don't underestimate the importance of communication skills

#### Keep In Mind

- A great assistant can make your life almost manageable
- You need people who can handle pressure and volume without breaking

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Hiring based on appearance rather than capability
  **Solution:** Look for brains, work ethic, and speed first; beauty is optional
- **Problem:** Assistants who cannot handle the volume or pace
  **Solution:** Test with real-time pressure during the hiring process

### Lesson 5: Remember — The Buck Starts Here

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Trump Desk

Trump keeps a quote on his desk that reads: "THE BUCK STARTS HERE" — his variation on Harry Truman's "The buck stops here." The strategy is up to you, and so are the results.

##### Example 2: The Dysfunctional Family

Your company will sometimes function as an extended, dysfunctional family. That's natural — people spend more waking hours with coworkers than with their families. Winners see problems as just another way to prove themselves.

#### Step 1: Set the Standard Yourself

Don't expect your employees to work harder than you do. Work seven days a week and love it. Your energy will affect everyone around you.

#### Step 2: View Your Company as a Living Organism

Watch out for bad cells while allowing good cells to flourish. The figures on your spreadsheets reflect the health of that organism.

#### Step 3: Keep Your Door Open

Don't intimidate people — if you do, you'll never get a straight answer. Keep your door open and your people know you're available and approachable.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Set the example by working harder than anyone else
- ✅ Regard your company as a living, breathing organism
- ✅ Keep your door open to encourage honest communication
- ✅ See problems as opportunities to prove yourself
- ❌ Don't intimidate your employees
- ❌ Don't expect others to carry the load if you won't

#### Keep In Mind

- If you can't get excited about what you're doing, how can you expect anyone else to?
- Your organization is your organization — bottom line, it's your ball game

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Being intimidating and shutting down honest feedback
  **Solution:** Keep your door open and practice approachability
- **Problem:** Letting the company drift without your active involvement
  **Solution:** Stay engaged in daily details while keeping the big picture in mind

### Lesson 6: Don't Equivocate — Be Decisive

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Ten-Word Executive

Trump asked an executive for a synopsis of a new development. The executive talked for ten minutes describing pros, cons, good things, bad things — every reason to drop the project and every reason to proceed. Trump finally asked him what he thought in ten words or less. "It stinks," he said. He had eight words left but didn't need them.

#### Step 1: Recognize Equivocation as a Sign of Uncertainty

If you equivocate, it's an indication that you're unsure of yourself and what you're doing. It's also what politicians do all the time — inappropriate, insulting, and condescending.

#### Step 2: Practice Being Blunt and Fast

Trump prides himself on being blunt and fast. Make your position clear without wrapping it in endless hedging.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Give clear, concise answers
- ✅ Cut to the chase — time is valuable
- ✅ Be willing to take a stand even if it's unpopular
- ❌ Don't talk in circles around a decision
- ❌ Don't hide behind endless analysis to avoid commitment

#### Keep In Mind

- Equivocation wastes time and confuses everyone
- Your team needs clear direction, not verbal gymnastics

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Over-analyzing decisions to the point of paralysis
  **Solution:** Set a deadline for decisions and commit to it; gather only the essential information
- **Problem:** Trying to please everyone with your position
  **Solution:** Accept that clear positions will not satisfy everyone — that's normal

### Lesson 7: Ask Yourself Two Questions — Practice Self-Awareness

#### Examples

Trump asks two critical questions before any major decision:

1. Is there anyone else who can do this better than I can?
2. What am I pretending not to see?

The first is about knowing yourself and your competition. The second is about avoiding the "lift of a dream" that can blind you to facts on the ground.

#### Step 1: Know Your Competition and Yourself

If your competition is better, you need to offer some quality they lack. Be honest about your strengths and weaknesses.

#### Step 2: Look Hard at the Facts Before You Dream

Before the dream lifts you into the clouds, make sure you've looked hard at the facts on the ground. Don't let euphoria blind you to real risks.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Regularly assess whether others could do your job better
- ✅ Confront uncomfortable truths you may be avoiding
- ✅ Use self-awareness as a competitive advantage
- ❌ Don't let ego prevent you from seeing your own weaknesses
- ❌ Don't get swept up in creative euphoria without reality checks

#### Keep In Mind

- Self-awareness is the foundation of good decision-making
- The only person you ever fool is yourself

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Overestimating your own abilities relative to the market
  **Solution:** Regularly ask if someone else could do it better; if yes, learn from them or hire them
- **Problem:** Ignoring warning signs because you're excited about a project
  **Solution:** Force yourself to list the risks and downsides before proceeding

### Lesson 8: Bullshit Will Only Get You So Far — Prioritize Performance

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Two-Year String-Along

Trump delayed firing someone for two years because the employee always had a great line of bullshit about a huge deal about to come through. He managed to string Trump along for two solid years, and Trump believed him every time — or wanted to. Finally, he was forced to realize the claims were bogus.

##### Example 2: The Receptionist Who Left After Six Hours

A qualified receptionist worked for a grand total of six hours. She realized the pace wasn't suited to her and politely left. Trump appreciated her quick thinking and efficient decision-making.

#### Step 1: Evaluate People by Their Results, Not Their Stories

Look for employees who take pride in their work and are concerned about the organization as a whole. They see themselves as having a direct relationship to the success or failure of the company.

#### Step 2: Take Control If You Think You Might Be Fired

If you think you're in danger of being fired, take control of the situation. Ask your boss for a meeting to make sure you are communicating and doing your job to everyone's satisfaction.

#### Step 3: Know When to Quit or When to Fire Your Boss

If your boss is a sadist or lousy communicator, fire your boss and get a better job. There's no sense trying to cope with a bad situation that will never improve.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Watch for employees who view the company's bottom line as their own
- ✅ Value people who can think on their feet
- ✅ Appreciate quick, honest decision-making
- ❌ Don't tolerate bullshit artists who produce no results
- ❌ Don't stay in a toxic work environment hoping it will improve

#### Keep In Mind

- Unless your boss is a total sadist, they don't want to fire you
- If you don't want to be somewhere, your boss probably doesn't want you there either

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Keeping underperformers because they are good at storytelling
  **Solution:** Set clear metrics and timelines; if results don't appear, act decisively
- **Problem:** Staying in a bad job too long
  **Solution:** If the situation won't improve, move on — your time is too valuable

### Lesson 9: Every New Hire Is a Gamble — Hire Carefully

#### Examples

Some people give such great interviews that you're ready to make them vice presidents on the spot, until you realize their true talent is simply giving a great interview. Impressive credentials don't always add up to great performance or a good fit.

#### Step 1: Look Beyond Credentials

Nonexistent credentials don't necessarily mean no talent. Be circumspect and keep yourself from being surprised. People can offer an interesting mix of pros and cons.

#### Step 2: Test for Sense of Responsibility

Look for a sense of responsibility that goes beyond what is merely sufficient. Employees who view the company's bottom line as an extension of their own personal savings account are the ones who get the best results.

#### Step 3: Let Time Do the Weeding Out

Time will do the weeding out for you. All you have to do is pay attention. Watch how people handle pressure, deadlines, and unexpected problems.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Hire people who save company money as if it were their own
- ✅ Look for employees who think on their feet
- ✅ Value pride in work above all else
- ❌ Don't hire based on interview performance alone
- ❌ Don't ignore red flags just because credentials are impressive

#### Keep In Mind

- If you can instill a sense of worth in your employees, you will have a tip-top team
- Personal ambition is necessary, but not if it undermines the common goal of the company

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Hiring for credentials over character
  **Solution:** Use behavioral interviews and reference checks to assess how candidates actually perform
- **Problem:** Ignoring cultural fit
  **Solution:** Watch for whether the candidate's working style matches your organization's pace and values

### Lesson 10: Ideas Are Welcome, but Make Sure You Have the Right One

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Jumping Bean

A former employee was like a jumping bean — couldn't keep still for more than three seconds. Being in an enclosed space with him was an ordeal. Trump finally learned to avoid him as much as possible. Too much will cause people to tune you out — or wish you would move to another state.

##### Example 2: Presenting Ideas to the Boss

If you run a company, make yourself accessible to employees. If they feel they can bring ideas to you, they will. If they feel they can't, they won't. But be prepared, succinct, and recognize when not to press an issue.

#### Step 1: Make Yourself Accessible

Allow people to run their ideas by you. You might miss out on a lot of good ideas, and pretty soon you might be missing a lot of employees.

#### Step 2: Be Prepared and Succinct

If you're going to be bold enough to present your idea, make it as clear as possible. Think of it as a presentation that could cost you a lot of money if you were to lose the client.

#### Step 3: Recognize When Not to Press an Issue

The boss has the big picture; you don't. If your idea doesn't meet with hurrahs, it could be that a similar idea is already in development. Don't expect a lengthy explanation of why.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Present ideas clearly and concisely
- ✅ Read the room before pushing an idea
- ✅ Take no for an answer gracefully
- ❌ Don't be a pest — merrily being a pest is detrimental
- ❌ Don't take it personally if your idea isn't the right one for now

#### Keep In Mind

- Your initiative will always be noticed even if the idea is rejected
- If your boss says no, there's likely a good reason

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Pushing an idea after being told no
  **Solution:** Accept the no, look for another opportunity, and consider whether you should go off on your own
- **Problem:** Wasting the boss's time with poorly prepared pitches
  **Solution:** Come with a clear, well-researched presentation that respects their time

### Lesson 11: Focus on the Talent Instead of the Title

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Matthew Calamari

Matthew Calamari, executive vice president of operations, started as a security guard. Trump recognized he had a lot more to offer than his job title warranted. Now he runs building operations and the entire security organization.

##### Example 2: Vinnie Stellio

Vinnie started as Trump's bodyguard and is now a vice president. He would drive executives and architects to look at developments and now they report to him. Trump is one of the largest owners of land in Westchester County, and now Vinnie keeps his eye on it all.

##### Example 3: Meredith McIver

Meredith started as a media assistant. Trump recognized her talents encompassed much more. She made writing this book a pleasure instead of a headache.

#### Step 1: Look for Hidden Potential

Never let someone's job title be the sole indication of their worth. Look around your organization now and then for fresh possibilities.

#### Step 2: Give People a Chance to Unfold

Most people don't like to stagnate. If you want to keep your company moving forward, let people function in a way that will allow them to shine.

#### Step 3: See Beyond the Title

Try to see beyond a person's title. You can find talent in unlikely places. Very often, your resources are greater than you might think.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Promote from within when possible
- ✅ Assess people based on their capabilities, not their current position
- ✅ Be the catalyst for putting success into people's personal vocabulary
- ❌ Don't underestimate anyone
- ❌ Don't confine people to the boundaries of their job descriptions

#### Keep In Mind

- People are multifaceted and it's important to let them function in a way that will allow them to shine
- Most people would rather succeed than fail — sometimes the leader has to be the catalyst

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Overlooking internal talent and hiring externally
  **Solution:** Audit your organization's talent regularly before looking outside
- **Problem:** Keeping people in roles that underutilize their abilities
  **Solution:** Create growth paths that allow top performers to expand their impact

### Lesson 12: Manage the Person, Not the Job

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The "Lazy" Writer

An advertising agency had a writer who appeared to do nothing in his office. His colleagues complained to the boss about his laziness. The boss said: don't disturb him. The last two times he was acting this way, he came up with ideas worth many millions of dollars.

#### Step 1: Learn How Each Key Employee Excels

People have different ways of achieving results. Enjoy figuring out how each of your key employees excels. Being able to do so is what makes a good manager a great one.

#### Step 2: Don't Demoralize Your Employees

You can crush people if you don't weigh your words carefully. Your power as a leader should be used in the most positive way, which sometimes calls for a great deal of restraint as well as patience.

#### Step 3: Use Power Wisely

Abraham Lincoln said: Nearly all men can stand adversity, but if you want to test a man's character, give him power. Be fair even when you are tough.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Adapt your management style to each individual
- ✅ Recognize that different people achieve results in different ways
- ✅ Use restraint and patience when exercising authority
- ❌ Don't crush people with your words or power
- ❌ Don't expect everyone to work the same way

#### Keep In Mind

- Being able to learn something useful about your people is what makes a good manager great
- If people are your resource, you'd better try to learn something useful about them

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Managing by uniform standards that don't fit everyone
  **Solution:** Observe how each person works best and adapt your approach accordingly
- **Problem:** Using fear as the primary motivator
  **Solution:** Use fear sparingly — it works against you if you demoralize people

### Lesson 13: Keep Your Door Open — Stay Curious and Approachable

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Open Door Policy

Everyone who knows Trump knows he keeps the door to his office open. It's symbolic of the way he chooses to think and operate. His father was much the same — he once said: "The more I learn, the more I realize I don't know."

#### Step 1: Learn Something New Every Day

If you end the day without knowing more than you did when you woke up, it makes you wonder: What did I miss out on today? Am I getting lazy?

#### Step 2: Read Widely and Often

Trump reads between five and seven newspapers every morning and receives between ten and twelve magazines a day. He reviews them in his apartment at the end of the day.

#### Step 3: Be Interested in Other Cultures

We live in a big world and it's important to be aware of cultures other than our own. Trump makes an effort to be informed about other cultures, which has helped him sell and rent apartments to people of many foreign nationalities.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Keep your door open physically and metaphorically
- ✅ Read every day to expand your horizons
- ✅ Learn something new whether you think you're interested in it or not
- ❌ Don't be a know-it-all
- ❌ Don't close yourself off from new information

#### Keep In Mind

- A know-it-all is like a closed door
- Every day is a new beginning, not an ending
- Learning begets learning

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Thinking you have arrived and can stop learning
  **Solution:** Treat every success as a new beginning; keep the door open to new ideas
- **Problem:** Being culturally insular in a global market
  **Solution:** Make an effort to understand where other people are coming from — it will pay off in business

### Lesson 14: Think Big and Live Large — Aim Higher Than You Think

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Buying a Skyscraper vs. a Small House

Trump explains that in some ways it's easier to buy a skyscraper than a small house in a bad section of Brooklyn. Either way, you'll probably need financing, and most people would rather invest in a great building than a dilapidated duplex. With the skyscraper, if you hit, at least you hit big.

#### Step 1: Expand Your Thinking

The possibilities are always there. If you're thinking too small, you might miss them. Think about the entire planet as an emerging market.

#### Step 2: Take the Long View on Big Projects

Trump Place took over thirty years to develop. Some things are worth waiting for. Don't rush — build something that endures.

#### Step 3: Live Large

If you're going to think, think big. If you're going to live, live large. Trump's cartoon is real, and he loves living in it.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Think bigger than your current resources suggest
- ✅ Aim for projects that can make a real impact
- ✅ Don't be afraid of big failures — aim high
- ❌ Don't limit yourself to small, safe bets
- ❌ Don't let fear of failure keep you from ambitious goals

#### Keep In Mind

- It's a big world with a lot to discover and accomplish
- The difference between losing $100,000 and losing hundreds of millions is just scale — aim big

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Thinking too small and missing opportunities
  **Solution:** Force yourself to consider the largest possible version of every idea
- **Problem:** Letting small thinking limit your ambition
  **Solution:** Study what the biggest players in your field are doing and aim to match or exceed them

### Lesson 15: Take Control of the Job Interview

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Norma Foerderer's Interview

Trump wasn't sure about Norma after her first interview — she seemed too prim, like she belonged on a family sitcom. Norma persisted, recognizing a good match better than Trump did. She offered to work for one month at a low salary just to see whether they clicked. No strings attached. Trump underestimated her completely. She was persistent and committed, and she got the job with elegance.

#### Step 1: Be Persistent and Committed

If you want the job, show your commitment. Norma didn't take no for an answer. She demonstrated her value before being hired.

#### Step 2: Offer to Prove Yourself

Consider offering a trial period at a lower salary to demonstrate your fit. No strings attached shows confidence in your abilities.

#### Step 3: Don't Let First Impressions Fool You

First impressions can be deceptive. The person who seems like a bad fit may turn out to be your best hire. Give people a chance to show what they can do.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Persist when you believe you are a good fit
- ✅ Offer concrete proof of your capabilities
- ✅ Be willing to start low to prove your value
- ❌ Don't let initial impressions close the door on great candidates
- ❌ Don't be arrogant — demonstrate value through action

#### Keep In Mind

- Deceptive first impressions work both ways — the best hires often look unlikely at first
- Persistence with elegance is a powerful combination

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Giving up after a mediocre first interview
  **Solution:** Follow up with additional evidence of your value; persistence pays off
- **Problem:** Hiring based on first impressions alone
  **Solution:** Give every candidate a chance to prove themselves in a real-work scenario

### Lesson 16: Ask for Your Raise at the Right Time

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Jason Greenblatt's Timing

Jason Greenblatt, a brilliant lawyer, asked Trump for a raise on the worst day possible — a grand-slam rotten day that seemed never-ending. Trump was ready to kill him. Jason almost got fired for stupidity. He got the message: now he waits for sunny days, blue skies, and puffy white clouds before approaching Trump.

#### Step 1: Read the Room Before Asking

Before asking for a raise, look around first. Timing is everything. Don't approach your boss when they're having a terrible day or when the company is facing a crisis.

#### Step 2: Demonstrate Your Value First

Make sure your work speaks for itself. Ask yourself: What do I contribute to the welfare of the organization? Am I instrumental in keeping it humming?

#### Step 3: Choose the Right Moment

Wait for a win, a successful project completion, or a positive moment in the company. Ask when your boss is in a good mood and your recent performance is strong.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Wait for the right time — sunny days, blue skies
- ✅ Let your work results justify the raise request
- ✅ Show discernment and appreciation for what your boss is going through
- ❌ Don't ask for a raise during a crisis or on a bad day
- ❌ Don't assume your boss owes you a raise just because you want one

#### Keep In Mind

- Money, like comedy, is all about timing
- Your boss needs people who are plugged in to what's going on with them

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Asking for a raise at the wrong time and being rejected
  **Solution:** Read the emotional and business climate before making the request
- **Problem:** Assuming hard work automatically equals a raise
  **Solution:** Results matter most — make sure your contributions are visible and documented

### Lesson 17: Be Tenacious — Persistence Wins

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Trump Place (West Side Yards)

Trump bought the West Side yards property in 1974. Seventeen years later it was still a work in progress. Trump Place is a $5-billion project, the biggest development ever approved by the New York City Planning Commission. It will have 5,700 residential units and more than five million square feet of commercial space. At times, just about every executive will appear impatient, but to build something that endures, you have to take the long view.

#### Step 1: Set Your Sights on the Long Term

Some things are worth waiting for. Build a firm foundation. Each building could be a hundred stories high, considering the meticulous foundation work.

#### Step 2: Persist Through Setbacks

You don't create a classic overnight. There will be snags and changes along the way. Persistence is what separates the successful from the quitters.

#### Step 3: Take the Long View

At times, executives will appear impatient. To build something that endures, you have to take the long view and not rush.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Set long-term goals and stick with them
- ✅ Build meticulous foundations
- ✅ Persist even when others are impatient
- ❌ Don't give up when setbacks occur
- ❌ Don't rush critical foundation work

#### Keep In Mind

- Trump Place is a good example of why tenacity is crucial in business
- A firm foundation supports everything built on top of it

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Giving up on long-term projects when progress is slow
  **Solution:** Break the project into smaller milestones and celebrate each one
- **Problem:** Letting impatience drive bad decisions

### Lesson 26: Negotiate Like a Pro

#### Examples

##### Example 1: 40 Wall Street

Trump got 40 Wall Street for $1 million. The Kinson group from Hong Kong had bought it and poured tens of millions into it but were getting nowhere. They wanted out fast. Trump offered them $1 million plus assuming and negotiating their liens, subject to a restructured ground lease. They accepted without question because they wanted out — and fast. He also flew to Germany to meet the Hinneberg family directly, learning they wanted peace of mind, not more money. He agreed to forgo all rent during renovation in exchange for a revised lease.

##### Example 2: The Hilton Property in Atlantic City

Trump acquired the Hilton property (now Trump Marina) for $320 million. Steve Wynn launched a hostile takeover bid, making it a competition of heavy hitters. In a good negotiation, all sides win. Steve Wynn is thriving today — the battle enlarged his reputation and probably helped him move on to even bigger deals.

#### Step 1: Consider What the Other Side Wants

Know the party on the other side of the table before sitting down with them. Research who you're dealing with, how they negotiate, and what they want from you.

#### Step 2: Be Reasonable and Flexible

A good negotiator must be flexible. The broker at 40 Wall Street demanded a $60,000-per-month retainer. Trump refused and hired another broker who accepted the usual terms. The original broker's inflexibility cost him a small fortune.

#### Step 3: Know Exactly What You Want and Keep It to Yourself

Before you begin any negotiation, write down your objectives. Then try to anticipate what the other side might want. Find a way of talking about the deal that will keep either of you from getting locked into an impossible position.

#### Step 4: Make Sure Both Sides Come Out Winning

In a good negotiation, all sides win. Let everyone come out a winner. If the other party wants to acquire something you own, let them convince you that you really don't want it or need it. In doing so, they'll convince you of just how badly they want it.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Research the other party thoroughly before negotiating
- ✅ Be flexible — rigid positions kill deals
- ✅ Let the other side feel like they won
- ✅ Use silence and aloofness strategically to gain leverage
- ❌ Don't reveal your bottom line prematurely
- ❌ Don't burn bridges — you may need to deal with the same people again

#### Keep In Mind

- He who has the gold makes the rules
- Power is not just about calling all the shots — it's about ability
- The boardroom is not the pub down on the corner

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Revealing your hand too early in negotiations
  **Solution:** Keep your bottom line to yourself until a strategically necessary moment
- **Problem:** Being inflexible and losing deals
  **Solution:** Find creative solutions that satisfy both sides — rigidity is expensive
