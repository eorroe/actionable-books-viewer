## Introduction: Strategy Before Tactics—Always (pages 87–88)

### Actionable Lesson: Distinguish Strategy from Tactics in Every Endeavor
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Before any action, ask whether you are defining direction (strategy) or executing means (tactics).
2. Treat strategy as the "rudder" that sets course; treat tactics as the "power" that propels execution.
3. Ensure strategy is defined first; tactics should always support and never precede strategy.

**Examples from the Text:**
- A boat race: strategy is the rudder determining direction; tactics is the power propelling you to the goal.
- If your strategy is to find new employment by personally contacting people, your tactic is the process of making actual contacts.
- When a company president determines the course of action, that activity is strategic; when the president talks to a customer, that action is tactical.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** create strategy before tactics.
- **DO** allocate resources strategically before deploying them tactically.
- **DON'T** separate planning from implementation, as this separates thinking from doing.
- **DON'T** let responsibility become diffused by disconnecting strategy from tactics.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Treating tactical action as a substitute for strategic planning.
- "Steering by the wake"—looking only at the past and adjusting reactively without a forward plan.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific to this section.

## Know Your Battleground (pages 89–93)

### Actionable Lesson 1: Master Your Subject Through Deep Study and Teaching
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Learn your subject well enough to teach it to others.
2. Use the "five why's" technique to get to the root cause of problems.
3. Break your subject into smaller pieces to understand each component and how they meld into the whole.
4. Study information close to the time of use to increase retention.

**Examples from the Text:**
- The "five why's" quality management example:
  - Q1. Why was the delivery late? A1. The truck broke down.
  - Q2. Why did the truck break down? A2. It had not been lubricated.
  - Q3. Why wasn't it lubricated? A3. The driver had not been trained.
  - Q4. Why hadn't the driver been trained? A4. The training was canceled.
  - Q5. Why was the training canceled? A5. The budget had been cut.
  - Root cause identified: failure of funds to provide training.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** ask deeper questions and anticipate questions that might be asked.
- **DO** segment complex subjects into manageable units ("eating the elephant one bite at a time").
- **DO** schedule training close to the time of use.
- **DON'T** stop at surface-level answers; keep asking "why" until you reach the heart of an issue.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Thinking you know a subject until you try to teach it and discover gaps.
- Failing to drill down past symptomatic causes to root causes.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 2: Expand Your Knowledge Horizons Beyond Your Immediate Field
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Develop a networking mindset with bright people who can provide important information.
2. Take advantage of learning opportunities: attend trade shows, industry meetings, and volunteer organization retreats.
3. Spend time with people who have different interests and expertise.
4. Take courses in other fields; join organizations with other interests; visit trade shows in different industries.
5. Seek new experiences to avoid getting stuck in a rut.
6. Learn new questions through observation and hands-on experience.

**Examples from the Text:**
- A consultant visited four of the five largest grocery retailers during a consulting assignment to gain on-site knowledge, enabling him to know the retail store component better than anyone else.
- The client reported "outstanding success—'kicked butt' was his exact phrase."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** be prepared—"Be Prepared" (Boy Scouts motto).
- **DO** steal and apply new ideas from other fields.
- **DO** gain hands-on, on-site knowledge when possible.
- **DON'T** limit yourself to a narrow focus that prevents seeing new and creative solutions.
- **DON'T** claim you are "too busy chopping wood to stop and sharpen your ax."

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Spending all time with people who know the same things you do, leading to confirmation bias.
- Letting schooling interfere with education (Mark Twain's observation).

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

## Build a Personal Network (pages 94–97)

### Actionable Lesson 1: Set Contact Goals and Actively Expand Your Network
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Set specific, stretch goals for networking (e.g., make a list of the first twenty people who might help you).
2. Include names and the person's relationship or role.
3. Push yourself to reach the goal even if you feel uncomfortable.
4. Remember: quality of contacts is important, but high-quality networks almost always have high quantity as well.
5. Keep in touch with contacts as they advance in their careers.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Transition Team outplacement advice: "Make a list of the first twenty people you can think of who might be able to help you with your search. Push yourself to reach the goal of twenty names!"
- Meeting a Finnish manager at a Helsinki conference who was searching for a town to build his factory; common hometown created a bond.
- Meeting a stranger at a luncheon who had been at the same New Year's Eve party.
- Meeting a passenger on an airplane who had lived in the same neighborhood in a distant city.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** set specific, measurable contact goals.
- **DO** reach out to people even if you haven't spoken to them in a long time.
- **DO** push yourself beyond your comfortable circle of friends.
- **DON'T** be overly frugal about whom you call or the time you spend developing relationships.
- **DON'T** worry about comfort level when making the initial list.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Believing you have a high-quality network without also having a high-quantity network.
- Failing to keep in touch with contacts as they advance.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 2: Solidify Every New Contact with Personalized Follow-Up
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. When meeting someone new, find out their interests.
2. Promise to send something they might like to have (e.g., keep a file of interesting articles).
3. Get their business card.
4. Write down the promised action along with the date and place you met.
5. Keep your promise to send something.
6. Include a handwritten note for a personal touch.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Keeping a file of interesting articles to send to new contacts.
- Writing down promised actions with dates and places met.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** send handwritten notes to create personal relationships.
- **DO** keep promises to send materials.
- **DON'T** rely solely on digital communication for initial relationship building.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Forgetting why you called someone if you leave a message and they call back.
- Failing to follow through on promised actions.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 3: Use the Telephone Strategically for Networking
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. When placing a call, ask if the person has a moment before launching into conversation.
2. When contacting someone you do not know well (or at all), do not leave a message.
3. Use this approach to maintain control of when the call is initiated and avoid embarrassment of not remembering why you called.

**Examples from the Text:**
- The author's traveling companion observed: "I've never been able to walk past a phone in an airport without using it."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** ask if the person has a moment before talking.
- **DO** maintain control of call initiation timing.
- **DON'T** leave messages when contacting someone you don't know well—this abrogates your right to call back.
- **DON'T** risk being cut short if the person is in the middle of a conference.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Leaving a message and then not knowing when or if to call again.
- Receiving a return call and not remembering why you called.
- Being mentally unprepared when a call is returned.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 4: Use the Internet Wisely for Networking
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Keep messages brief and informative.

**Examples from the Text:**
- General reference to internet as a great networking tool when used wisely.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** keep messages brief and informative.
- **DON'T** overcommunicate or send long, rambling messages.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Being overly verbose or unclear in digital communications.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 5: Actively Work Your Network
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. When planning to visit a city, call people in your network and ask to meet them.
2. Your contacts will be flattered by your interest.
3. Whether or not you meet, the offer breeds a stronger relationship.
4. Cultivate multiple friendships and acquaintances; some contacts will breed other contacts.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Having three breakfasts with three different groups of people in some cities.
- A friend facetiously commented that the way to "do New York" is to plan three lunches on the same day.
- Statisticians say only four contact links separate you from anyone in the world—if you know the name of the person you want to reach.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** actively work your network continuously.
- **DO** be proactive in reaching out when traveling.
- **DON'T** let your network go stale.
- **DON'T** assume you don't need a broad network if you know who you're looking for.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Believing networking is a one-time effort rather than continuous investment.
- Failing to recognize that the broadest base yields the best responses when you don't know precisely who can help.

## Develop Great Strategies (pages 98–100)

### Actionable Lesson 1: Develop a Vision of Your Future Success
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Identify your core competencies: What do others say you do best? What do you like to do?
2. Ask trusted advisors to comment on your identification of core competencies.
3. Determine applications: Think about how you can be most productive using these core competencies.
4. Establish a direction: Develop a clear mind-picture of where you want to go and what you want to be.
5. Explore factors critical to achieving your vision.

**Examples from the Text:**
- A consulting organization head stated: "Visions are the vehicles that transport us across the boundaries of current reality to the boundless hopes of a future seemingly beyond our grasp. What once we deemed impossible becomes not only possible but probable when we live out our vision through actions."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** solicit feedback from trusted advisors on your core competencies.
- **DO** think deeply about where you can best apply your talents and skills.
- **DON'T** skip the "sweat equity" of careful consideration—vision is not a flash of insight.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Confusing a vision statement with actual vision (visions are discoveries, not just words).
- Failing to explore critical factors before setting direction.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 2: Express Your Vision as a Vivid Image
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Write a vivid image of your vision in a single session.
2. Start with a sentence like "Five years from now I will..."
3. Let thoughts flow out of your head onto paper without stopping.
4. Continue until you have covered several sheets of paper.
5. Set your comments aside for a day or two.
6. Simplify this vivid image into a short paragraph or two.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Transform your mission into word pictures by writing continuously without editing during the initial session.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** write continuously without stopping during the initial session.
- **DO** set the draft aside before simplifying.
- **DO** focus on the mental exercise, not just the words on paper.
- **DON'T** edit while writing the first draft.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Over-editing during the initial writing session.
- Skipping the mental exercise by treating it as purely a writing task.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 3: Develop Personal Strategic Initiatives
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Think about existing opportunities.
2. Review threats and blockers to your vision.
3. Consider optional courses of action.
4. Write out your strategic initiatives using statements like:
   - Career: I will get a degree at night school that makes me eligible for promotion.
   - Family: I will send our children to college.
   - Renewal: I will go on an educational trip overseas for a week.
   - Health: I will train and attempt to run a marathon.
5. Prioritize the most important three to five initiatives.
6. Do not select more than this number or you will be attempting too much.
7. Develop a plan of action for each initiative.
8. Be diligent in follow-through.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Career, family, renewal, and health initiative examples provided in the text.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** limit to three to five initiatives to avoid overextension.
- **DO** develop specific plans of action for each initiative.
- **DO** be diligent in follow-through.
- **DON'T** select too many initiatives.
- **DON'T** have initiatives without corresponding action plans.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Overloading with too many strategic initiatives.
- Failing to follow through on initiatives.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

## Win Without Fighting (pages 101–104)

### Actionable Lesson 1: Innovate to Find Victory Without Competition
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Define the challenge in words or pictures.
2. Develop "How about..." statements that lead to the radical and unusual.
3. Go beyond the boundaries of the normal.
4. Visualize action for several intriguing ideas.
5. Focus on the action, not the results.
6. Diagram input from these ideas and look for a new way to fulfill the challenge.
7. Innovate by finding a new way to fulfill the challenge.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Dick Fosbury innovating the Fosbury Flop in the high jump.
- Dell selling computers direct to customers, ignoring traditional distribution channels.
- Bob Sutherland creating the Cherry Republic in Leelanau, Michigan, themed around a fictitious cherry nation.
- A young man in Australia wedging a running shoe in an advertising agency owner's office door with his resume to get an interview and job.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** think radically and go beyond normal boundaries.
- **DO** visualize putting ideas into action.
- **DO** focus on the action itself during visualization.
- **DON'T** limit yourself to conventional thinking.
- **DON'T** confuse thinking up new ideas with implementing them—the test for success is implementation.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Thinking innovation is only about generating ideas rather than implementing them.
- Staying within the boundaries of the normal.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 2: Develop Strategy So Strong That You Win Without Fighting
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Develop a great strategy prior to engaging the opponent.
2. Be so fundamentally sound and in such good condition that you can outlast and outwork opponents.
3. Make your performance itself intimidating.
4. Avoid battle by intimidating opponents into expending their energy elsewhere.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Switzerland's "Porcupine Strategy": Switzerland doesn't have an army; Switzerland is an army. The Swiss army is composed of many hundreds of thousands of civilians who train regularly and keep weapons in their home.
- Phillip Fulmer, national-championship-winning football coach: "I constantly coach our players about having the intensity to be able to intimidate our opponents. This doesn't mean to play outside the rules. It means that our players should be fundamentally sound and in such good physical condition that they can outlast and outwork their opponents."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** develop strategy before tactics.
- **DO** focus on being so good that your performance is intimidating.
- **DO** avoid playing outside the rules while still being intimidating.
- **DON'T** rely on tactical brilliance alone if the strategy is weak.
- **DON'T** engage in battle if you can win without fighting.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Thinking tactical success can overcome an inadequate strategic posture.
- Engaging in fierce fighting when a strategic victory was possible without conflict.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

## Apply Strength Against Weakness (pages 105–108)

### Actionable Lesson 1: Concentrate Strength at the Decisive Point
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Identify where you want to win (the decisive point).
2. Concentrate your strategy and tactics at that selected place.
3. Use many to strike the few at the selected place.
4. Where absolute superiority is not obtainable, produce relative superiority at the decisive point by making skillful use of what you have.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Peter the Great at the Russian town of Dorpat in 1704: His commander was firing at the town's strongest bastions. Peter switched the artillery to the most vulnerable walls and a breach was made for Russian troops to enter. Victory came five weeks after the siege began but only ten days after Peter arrived to focus the strength of his cannons against the weakest walls.
- Carl von Clausewitz: "Where absolute superiority is not obtainable, you must produce a relative one at the decisive point by making skillful use of what you have."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** concentrate strength at the decisive point.
- **DO** look for the weakness of your opponent's strength.
- **DO** make skillful use of what you have.
- **DON'T** waste strength on strong points where it is less effective.
- **DON'T** assume you need absolute superiority—relative superiority at the decisive point is sufficient.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Mistaking mass of numbers for concentration of strength.
- Failing to identify the decisive point.
- Spreading resources too thin across multiple fronts.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 2: Find a Niche Where You Can Apply Strengths Against Weaknesses
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Select a niche where you can apply your strengths.
2. Look for areas where there is limited competition or none at all.
3. Concentrate your quest in a narrow area.
4. Build expertise in that niche.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Dan Poynter earned his reputation as an expert in sky diving by jumping from an airplane over the North Pole and then writing a book on the subject.
- Jim King parlayed his corporate specialty in analyzing environmental risk into a position of expertise by writing the 1,290-page Environmental Dictionary.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** select a niche where competition is limited or nonexistent.
- **DO** concentrate your efforts in a narrow area.
- **DON'T** try to be everything to everyone.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Trying to compete in broad, crowded markets where you cannot achieve relative superiority.
- Failing to recognize niche opportunities.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 3: Obtain Relative Superiority Through Organization or Technology
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. For organizational superiority: marshal your personal resources ("get your ducks in a row").
2. For technological superiority: keep up with the rapid pace of change in technology.
3. Apply the latest technological knowledge in your chosen areas.
4. Stay on the leading edge to gain distinct advantages.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Greeks fighting in a phalanx won the Battle of Marathon against the Persians who were fighting one to one.
- Sports teams and business franchises succeed through organizational superiority.
- Technology helps you look like a leader and actually be a leader.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** get your ducks in a row.
- **DO** keep up with technological changes.
- **DO** apply leading-edge technology in your field.
- **DON'T** fall behind the rapid pace of technological change.
- **DON'T** neglect organizational strengths in favor of technology alone.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Falling behind technology and losing leadership position.
- Having technology but failing to organize effectively.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 4: Reinforce Strength—Not Weakness
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Spend time, money, and energy reinforcing what is working.
2. Leverage off what you do well.
3. Do NOT spend resources shoring up what's not working.
4. Ensure all resources are focused on predetermined strategic initiatives.

**Examples from the Text:**
- "Guns that have not fired, have not attacked, no matter how long they have been in position."
- "In the world of success, coaches coach and players play. When coaches try to play and players try to coach, the results can be disastrous."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** focus resources on areas of strength.
- **DO** leverage off what you do well.
- **DO** ensure all resources are focused on predetermined strategic initiatives.
- **DON'T** spend time and resources shoring up weaknesses.
- **DON'T** have managers involved in day-to-day operations, giving support to the weak instead of attacking.
- **DON'T** have coaches try to play or players try to coach.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Managers getting so involved in giving support to weak areas that they end up defending instead of attacking.
- Reinforcing weakness instead of strength.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

## Avoid the Avoidable (pages 109–110)

### Actionable Lesson 1: Eliminate Predictable Physical Hazards First
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Identify physical hazards that are identifiable obstacles.
2. Take specific measures to reduce the possibility of defeat by physical conditions.
3. Devote full resources to dealing with unpredictable human elements only after physical risks are mitigated.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Schedule regular training that increases professional skills or prepares for an alternate career.
- Always arrive early for appointments.
- Ensure documents have ultimate quality in physical form (spelling, language usage) so they do not distract from substance.
- Never allow a plan to fail because of quality of product/service, delivery time, or any physical element.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** correct predictable components first.
- **DO** take specific measures against physical hazards.
- **DO** pay attention to form (spelling, grammar, formatting) so it doesn't destroy substance.
- **DON'T** allow physical failures to derail your plans.
- **DON'T** neglect form in favor of substance alone.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Allowing sloppy form to destroy the content of substance.
- Failing to address predictable physical risks.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

4. Don't drink and drive.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Economy class syndrome prevention.
- Waiting to send letters for a second look.
- Carrying two credit cards in different places.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** follow simple, commonsense rules.
- **DO** allow time for second looks before sending important communications.
- **DO** have redundancy for critical items (credit cards).
- **DON'T** drink and drive.
- **DON'T** send letters or emails immediately without review.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Sending communications on the day they are written without review.
- Carrying only one credit card.
- Ignoring simple health and safety precautions.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- **SAFETY:** Don't drink and drive.
- **SAFETY:** Take aspirin before long flights and walk around to prevent blood clots (economy class syndrome).

1. Recognize that relationships are the foundation of position strength.
2. Fulfill your roles in each family (home, religious, career, other interests).

- Other interests: A parent coaching a child's sport intertwines with other positions.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** devote time and energy to your roles in each family.
- **DO** practice give-and-take in relationships.
- **DO** excel in your roles to build invincible positions.

- **DON'T** engage in false positioning—actions must be "for real" to create permanent change.

- Attempting false positioning, which is destructive.
- Neglecting relationship positions.
- Confusing perception with reality—perception becomes reality only when actions are genuine.

**Security/Safety Notes:**

- **DON'T** stop learning—the quest never ends.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Pursuing areas that don't reinforce current strengths (becoming hobbies instead of expertise).
- Failing to actively announce and build your own reputation.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

## Organize a Team (pages 116–118)

### Actionable Lesson 1: Form Teams to Achieve Better Results
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Recognize that individuals working alone cannot achieve the same level of success as team members.
2. Understand that you cannot compete as an individual against others who are functioning as a team.
3. Build teams with people who know and trust each other.
4. Invest time in learning team skills and working together.
5. Share experiences working toward a common goal.

**Examples from the Text:**
- The "f" exercise: Individuals counting the letter "f" in a paragraph gave answers ranging from low-twenties to mid-thirties. When working in teams of three or four, every team got the right answer (39) by dividing the work.
- "Four brave men who do not know each other will not dare attack a lion. Four less brave, knowing each other well, sure of their reliability and consequently of mutual aid, will attack resolutely."—Ardant du Picq.
- Bo Schembechler: "You will never get the same effort from one man seeking glory as from a group of men pulling for a shared goal."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** form teams for complex problems.
- **DO** invest time in building trust and mutual aid.
- **DO** share experiences working toward a common goal.
- **DON'T** attempt to solve complex problems alone when teams can do better.
- **DON'T** assume teams are inefficient—defeat is more inefficient.
- **DON'T** believe successful teams are made up of people who have only been lectured about teamwork.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Believing teamwork is inefficient.
- Forming teams without building trust and shared experiences.
- Trying to compete individually against teams.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 2: Share a Common Purpose
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**

2. Help team members commit with their hearts, not just their heads.
3. Build team consistency through a common purpose.
4. Integrate "who we are" into the personal style of the team.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Sun Tzu advises: "he whose ranks are united in purpose will be victorious."
- Teams of motivated, mature people accomplish amazing things.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** establish a common, unifying purpose.
- **DO** energize team members to commit with their hearts.
- **DO** build team consistency.
- **DON'T** have a purpose that is only intellectual, not emotional.
- **DON'T** neglect the integration of "who we are" into team style.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Having a purpose that doesn't energize or excite.
- Failing to get heart-level commitment.

## Go for a Breakthrough (pages 119–122)

### Actionable Lesson 1: Strive for Big Wins, Not Just Small Goals
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Focus your strategy on meaningful gains.
2. Plan a strategy that achieves a big goal wherever you are going.
3. Merge big responsibilities with big goals to create energizing reasons for effort.
4. Remember: only great battles produce great results, and only the prospect of great results can produce the means to fight great battles.

**Examples from the Text:**
- General MacArthur advised Olympic athletes: "We are here to represent the greatest country on earth. We did not come here to lose and lose gracefully, we came to win and win decisively."
- Carl von Clausewitz: "Only great battles produce great results."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** think big and focus on meaningful gains.
- **DO** use big goals as energizing reasons for effort.
- **DO** make small goals steps to larger goals.
- **DON'T** focus only on small goals that don't lead to major results.
- **DON'T** settle for losing gracefully—plan to win decisively.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Losing interest in small goals that don't lead to major results.
- Believing small victories are less time-consuming than big victories (they are often not).

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 2: Plan Breakthroughs by Disrupting and Intruding
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Recognize that "just a little more" effort doesn't work—it creates stalemate.
2. Don't renew an attack along the same line (or in the same form) after it has once failed.
3. Trying harder is not the solution; trying smarter is.
4. Dislocate the enemy (or disrupt the mind) and exploit that dislocation.
5. Gain mindshare by disrupting the mind and then intruding into that disruption.
6. Be pleasantly disruptive to capture attention.
7. Be intrusive with real benefits.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Giving away six-foot-high stuffed Pink Panthers as premiums with product purchases to create a disruptive, attention-grabbing promotion.
- Using a messenger to deliver six-foot Pink Panthers with a pink tag reading, "You'll be tickled pink with the opportunity I have for you."
- Sending brochures again in a box with a chocolate chip cookie the size of a pizza, with the message "Here's the cookie with the message" written in frosting.
- Sending twenty-five different boxes of chocolate-chip cookies to business writers along with a book copy.
- Sending every salesperson a case of Kellogg's cereal to pass out to retailers when the product was featured on a cereal package.
- A young man in Australia wedging a running shoe in an office door with his resume to get an interview.
- Stacking a display of one million dollars in one-dollar bills at a trade show to illustrate profit opportunity, resulting in tripled sales volume.

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** be pleasantly disruptive to capture attention.
- **DO** be intrusive with real benefits.
- **DO** plan for breakthroughs rather than hoping they happen.
- **DO** disrupt the mind to gain mindshare.
- **DON'T** try harder along the same failed line—try smarter.
- **DON'T** rely on incremental improvements alone for big wins.
- **DON'T** be crass or obnoxious in your disruption.
- **DON'T** let criticism of big efforts stop you—proof that great efforts produce big wins overpowers criticism.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Trying harder with the same approach after failure.
- Believing incremental improvements alone will produce breakthroughs.
- Being disruptive in a crass or obnoxious way.
- Worrying about cost when breakthrough results are overwhelming.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

## Have a Plan (pages 123–126)

### Actionable Lesson 1: Put Your Plan in Writing
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Answer Kipling's "six honest serving men": What, Where, When, Why, How, and Who.
   - Why am I taking this course of action?
   - What am I going to achieve?
   - How am I going to proceed?
   - When am I going to do it?
   - Where will it happen?
   - Who is going to be involved?
2. Write these answers down in a single place (legal pad, bound journal, or notebook planner).
3. Set long-range goals, annual goals, monthly, weekly, and daily goals.
4. Use daily goals as to-do lists and long-range goals as dream sheets.
5. Focus on the thinking that goes into the writing, not just the words on paper.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Tom Monaghan, founder of Domino's Pizza: "Writing is the key to my system. I carry a legal pad with me everywhere I go. All my thoughts, my plans, my dreams, and my analyses of problems are written down in my pad. I sometimes have several pads going at once for different kinds of thoughts. I have accumulated dozens of these pads—although I never look at them again. What's important is the thinking that goes into the writing, not the words that wind up on paper."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** put your plan in writing—a plan only exists as a written document.
- **DO** answer all six questions: What, Where, When, Why, How, Who.
- **DO** use a single place to record everything.
- **DO** set goals at multiple time horizons (long-range, annual, monthly, weekly, daily).
- **DON'T** keep plans only in your head.
- **DON'T** think that the words on paper are more important than the thinking that went into writing them.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Having a dream or vision but not a plan (a plan must be written).
- Failing to answer all six questions.
- Not using a single, organized place for plans.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 2: Make Your Plan Flexible and Adaptable
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Design your plan with branches, like a tree, so it can bear fruit.
2. Include options in your plan—a plan without options is like a barren pole.
3. Recognize that tactics change in an infinite variety of ways to suit changes in circumstances.
4. Don't let the possibility of disruption deter you from planning—simply consider plans as a basis for change.
5. Ensure your planning is done in touch with the situation, not in a vacuum.
6. Keep your plan simple—less is more.

**Examples from the Text:**
- "A plan like a tree must have branches if it is to bear fruit. A plan without options is like a barren pole."
- Moses brought down just ten commandments. The U.S. Army sets forth only nine principles of war. At Gettysburg, Abraham Lincoln's speech lasted less than ten minutes.
- Sun Tzu: "Tactics change in an infinite variety of ways to suit changes in the circumstances."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** make your plan flexible and adaptable.
- **DO** include options and alternatives.
- **DO** keep plans simple.
- **DO** plan in touch with the actual situation.
- **DON'T** make plans too rigid.
- **DON'T** let the possibility of disruption deter you from planning.
- **DON'T** plan in a vacuum.
- **DON'T** make your plan complicated.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Having a plan without options.
- Planning in a vacuum without knowledge of the situation.
- Making plans too rigid to adapt.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 3: Implement Your Plan with Three Simple Rules
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Set a goal and timetable. Although you cannot be number one in everything, you can be number one in something.
2. Get started. The greater your initiative, the greater your momentum, and the faster your journey to success.
3. Leverage every gain. Use each success to attain the next level.
4. Develop a contingency plan to provide for the next step following a success or failure.
5. Examine the worst that can happen and provide for such a contingency.
6. Don't rely on crystal-ball assumptions.

**Examples from the Text:**
- Frederick the Great: "There is no dishonor in a hard fought defeat and no excuse in being surprised."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** set specific goals with timetables.
- **DO** get started to build momentum.
- **DO** leverage every gain to attain the next level.
- **DO** develop contingency plans for both success and failure.
- **DO** examine worst-case scenarios.
- **DON'T** rely on assumptions about the future.
- **DON'T** fail to get started.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Failing to set a goal and timetable.
- Delaying start, which reduces momentum.
- Not having contingency plans.
- Being surprised because worst-case scenarios weren't planned for.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

### Actionable Lesson 4: Act on Your Plan
**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Recognize that planning without acting is a waste of time.
2. Recognize that acting without planning is a recipe for defeat.
3. Think of every plan as a plan of action.

**Examples from the Text:**
- General principle: "Planning without acting is a waste of time. Acting without planning is a recipe for defeat."

**Best Practices (Do's and Don'ts):**
- **DO** think of every plan as a plan of action.
- **DO** act on your plans.
- **DON'T** plan without acting.
- **DON'T** act without planning.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Getting stuck in planning without execution.
- Acting impulsively without a plan.

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- None specific.

## Summary of All Actionable Lessons

1. **Strategy Before Tactics:** Define direction (strategy) before executing means (tactics).
2. **Master Your Subject:** Learn to teach, use the five why's, break subjects into pieces, study close to time of use.
3. **Expand Knowledge Horizons:** Network with bright people, attend learning opportunities, spend time with different interests, seek new experiences, learn new questions.
4. **Set Contact Goals:** Make lists of potential contacts, push beyond comfort zones, maintain high quantity and quality.
5. **Solidify Contacts:** Find interests, promise to send something, get business cards, write down promises, follow up with handwritten notes.
6. **Use the Phone Strategically:** Ask if they have a moment, don't leave messages with unknown contacts, maintain control of call timing.
7. **Use the Internet Wisely:** Keep messages brief and informative.
8. **Actively Work Your Network:** Call people when visiting cities, be proactive, cultivate continuously.
9. **Develop a Vision:** Identify core competencies, determine applications, establish direction.
10. **Express Vision as Vivid Image:** Write continuously, set aside, simplify.
11. **Develop Strategic Initiatives:** Prioritize 3-5 initiatives, write specific statements, develop action plans, be diligent in follow-through.
12. **Innovate to Win Without Fighting:** Define challenges, develop "How about..." statements, visualize action, diagram inputs, implement.
13. **Develop Intimidating Strategy:** Be so good you avoid battle, be fundamentally sound, outperform opponents.
14. **Concentrate Strength at Decisive Point:** Use many to strike few, produce relative superiority, focus at selected place.
15. **Find a Niche:** Select narrow fields with limited competition, apply strengths against weaknesses.
16. **Obtain Relative Superiority:** Marshal personal resources, keep up with technology, apply leading-edge knowledge.
17. **Reinforce Strength:** Focus resources on what works, leverage strengths, don't shore up weaknesses.
18. **Avoid Physical Hazards:** Identify and mitigate predictable physical risks first.
19. **Follow Simple Rules:** Take aspirin before flights, don't send letters same day, carry two credit cards, don't drink and drive.
20. **Build Relationship Positions:** Fulfill roles in home, religious, career, and other-interest families.
21. **Define Personal Brand:** Use the "descriptive middle name" technique, define narrowly, be genuine.
22. **Build Expertise:** Select narrow niche, reinforce current strengths, explore, build reputation, quest never ends.
23. **Form Alliances:** Get coaches, agents, advisors; communicate about your position.
24. **Form Teams:** Recognize teams outperform individuals, build trust, share experiences toward common goals.
25. **Share Common Purpose:** Energize team members, commit hearts and heads, build consistency.
26. **Strive for Big Wins:** Focus on meaningful gains, merge responsibility with big goals.
27. **Plan Breakthroughs:** Don't try harder along failed lines, try smarter, disrupt minds, be pleasantly disruptive, be intrusive with real benefits.
28. **Use Extraordinary Effort:** Find creative, inexhaustible ways to do the extraordinary.
29. **Put Plans in Writing:** Answer six honest serving men (What, Where, When, Why, How, Who), use a single place for all plans.
30. **Make Plans Flexible:** Include options, adapt to circumstances, keep it simple.
31. **Implement with Three Rules:** Set goal and timetable, get started, leverage every gain, develop contingency plans.
32. **Act on Plans:** Every plan is a plan of action; planning without acting is waste, acting without planning is defeat.
