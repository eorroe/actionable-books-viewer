## 1. Inconsistent/Vague Reference to "the enemy"
- The term "the enemy" is used throughout the document to mean: competitor, adversary, opponent, market force, or rival. There is no consistent definition, and in business contexts the referent shifts between paragraphs without clarification.
- Examples: lines 149, 151, 193, 245, 503, 505, 535, 537, 541, 596, 638, 754, 794, 807, 838, 841, 842, 907, 967, 1012, 1141, 1179, 1196, 1200, 1202, 1238, 1266, 1268, 1280, 1308, 1367, 1377, 1379, 1417, 1515, 1567, 1637, 1651, 1661, 1689, 1691, 1703, 1731, 1885, 1893, 1901, 1913, 1955, 1995.
- **Impact**: Readers cannot determine whether "enemy" refers to a specific competitor, a category of competitors, an internal obstacle, or an abstract market force.

## 2. "One zhong of grains obtained from the local area is equal to twenty zhong shipped from the home country" (lines 235, 273)
- The term "zhong" (a Chinese unit of volume/weight) is used without definition or conversion to modern business metrics. The ratio is repeated as a literal fact but is never explained or contextualized for contemporary readers.
- **Impact**: The statement reads as either a vague metaphor or an unexplained numerical claim. It is unclear what the ratio is meant to represent (cost? time? efficiency?) and whether it should be applied literally.
