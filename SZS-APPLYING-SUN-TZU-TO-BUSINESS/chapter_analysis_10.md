## Lesson 1: Occupy Strong Natural Positions

### Core Principle
Choose camps and marching positions based on terrain type. The right position provides defensive advantage, logistical security, and tactical superiority.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

#### Mountains
1. Stay in the neighborhood of valleys when crossing mountains.
2. When encamping, select high ground facing the sunny side.
3. If the enemy already occupies the high ground, do not ascend to attack.

#### Rivers
1. After crossing a river, move far away from it — do not camp on the near bank.
2. If an advancing invader is crossing a river, do not engage him in midstream.
3. It is advantageous to let half his force cross before striking.
4. Do not go to meet an invader near a river he must cross.
5. When encamping near a river, take high ground facing the sun.
6. Do not take a position at the lower reaches relative to the enemy.

#### Salt Marshes
1. Cross salt marshes as quickly as possible — speed is the only concern.
2. If forced to engage the enemy in a salt marsh, position close to grass and water with trees to your rear.

#### Level Ground
1. Take up an accessible position.
2. Deploy main flanks on high ground.
3. Keep the front lower than the back.

### Examples from the Text
- *"By employing them, the Yellow Emperor conquered his four neighboring sovereigns."* — The principles of terrain positioning have historically decisive results.
- *"When encamping in the riverine area, take a position on high ground facing the sun. Do not take a position at the lower reaches of the enemy."*

### Best Practices (Do's)
- **Do** prioritize high ground whenever possible.
- **Do** face the sunny side when encamping.
- **Do** put natural barriers (trees, water) to your rear when in a marsh.
- **Do** deploy with front lower than back on level ground to force attackers uphill.

### Common Pitfalls (Don'ts)
- **Don't** ascend to attack an enemy already on high ground.
- **Don't** camp near a river on the near bank after crossing it.
- **Don't** engage an enemy in midstream while he is crossing.
- **Don't** delay crossing salt marshes.
- **Don't** position yourself at lower elevations relative to the enemy.

### Security/Safety Notes
- Camping on low ground near rivers exposes you to flash flooding and surprise attacks from above.
- Attacking uphill against an entrenched enemy multiplies your casualty risk.
- Salt marshes slow movement and can trap forces; speed minimizes exposure.

## Lesson 2: Always Seek the High Ground

### Core Principle
High ground and sunny positions confer physical health, logistical advantage, and defensive superiority. Natural hazards must be avoided or turned against the enemy.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

#### General Positioning
1. Prefer high ground to low ground in all battle and maneuvering situations.
2. Prefer sunny places to shady places.
3. Encamp close to water and grass with adequate supplies to minimize disease.
4. When encountering hills, dikes, or embankments, occupy the sunny side with your main flank at the back.

#### Natural Hazards
1. When heavy rain falls upstream and foaming water descends, do not ford — wait until it subsides.
2. When encountering named dangerous terrain features — "Precipitous Torrents," "Heavenly Wells," "Heavenly Prison," "Heavenly Net," "Heavenly Trap," and "Heavenly Cracks" — march speedily away.
3. Do not approach these hazards.
4. While keeping distance from them, draw the enemy toward them.
5. Face the hazard and force the enemy to put his back to it.

#### Ambush Prevention
1. Thoroughly search all dangerous defiles, ponds, low-lying ground overgrown with aquatic grass and reeds, and forested mountains with dense tangled undergrowth near your camp.
2. Treat these areas as potential ambush sites and spy hiding places.

### Examples from the Text
- *"If an army encamps close to water and grass with adequate supplies, it will be free from countless diseases and this will spell victory."*
- *"When encountering 'Precipitous Torrents,' 'Heavenly Wells,' 'Heavenly Prison,' 'Heavenly Net,' 'Heavenly Trap,' and 'Heavenly Cracks,' you must march speedily away from them. Do not approach them. While we keep a distance from them, we should draw the enemy toward them."*

### Best Practices (Do's)
- **Do** consistently seek high, sunny positions.
- **Do** maintain proximity to water and grass for supply and health.
- **Do** identify and avoid natural chokepoints and flood zones.
- **Do** use natural hazards as force multipliers by drawing the enemy into them.

### Common Pitfalls (Don'ts)
- **Don't** ford rivers during or immediately after heavy upstream rain.
- **Don't** camp near or encamp in low-lying, reed-choked, or heavily forested areas without first scouting them.
- **Don't** approach named dangerous terrain features.
- **Don't** underestimate the disease risk from poor campsite selection near stagnant water.

### Security/Safety Notes
- Flooding from upstream rain can sweep away forces attempting to ford.
- Unscouted defiles and reed beds are classic ambush locations.
- Disease from stagnant water and poor sanitation has historically killed more soldiers than combat — campsite selection is a force preservation issue.

## Lesson 4: Generate a Fair and Harmonious Relationship

### Core Principle
Effective leadership requires balancing humanity (to build attachment) with iron discipline (to ensure obedience). One without the other renders troops unusable. Rewards and punishments must be timely and consistent.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

#### Building Attachment Before Enforcing Discipline
1. Treat soldiers with humanity first — before they have grown attached to you.
2. Do not punish troops before they have grown attached to you — they will be disobedient.
3. If troops are not obedient, they cannot be employed effectively.

#### Enforcing Discipline After Attachment Is Formed
1. Once troops have become attached to you, enforce discipline rigorously.
2. If troops are attached to you but discipline is not enforced, you cannot employ them.

#### Applying Rewards and Punishments
1. Reward good performance promptly and consistently.
2. Punish misconduct promptly and consistently.
3. Do not delay rewards or punishments — timing is critical to their effectiveness.
4. Be fair and impartial in applying rewards and punishments.

#### The Dual Formula
1. Treat soldiers with humanity in the first instance.
2. Keep them under control by iron discipline.
3. Ensure rewards and punishments are timely and consistent.
4. This combination assures allegiance.

#### Maintaining Order Through Supervision
1. If orders are consistently carried out and troops are strictly supervised, they will be obedient.
2. If orders are never carried out, they will be disobedient.
3. Smooth implementation of orders reflects a harmonious relationship between commander and troops.

### Examples from the Text
- *"If troops are punished before they have grown attached to you, they will be disobedient. If not obedient, it is difficult to employ them."*
- *"If troops have become attached to you, but discipline is not enforced, you cannot employ them either."*
- *"Thus, soldiers must be treated in the first instance with humanity, but kept under control by iron discipline. In this way, the allegiance of soldiers is assured."*
- *"If orders are consistently carried out and the troops are strictly supervised, they will be obedient."*
- *"If orders are never carried out, they will be disobedient."*

### Best Practices (Do's)
- **Do** build relational attachment with your team before enforcing strict discipline.
- **Do** enforce discipline consistently once attachment is established.
- **Do** reward good performance promptly and punish misconduct without delay.
- **Do** supervise troop execution of orders closely to maintain standards.
- **Do** aim for the smooth, consistent implementation of orders as a measure of relationship health.

### Common Pitfalls (Don'ts)
- **Don't** punish or discipline before earning trust and attachment.
- **Don't** be so lenient that discipline erodes after attachment is built.
- **Don't** delay rewards or punishments — timing is critical to their effectiveness.
- **Don't** issue orders that are consistently ignored — this signals a broken relationship.
- **Don't** confuse permissiveness with humanity.

### Security/Safety Notes
- Troops that are disciplined without attachment will be disobedient and unreliable in crisis.
- Troops that are attached without discipline are "spoiled children" — useless for any practical purpose.
- The combination of premature punishment and lack of post-attachment discipline is the worst leadership failure mode in this framework.

## Lesson 5: Avoid the Six Disasters

### Core Principle
Six specific situations — flight, insubordination, collapse, disorganization, rout, and ruin — can afflict any army. None of these disasters are caused by natural circumstances; all are the result of leadership failure. Recognizing and preventing these conditions is a commander's primary responsibility.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

#### Understanding the Six Disasters
1. **Flight** — When troops flee in panic at the first sign of danger. This occurs when soldiers are not properly trained, disciplined, or motivated.
2. **Insubordination** — When troops refuse to obey orders. This occurs when leaders have not built attachment or when discipline is applied unfairly.
3. **Collapse** — When the army's structure breaks down under pressure. This occurs when the general is weak, officers are incompetent, or the command structure is unclear.
4. **Disorganization** — When troops move without coordination or purpose. This occurs when orders are unclear or when the general cannot articulate a coherent plan.
5. **Rout** — When the army is thrown into confusion and retreats uncontrollably. This occurs when forces are not properly prepared or when they face an unexpected crisis.
6. **Ruin** — When the army is completely destroyed. This occurs when a commander pits a weak force against a much stronger one without adequate preparation or strategy.

#### Prevention Steps
1. Ensure clear, consistent communication from leadership at all levels.
2. Build strong relationships with your team before demanding obedience.
3. Enforce discipline fairly and consistently.
4. Train thoroughly so that troops respond automatically to commands under stress.
5. Assess your forces honestly before engaging in conflict.
6. Do not commit a weak or unprepared force against a strong enemy.
7. Maintain strong leadership at every level — from general to junior officers.

### Examples from the Text
- *"When the army is overthrown and the general slain, the cause will surely be found among these five dangerous faults."* (Note: The text identifies these as leadership failures, not natural causes.)
- *"These are the six ways of courting defeat. They are not due to natural causes but to the faults of the general."*

### Best Practices (Do's)
- **Do** conduct regular readiness assessments to identify early signs of the six disasters.
- **Do** train troops under realistic conditions so that they learn to operate under stress.
- **Do** build strong command relationships at every level of the organization.
- **Do** assess your own forces and the enemy's strength before committing to battle.
- **Do** maintain clear, simple communication that cannot be misunderstood.

### Common Pitfalls (Don'ts)
- **Don't** assume that a well-equipped or large force is automatically immune to these disasters.
- **Don't** ignore early warning signs such as low morale, insubordination, or disorganization.
- **Don't** pit a weak or unprepared force against a strong enemy out of pride or desperation.
- **Don't** blame external circumstances for failures that are clearly caused by leadership.
- **Don't** assume that troops will perform well under stress if they have not been trained for it.

### Security/Safety Notes
- These six disasters are existential threats. Once flight or rout begins, it is extremely difficult to reverse.
- In organizational contexts, these translate to: mass resignations (flight), employee disobedience (insubordination), organizational collapse, operational disarray, market rout (loss of customers), and business ruin.
- Prevention through leadership, training, and fair treatment is infinitely less costly than recovery after a disaster.
