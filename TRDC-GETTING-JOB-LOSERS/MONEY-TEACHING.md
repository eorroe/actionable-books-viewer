# Money Teaching: The Rich Don't Work for Money

## Overview

Traditional education trains students to get jobs and work for paychecks, leaving them dependent on employers and vulnerable to being fired or losing a pension. They lose all security they were promised. Robert Kiyosaki teaches that the wealthy instead focus on acquiring assets—businesses, real estate, and commodities—that generate cash flow, making never needing a paycheck the goal rather than a secure paycheck.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When choosing between pursuing a job or building a business
- When evaluating whether to use debt as leverage for investments
- When planning for financial independence beyond a 401K or pension
- When deciding how to allocate savings across different asset classes
- When considering student loans, mortgages, or credit card debt
- When analyzing whether an expense is truly an asset or a liability

## Steps

### Step 1: Reject the paycheck as the ultimate goal

Stop pursuing a job as the promised outcome of education. Recognize that, according to Kiyosaki, working for money makes you dependent on an employer and subject to higher taxes than business owners and investors with asset income, and vulnerable to being fired or losing a pension. Set your goal to never need a paycheck again from any source, from anybody, even the government.

### Step 2: Understand the four asset classes

Learn the four asset classes and their tax implications:
- **Businesses** — generate cash flow and provide tax benefits through employees
- **Real estate** — allows use of debt as leverage and offers tax breaks
- **Paper assets** — savings, stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs, and 401Ks. As Kiyosaki states about employees who rely on these assets, \"These people pay the highest taxes. And they work the hardest.\" In the original recording, Kiyosaki explicitly calls these investors \"Loser\" when referring to those dependent on 401Ks and paper assets.
- **Commodities** — gold, silver, oil, land, water, and food

### Step 3: Choose which asset class to focus on first

Decide which quadrant aligns with your skills and risk tolerance. Start with one area—Kiyosaki began with commodities (gold in 1972), then oil, then real estate, then businesses.

### Step 4: Use debt strategically for real estate

Learn to use other people's money (debt) to acquire income-producing real estate. Unlike paper assets, Kiyosaki teaches that real estate purchased with debt provides tax advantages. Understand that everybody else is saying get out of debt, while Kiyosaki uses it to acquire income-producing real estate and pay no taxes.

### Step 5: Build or buy a business

Create or acquire businesses that generate cash flow independently of your daily labor. Even failed ventures teach critical lessons about entrepreneurship. The goal is to build systems that produce income.

### Step 6: Eliminate bad debt and avoid liabilities

Avoid student loans, which are just the worst type of debt of all because you cannot declare bankruptcy on it, credit card debt, and purchasing primary residences that drain monthly cash flow. Distinguish between assets (which put money in your pocket) and liabilities (which take money out) using cash flow direction, not book value.

### Step 7: Study financial statements instead of academic scores

Replace the pursuit of Fair Isaac Corporation (FICO) credit scores and academic credentials with the ability to read and understand financial statements. Your banker wants to see your financial statement, not your report card.

## Examples

### Example 1: Alexandra quits living paycheck to paycheck

Alexandra worked at a bank but still could not afford the goods and lifestyle she aspired to because she lacked financial education. After learning to invest in real estate, she now owns properties that provide income. Even if she is fired tomorrow, her assets support her family—demonstrating the difference between working for money and having money work for you.

### Example 2: Robert Kiyosaki buys gold in 1972

When gold was illegal for Americans to own, can you imagine that? Back in '72, Kiyosaki began purchasing gold as his first commodity investment during that restriction. He later adds, "Today you have Bitcoin, it's different," meaning the range of alternative assets has expanded since 1972, though his core message — avoid dependence on paper assets — remains unchanged.

### Example 3: Kiyosaki's nylon wallet business

I launched a nylon and velcro surfer wallet business. I went worldwide right away, the trouble is I was an idiot and the business went up and the business came down. What kept me alive was cash flow from my assets.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Prioritize financial education over academic credentials and grades
- ✅ Set the goal to never need a paycheck from anybody, even the government
- ✅ Use debt as leverage for real estate and business acquisitions
- ✅ Focus on cash flow from assets rather than accumulating a salary
- ✅ Build businesses that generate income without requiring your daily presence
- ✅ Read financial statements, not a Fair Isaac Corporation (FICO) credit score
- ❌ Don't rely on 401K plans, pensions, or Social Security as your retirement strategy. Kiyosaki says he \"wouldn't touch [a 401K] with a 10 foot pole,\" an idiom meaning to avoid something completely and keep all distance from it.
- ❌ Don't take on student loans, which are just the worst type of debt of all because you cannot declare bankruptcy on it.
- ❌ Don't assume your house is an asset. Because the cash is flowing out, not in. Your house costs you money every month. If you rent a house, it is all costing you money.

## Keep In Mind

- Schools teach you how to get a job, not how to build wealth.
- According to Kiyosaki, the rules of money are written by the rich for the rich. As Kiyosaki says, \"The Golden Rule: he who has the gold makes the rules.\"
- Having employees creates tax benefits for business owners that employees themselves do not receive.
- Admitting mistakes is acceptable; covering them up is what gets people fired.
- The world changed since the 1960s, but schools still promise the same outcome: get a job.

## Security & Safety Notes

- A student loan was just the worst type of debt of all because you cannot declare bankruptcy on it.
- Relying on a single paycheck exposes you to the risk of job loss, losing all security you were promised, and being fired.
- Government programs like Social Security and pensions can be lost entirely.
- Buying real estate with debt requires understanding cash flow, tax laws, and market cycles to avoid losses.
- Kiyosaki cites Tesla receiving approximately $50 billion from the state of Nevada as an example of government incentives for job-creating businesses, noting this is his approximate recollection rather than an exact audited figure.
- Kiyosaki identifies being fired or losing a pension as the biggest terror of most people.
- Kiyosaki states he carries approximately $600 million in debt at the time of recording, contrasting his strategic use of debt for asset acquisition with average Americans struggling to pay credit card debt. He references a recent Trump tax cut as part of the discussion on how tax rules favor the wealthy.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that getting a job after graduation is the natural and safe next step
  **Solution:** Treat employment as experience, not a permanent career destination. Build assets on the side from day one.
- **Problem:** Confusing a primary residence with an investment asset
  **Solution:** Because the cash is flowing out, not in. Your house costs you money every month. If you rent a house, it is all costing you money.
- **Problem:** Investing exclusively in paper assets like a 401K because "that is what everyone does"
  **Solution:** Study the tax treatment of every asset class and build a portfolio that includes assets with more favorable tax treatment than paper assets and stronger cash flow characteristics.
- **Problem:** Using debt to fund consumption (cars, credit cards, vacations) instead of investment
  **Solution:** Restrict debt to acquisitions that produce income or offer tax benefits. If debt does not pay you, it is bad debt.
- **Problem:** Believing that employment alone provides financial security
  **Solution:** Avoid spending every penny of income; instead, invest in assets that generate cash flow independent of your labor.
