# The Magic of Thinking Big

## Overview

"The Magic of Thinking Big" by David J. Schwartz is a self-help classic that teaches readers how to unlock their potential by changing the size of their thinking. Schwartz argues that the secret of success is not in what you know or who you are, but in how big you think. Through practical strategies, he shows how small habits of mind—belief, confidence, goal-setting, and how you relate to people—can be transformed to produce extraordinary results in any area of life. This book exists as a direct call to action: stop thinking small, adopt the habits of big thinkers, and begin living at a level that matches your true capacity.

## When to Follow Book Teachings

- When you feel stuck in a rut, limited by your current circumstances, or convinced your goals are out of reach
- When you are leading or managing people and need to build confidence, eliminate fear, and inspire big thinking in others
- When you are setting goals, making career decisions, or planning the next phase of your life and need a framework for thinking beyond perceived limits
- When you catch yourself making excuses, blaming others, or retreating after setbacks
- When you want to improve your communication, relationships, and influence by adopting the attitudes of successful people
- When you are rebuilding confidence after a defeat or failure and need proven strategies to turn defeat into victory

## Lessons From Book

### Lesson 1: Believe You Can Succeed and You Will

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Storekeeper and the Big Idea

A storekeeper in a small town believed his business could grow beyond a local shop. Instead of accepting the belief that his location limited him, he thought big and began advertising to a wider region, offering delivery services, and expanding his product line. His belief became self-fulfilling because belief is the starting point of all achievement—it creates the mental atmosphere in which success grows. People who believe they can succeed see opportunities where others see obstacles, and they act on those opportunities.

##### Example 2: The $10,000-a-Year Man

A man earning $10,000 a year believed he was worth more and began acting, dressing, and thinking like someone worth $25,000 a year. Within a short period, he received a promotion and a salary increase to $25,000. His belief changed his actions, which changed his results. Belief is not just a mental exercise—it drives the behaviors that produce real-world outcomes.

##### Example 3: The Power of "I Can"

Schwartz describes how the simple phrase "I can" activates the subconscious mind and mobilizes the faculties needed to achieve a goal. People who habitually say "I can" develop a psychology of success that propels them forward, while those who say "I can't" close the door before they even try. The words you use program your belief system and, ultimately, your reality.

#### Step 1: Develop the Power of Belief

Believe it is possible to succeed. Your belief creates the mental atmosphere in which success grows. Accept the fact that what you believe you can do, you can do—and that belief in yourself is the first requirement for all success. Write down three things you currently believe you cannot do, and for each one, write a belief statement in the present tense: "I can and will succeed at [goal]."

#### Step 2: Feed Your Mind Positive Success Images

Your mind will produce what you feed it. Feed it success images, success thoughts, and success expectations. Each morning, spend five minutes visualizing yourself succeeding at your most important goal. See it in detail—the environment, the people, the outcome. This practice conditions your subconscious to accept success as normal and expected.

#### Step 3: Refuse to Accept Limiting Labels

Do not accept the labels others place on you. Other people's opinions of your ability are not your concern. When someone says you cannot do something, treat it as their limitation, not yours. Politely decline the label and continue thinking and acting in ways that support your own beliefs about yourself.

#### Step 4: Build Confidence Through Small Wins

Confidence grows from doing. Identify one small, actionable step you can take today toward a goal you have been putting off. Complete it. Then take another. Each small win builds the belief that bigger wins are possible. Do not wait for big confidence to appear before acting—confidence is built through action.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Start each day by affirming your belief in your ability to succeed
- ✅ Visualize success before beginning any important task or meeting
- ✅ Keep a written record of past successes to reinforce your belief during difficult times
- ✅ Speak in positive, belief-oriented language ("I will" instead of "I'll try")
- ✅ Associate with people who believe in you and your potential
- ❌ Do not let past failures define your future capabilities
- ❌ Do not accept labels or opinions about your limits from others
- ❌ Do not wait until you feel completely confident before taking action
- ❌ Do not feed your mind negative news, pessimistic conversations, or fear-based content

#### Keep In Mind

Belief is not blind optimism. It is a decision to focus on what is possible rather than what is impossible, combined with the willingness to take the actions that make success possible. Your belief level directly controls your level of action and, therefore, your results. You do not rise to the level of your goals; you fall to the level of your beliefs.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Unrealistic belief that ignores real risks can lead to reckless decisions. Healthy belief means confidence combined with planning and preparation. Assess risks honestly, then decide to proceed with faith in your ability to handle whatever comes. Do not let belief become an excuse to avoid planning or to take unnecessary risks.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing in success but failing to take action because you are waiting for more confidence or perfect conditions.
  **Solution:** Understand that action builds confidence, not the reverse. Take the smallest possible step today.
- **Problem:** Letting a single failure destroy your belief system.
  **Solution:** Reframe failures as temporary setbacks and feedback, not evidence of inability. Every successful person has failures.
- **Problem:** Relying on other people's belief in you instead of building your own self-belief.
  **Solution:** Your belief must come from within. External validation is helpful but unreliable; self-belief is permanent.

### Lesson 2: Cure Yourself of Excusitis, the Failure Disease

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Economic Condition Excuse

Many people blame poor economic conditions for their lack of progress. Schwartz points out that economic conditions are never perfect for everyone—there are always people who succeed even in the worst economies. Those who use economic conditions as an excuse are choosing to be victims rather than agents of their own success. The cure is to stop blaming external conditions and start focusing on what you can control: your own actions, attitudes, and decisions.

##### Example 2: The Health Excuse

"Poor health" is another common excuse. While health is important, many people with genuine health challenges achieve extraordinary success by refusing to let their condition become an excuse. The key is to accept your condition, adapt your methods, and continue moving forward. Excusitis says, "I cannot succeed because of my health." The cure says, "Given my condition, here is how I will succeed."

##### Example 3: The Intelligence Excuse

Many people believe they are not smart enough to succeed. Schwartz refutes this by showing that success depends more on attitude, persistence, and thinking habits than on raw IQ. Average people with big thinking habits consistently outperform highly intelligent people with small thinking habits. Your intelligence is not the barrier—your thinking is.

#### Step 1: Identify Your Excuses

Make a list of the excuses you use to explain why you have not achieved your goals or why you are stuck. Common excuses include economic conditions, poor health, lack of education, bad luck, age, other people, and unfair treatment. Write each one down honestly.

#### Step 2: Test the Excuse for Validity

For each excuse, ask: Is this really preventing me from succeeding, or is it just a convenient explanation? Is there someone who has succeeded despite this same condition? If someone else has overcome it, so can you. Excusitis thrives on unexamined assumptions.

#### Step 3: Replace Excuses with Action Plans

For each excuse, write a counter-statement and a corresponding action plan. Instead of "I cannot advance because of the economy," write "The economy affects everyone, but I will differentiate myself through [specific skills or actions]." Then list three concrete actions you will take.

#### Step 4: Develop an Anti-Excusitis Mindset

Build the habit of catching yourself when you make excuses and immediately redirecting to solutions. Treat excuses as red flags that indicate you are operating from a small-thinking mindset. When you hear yourself making an excuse, pause, acknowledge it, and ask: "What can I do instead?"

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Keep a written record of excuses you catch yourself making and the solutions you applied
- ✅ Seek out people who have overcome the same excuses you face and study their strategies
- ✅ Replace "I can't because..." with "Here is how I will..."
- ✅ Practice gratitude for what you do have instead of focusing on what you lack
- ✅ Take responsibility for your results rather than blaming external forces
- ❌ Do not use health, economic conditions, or lack of education as permanent barriers
- ❌ Do not compare yourself to others as an excuse for inaction
- ❌ Do not wait for perfect conditions before taking action
- ❌ Do not blame other people for your circumstances

#### Keep In Mind

Excusitis is contagious—it spreads through association. If you spend time with chronic excuse-makers, you will absorb their mindset. The cure is to limit exposure to excuse-based thinking and to surround yourself with people who take responsibility for their results. Excuses are easy to make and hard to break, but the moment you stop making them, you begin the process of real achievement.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Some health conditions genuinely require attention and medical care. Do not dismiss legitimate health concerns by labeling them as excuses. The goal is to refuse to let any condition become a permanent barrier to forward movement, not to ignore real limitations. Consult medical professionals for health issues, and adapt your strategies accordingly.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that because you have a legitimate limitation, you are justified in making no progress at all.
  **Solution:** Adapt your approach to work within your limitations rather than waiting for them to disappear.
- **Problem:** Using past failures as evidence that your excuses are valid.
  **Solution:** Separate past experiences from future possibilities. Past conditions do not determine future results.
- **Problem:** Surrounding yourself with people who reinforce your excuses.
  **Solution:** Intentionally seek relationships with people who model responsibility and solution-oriented thinking.

### Lesson 3: Build Confidence and Destroy Fear

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Salesperson Who Faced Rejection

A salesperson who feared rejection began using the "deposit" method: each morning, he deposited a mental image of himself making successful presentations into his subconscious. He also practiced the "next step" technique—focusing only on the immediate next action rather than the entire sales call. Over time, his fear decreased and his confidence grew because he trained his mind to focus on action rather than anxiety.

##### Example 2: The Executive and the Stage Fright

An executive who feared public speaking began taking every opportunity to speak in front of small groups, gradually working up to larger audiences. He used the technique of focusing on the message he wanted to deliver rather than on the audience's judgment. By concentrating on helping others rather than on his own fear, he transformed his anxiety into confidence.

##### Example 3: Memory-Picture Method

Schwartz recommends the "memory-picture" method for building confidence: sit quietly, close your eyes, and vividly picture yourself performing the task that scares you successfully. Your subconscious cannot distinguish between a real experience and a vividly imagined one. By repeatedly imagining success, you build a mental memory of success that your mind accepts as real.

#### Step 1: Use the Memory-Picture Method Daily

Spend 10 minutes each morning in a quiet place, close your eyes, and vividly picture yourself performing the task that scares you. See yourself confident, competent, and successful. Feel the emotions of confidence. Do this daily for at least 30 days. Your mind will build a new memory of success.

#### Step 2: Focus on the Message, Not Yourself

When you feel fear in social or professional situations, redirect your attention from yourself to the message you want to deliver or the value you want to provide. Fear shrinks when you focus on helping others. Ask yourself: "What does this person need to hear?" rather than "How am I being perceived?"

#### Step 3: Take Action in the Face of Fear

Fear shrinks when you move toward it. The moment you begin acting, fear begins to dissolve. If you are afraid to make a phone call, make it immediately. If you are afraid to speak up in a meeting, say the first thing that comes to your mind. Action is the antidote to fear.

#### Step 4: Build a Confidence File

Keep a record of your past successes, compliments you have received, and challenges you have overcome. When fear appears, open the file and remind yourself of your track record. Your past success is the best predictor of your future success.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Use the memory-picture method every morning before starting your day
- ✅ Reframe fear as excitement—your body responds similarly to both, and you can choose which label to apply
- ✅ Focus on what you want to give to others rather than what you fear receiving
- ✅ Take small, brave actions daily to build your confidence muscle
- ✅ Review your confidence file whenever fear appears
- ❌ Do not avoid situations that trigger fear—avoidance makes fear stronger
- ❌ Do not rehearse fear in your mind by imagining worst-case scenarios
- ❌ Do not let fear make decisions for you
- ❌ Do not compare your behind-the-scenes struggles to other people's polished results

#### Keep In Mind

Confidence is not the absence of fear; it is the willingness to act despite fear. Every confident person feels fear—they have simply trained themselves to move forward anyway. Confidence is built through repeated action, not through thinking about action. The more you act in the face of fear, the more your confidence grows.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Do not confuse confidence with recklessness. Confidence combined with preparation is powerful; confidence without preparation is dangerous. Always prepare thoroughly for important tasks, and then act with the confidence that comes from being ready. Do not use "confidence building" as an excuse to skip necessary preparation.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting to feel confident before taking action.
  **Solution:** Action creates confidence, not the other way around. Take the smallest step forward today.
- **Problem:** Letting fear of embarrassment prevent you from speaking up or taking risks.
  **Solution:** Most people are too focused on themselves to judge you as harshly as you imagine. Focus on the value you provide.
- **Problem:** Allowing a single setback to destroy your confidence.
  **Solution:** Treat setbacks as data, not as verdicts on your ability. Analyze, adjust, and continue.

### Lesson 4: How to Think Big

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Secretary Who Became an Executive

A secretary was told she could never advance beyond her position because she had no college degree. Instead of accepting this small thinking, she thought big: she enrolled in night school, developed her skills, and within a few years was promoted to a management position and later to an executive role. Big thinking changed her trajectory.

##### Example 2: The Hardware Store That Became a Chain

A small hardware store owner could have been satisfied with his local shop. Instead, he thought big: he studied the market, identified unmet needs, expanded his product lines, and eventually grew a regional chain. Big thinking is not wishful thinking—it is disciplined thinking that sees beyond current limitations.

##### Example 3: The "Clic" and "Boom" Thinking Pattern

Schwartz introduces the concept of "Clic" and "Boom" thinking. "Clic" thinkers see only what is immediately in front of them and accept limitations as permanent. "Boom" thinkers look beyond the present situation, see possibilities, and plan for growth. A "Clic" thinker sees a small office and thinks, "This is all I have." A "Boom" thinker sees the same office and thinks, "This is the beginning; I will expand."

#### Step 1: Train Your Mind to See the Big Picture

Each time you face a situation, force yourself to look beyond the immediate limitation. Ask: "What is the opportunity here? What could this become?" Practice big-picture thinking by writing down three possible "big" outcomes for every challenge you face.

#### Step 2: Add Value to Everything You Do

Big thinkers do not just complete tasks—they add value. Ask yourself with every task: "How can I add extra value, extra quality, extra benefit?" When you add value beyond expectations, you create opportunities for growth, recognition, and advancement. Big thinking means always doing more than the minimum.

#### Step 3: Think in Terms of "We," Not Just "Me"

Big thinkers build bridges, teams, and relationships. They think about how their success can benefit others, and how other people's contributions can amplify their own success. Stop thinking solely about your own interests and start thinking about how you can contribute to larger goals and help others succeed.

#### Step 4: Cultivate the Habit of Doing More Than Expected

Deliberately choose to do more than is required in every situation. Return borrowed items better than you received them. Complete projects before deadlines. Offer helpful suggestions. When you make a habit of doing more than expected, you stand out and create opportunities that small thinkers never see.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Ask "How can I add value?" before beginning every task
- ✅ Look at problems as opportunities in disguise
- ✅ Think about how your work benefits the larger organization or community
- ✅ Practice seeing possibilities rather than limitations
- ✅ Invest time in learning skills that expand your capacity
- ❌ Do not accept "the way things are" as permanent
- ❌ Do not limit your vision to what you can see today
- ❌ Do not focus only on short-term gains at the expense of long-term growth
- ❌ Do not be satisfied with doing only what is required

#### Keep In Mind

Thinking big is a habit that can be developed through daily practice. It is not about daydreaming or wishful thinking—it is about training your mind to see opportunities, possibilities, and pathways to growth that small thinkers miss. Big thinking leads to big action, and big action leads to big results. Your thinking determines your reality.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Big thinking should always be grounded in reality. Unrealistic planning without research and preparation can waste resources and create unnecessary risk. Before committing to a big plan, gather data, assess risks honestly, and develop contingency plans. Big thinking combined with careful planning is powerful; big thinking without planning is fantasy.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Thinking big but failing to act because the steps feel overwhelming.
  **Solution:** Break big goals into small, daily actions. Focus on the next step, not the entire mountain.
- **Problem:** Allowing other people's small thinking to infect your mindset.
  **Solution:** Limit exposure to people who reinforce limitations. Surround yourself with big thinkers.
- **Problem:** Confusing big thinking with ignoring practical constraints.
  **Solution:** Think big about the destination, but plan carefully about the path. Balance vision with execution.

### Lesson 5: How to Think and Dream Creatively

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Subway Advertising Breakthrough

A man who sold advertising space in subways was told that subway walls were not a good place for advertising. He thought creatively: what if the advertising was not on the walls but on the trains themselves? The idea was initially dismissed, but he persisted, and train advertising became one of the most successful advertising mediums in history. Creative thinking broke through a perceived limitation.

##### Example 2: The Insurance Agent Who Sold Peace of Mind

An insurance agent was struggling because everyone else was selling insurance. He thought creatively: instead of selling insurance policies, he sold "peace of mind" and "family protection." He reframed his entire presentation around the emotional benefit rather than the technical features. His sales doubled because he offered something unique.

##### Example 3: The Lazy Person's Secret

Schwartz points out that creative thinkers often appear to be "lazy" because they are always looking for easier, better ways to do things. They refuse to accept the statement "We have always done it this way." Creative thinking is the willingness to ask "What if?" and to experiment with new approaches.

#### Step 1: Develop the "What If" Habit

Train yourself to ask "What if?" in every situation. "What if I approached this differently? What if I combined two ideas? What if I removed this constraint?" Creative ideas begin with curiosity and the refusal to accept the status quo. Write down three "What if" questions for every challenge you face this week.

#### Step 2: Believe That Creative Solutions Exist

Creative thinking requires the belief that better ways exist. If you believe a problem has no solution, you will stop looking. If you believe a solution exists, your mind will work to find it—even subconsciously. Approach every problem with the assumption that a better way is waiting to be discovered.

#### Step 3: Combine Ideas Creatively

Creative breakthroughs often come from combining two existing ideas in a new way. Study successful ideas in different fields and ask: "How could I combine this with something else to create something new?" The history of innovation is largely a history of idea combination.

#### Step 4: Create and Capture Ideas

Keep an idea journal. Write down every idea, no matter how small or impractical it seems. Review your journal weekly and look for patterns, combinations, and new applications. Creative thinking is not a single event—it is a habit of mind that develops through consistent practice.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Ask "What if?" in every situation
- ✅ Believe that creative solutions always exist
- ✅ Study successful ideas from other fields and look for connections
- ✅ Keep an idea journal and review it regularly
- ✅ Experiment with new approaches and learn from failures
- ❌ Do not accept "We have always done it this way" as a valid reason
- ❌ Do not dismiss half-formed ideas before exploring them
- ❌ Do not fear being wrong—wrong ideas often lead to right ones
- ❌ Do not let the fear of criticism suppress your creative thinking

#### Keep In Mind

Creative thinking is not reserved for artists and inventors—it is a skill that anyone can develop. It requires curiosity, open-mindedness, and the willingness to experiment. The most successful people in any field are those who think creatively about how to improve, differentiate, and add value. Your creative potential is unlimited if you will release it.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Creative ideas should be evaluated for feasibility, ethics, and potential consequences before implementation. Not every creative idea is a good idea. Test new ideas on a small scale before full implementation, and ensure they align with legal, ethical, and organizational standards.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing you are not a creative person.
  **Solution:** Creativity is a skill, not a talent. Practice asking "What if?" daily and your creative muscles will strengthen.
- **Problem:** Dismissing creative ideas because they seem impractical at first.
  **Solution:** Most breakthrough ideas seemed impractical at first. Explore ideas before judging them.
- **Problem:** Waiting for inspiration before acting.
  **Solution:** Creativity comes from action, not waiting. Start working on a problem and ideas will emerge.

### Lesson 6: You Are What You Think You Are

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Receptionist Who Became a Manager

A receptionist who thought of herself as "just a receptionist" remained in that role for years. When she began to think of herself as a key contributor to the organization, she started acting with more initiative, suggesting improvements, and developing her skills. Within a year, she was promoted to an office manager role. She did not change her job title first—she changed her self-image, and the promotion followed.

##### Example 2: The Prisoner of Self-Image

Schwartz describes how people who think of themselves as old, sick, or weak actually become more old, sick, and weak. Your self-image determines your actions, and your actions determine your results. If you think you are young and energetic, you will act in ways that keep you young and energetic. If you think you are old and declining, you will act in ways that accelerate your decline.

##### Example 3: The "I Am" Statements

The words you use to describe yourself shape your reality. People who habitually say "I am lucky," "I am capable," or "I am successful" develop those qualities. People who habitually say "I am unlucky," "I am not good enough," or "I am stuck" develop those qualities. Your self-talk programs your subconscious and, ultimately, your life.

#### Step 1: Audit Your Self-Image

Spend a day observing your self-talk. Every time you say something negative about yourself, write it down. Common patterns include "I am not good at...", "I always mess up...", "I am too old/young/inexperienced." Awareness is the first step to change.

#### Step 2: Replace Negative Self-Descriptions

For every negative self-description, write a positive replacement. Instead of "I am not good at sales," write "I am developing my sales skills and improving every day." Instead of "I am too old to change careers," write "I have valuable experience that makes me an asset in any role." Repeat these new statements daily.

#### Step 3: Act the Way You Want to Be

Your actions reinforce your self-image. If you want to think of yourself as a confident leader, act like a confident leader—even before you feel like one. Dress the part, speak the part, and take on responsibilities that match the person you want to become. Acting your way into a new way of thinking is faster than thinking your way into a new way of acting.

#### Step 4: Surround Yourself with People Who Reflect Your Desired Self-Image

The people you spend time with influence your self-image. If you spend time with people who see you as successful, capable, and valuable, you will begin to see yourself that way too. If you spend time with people who reinforce small thinking about you, you will stay small. Choose your relationships deliberately.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Speak about yourself in positive, capability-oriented language
- ✅ Act in ways that match the person you want to become
- ✅ Review and update your self-descriptions regularly
- ✅ Seek feedback from people who see your potential
- ✅ Visualize yourself as the person you want to become
- ❌ Do not accept negative labels from your past or from other people
- ❌ Do not define yourself by a single failure or setback
- ❌ Do not let your current circumstances dictate your future self-image
- ❌ Do not compare your beginning to someone else's middle

#### Keep In Mind

Your self-image is not fixed—it is a habit that can be changed. The person you see yourself as today is not the person you must be tomorrow. You can reshape your self-image through deliberate practice of new thoughts, words, and actions. Change your self-image, and you change your life.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Building a positive self-image should not become an excuse to ignore genuine areas for improvement. Confidence combined with humility and a willingness to learn is the ideal. Acknowledge your weaknesses honestly, then decide to grow in those areas rather than denying them or letting them define you.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to build self-image through external validation alone.
  **Solution:** External validation is unreliable. Build your self-image through internal standards, personal values, and consistent action.
- **Problem:** Overcorrecting into arrogance or false confidence.
  **Solution:** Confidence and humility can coexist. Be confident in your potential while remaining open to growth.
- **Problem:** Allowing a single negative comment to destroy your self-image.
  **Solution:** Other people's opinions are data, not truth. Evaluate feedback objectively and retain what is useful; discard the rest.

### Lesson 7: Manage Your Environment: Go First Class

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Two Salespeople in Different Offices

Two salespeople with identical skills and experience were assigned to different offices. One was placed in a modern, well-appointed office that inspired confidence. The other was placed in a rundown, poorly furnished office that communicated low standards. Within months, the first salesperson was outperforming the second. Environment had shaped their self-image, their confidence, and their results.

##### Example 2: The Executive Who Dressed for Success

An executive who dressed in expensive, well-tailored clothes found that people treated him with more respect, offered him better opportunities, and responded more positively to his ideas. His appearance was not superficial—it was a signal to himself and to others that he was a person of value who deserved first-class treatment.

##### Example 3: The Home Environment

A person who filled their home with cheap, worn-out furniture, poor lighting, and clutter felt drained and uninspired. When they upgraded their environment—investing in better furniture, good lighting, and organized spaces—their energy, mood, and productivity improved significantly. Your physical environment shapes your mental environment.

#### Step 1: Audit Your Environment

Examine the environments where you spend most of your time: your home, your office, your car, the places you socialize. Ask: Does this environment lift me up or drag me down? Does it communicate success and possibility, or limitation and mediocrity? Be honest in your assessment.

#### Step 2: Go First Class in Your Physical Space

Upgrade the physical environments you control. Invest in better furniture, lighting, and organization. Clean and declutter your spaces. Make your environment reflect the level of success you want to achieve. You do not need to spend a fortune—you need to make intentional choices that signal "first class" to yourself and others.

#### Step 3: Choose Your Social Environment Deliberately

Evaluate the people you spend time with. Do they inspire you to grow, or do they reinforce small thinking? Limit your time with people who drag you down and increase your time with people who lift you up. Your social environment is one of the most powerful determinants of your success.

#### Step 4: Manage Your Information Environment

Be selective about the books you read, the media you consume, and the conversations you participate in. Fill your mind with ideas that expand your thinking rather than shrink it. Unfollow or limit exposure to sources that feed fear, negativity, or small thinking. Curate your mental environment with the same care you give your physical environment.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Invest in your physical environment as a statement of your self-worth and aspirations
- ✅ Surround yourself with people who think big and support your growth
- ✅ Consume content that expands your thinking and reinforces your goals
- ✅ Keep your primary environments clean, organized, and uplifting
- ✅ Regularly audit your environments and make adjustments
- ❌ Do not accept a rundown, cluttered, or depressing environment as permanent
- ❌ Do not spend time with people who consistently reinforce small thinking
- ❌ Do not consume media that makes you feel fearful, inadequate, or hopeless
- ❌ Do not neglect your environment because you are too busy or too focused on other things

#### Keep In Mind

Environment is not just the physical space around you—it is also the people, information, and ideas that surround you. All of these forces shape your thinking, your attitudes, and your results. You have more control over your environment than you think. Manage it deliberately, and you will manage your results.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Upgrading your environment should be done within your means. Financial responsibility is part of first-class thinking. Do not go into debt to create a perfect environment. Instead, prioritize improvements that have the highest impact on your mood and productivity, and make them gradually as your resources allow.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that a better environment will automatically produce better results without personal effort.
  **Solution:** Environment supports success; it does not replace it. Use your improved environment as fuel for action, not a substitute for it.
- **Problem:** Neglecting your environment because you are focused on other priorities.
  **Solution:** Environment is a priority, not a luxury. A poorly managed environment drains energy that could be used for achievement.
- **Problem:** Allowing toxic people to remain in your life because they are familiar.
  **Solution:** Familiarity is not a good reason to maintain relationships that limit your growth. Growth often requires uncomfortable changes.

### Lesson 8: Make Your Attitudes Your Allies

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The "I Think I Can" Engine

A person with a positive "I can" attitude approaches every situation expecting success. This expectation shapes their actions: they persist longer, try more solutions, and attract help from others. Their attitude becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy. Conversely, a person with a "I can't" attitude approaches situations expecting failure, gives up quickly, and misses opportunities that a positive thinker would see.

##### Example 2: The Gratitude Attitude

Schwartz shows that people who cultivate gratitude consistently report higher levels of happiness, better health, and greater success. Gratitude shifts your focus from what you lack to what you have, which opens your mind to opportunities rather than closing it in fear. The gratitude attitude is one of the most powerful tools for building a success psychology.

##### Example 3: The Service Attitude

People who approach life with an attitude of service—asking "How can I help?" rather than "What can I get?"—build stronger relationships, create more value, and achieve greater success. Service attitude attracts people, opens doors, and creates goodwill that money cannot buy. Success and service are not opposites—they are partners.

#### Step 1: Identify Your Dominant Attitudes

Spend a day observing your default attitudes toward work, people, challenges, and opportunities. Are they generally positive or negative? Do you approach situations expecting success or failure? Write down your dominant attitudes and assess whether they are helping or hindering you.

#### Step 2: Adopt the "I Can" and "I Will" Attitudes

Make "I can" and "I will" your default responses to challenges. When you face a difficult task, replace "I can't" with "I can and will find a way." When you see an opportunity, replace "I might" with "I will act." Language shapes attitude, and attitude shapes action.

#### Step 3: Cultivate Gratitude Daily

Each morning or evening, write down three things you are grateful for. They can be small or large. The practice of gratitude rewires your brain to focus on abundance rather than scarcity. Over time, gratitude becomes your default emotional state, and a grateful person attracts more reasons to be grateful.

#### Step 4: Adopt a Service Attitude

Before every interaction, ask yourself: "How can I help this person succeed?" Shift your focus from what you can get to what you can give. Service attitude creates goodwill, builds trust, and opens doors that self-interest cannot open. The person who serves the most wins the most.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Start each day with gratitude and positive affirmations
- ✅ Replace "I can't" with "How can I?" in your vocabulary
- ✅ Ask "How can I help?" in every professional interaction
- ✅ Celebrate small wins to reinforce positive attitudes
- ✅ Review your attitudes weekly and adjust where needed
- ❌ Do not allow negative attitudes to become your default setting
- ❌ Do not let a single setback turn into a permanent negative attitude
- ❌ Do not assume that positive attitudes replace the need for action
- ❌ Do neglect the power of gratitude because it seems simple

#### Keep In Mind

Attitudes are habits of thinking that you can choose, develop, and change. They are not fixed traits handed to you by genetics or circumstance. The attitudes you hold today are the result of habits you have built over time, and you can build new habits that produce better attitudes. Your attitudes are your allies when you choose them deliberately.

#### Security & Safety Notes

A positive attitude should not be used to ignore genuine problems or to dismiss valid concerns. Healthy positivity acknowledges reality and then chooses to focus on solutions rather than problems. Toxic positivity—pretending everything is fine when it is not—can be harmful. Be positive, but also be honest and realistic.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that a positive attitude alone will solve all problems without action.
  **Solution:** Attitude drives action; it does not replace it. Combine positive thinking with disciplined execution.
- **Problem:** Using positivity to suppress negative emotions rather than processing them.
  **Solution:** Feel your emotions fully, then choose a constructive attitude. Suppression creates more problems than it solves.
- **Problem:** Letting other people's negative attitudes influence your own.
  **Solution:** You are responsible for your own attitudes. Do not let other people's negativity infect your mindset.

### Lesson 9: Think Right Toward People

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Power of Acceptance

Schwartz explains that the deepest human need is to be appreciated and accepted. When you genuinely accept and appreciate others, they respond by liking you, cooperating with you, and wanting to help you succeed. Acceptance is the foundation of all positive human relationships. It is not manipulation—it is genuine recognition of the value in others.

##### Example 1: Let Others Feel Important

When you make others feel important, you create goodwill that multiplies. A simple genuine compliment, an expression of gratitude, or an act of recognition costs nothing but returns enormous value in relationships. People who feel important around you will become your greatest allies.

##### Example 2: Think "You" Instead of "I"

In every conversation, focus on the other person rather than on yourself. Ask questions, listen actively, and show genuine interest in their lives and concerns. When you make the conversation about them rather than about you, you build trust and rapport faster than any other technique.

#### Step 1: Develop a Genuine Interest in Others

Make a habit of asking people about themselves—their goals, their challenges, their interests. Listen without interrupting or immediately shifting the conversation back to yourself. Practice active listening: maintain eye contact, nod, ask follow-up questions, and show that you are genuinely engaged.

#### Step 2: Give Honest, Specific Compliments

Give compliments that are specific and genuine. Instead of "Good job," say "The way you handled that client's concerns was excellent—you turned a difficult situation into a positive outcome." Specific compliments are more meaningful and more credible than vague praise.

#### Step 3: Let Others Share Credit

When success occurs, be generous in sharing credit. Acknowledge the contributions of others publicly and specifically. When people feel that their contributions are recognized, they are motivated to contribute even more. Credit-sharing creates loyalty and teamwork.

#### Step 4: Accept Others Without Judging Them

Practice accepting people as they are rather than judging them for their flaws or differences. Acceptance does not mean agreement or approval of everything they do—it means recognizing their fundamental worth as human beings. When you accept others, they feel safe around you and are more likely to be honest and open.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Make people feel important through genuine recognition and appreciation
- ✅ Focus conversations on the other person rather than on yourself
- ✅ Give specific, honest compliments rather than generic praise
- ✅ Share credit generously and acknowledge others' contributions
- ✅ Practice acceptance by looking for the good in every person
- ❌ Do not manipulate or flatter people with insincere praise
- ❌ Do not dominate conversations by talking only about yourself
- ❌ Do not take credit for work that belongs to others
- ❌ Do not judge people harshly for their flaws or mistakes

#### Keep In Mind

Thinking right toward people is not a technique for getting what you want—it is a philosophy for building genuine, lasting relationships. When you genuinely care about others and want them to succeed, you create a network of goodwill that benefits everyone. The relationships you build through genuine acceptance and appreciation are your greatest assets.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Thinking right toward people does not mean tolerating abuse, exploitation, or unethical behavior. Accept people as human beings worthy of respect, but maintain clear boundaries and do not allow yourself to be mistreated. Genuine relationships require mutual respect and honesty, not one-sided sacrifice.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Using compliments and acceptance as manipulation rather than genuine connection.
  **Solution:** If your compliments are strategic rather than sincere, people will sense it. Focus on genuinely seeing and appreciating the good in others.
- **Problem:** Neglecting your own needs while trying to make others feel important.
  **Solution:** Thinking right toward people does not mean neglecting yourself. Balance service to others with self-care and self-respect.
- **Problem:** Trying to be liked by everyone by agreeing with everything.
  **Solution:** Being genuine and respectful is more important than being universally liked. Honest disagreement expressed with respect builds stronger trust than false agreement.

### Lesson 10: Get the Action Habit

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Man Who Talked Instead of Acted

Schwartz describes a man who spent years talking about the business he wanted to start, the book he wanted to write, and the changes he wanted to make. He was always planning, always preparing, always getting ready—but never acting. Years passed, and he had nothing to show for his thoughts. Action is the bridge between thinking and results, and without it, thinking remains empty.

##### Example 2: The Salesperson Who Made the Extra Call

A salesperson who was struggling decided to make one extra sales call each day. Over a year, that one extra call per day added up to more than 250 additional opportunities. Some of those opportunities converted into sales, and within a year, this salesperson was the top performer in the company. Action compounds.

##### Example 3: The Letter That Changed a Career

A person who was stuck in a dead-end job decided to take action: he wrote a letter to the CEO of a company he admired, expressing his interest and offering his skills. The letter was read, he was interviewed, and he was offered a position that doubled his income. Ideas and opportunities do not come to thinkers—they come to actors.

#### Step 1: Recognize That Action Cures Fear

Fear is reduced by action, not by thinking about action. When you feel fear or resistance, recognize it as a signal to act immediately. The moment you begin moving, fear begins to dissolve. Do not wait until fear disappears before acting—it will not.

#### Step 2: Lower the Activation Energy for Action

Make it as easy as possible to take action. Prepare your materials the night before. Put your gym clothes by your bed. Set up your workspace so that beginning is automatic. The less resistance between you and action, the more likely you are to act.

#### Step 3: Use the "Do It Now" Rule

When a task or opportunity presents itself, do it now—or schedule it for a specific time immediately. Do not let tasks sit in limbo. Procrastination is the thief of opportunity, and it feeds on the phrase "I will do it later." Replace "later" with "now" or with a specific commitment: "I will do this at 2 PM today."

#### Step 4: Build Momentum Through Small Daily Actions

Consistent small actions build momentum that leads to big results. Identify the one action that, if done daily, would move you toward your most important goal. Do it every day without exception. Momentum is more important than intensity—small daily actions outperform occasional heroic efforts.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Take action immediately when opportunities or tasks arise
- ✅ Lower the barrier to action by preparing in advance
- ✅ Build a daily action habit around your most important goal
- ✅ Celebrate completed actions, not just results
- ✅ Use the "do it now" rule for tasks that take less than two minutes
- ❌ Do not wait for perfect conditions or complete confidence
- ❌ Do not let procrastination become a habit
- ❌ Do not spend more time planning than acting
- ❌ Do not confuse busywork with productive action

#### Keep In Mind

The action habit separates successful people from unsuccessful ones. Ideas are valuable, but ideas without action are worthless. Every successful person you admire took massive action—often in the face of fear, uncertainty, and criticism. Action is not just important; it is everything.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Rash action without planning can create unnecessary risk and waste resources. The goal is to develop the habit of acting while still planning enough to act intelligently. Think big, plan well, and then act decisively. Do not let the pursuit of action become an excuse for reckless decision-making.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Taking random actions without a clear direction or goal.
  **Solution:** Connect every action to a specific goal. Random action produces random results; focused action produces focused results.
- **Problem:** Acting impulsively without considering consequences.
  **Solution:** Take time to evaluate the potential outcomes of important actions. Speed is valuable, but so is wisdom.
- **Problem:** Using planning and preparation as an excuse for inaction.
  **Solution:** Plan enough to act intelligently, then act. Over-planning is a form of procrastination.

### Lesson 11: How to Turn Defeat into Victory

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Inventor Who Failed 1,000 Times

Thomas Edison failed more than 1,000 times before successfully inventing the light bulb. When asked about his failures, he reframed them: "I have not failed. I have just found 1,000 ways that will not work." Each failure was not an endpoint—it was data that brought him closer to success. Defeat becomes victory when you use it as feedback rather than as a verdict.

##### Example 2: The Business That Survived the Fire

A business owner whose store burned down could have seen it as the end. Instead, he saw it as an opportunity to rebuild bigger and better in a better location. He used the insurance money and the momentum of public sympathy to build a larger, more successful business. Defeat can be a setup for a greater comeback if you choose to see it that way.

##### Example 3: The Salesperson Who Lost a Major Account

A salesperson who lost a major account could have given up. Instead, he analyzed what went wrong, improved his approach, and used the experience to win even larger accounts in the future. The loss became a turning point that led to greater success because he chose to learn from it rather than be defeated by it.

#### Step 1: Redefine Defeat as Temporary

Defeat is only permanent if you accept it as permanent. Reframe every setback as temporary and as an opportunity to learn. Say to yourself: "This is not the end. This is a chapter that teaches me something I need to know for the next chapter."

#### Step 2: Analyze What Went Wrong

When you experience defeat, conduct an objective post-mortem. What factors contributed to the outcome? What could you have done differently? What will you do differently next time? Analysis transforms emotion into strategy. Do not skip this step—it is the bridge between defeat and future victory.

#### Step 3: Extract the Lesson

Every defeat contains a lesson that can make you stronger, smarter, and more resilient. Identify the lesson explicitly: "I learned that [insight]. Next time I will [action]." Writing down the lesson solidifies it and ensures you do not repeat the same mistake.

#### Step 4: Recommit and Take Action

Once you have analyzed the defeat and extracted the lesson, recommit to your goal and take immediate action. Do not wallow in self-pity or replay the defeat in your mind. Redirect your energy into the next attempt with the wisdom you have gained.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Reframe setbacks as temporary rather than permanent
- ✅ Conduct objective analysis of what went wrong
- ✅ Extract explicit lessons from every defeat
- ✅ Recommit quickly and take action on the next attempt
- ✅ Keep a "defeat journal" to track lessons learned
- ❌ Do not allow a single setback to define your future
- ❌ Do not blame others for your defeats—take responsibility
- ❌ Do not repeat the same mistake without adjusting your approach
- ❌ Do not let fear of defeat prevent you from trying again

#### Keep In Mind

Defeat is not the opposite of success—it is part of success. Every successful person has a history of defeats that they transformed into stepping stones. The difference between winners and losers is not that winners never lose—it is that losers stay down after losing, while winners use losing as fuel for the next win.

#### Security & Safety Notes

After a significant defeat, take time to recover emotionally before jumping into the next attempt. Rushing to recover without processing emotions can lead to repeated mistakes. Give yourself permission to feel the loss, then redirect your energy constructively. Do not suppress emotions; channel them into action.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Blaming external forces for defeat instead of learning from it.
  **Solution:** Accept responsibility for what you can control, extract the lesson, and move forward.
- **Problem:** Letting fear of future defeat prevent you from trying again.
  **Solution:** Each attempt is independent of the last. Use the wisdom you gained to improve your next attempt.
- **Problem:** Repeating the same strategy that caused the defeat.
  **Solution:** Analyze honestly, adjust your approach, and try a different strategy next time.

### Lesson 12: Use Goals to Help You Grow

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Man Who Wrote Down His Goals

Schwartz describes a man who was drifting through life without direction. When he began writing down specific, measurable goals for every area of his life—career, finances, health, relationships—and reviewing them daily, his life transformed. Within three years, he had achieved more than he had in the previous ten. Goals gave him direction, focus, and a measuring stick for progress.

##### Example 2: The 13-Week Goal System

A manager used a 13-week goal system: every quarter, he set specific, measurable goals for his department and tracked progress weekly. The system created urgency, focus, and accountability. At the end of each quarter, he reviewed what worked, adjusted his approach, and set new goals. The 13-week cycle turned vague aspirations into concrete achievements.

##### Example 3: The Written Goal That Became Reality

A woman wrote down her goal to earn a specific income within two years and placed the paper where she could see it every day. She used the goal as a filter for every decision: "Does this move me closer to my goal or away from it?" Within the specified time, she had exceeded her income goal. Written goals have a way of becoming reality when they are reviewed and acted upon daily.

#### Step 1: Write Down Your Major Goals

Take time to write down specific, measurable goals for the major areas of your life: career, finances, health, relationships, and personal development. Goals must be written to be real—unwritten goals are merely wishes. Be specific: instead of "I want to earn more money," write "I will earn $X per year by [date] through [specific actions]."

#### Step 2: Visualize Your Goals Daily

Each morning, review your written goals and visualize yourself having already achieved them. See the details, feel the emotions of success, and commit to the actions required to get there. Visualization conditions your subconscious to accept the goal as achievable and to guide you toward it.

#### Step 3: Break Goals into Action Steps

For each major goal, break it down into specific, actionable steps. Then break those steps into weekly and daily actions. A goal without action steps is a fantasy; a goal with action steps is a plan. Schedule your action steps on your calendar and treat them as non-negotiable commitments.

#### Step 4: Review and Adjust Goals Regularly

Review your goals weekly and your major goals monthly. Track your progress, celebrate achievements, and adjust goals as needed. If a goal is no longer relevant or achievable, modify it. If you are ahead of schedule, raise the bar. Goals are not static—they are living documents that evolve as you evolve.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Write all goals in the present tense as if you have already achieved them
- ✅ Review goals daily to keep them top of mind
- ✅ Break major goals into small, weekly action steps
- ✅ Track progress and celebrate milestones
- ✅ Adjust goals as circumstances change
- ❌ Do not set vague or unmeasurable goals
- ❌ Do not set goals without corresponding action plans
- ❌ Do not set so many goals that you lose focus
- ❌ Do not abandon goals when progress slows—adjust your approach instead

#### Keep In Mind

Goals are the bridge between where you are and where you want to be. Without goals, you drift. With goals, you have direction, focus, and a way to measure progress. Goals are not rigid commitments—they are guides that help you allocate your time, energy, and attention toward what matters most.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Goal-setting should be balanced with flexibility. Rigid adherence to goals in the face of changing circumstances can be counterproductive. Set clear goals, but also build in review points where you can adjust based on new information. Goals are tools for direction, not chains that lock you into a single path.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Setting goals without action plans and then being surprised when nothing changes.
  **Solution:** Every goal must have corresponding action steps. If you cannot name the first action, the goal is not ready.
- **Problem:** Setting too many goals and spreading yourself too thin.
  **Solution:** Focus on 3-5 major goals at any given time. Quality of focus beats quantity of goals.
- **Problem:** Abandoning goals when progress is slower than expected.
  **Solution:** Progress is rarely linear. Adjust timelines and strategies, but keep the goal itself.

### Lesson 13: How to Think Like a Leader

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Leader Who Saw Potential

A manager who saw potential in every team member rather than focusing on their current limitations inspired extraordinary performance. By communicating belief in their people, expecting the best from them, and providing opportunities for growth, this leader developed a team of high achievers. Leadership begins with how you think about people.

##### Example 2: The Leader Who Took Responsibility

When things went wrong, a true leader did not blame the team—he looked in the mirror and asked what he could have done differently. This attitude created psychological safety, encouraged honest communication, and built trust. When leaders take responsibility, teams feel safe to take risks and innovate. When leaders blame, teams shrink and protect themselves.

##### Example 3: The Leader Who Communicated a Vision

A leader who could paint a vivid picture of the future inspired people to follow even without a complete roadmap. People do not follow leaders who have all the answers—they follow leaders who give them a reason to believe in the destination. Visionary thinking is the core of leadership.

#### Step 1: Develop a Leadership Mindset

Stop thinking of yourself as a follower and start thinking of yourself as a leader. Leadership is not a title—it is a mindset. A leader thinks about what needs to be done, takes initiative, accepts responsibility, and inspires others to do the same. Begin leading from where you are, regardless of your formal position.

#### Step 2: See Leadership in Others

A leader sees potential in people rather than focusing on their current limitations. Practice looking for the good in every team member and communicating your belief in their ability to grow. Expect the best from people, and they will often rise to meet your expectations.

#### Step 3: Take Responsibility for Outcomes

When things go wrong, look in the mirror first. Ask: "What could I have done differently? How can I help my team succeed?" Responsibility is the foundation of trust. When you take responsibility, you earn the right to lead.

#### Step 4: Communicate a Compelling Vision

Develop the ability to articulate a clear, inspiring vision of the future. Your vision should be specific enough to guide action but big enough to inspire. Communicate it often, in different ways, and connect it to the values and goals of the people you lead.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Take responsibility for outcomes, especially when things go wrong
- ✅ See and communicate the potential in every team member
- ✅ Develop and communicate a clear, compelling vision
- ✅ Lead by example—your actions teach more than your words
- ✅ Listen actively and value input from all levels
- ❌ Do not blame others when things go wrong
- ❌ Do not micromanage—trust your team and give them autonomy
- ❌ Do not communicate only when there is a problem
- ❌ Do not think of leadership as a privilege rather than a responsibility

#### Keep In Mind

Leadership is influence, not authority. You can lead from any position if you think like a leader, take responsibility, and inspire others. The most successful leaders are those who develop more leaders, not more followers. Your legacy as a leader is measured by the people you help grow, not by the titles you accumulate.

#### Security & Safety Notes

Leadership carries ethical responsibility. Do not use influence or authority to manipulate, exploit, or harm others. True leadership is rooted in service, integrity, and genuine concern for the well-being of others. Always align your leadership actions with your values and the best interests of those you lead.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that leadership is about authority rather than influence.
  **Solution:** Influence is earned through trust, respect, and service. Authority is given; leadership is chosen by those who follow.
- **Problem:** Blaming the team when things go wrong.
  **Solution:** The leader takes the first and last responsibility. Protect your team publicly, and address issues privately.
- **Problem:** Failing to communicate vision because it feels abstract or unnecessary.
  **Solution:** People need to know where they are going and why. Vision provides direction and motivation. Communicate it constantly.
