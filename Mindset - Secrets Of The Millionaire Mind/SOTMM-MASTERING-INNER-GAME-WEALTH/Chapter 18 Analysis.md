# Chapter 18 Analysis: Wealth File #6

## Core Intent (1 sentence)
To eliminate resentment toward rich and successful people and replace it with admiration and blessing, because you cannot become what you despise and the mindset you hold toward wealth directly determines your ability to attract it.

## Actionable Lessons
### Lesson 1: Reframe Your Perception of Rich People
- **Step 1:** Recognize when you think or speak negatively about rich people (e.g., calling them "jerks," "lucky," or "greedy").
- **Step 2:** Immediately neutralize the resentment by saying out loud, "Cancel, cancel, thank you for sharing."
- **Step 3:** Reframe the thought into admiration and blessing (e.g., "Way to go! You deserve it.").
- **Best Practices:** Catch the negative energy before it takes over; understand that thoughts are not inherently good or bad, but they are either empowering or disempowering to your success.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Assuming rich people are greedy or bad; believing that someone else's success takes away from your own; letting resentment build unconsciously until it sabotages your opportunities.

### Lesson 2: Practice Admiring and Blessing What You Want
- **Step 1:** Adopt the Huna philosophy: "Bless that which you want."
- **Step 2:** When you see a beautiful home, car, business, or success, consciously bless it and its owner.
- **Step 3:** Write and send a short letter or email to someone you know of who is highly successful, telling them how much you admire and honor their achievements.
- **Best Practices:** Make blessing a daily habit; focus on genuine admiration rather than forced positivity; understand that by blessing the rich, you unconsciously prepare yourself to be welcomed into that world.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Skipping the practice because it feels "hokey"; confusing admiration with envy; only blessing people you know personally instead of extending it to all successful people.

### Lesson 3: Internalize the Correct Declarations
- **Step 1:** Place your hand on your heart and say: "I admire rich people! I bless rich people! I love rich people! And I'm going to be one of those rich people too!"
- **Step 2:** Touch your head and say: "I have a millionaire mind!"
- **Step 3:** Repeat these declarations daily, especially in the morning and evening.
- **Best Practices:** Say them aloud with emotion; look in a mirror to accelerate the process; be consistent.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Skipping declarations because they feel unnatural; saying them without feeling; expecting instant results without practice.

## Key Examples
- The author's Jaguar experience: When driving a clunker, people let him merge; when driving a new black Jaguar, he was cut off, given the finger, and had beer cans thrown into his sunroof with people screaming "You rich bastard!"
- The car keying incident: In a lower-end neighborhood, the author's Jaguar was keyed on the side within ten minutes; when he rented a Ford Escort instead, he had no problems.
- Halle Berry interview: The author caught himself resenting Halle Berry's $20 million contract, immediately reframed it to "Way to go, girl! You rock!" and felt better.
- Russell Conwell's Acres of Diamonds quote: Rich people are generally honest and trustworthy; the idea that rich people are bad is a conditioned myth.
- The wealthy neighborhood experience: The author moved to a rich area expecting snobs, but found the neighbors were warm, generous, and informal, with one family being the single largest donor to the Children's Hospital Fund.
- The "vein doctor": A wealthy doctor who earned $5,000–$10,000 per surgery spent every Tuesday doing free surgeries for people who couldn't pay and led an organization encouraging other doctors to do the same.

## Notes
- The author emphasizes that you don't have to be perfect to get rich; you just need to recognize disempowering thoughts and quickly refocus on supportive ones.
- The chapter ties back to earlier conditioning themes: resentment is a learned behavior from childhood programming that must be unlearned.
- Resentment toward the rich is described as "victim talk" and a primary reason people stay broke.
- The author acknowledges that even he occasionally falls into resentment (the Halle Berry example), proving it's a habit anyone can develop and unlearn.
