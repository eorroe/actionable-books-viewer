# Chapter 12 Analysis: So What Is Your Money Blueprint Set For?

## Core Intent (1 sentence)
The chapter reveals that your financial blueprint functions like a thermostat, determining your permanent financial results, and teaches you to assess your current money blueprint through self-observation and awareness of your financial patterns.

## Actionable Lessons
### Lesson 1: Assess Your Current Money Blueprint
- **Step 1:** Look at your bank account, income, net worth, investment success, and business success
- **Step 2:** Observe whether you are a spender or saver, manage money well or mismanage it, are consistent or inconsistent
- **Step 3:** Notice if money is a struggle or comes to you easily, and whether you jump between jobs or stick with one
- **Step 4:** Identify if you are programmed for a specific income range ($20K-$30K, $40K-$60K, $75K-$100K, etc.)
- **Best Practices:** Use your current results as the clearest indicator of your blueprint; be brutally honest in your assessment
- **Common Pitfalls:** Blaming external factors (company folding, market drying up, partner leaving) instead of recognizing your blueprint at work

### Lesson 2: Understand the Thermostat Principle
- **Step 1:** Recognize that temporary fluctuations in income (a bonus, a downturn, a huge sale) are like opening a window—they will eventually return to your set level
- **Step 2:** Realize that if you are set for $50,000/year, you will get $50,000/year one way or another
- **Step 3:** Understand that developing business knowledge, sales skills, or investment expertise is useless without an inner toolbox big enough to create and hold large amounts of money
- **Best Practices:** Focus on resetting your internal thermostat rather than chasing external strategies alone
- **Common Pitfalls:** Assuming that more tools or better strategies alone will permanently change your financial results

### Lesson 3: Develop Consciousness
- **Step 1:** Put yourself under a microscope—watch your thoughts, fears, beliefs, habits, actions, and inactions
- **Step 2:** Recognize that for the most part, you run on automatic, ruled by past conditioning
- **Step 3:** Understand that you are the recorder, not the recording; the hardware, not the software
- **Step 4:** See your programming as information you received in the past, not who you are today
- **Best Practices:** Observe your thoughts and actions so you can live from true choice in the present moment rather than reacting to past programming
- **Common Pitfalls:** Believing your thoughts are true; being run by old habits without awareness

### Lesson 4: Choose Empowering Thoughts
- **Step 1:** Remember that no thought lives in your head rent-free—each thought is either an investment or a cost
- **Step 2:** Choose thoughts that move you toward happiness and success rather than away from it
- **Step 3:** Release beliefs that are not supportive to wealth and replace them with ones that are
- **Best Practices:** Don't believe every thought you think; entertain only thoughts that empower you
- **Common Pitfalls:** Holding onto disempowering beliefs simply because they feel familiar or have been passed down through generations

## Key Examples
- A well-dressed gentleman earning $500,000/year who was stuck at that level until he reset his blueprint to $2 million, then later to $10 million
- Larry, who has a high-income blueprint but an investment "kiss of death" blueprint—whatever he buys drops like a rock
- Donald Trump, whose financial thermostat is set for billions, not millions, explaining why he rebuilt his wealth after losing it all
- A salesperson set for $50,000/year who made $90,000 one year but had a crummy follow-up year to bring him back to his blueprint
- Rhonda & Bob Baines, who were trapped in debt cycles until they learned how their different money personalities (spender vs. avoider) created conflict, and then stopped blaming each other and started understanding each other

## Notes
- The chapter uses the thermostat analogy extensively: your blueprint maintains a set financial temperature, and external events are like opening a window—they temporarily change the room temperature but the thermostat always brings it back to the set point
- The only way to permanently change your financial temperature is to reset the thermostat
- Your money blueprint affects more than just money—it influences relationship choices (attracting partners who validate your financial comfort zone) and business results
- Wealth Principle stated: "The only way to permanently change the temperature in the room is to reset the thermostat. In the same way, the only way to change your level of financial success 'permanently' is to reset your financial thermostat."
- Consciousness is defined as observing your thoughts and actions so you can live from true choice in the present moment rather than being run by programming from the past
- The chapter includes a declaration: "I observe my thoughts and entertain only those that empower me" and "I have a millionaire mind!"
