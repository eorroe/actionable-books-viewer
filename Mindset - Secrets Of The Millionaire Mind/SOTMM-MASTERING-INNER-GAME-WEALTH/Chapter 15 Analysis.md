# Chapter 15 Analysis: Wealth File #3

## Core Intent (1 sentence)
The chapter teaches that commitment—not mere wanting—is the defining distinction between rich and poor people, and it provides concrete steps to help readers commit unreservedly to wealth while eliminating internal mixed messages and negative beliefs about wealth.

## Actionable Lessons
### Lesson 1: Eliminate mixed messages about wealth
- **Step 1:** List all negative beliefs you have about being rich (e.g., taxes, responsibility, danger, people wanting handouts, losing health or friends).
- **Step 2:** Recognize that these mixed messages confuse the universe (Law of Attraction) and block wealth creation because you send contradictory signals.
- **Step 3:** Replace mixed messages with clarity by affirming your commitment to wealth.
- **Best Practices:** Be specific about negative beliefs; understand that internal conflict between "wanting wealth" and "fearing wealth" prevents results; acknowledge that your subconscious beliefs override your conscious wishes.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Letting the "poor" part of your mind override the "rich" part; assuming that wanting alone is sufficient to create wealth; remaining unconscious of mixed signals.

### Lesson 2: Move from wanting to choosing to committing
- **Step 1:** Acknowledge that "wanting" alone is useless and leads to more wanting (a habit that goes nowhere).
- **Step 2:** Recognize the three levels: "I want to be rich" (passive), "I choose to be rich" (deciding), and "I commit to being rich" (unreserved devotion).
- **Step 3:** Make the decision to commit unreservedly to being rich—do whatever it takes for as long as it takes, with no excuses, ifs, buts, or maybes.
- **Best Practices:** Use the definition of commit: "to devote oneself unreservedly"; adopt the warrior's way: "I will be rich or I will die trying"; understand that once committed, the universe will assist, guide, and even create miracles for you.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Having limits on how much you're willing to do, risk, or sacrifice; confusing "trying" with true commitment; thinking working hard automatically equals commitment.

### Lesson 3: Publicly declare your commitment
- **Step 1:** Write a short paragraph on exactly why creating wealth is important to you (be specific).
- **Step 2:** Find a friend or family member who is willing to support you.
- **Step 3:** Put your hand on your heart, look your partner in the eye, and repeat: "I, [your name], do hereby commit to becoming a millionaire or more by [date]."
- **Step 4:** Have the partner say, "I believe in you," and then respond with "Thank you."
- **Step 5:** Print out your commitment and post it on your wall.
- **Best Practices:** Perform declarations aloud each morning and evening; look into a mirror when doing declarations; touch your head after touching your heart to reinforce the message to your subconscious.
- **Common Pitfalls:** Not doing the exercises; dismissing the declaration process as "weird"; failing to follow through after the declaration; remaining in "I don't need to do any of this weird stuff" mode.

## Key Examples
- **The universe as a mail-order department:** Mixed messages confuse the universe, which sends contradictory opportunities because it cannot determine what you truly want.
- **The three levels of wanting:** "I want to be rich" (passive, goes nowhere), "I choose to be rich" (stronger but not best), and "I commit to being rich" (warrior's way, unreserved).
- **W.H. Murray's commitment passage:** "Until one is committed, there is hesitancy, the chance to draw back, always ineffectiveness... the moment one definitely commits oneself, then providence moves too."
- **Millionaire Mind Intensive audience responses about negatives of being rich:** Responses included fear of failure, fear of people liking you for your money, high taxes, too much work, losing health, criticism from friends/family, handouts, robbery, kidnapping, and overwhelming responsibility.
- **The author's personal story:** Modeling rich people's thinking and committing unreservedly to building a fitness store led to rapid success (ten stores in 2.5 years, sold half the company for $1.6 million).

## Notes
- Commitment means being willing to work 16 hours a day, 7 days a week, sacrifice time with family and friends, and risk all your time, energy, and capital with no guarantee of returns—for as long as it takes.
- Rich people are not smarter, more talented, or luckier; they are simply willing to do whatever it takes to become wealthy.
- The chapter emphasizes that your way has gotten you exactly where you are right now, so if you don't like your current results, you must change your approach.
- Once truly committed, the universe will "bend over backward to support you" by creating unforeseen incidents, meetings, and material assistance.
