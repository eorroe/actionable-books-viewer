# Chapter 25 Analysis: Wealth File #13

## Core Intent (1 sentence)
The chapter teaches readers to shift their financial focus from working income alone to building and tracking net worth, using a four-part equation of income, savings, investments, and simplification.

## Actionable Lessons
### Lesson 1: Understand Net Worth vs. Working Income
- **Step 1:** Recognize that net worth (total assets minus total liabilities) is the true measure of wealth, not working income
- **Step 2:** Learn that net worth is the ultimate measure because owned assets can be liquidated into cash
- **Step 3:** Understand that working income is only one of four factors determining net worth
- **Best Practices:** Ask "What is your net worth?" rather than "How much do you make?"; view working income as money that fills your financial "funnel" for saving and investing
- **Common Pitfalls:** Focusing exclusively on working income; believing a high salary alone creates wealth; confusing income with actual wealth

### Lesson 2: Apply the Four Net Worth Factors
- **Step 1:** Increase your working income (active earnings from jobs or business profits) to fill your financial funnel
- **Step 2:** Build passive income streams (money earned without actively working)
- **Step 3:** Save a decent portion of your income rather than spending it all
- **Step 4:** Learn about investing and make your money grow through investments, or hire excellent investors to invest for you
- **Step 5:** Practice simplification by consciously creating a lifestyle that needs less money, thereby decreasing your cost of living
- **Best Practices:** Focus on all four factors simultaneously (like driving a bus on four wheels); prioritize increasing passive income over time
- **Common Pitfalls:** Spending all money instead of saving; believing investing is only for rich people; allowing expenses to rise proportionally with income (Parkinson's Law); choosing immediate gratification over long-term balance

### Lesson 3: Track Your Net Worth
- **Step 1:** Create a net worth statement by adding the current dollar value of everything you own (assets) and subtracting everything you owe (liabilities)
- **Step 2:** Set a specific net worth objective
- **Step 3:** Make it a policy to know your net worth to the penny
- **Step 4:** Update and track your net worth every ninety days using a simple chart
- **Best Practices:** Use the law of focus—what you track increases; print a free net worth tracking sheet from www.millionairemindbook.com
- **Common Pitfalls:** Not tracking net worth at all; relying on income alone as a measure of financial progress; letting net worth remain unknown

### Lesson 4: Work with a Financial Planner
- **Step 1:** Seek referrals from friends or associates who are happy with their financial planner
- **Step 2:** Choose a planner who works with an array of financial products rather than just one specialty (not just insurance or just mutual funds)
- **Step 3:** Use the planner for tools, software, knowledge, and recommendations
- **Best Practices:** Find a planner from a well-known, reputable company who is highly successful; don't take everything they say as gospel—do your own due diligence
- **Common Pitfalls:** Choosing a planner who only specializes in one area; taking all advice as gospel without independent verification; hiring someone without checking referrals

## Key Examples
- **Country club example:** Wealthy people at country clubs discuss net worth (e.g., "Paul's company just went public; he's worth eight million") rather than working income (e.g., "Joe got a two percent cost-of-living raise")
- **Sue's story:** At 23, Sue bought a home for $300,000, sold it seven years later for over $600,000, moved in with her sister, invested the profit at 10% interest in a secure second mortgage, and became financially free at age 30, working only six months a year and spending the rest in Fiji
- **Bus analogy:** Driving a bus on one wheel (focusing only on working income) results in a slow, bumpy, circular ride, whereas driving on all four wheels (income, savings, investments, simplification) creates a fast, smooth, direct journey to wealth
- **Spenders' mottoes:** People who spend everything live by three mottoes: "It's only money," "What goes around, comes around," and "Sorry, I can't right now. I'm broke."

## Notes
- The chapter emphasizes the "law of focus": what you track increases, and "where attention goes, energy flows and results show"
- Net worth is the basis for the term "millionaire"—not income
- Simplification is described as the "dark horse" of the four factors because few people recognize its importance
- Passive income is mentioned as a key income type that will be discussed in greater detail later
