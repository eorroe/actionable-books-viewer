# Pillar 7: Being a Wish Maker or a Risk Taker
## Analysis of "Building a Millionaire Mindset" by Johnny Wimbrey

---

## Core Thesis

The central question of this pillar is: **Are you a wish maker or a risk taker?** Vision without action is illusion. Wishing for outcomes without participating in the process produces nothing. The pillar illustrates that wealth and success require participation, calculated risk-taking, strategic patience, and the discipline to prepare for both opportunity and disaster.

---

## Actionable Lessons

### Lesson 1: Create Strategic Alliances with Like-Minded People

**Principle:**
No one individual is as powerful as the collective. Surrounding yourself with people on the same path amplifies your capability and resilience.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Actively seek out and connect with individuals who share your goals and mindset
2. Use available platforms (social media, communities, masterminds) to find these connections
3. Document and share your progress publicly to attract like-minded allies
4. Build relationships before you need them, not just during crisis

**Best Practices:**
- Share your journey openly to find your "global brothers and sisters" on the same path
- Tag and engage with others in your community to strengthen bonds
- Invest in relationships that challenge and elevate you

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Going it alone because you think you can do everything yourself
- Only reaching out to people when you are in crisis
- Surrounding yourself with people who do not share your vision

---

### Lesson 2: Always Live Below Your Means

**Principle:**
Multiplying your financial success is never the reason to multiply your lifestyle. True wealth is demonstrated not by consumption, but by preservation and growth.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Distinguish between wants and needs before every purchase
2. If a purchase requires credit and you cannot pay it off immediately, walk away
3. Build a buffer of stored assets specifically for unexpected downturns
4. When disaster hits (not if, but when), access your prepared assets rather than liquidating investments

**Best Practices:**
- Adopt a "future-focused" spending mindset rather than living only for the present moment
- Build reserves that allow you to absorb shocks without being forced into bad decisions
- Think several steps ahead about how today's spending affects tomorrow's resilience

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Using credit cards to fund wants, creating lifestyle inflation
- Assuming good times will last forever ("thinking only about summer during the summer")
- Failing to prepare for the unknown, leaving you vulnerable to economic downturns

---

### Lesson 3: Avoid Permanent Solutions for Temporary Situations

**Principle:**
Do not make irreversible decisions while in the heat of a temporary crisis. Measured responses in distress create long-term winners.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. When a crisis emerges, pause before making any permanent decision
2. Weigh all available options, including waiting and allowing time to pass
3. Assess whether your emotional response is driving a decision that you may later regret
4. Make moves only when it is obvious there is no better option

**Best Practices:**
- Wait it out when you have nothing to lose by waiting and everything to lose by acting rashly
- Communicate honestly as a leader, but do not over-share panic, anger, or frustration
- Remember that being right in the moment is rarely more important than long-term positioning

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Making knee-jerk decisions driven by anger, betrayal, or fear
- Walking away from situations that are temporary but feel permanent in the moment
- Over-sharing negative emotions with your team, causing them to make premature moves without you

---

### Lesson 4: Participate in Your Own Rescue — Buy the Ticket

**Principle:**
You cannot win if you do not play. Vision without participation remains fantasy. The only way to access any reward is to take action and enter the game.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Identify what you say you want
2. Determine what action or risk is required to pursue it
3. Take that action, even if it feels small or uncertain
4. Stop wishing and start participating

**Best Practices:**
- Apply this principle universally — from tangible opportunities (like buying a lottery ticket) to business and life decisions
- Shift your identity from someone who wishes to someone who acts
- Recognize that inaction is itself a decision — and it guarantees no reward

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Wanting outcomes without being willing to pay the entry price
- Spectating life instead of participating in it
- Believing that luck alone will deliver what you have not pursued

---

### Lesson 5: Take Calculated Risks — Your Greatest Regret Will Be Not Taking Them

**Principle:**
The number one regret of elderly people on their deathbeds is that they did not take more risks. Risk aversion becomes a lifestyle, and that lifestyle becomes a deeply regretted identity.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Acknowledge that risk is the price of admission for any meaningful reward
2. Evaluate risks based on long-term upside, not short-term fear
3. Build a lifestyle that normalizes measured risk-taking rather than avoidance
4. Regularly ask yourself: "What am I avoiding out of fear, and what will I regret not doing?"

**Best Practices:**
- Reframe risk as participation rather than gambling
- Make risk-taking habitual so it becomes part of your identity
- Use your stored assets and alliances as a safety net that allows you to take smarter risks

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Playing it safe to the point that your life becomes defined by what you did not attempt
- Avoiding risk because temporary discomfort feels worse than long-term regret
- Letting fear of loss override the greater fear of never trying

---

### Lesson 6: Build Your Faith Muscle for Hard Times

**Principle:**
Faith cannot be conditional to easy moments. It must be exercised in advance so that it is strong enough to withstand the times when you need it most.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Practice faith in yourself, your team, and your vision during calm periods
2. When difficulty arrives, consciously choose to maintain faith rather than abandon it
3. Draw on your belief in your ideas and business when external evidence is discouraging

**Best Practices:**
- Train your faith muscle through daily practice, not crisis reliance
- Anchor your faith in preparation, strategy, and proven principles
- Surround yourself with alliances that reinforce your faith when yours is weak

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Only having faith when things are going well
- Abandoning your vision at the first sign of trouble
- Letting temporary setbacks permanently damage your belief system

---

### Lesson 7: Think in Chess, Not Checkers

**Principle:**
Millionaire mindset requires strategic thinking several moves ahead. Every decision should serve the end result, not just the immediate moment.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Before making any major move, visualize the end state you are trying to reach
2. Build strategic alliances that challenge and elevate you
3. Prioritize strategies that benefit your future over temporary relief or gratification
4. Evaluate each decision by asking: "Does this move me closer to the ultimate outcome?"

**Best Practices:**
- Use long time horizons when assessing the value of decisions
- Choose partners and alliances based on future alignment, not just present convenience
- Accept short-term discomfort when it serves a greater long-term strategy

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Making reactive, short-term decisions that sacrifice long-term positioning
- Choosing checkers-style immediacy over chess-style strategy
- Underestimating the compounding power of aligned, strategic relationships

---

### Lesson 8: Prepare for Setbacks Before They Hit

**Principle:**
You do not want to be improvising when disaster strikes. Preparation transforms catastrophe into a manageable event.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Write about a past moment when you gave up or quit — relive it fully
2. Honestly assess what you could have done differently in that moment
3. Identify specific alternative actions that reflect a wealth mindset
4. Document three past situations where you quit, and for each, write two alternative approaches you could have taken
5. Build a documented plan for how you will respond to future setbacks before they occur

**Best Practices:**
- Be raw and honest in your self-assessment — this is not performance, it is preparation
- Revisit these written plans regularly so they are ready when needed
- Use past pain as a training tool, not just a memory

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Avoiding reflection on past failures because they are painful
- Assuming future setbacks will be easier to handle without preparation
- Believing that discipline in the moment will appear spontaneously without advance practice

---

### Lesson 9: Lead Without Panic

**Principle:**
Honesty as a leader does not require volunteering excess emotion or information that can destabilize your team.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Communicate directly and truthfully with your team during crises
2. Share enough to inform, but withhold panic, anger, and frustration that could cause premature reactions
3. Give your team time to process before they make irreversible decisions
4. Model the measured response you want them to adopt

**Best Practices:**
- Recognize that your emotional state as a leader directly influences team decisions
- Process your own feelings privately before addressing the team
- Create a culture where waiting and thinking is valued over reacting

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Using your team as an emotional outlet during crisis
- Allowing your panic to trigger a cascade of bad decisions among your leaders
- Forgetting that your team is watching you for cues on how to respond

---

### Lesson 10: Build and Deploy a Disaster Response System

**Principle:**
When disaster hits, you must be able to access prepared stored assets, lean on alliances for strategic mental fortitude, and exercise your preparedness — rather than being forced to liquidate and start over.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Build stored assets (financial reserves, skills, relationships) during good times
2. Maintain strategic alliances specifically for crisis support
3. When crisis arrives, activate your prepared response rather than improvising
4. Focus on thriving through preparation, not scrambling to survive

**Best Practices:**
- Prepare during calm periods so disaster does not floor you
- Treat preparation as a continuous habit, not a one-time event
- Use alliances not just for celebration, but for strength during difficulty

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Assuming good times will continue indefinitely
- Building no safety net, leaving you forced into liquidation mode during downturns
- Isolating yourself during hard times instead of leveraging your alliances

---

## Summary

Pillar 7 draws a clear line between those who achieve wealth and those who merely imagine it. The wish maker waits for luck, avoids risk, makes emotional decisions, and is destroyed by setbacks. The risk taker participates fully, builds alliances, lives below their means, thinks strategically, and prepares in advance for both opportunity and disaster. The choice between these two identities is made daily through the small decisions that compound into a lifetime.
