# Pillar 13: "Staying in Position" — Analysis
## Building a Millionaire Mindset (BAMM) by Johnny Wimbrey

---

## Chapter Overview

Pillar 13 focuses on the critical importance of "staying in position" — maintaining your role as a **Participator** rather than slipping into the roles of **Instigator** or **Spectator**. The chapter teaches that your mindset and the people/influences around you either make you millions or cost you millions, and it provides a practical exercise to audit your daily life and identify who and what belongs in your world.

---

## Core Framework: The Three Personality Types

The chapter establishes a three-category model that applies to every area of life and every relationship:

### 1. Instigator
- **Definition**: People who focus on the current reality, point out problems, and linger on issues while putting little effort into solutions.
- **Behavior**: Talk about what's happening, complain, dramatize difficulties, and avoid action.
- **Impact**: Drain energy, derail focus, and keep you anchored to problems rather than solutions.
- **Example behaviors**: Constantly discussing challenges, playing the victim, making excuses.

### 2. Spectator
- **Definition**: People who have no skin in the game but posture as if they do. They watch the game but don't play.
- **Behavior**: Fair-weather friends, observers who are never truly invested, offer opinions without accountability.
- **Impact**: Provide no real support, absence during critical moments, superficial engagement.
- **Example behaviors**: Cheering from the sidelines, giving unsolicited advice, not showing up when it matters.

### 3. Participator
- **Definition**: People who make things happen. A rare breed but extremely valuable to identify.
- **Behavior**: Actively take part in the game, focus on the end result, constantly sharpen skills, prepare to execute.
- **Impact**: Drive progress, create solutions, elevate everyone around them, stay focused regardless of hype.
- **Example behaviors**: Address challenges with solutions, train themselves to ignore distractions, protect their focus and the focus of fellow Participators.

> **Key Insight**: "We are all one of these three personalities in everything we do and/or entertain."

## Actionable Lessons & Principles

---

### Lesson 1: Identify Your Current Category in the Now

**Principle**: You must honestly assess which of the three roles you are currently playing in every area of your life. Self-awareness in the present moment is the prerequisite for change.

**Step-by-Step Instructions**:
1. Pause and reflect on your current daily activities, conversations, and relationships.
2. Ask yourself honestly: Am I instigating problems, spectating from the sidelines, or participating in solutions?
3. Do not rationalize or justify your current behavior.
4. Accept your current category without judgment — awareness is the first step.

**Best Practices**:
- Practice daily self-reflection, especially before starting your day.
- Keep a mental or written log of conversations and activities to track patterns.
- Ask trusted mentors or peers for honest feedback about your behavior.

**Common Pitfalls**:
- **Rationalization**: Convincing yourself that complaining is "just being realistic."
- **Ego protection**: Refusing to see yourself as an Instigator because it threatens your self-image.
- **Passive identification**: Going through life without ever evaluating your role.

---

### Lesson 2: Exercise Immediate Self-Correction

**Principle**: "Applied knowledge is power." Knowing the three categories means nothing unless you act on it. Self-correction the moment you catch yourself slipping into Instigator or Spectator behavior is a definitive sign you are mastering the first-generation millionaire mindset.

**Step-by-Step Instructions**:
1. Notice the shift the moment you catch yourself complaining about a problem without offering a solution.
2. Pause and stop the Instigator or Spectator behavior immediately.
3. Redirect your thoughts and words toward a solution or an actionable next step.
4. If you catch yourself gossiping, criticizing without purpose, or wallowing in drama, consciously pivot.
5. Repeat this process every single time you notice the drift.

**Best Practices**:
- Treat self-correction like a muscle — it gets stronger with repetition.
- Celebrate small wins when you catch and correct yourself; this reinforces the new habit.
- Use a phrase or mantra to snap yourself back (e.g., "Solution, not problem.").

**Common Pitfalls**:
- **Shame spiral**: Beating yourself up for slipping up instead of simply correcting and moving forward.
- **Delayed correction**: Letting negative behavior continue because "I'll fix it later."
- **Inconsistency**: Correcting only when others are watching.

---

### Lesson 3: Distance Yourself from Instigators and Spectators

**Principle**: You will never learn to value yourself and your efforts if you let Instigators and Spectators infect your life, goals, and mindset. It is your responsibility to determine who is a friend to your goals and who is a foe — and then definitively distance yourself from the foes.

**Step-by-Step Instructions**:
1. Audit the people in your inner circle (see Lesson 4 for the full audit process).
2. Identify which people consistently drain energy, create drama, or fail to support your goals.
3. Have the courage to limit or eliminate contact with those who are not Participators in your journey.
4. Do not feel guilty — protecting your goals is not an act of cruelty; it is an act of discipline.
5. Seek out and surround yourself with people who are actively playing the game at the level you aspire to reach.

**Best Practices**:
- You do not need to announce your distance — gradual disengagement is often more effective and less dramatic.
- Replace time spent with energy-draining people with time spent with Participators or focused solo work.
- Remember: "I am giving you permission to take control of that focus. You need it if you are going to become a first-generation millionaire."

**Common Pitfalls**:
- **Guilt-based loyalty**: Staying in toxic relationships out of obligation to family, old friends, or shared history.
- **Isolation fear**: Worrying that cutting people off will leave you lonely, rather than opening space for better connections.
- **Hope-based retention**: Keeping someone around because you hope they will change, rather than accepting who they are today.

---

### Lesson 4: The Daily Life Audit — Categorize Everything

**Principle**: Every person, activity, conversation, and environment you engage with on a daily basis is either an Instigator, Spectator, or Participator in your goal of becoming a first-generation millionaire. You must audit your entire life and categorize accordingly.

**Step-by-Step Instructions**:
1. **Make a comprehensive list** of everything you do, everyone you talk to, everywhere you go, and everything you touch on a daily basis.
2. **Ask hard questions** about each item:
   - Am I constantly covering for someone else's mistakes?
   - Am I picking up work I don't need to be doing?
   - Is my socializing getting in the way of my productivity?
   - Am I wasting time being busy but not productive?
3. **Categorize each item** as Instigator, Spectator, or Participator in relation to your millionaire goal.
4. **Identify patterns**: Notice which categories dominate your days.
5. **Take action**: Begin eliminating, reducing, or replacing Instigators and Spectators. Increase and protect time with Participators.

**Best Practices**:
- Be ruthlessly honest. No item is too small to evaluate.
- Include both people AND activities (e.g., scrolling social media mindlessly is a Spectator activity).
- Revisit this audit regularly — your categories may shift over time.
- Work on putting anything NEW in your life into one of these three categories before allowing it in.

**Common Pitfalls**:
- **Incomplete audit**: Forgetting to include passive activities like TV, social media, or commuting habits.
- **Soft categorization**: Giving someone the benefit of the doubt when their track record clearly shows Instigator or Spectator patterns.
- **One-time exercise**: Doing the audit once and never revisiting it as your life and goals evolve.

---

### Lesson 5: Develop Instant Recognition of Categories

**Principle**: With practice, you can develop the ability to instantly recognize Instigators, Spectators, and Participators "right off the bat" and react accordingly. This instinct becomes a millionaire mindset muscle.

**Step-by-Step Instructions**:
1. After completing your initial audit, actively practice categorizing new people and situations as you encounter them.
2. When meeting someone new or entering a new environment, observe quickly: What is their energy? What do they talk about? Do they focus on problems or solutions? Do they have skin in the game?
3. Adjust your engagement level immediately based on your assessment.
4. Over time, this categorization becomes automatic and instantaneous.

**Best Practices**:
- Trust your gut — if someone feels off or draining, they probably are.
- Look for action, not words. Participators show up; Spectators talk about showing up.
- Use first meetings, group dynamics, and social media behavior as testing grounds for your assessment skills.

**Common Pitfalls**:
- **Snap judgments**: Dismissing someone too quickly without giving them a chance to show their true nature.
- **Confirmation bias**: Seeing only what confirms your initial impression and ignoring contradictory evidence.
- **Over-reliance on labels**: Forgetting that people can evolve — a current Instigator may become a Participator with the right environment.

---

### Lesson 6: Trim the Fat

**Principle**: "Trimming the fat" means cutting off and discarding anything that is hindering you from reaching your goal. Becoming a first-generation millionaire is your "Prime Grade A Meat," and anything attached to you that is holding you back is your fat.

**Step-by-Step Instructions**:
1. After your audit, identify all the "fat" — the people, habits, environments, and commitments that are not serving your goal.
2. Create a "trimming list" with specific items to cut, reduce, or replace.
3. Develop an execution plan:
   - What will you eliminate entirely?
   - What will you reduce?
   - What will you replace with Participator-level inputs?
4. Execute the trim. Do not look back.
5. Build the habit of continuous trimming — as you grow, your old attachments may no longer fit.

**Best Practices**:
- Start with the easiest cuts to build momentum (e.g., canceling a low-value weekly commitment).
- Move to harder cuts (e.g., difficult conversations with people you care about).
- Replace trimmed items with high-value alternatives to avoid leaving a void.
- Visualize the "meat" (your millionaire future) to stay motivated during the trimming process.

**Common Pitfalls**:
- **Attachment to comfort**: Keeping things that feel familiar even when they are toxic.
- **All-at-once approach**: Trying to cut everything simultaneously and burning out or triggering an emotional crisis.
- **Guilt**: Feeling bad about "abandoning" people or habits, even when they are clearly holding you back.

---

### Lesson 7: The Mindset Either/Or Truth

**Principle**: "From this day forward your mindset is either making you millions or it is costing you millions. There is no in-between." Adopting this binary view of your mindset is a game-changer.

**Step-by-Step Instructions**:
1. Internalize the truth that there is no neutral ground in mindset. Every thought, habit, and association either moves you toward or away from your goal.
2. When evaluating any decision, relationship, or activity, ask: "Is this making me millions or costing me millions?"
3. Use this filter as a decision-making compass.
4. Hold yourself accountable to this standard without exception.

**Best Practices**:
- Write this truth down and place it somewhere visible daily.
- Apply it to both major decisions (career moves, investments) and small daily choices (how you spend 30 minutes of free time).
- Share this principle with accountability partners to reinforce it.

**Common Pitfalls**:
- **Gray-area thinking**: Believing that "neutral" activities are harmless — they are not, because they consume time and attention that could be invested in Participator activities.
- **Exception-making**: Justifying Instigator or Spectator behaviors as "stress relief" or "balance" when they are actually sabotaging your progress.
- **Short-term thinking**: Focusing on immediate comfort rather than long-term millionaire outcomes.

---

### Lesson 8: Be a Participator in Your Own Rescue

**Principle**: You can eliminate people, behaviors, and mentalities out of your life all you want, but all the self-improvement work you've done doesn't mean anything if you throw it all away in an instant. You must be a friend to your own future self.

**Step-by-Step Instructions**:
1. Visualize your future self — the millionaire version of you with the house, car, financial freedom, and family security you desire.
2. Make decisions today as if you are already that person. Comport yourself as if you have everything to lose — because you do.
3. Before reacting to any situation, ask: "How would my future self handle this?"
4. Protect your future self from your present impulses — whether that means controlling your temper, resisting shortcuts, or avoiding toxic environments.
5. Understand that your actions now directly determine your future outcomes.

**Best Practices**:
- Create a vivid future-self vision and review it regularly.
- Before high-stakes interactions (meetings, difficult conversations), mentally step into your future-self persona.
- Practice the pause — between stimulus and response, there is a space. Use that space to choose a Participator response.

**Common Pitfalls**:
- **Emotional sabotage**: Letting anger, frustration, or ego cause you to act in ways that damage your future (e.g., slapping back, starting fights, burning bridges).
- **Present bias**: Prioritizing immediate gratification or emotional release over long-term gain.
- **Forgetting the vision**: Under stress, reverting to old Instigator/Spectator patterns because the future-self vision feels too distant.

## Central Chapter Exercise

The chapter includes a companion workbook exercise (referred to as "Pillar 11" in the text, likely a numbering inconsistency) titled **"Determining Your Instigators, Spectators, and Participators: Inventory Your Influences"**.

### Exercise: The Five People Inventory

**Instructions**:
1. List five of the closest and most important people in your life.
2. For each person, describe:
   - Their name
   - Their personality type (Instigator, Spectator, or Participator)
   - How they influence your goals (positively or negatively)
3. After completing the list, reflect on the overall composition of your inner circle.
4. Take action based on your findings — strengthen relationships with Participators and create distance from Instigators and Spectators.

**Why This Exercise Matters**:
> "Your ability or inability to immediately effectively identify Participators can make you or cost you millions."

The exercise forces you to confront the uncomfortable reality of who is truly in your corner and who may be silently undermining your success.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Applied knowledge is power** — knowing the three categories means nothing without immediate, consistent self-correction.
2. **There is no middle ground** — your mindset is either making you millions or costing you millions.
3. **You have permission to take control of your focus** — protecting your goal is not selfish, it is necessary.
4. **Self-correction in the moment is the hallmark of mastery** — the faster you catch yourself drifting into Instigator or Spectator mode, the faster you are mastering the millionaire mindset.
5. **Your inner circle determines your outcome** — audit, categorize, and curate your influences relentlessly.
6. **Trimming the fat is non-negotiable** — anything that hinders you from your "Prime Grade A Meat" (first-generation millionaire status) must go.
7. **Be a friend to your future self** — every present action either builds or destroys your future. Choose accordingly.
