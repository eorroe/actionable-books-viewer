# Pillar 1 Analysis: "Participating in Your Own Rescue"
## Building a Millionaire Mindset by Johnny Wimbrey

---

## Overview

This chapter centers on the **core principle that you must take initiative for your own success** rather than waiting for circumstances, people, or luck to rescue you. The primary vehicle for this principle is the strategic acquisition of mentors — people who represent the life you want and can guide you there faster. The chapter blends personal narrative (Johnny Wimbrey's experience securing Les Brown as a mentor) with direct actionable guidance on how to find, approach, and cultivate mentor relationships.

---

## Core Principle: Participate in Your Own Rescue

### Summary
Success is not something that happens to you; it is something you actively pursue. Waiting for someone to notice you, hand you an opportunity, or "take you under their wing" is a losing strategy. You must be the architect of your own advancement.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Stop waiting.** Acknowledge that no one is coming to save you. If you only submit to your own thinking, your own way of doing things, and your own worldview, you will never grow.
2. **Identify what you want.** Be crystal clear about the lifestyle, income level, or status you desire.
3. **Find someone who already has it.** Seek out a person whose life represents exactly what you value.
4. **Get access to that person's thinking and lifestyle.** This may require investment of time, money, ego, and comfort.
5. **Act with urgency.** When opportunity presents itself (like Les Brown's $5,000 buy-in challenge), move immediately without overthinking.

### Best Practices
- **Choose the mentor before they choose you.** Never wait to be selected. You pick the mentor based on their proven results, not their reputation alone.
- **Look for proven success, not just credentials.** Wimbrey explicitly states: "I don't follow people who just sound good. I don't want a mentor who looks good on paper. I only look up to people who have proven success."
- **Get close to their voice.** The goal is proximity to excellence — absorbing their way of thinking, decision-making, and operating.
- **Embrace humility over pride.** Wimbrey had to swallow his pride and ask for money he never thought he'd ask for, destroying his personal "trophy" of self-accomplishment to reach a greater one.

### Common Pitfalls
- **The lone-ranger mindset:** Believing you can and should do everything alone. Wimbrey admits he wasted tens of thousands of dollars and years of effort trying to publish his first book alone, when working with a mentor could have accelerated his results.
- **Waiting to be chosen:** Thinking a mentor will discover you. This is passive and will not happen.
- **Asking "What if" from a place of fear:** A whiner asks, "What if they ignore me? What if I embarrass myself?" A winner asks, "What if I can? What if they answer me? What if it works?"
- **Choosing a mentor who is intimidated by your success:** Avoid mentors who will hold you down or make you feel less than your true value.

## Actionable Lesson 1: Choose the Right Mentor

### Summary
Not everyone who is successful is a good mentor. The right mentor has the lifestyle you desire, genuinely wants you to win, supports your surpassing them, and is firm yet fair.

### The Four Essential Qualities of a Mentor
1. **They have a lifestyle you desire and respect.**
2. **They have a genuine desire to see you win.**
3. **They are supportive of your surpassing them in business** and potentially no longer needing their mentorship.
4. **They are firm, yet fair and friendly.**

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Make a list of 10 people — local or international, in your field or not — whose lifestyles you desire.
2. Narrow it to the top 3 you want to contact first.
3. Research them thoroughly: read everything they've written, listen to every podcast, watch every video.
4. Evaluate them against the four qualities above.
5. Choose the person who aligns with your values and goals.

### Best Practices
- **Anyone can be your mentor.** You don't need a "titan of industry" or celebrity. A mentor can be an esteemed coworker, a friend you look up to, a trusted teacher, or an older family member.
- **True mentors accelerate you.** Their mission should be to help you avoid the pitfalls they experienced and skip the mistakes that waste time and energy.
- **Look for selflessness, benevolence, and empathy.** Avoid mentors who want you to "pay your dues" by going through everything they went through. The point of mentorship is to shortcut that pain.
- **A true mentor will be happy when you surpass them.** This is a critical test of their character.

### Common Pitfalls
- **Choosing someone who only looks good on paper.** Credentials without proven results are insufficient.
- **Selecting a mentor who wants to keep you dependent.** If they are threatened by your potential success, they are not the right mentor.
- **Thinking you must meet in person.** Holton Buggs never met Bill Britt in person, yet Bill mentored him through books, seminars, and philosophy. Remote mentorship is fully valid.

## Actionable Lesson 2: How to Approach a Potential Mentor

### Summary
Once you have identified your desired mentor, you must actively and strategically pursue access to them. This requires offering value, demonstrating seriousness, and being willing to serve without immediate compensation.

### Four Strategies for Approaching a Mentor

#### Strategy 1: Offer to Serve and Volunteer
- **What to do:** Offer to help in any capacity — airport pickups, carrying luggage, providing personal security, event setup, etc.
- **How to do it well:** Keep your eyes and ears open 100% of the time. Only speak when spoken to. Articulate your reasons for wanting to be close and soak up wisdom — this shows transparency, which builds trust.
- **Key insight:** "You'll be amazed by how far volunteering can get you. And when the right door opens, it's because there is something about you that your mentor likes."

#### Strategy 2: Become Beneficial to Their Mission
- **What to do:** Offer your personal expertise without compensation or hidden motive.
- **How to do it well:** A true mentor will recognize your sincerity. There is no need to explain your motives unless asked.
- **Key insight:** You are not trading favors; you are demonstrating value and building a relationship.

#### Strategy 3: Communicate Their Impact on Your Life
- **What to do:** Let the mentor know how their work has personally affected you.
- **How to do it well:** With confidence and transparency, express your desire to be an ambassador of their cause or mission. Know their works, publications, accomplishments, and achievements inside and out before you approach them.

#### Strategy 4: Anticipate Their Needs
- **What to do:** Observe and prepare to meet needs before the mentor asks.
- **How to do it well:** Wimbrey shares that when his mentors coughed or licked their lips, he was already prepared with water, a cough drop, or a tissue. Be ready at all times; you never know when it will be your moment.
- **Key quote:** "Stay ready, so you never have to get ready."

### Step-by-Step Instructions for First Contact
1. Prepare your research. Know their work deeply.
2. Reach out through multiple channels: email, social media messages, phone calls, in-person visits to their office if possible.
3. Lead with service, not requests. Offer value first.
4. If they say they cannot take on a mentee, respond with grace: ask to be added to the top of their list for future opportunities, regardless of time, distance, or convenience.
5. Make a verbal confession of your burning desire to be close to greatness, e.g., "My desire is not to be seen or heard. My desire is to serve so as to be close to your wisdom."

### Best Practices
- **Be relentless.** Wimbrey called Les Brown constantly for two years, asking for advice, asking to meet, asking what he could do to help. Persistence pays.
- **Demonstrate your story.** When you finally get time with them, have a compelling narrative of transformation ready. Wimbrey shared his journey from a teenager with a felony arrest to a top insurance sales rep in under five years.
- **Be prepared to invest financially when the opportunity arises.** Les Brown asked Wimbrey for $5,000 (a reduced test amount from $20,000) on the spot. Wimbrey didn't hesitate.
- **Accept the test.** When a mentor presents a challenge or buy-in, recognize it as a test of your seriousness, not a transaction.

### Common Pitfalls
- **Asking before offering.** Leading with "Will you mentor me?" before demonstrating your value or commitment is ineffective.
- **Being passive after initial outreach.** One email is rarely enough. Consistent, respectful follow-up is necessary.
- **Letting pride block opportunities.** Wimbrey hated asking for money but conquered his ego because the opportunity was more important than his vanity.
- **Giving up after a "no."** If they can't take you on now, position yourself for future possibility.

## Actionable Lesson 3: Invest Without Guarantee of Return

### Summary
Access to mentors often requires financial investment — sometimes significant — with no guaranteed return. This is the price of admission to greatness.

### Wimbrey's Case Study
- Les Brown's full buy-in was $20,000.
- Wimbrey was offered a reduced challenge: $5,000, to be delivered that night, to go onstage with Les Brown.
- Wimbrey didn't have the money. He had to make two phone calls.
- Within minutes, two different people (his stepmother Joyce and his friend Tony Hobbs) provided the full $5,000.
- The lesson: When you are committed to your own rescue, resources and people will appear.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. When a genuine opportunity to access your mentor arises, do not hesitate due to cost.
2. Put your vanity aside and reach out to your network.
3. Be transparent about why the opportunity matters.
4. Move with urgency — Wimbrey needed the money in "the next few hours."

### Best Practices
- **Conquer your ego.** Wimbrey's personal trophy was never having to ask anyone for anything as an adult. Destroying that trophy led to a far greater reward.
- **Your network will show up when you are committed.** People want to support genuine ambition and transformation.
- **Treat financial asks as investments in your future self.** The $5,000 was not an expense; it was a seed that grew into a lifetime of mentorship and transformation.

### Common Pitfalls
- **Letting pride become a barrier.** Wimbrey explicitly says, "If I had let my pride get in the way, there is no way I would have found the money I needed. There is no way I would have become what I am today."
- **Waiting until you can afford it.** You may never have the money if you wait. You must ask, borrow, or find a way.
- **Viewing the cost as a risk rather than an investment.** The real risk is staying where you are.

## Actionable Lesson 4: Remote Mentorship Is Real Mentorship

### Summary
You do not need personal, one-on-one access to a mentor to benefit from them. You can be mentored from a distance through their public content, philosophy, and published works.

### Holton Buggs Case Study
- Holton decided Bill Britt was his mentor after hearing him speak at a seminar.
- Over the years, he never met Bill in person or shook his hand.
- He listened to every word Bill said publicly and followed every piece of advice.
- Bill never knew who Holton was, yet Bill mentored him. Holton gained confidence, learned to speak in terms of goals, and transformed his life.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Identify a mentor whose philosophy resonates with you.
2. Consume all their content: books, podcasts, newsletters, videos, speeches.
3. Adopt their philosophy as your own. Act on their advice even without personal contact.
4. Treat their principles as if they were spoken directly to you.

### Best Practices
- **Be a "professional stalker" in the positive sense.** Follow them on every platform. Absorb everything.
- **Apply what you learn.** Mentorship is not passive consumption — it is active implementation.
- **Let their mindset become your mindset.** If you follow and adopt their philosophy, they have mentored you regardless of whether they know your name.

### Common Pitfalls
- **Thinking you need permission.** You do not need a formal mentorship agreement to be mentored by someone's wisdom.
- **Consuming without applying.** Reading or listening without taking action is not mentorship — it is entertainment.
- **Using distance as an excuse.** If you cannot access them personally, use their public work as your classroom.

## Actionable Lesson 5: Resist the Lone-Ranger Mindset

### Summary
The belief that you must do everything alone is a trap that wastes time, money, and energy. Winners surround themselves with thinkers who outthink them. Whiners insist on going it alone.

### Wimbrey's First Book Experience
- Wimbrey spent tens of thousands of dollars on printers, distribution, and storage for his first book.
- He navigated the publishing world alone and took full responsibility.
- The book became his bestseller (250,000+ copies), but he acknowledges that resisting mentorship cost him dearly.
- Now he passes those lessons forward by creating a system where up-and-coming speakers write chapters in a book he spearheads, markets, and publishes — so they don't have to repeat his mistakes.

### What a Good Mentor Does
- Shows you the right path.
- Makes sure you learn from their mistakes.
- Accelerates you while keeping you safe.
- Gifts you with their experience.
- Shows you the best way forward.
- Wants more for you than what they have achieved.

### What a Bad Mentor Does
- Wants you to pay your dues by going through everything they went through.
- Is intimidated by your rise.
- Holds you down rather than lifting you up.
- Punishes you or rubs your failures in your face.
- Makes you feel less than your true value.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Audit your current mindset: Are you trying to do everything alone?
2. Identify areas where a mentor could shortcut your learning curve.
3. Seek out people who have already solved the problems you are facing.
4. Be open to other possibilities, other paths, and other points of view.
5. Build the relationship from the ground up.

### Best Practices
- **Build relationships that last a lifetime.** Once you find a great mentor, nurture that connection.
- **Look for selflessness.** A mentor should want to spare you pain, not inflict it.
- **Be open to different paths.** If you think there is only one way to become a first-generation millionaire, you will fail. A mentor expands your vision.

### Common Pitfalls
- **The "I am my own person" lie.** Wimbrey calls this whiner talk masquerading as strength. True strength is knowing when to ask for guidance.
- **Thinking you don't need anyone.** This mindset guarantees failure. "Without a mentor, you will never have the mindset of a winner. You will never achieve millionaire status."
- **Being too proud to serve.** You must be willing to start at the bottom to get to the top.

## Actionable Lesson 6: Commit Fully or Step Aside

### Summary
Finding a mentor is not for the faint of heart. It requires tenacity, commitment, and the willingness to leave your comfort zone permanently.

### Direct Challenge from the Author
> "Do you have the tenacity and commitment to find someone you deserve? I never said this was going to be easy. Not everyone has the stomach to leave their comfort zone. If this is not you, then give this book to someone else."

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Honestly assess your commitment level.
2. If you are not willing to be relentless, humble, and persistent, acknowledge that this path is not for you.
3. If you are committed, go all in. This is a hard journey.
4. Choose a mentor and then "put in that whole dollar" — give 100% effort to the relationship.

### Best Practices
- **Go all in.** Half-hearted effort yields half-hearted results.
- **Embrace the discomfort.** Leaving your comfort zone is not optional; it is required.
- **Understand the return on investment.** Wimbrey guarantees you will be "pleasantly surprised by your return on that investment" — referencing his "whole dollar" philosophy of giving 100%.

### Common Pitfalls
- **Dabbling in mentorship.** Casual interest does not produce transformative results.
- **Expecting ease.** This journey is explicitly described as hard.
- **Staying in the comfort zone.** Comfort is the enemy of growth.

## Actionable Lesson 7: Build a Network of Influence

### Summary
Success is contagious. By surrounding yourself with people who have already achieved what you want, you exponentially accelerate your own path.

### The "Professional Stalker" Assignment
1. Find 10 people on social media who are doing exactly what you are doing — locally or internationally.
2. Follow all of them.
3. Read everything they have written. Listen to every podcast. Watch every video.
4. Contact and connect with them. Mark the date in the "Connected" column only after you are actually in touch.
5. Offer value or services instantly: airport shuttles, carrying bags, serving in any capacity.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Open your social media platforms.
2. Search for people in your industry or with the lifestyle you desire.
3. Curate a list of 10.
4. Deep-research the top 3.
5. Contact the top 3 with service offers, not requests.
6. Move to the next 3, and continue cycling through.

### Best Practices
- **Be proactive with outreach.** Email, message, call, and even stop by their offices.
- **Offer service before asking for anything.** The most respectful and beneficial approach is to offer immediate value.
- **Be relentless.** Wimbrey's two-year campaign to connect with Les Brown exemplifies persistence.
- **Share your progress publicly.** Tag your connections, use hashtags (#BAMM, #WIMBREY, #CoachJohnny), and cheer on others. Community accelerates growth.

### Common Pitfalls
- **Contacting without research.** Approaching a potential mentor without knowing their work is disrespectful and ineffective.
- **Asking for mentorship immediately.** Build the relationship through service first.
- **Keeping your goals private.** Sharing your journey publicly creates accountability and attracts opportunities.
