## 1. The Principle: Get Off Your "But"

### Core Teaching
Stop allowing excuses and self-imposed limitations to block your path to success. The word "but" is a dream-killer.

### Johnny's Personal Example
- He declared he wanted to be an international speaker
- Opportunity came: a 10-minute showcase in London, requiring him to pay his own travel and hotel
- Everyone advised against it (Les Brown, his wife, financial concerns)
- He went anyway, traveled standby, stayed in the cheapest hotel
- He got only 3.5 minutes on stage (speaker before him went over time)
- He forgot to pitch his products during the speech, but sold double what he expected afterward
- This opened doors that led to keynote speaking, TV interviews, and a business venture worth $100 million

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. State your goal publicly to create accountability
2. When opportunity arises, even if imperfect, seize it
3. Ignore advice that tells you to play it safe when it contradicts your mission
4. Stay prepared and in position for your opportunity
5. Kick the door down when it cracks open

### Best Practices
- Make your goals known to create social accountability
- Focus on serving the audience/people first; the money often follows naturally
- Be willing to invest in yourself even when money is tight
- Travel light and humble when entering a new market (standby travel, cheap hotels)

### Common Pitfalls
- Listening to well-meaning advisors who anchor you to current reality
- Waiting for perfect conditions
- Letting financial constraints prevent you from seizing opportunity
- Forgetting your own agenda while serving others (Johnny forgot to sell his products during the speech)

## 2. Give Yourself Permission to Be the First

### Core Teaching
Don't wait for someone else to break the barrier first. Being the first in your family or community to achieve something creates a new path for generations to follow.

### Roger Bannister Analogy
- First person to run a sub-4-minute mile in 1954
- Doctors warned it could kill athletes (heart explosion, injuries)
- His record was broken 46 days later
- Within a few years, hundreds broke it; now thousands have
- One man's breakthrough gave permission to everyone else

### Johnny's Application
- First-generation millionaire with no family examples to follow
- No evidence to support that he could do it
- He created a new standard for his family name
- He believes his success will lead to hundreds, then thousands in his family achieving more

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Identify a barrier that no one in your family/community has broken
2. Give yourself explicit permission to be the first
3. Stop waiting for role models to appear
4. Create evidence through your own action
5. Your breakthrough becomes permission for others

### Best Practices
- Use the success of others as motivation, not as a ceiling
- Recognize that "impossible" is often just a current belief, not a truth
- Understand that being first creates a legacy that outlives you

### Common Pitfalls
- Waiting for someone else to prove it's possible first
- Using lack of family precedent as an excuse
- Believing statistics and experts who say something can't be done
- Arguing for your limitations instead of your potential

## 3. The Slingshot Principle: Participating in Your Own Rescue

### Core Teaching
Escaping a difficult situation requires extraordinary effort—not just climbing out, but launching yourself far beyond the hole.

### Johnny's Background
- Homeless shelter as a child (denied milk - first memory)
- Failed second grade
- Abusive alcoholic father
- Mother left him
- Felony arrest in high school
- Statistics said he should be in prison, dead, or in a gang
- Became a first-generation millionaire in less than a decade

### The Slingshot Metaphor
- Pulling back a slingshot creates friction and adversity
- You must pull against gravity and the object's natural comfort zone
- It fights you every step of the way
- Pull it back to just before it breaks
- When released, there's no friction, nothing holding it back
- The farther back you pull, the farther forward it flies

### Key Quote
> "I couldn't just climb out though. Climbing out would only bring me to the edge of the hole. No, I had to slingshot myself out. I needed to pour on 10 times the effort—or 100 times the effort, or 1,000—whatever it took—to get me out and as far away from that hole as possible."

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Acknowledge the depth of your current situation
2. Accept that climbing out is not enough—you must launch forward
3. Pull back deliberately (create friction/adversity through hard work, study, discipline)
4. Pull as far back as possible without breaking (push yourself to your limits)
5. Release with full force toward your new goal
6. Continue applying extraordinary effort until you are far beyond your starting point

### Best Practices
- Recognize that your current circumstances are not your fault, but your response is your responsibility
- Understand that extraordinary results require extraordinary effort
- Embrace the friction and adversity as necessary components of the launch
- Don't stop at "normal"—use your comeback to create exceptional results

### Common Pitfalls
- Thinking that just escaping a bad situation is success
- Stopping effort once you reach "normal" or "stable"
- Blaming external circumstances for your current position
- Underestimating how much effort is needed to truly transcend your past

## 4. Discover and Live Your WHY

### Core Teaching
Your driving motivation (your WHY) is the foundation of all your actions and will outlive you. It determines what kind of problem-solver you become.

### The Formula
> Problem + solution = opportunity. But if you can invent new paths for forward before there's urgency, you'll uncover greatness. The nature of your motivation determines which kind of problem solver you'll become.

### Johnny's WHY
> "My WHY is to empower every one of you to reach your full potential. I want you to experience greatness. This reason—this mission—is going to outlive me."

### Action Steps Provided

2. Answer honestly: Why did you make it to the end?
3. Answer honestly: Why are you allowing me to be your coach?
4. Answer honestly: Why do you want to be wealthy?
5. Answer honestly: Why do you want to take your life to the next level?
6. Write down your answers
7. Take a picture with the book and your filled-in answers
8. Tag @wimbrey on Instagram, Twitter, Facebook, and LinkedIn
9. Share with hashtags: #BAMM #WIMBREY #CoachJohnny

### Best Practices
- Be brutally honest with yourself
- Your WHY must be bigger than temporary motivation
- Write it down—don't just think it
- Understand that your WHY will outlive you; it's a legacy
- Return to your WHY when motivation wanes

- Having a superficial WHY (money, status) without deeper purpose
- Not writing it down
- Allowing others to define your motivation
- Forgetting your WHY when challenges arise

## 5. Eliminate Excuses and Argue for Your Possibilities

### Core Teaching
Every excuse you argue for becomes a limitation you get to keep. Stop arguing for your limitations.

### Les Brown's Lesson to Johnny
> "Johnny, if you continue to argue for your limitations, I'm going to allow you to keep them, and our relationship is over."

### Common Excuses Clients Give
- "I'm too young"
- "I'm too old"
- "I'm from the wrong side of the tracks"

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. List every excuse you've been using for why you can't succeed
2. For each excuse, recognize that you are arguing for a limitation
3. Stop defending your limitations
4. Replace each excuse with a possibility statement
5. Accept that climbing up the ladder (point-of-no-return mindset) is the only way

### Best Practices
- Recognize excuses as self-imposed prisons
- Use others' testimonies as proof that your excuses are false
- Understand that "point-of-no-return" means there's no going back to old ways
- Every excuse you eliminate is a door you open

### Common Pitfalls
- Having ready-made excuses for every potential failure
- Using circumstances as identity ("I'm a victim of...")
- Arguing fiercely for why you can't do something
- Waiting for someone else to validate your potential

## 6. Success is a Direction, Not a Destination

### Core Teaching
Becoming a millionaire is not a trophy to put on a shelf. It's a direction you must maintain forever. You are either progressing or regressing—there is no standing still.

### Johnny's Perspective
- First million was not the goal; it was a milestone on the way
- Father was a trash man
- Grandfather spent life on the run after killing a white man trying to kill him
- Ancestors were slaves
- Only a few generations out of slavery, now a multimillionaire
- His children (Psalms, Hannah, Honor) will be more successful than he is

### The Choice
> "You cannot stand still, no matter what. You are either progressing or regressing. There is no in-between. If you stand still while the world moves around you, you will only move backward."

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Define success as a continuous direction, not a finish line
2. Set new goals immediately after achieving the current one
3. Never rest on past accomplishments
4. Use each success as a stop on the way to something greater
5. Return to the book's lessons regularly to set and reach new goals
6. Remember that your potential is unlimited

### Best Practices
- Set goals that are bigger than your current achievements
- Celebrate wins briefly, then set new targets
- Teach your children to continue the trajectory
- Keep returning to foundational principles (the pillars)

### Common Pitfalls
- Achieving a goal and then coasting
- Believing you've "made it" and stopping the work
- Allowing your children to start from where you finished instead of where you started
- Forgetting that standing still means moving backward in a progressing world

## 7. Accept Full Responsibility: Participate in Your Own Rescue

### Core Teaching
You are the only one who can save yourself. Others may complement your success, but you must put in more energy, time, and effort than anyone else.

### Johnny's Breakdown of Control
- The abusive father wasn't his fault
- The shelter wasn't his fault
- The unstable mother wasn't his fault
- But he had control over:
  - Smoking weed and drinking alcohol as a child
  - Where and with whom he spent time in middle school and high school
  - The choices that led to his arrest
- He participated in his own downfall, so he had to be the one to get himself out

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Separate what was out of your control from what was in your control
2. Acknowledge your role in your current situation
3. Accept that no one is coming to rescue you
4. Commit to putting in more energy, time, and effort than anyone else
5. Take full ownership of your exit strategy
6. Understand that only you can stop yourself, and only you can make it happen

### Best Practices
- Stop blaming external circumstances for your current position
- Recognize that you have more control than you think
- Accept that rescue requires your active participation, not passive waiting
- Be the person who puts in the most effort in your own success story

### Common Pitfalls
- Waiting for a savior (mentor, government, luck, family member)
- Playing the victim of circumstances
- Expecting others to work harder for your success than you do
- Not acknowledging your own role in creating your problems

## 8. The Power of Public Declaration and Accountability

### Core Teaching
When you declare your goals publicly, you create a responsibility to make them happen. You must stay in position and prepare for the call.

### Johnny's Approach
- Told everyone he was going to be an international speaker by the end of the year
- Created social accountability
- Had to stay in position and prepare for the call
- Had to expand his abilities to relate to an international audience

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Make your goal public (tell friends, family, social media)
2. Set a specific deadline
3. Create daily/weekly actions that align with the goal
4. Expand your skills to match your expanded vision
5. Stay in position and be ready when opportunity arrives

### Best Practices
- Make declarations specific and time-bound
- Use public commitment as a forcing function
- Prepare extensively so you're ready when opportunity strikes
- Expand your capabilities ahead of the opportunity

### Common Pitfalls
- Making vague declarations without specificity
- Not preparing for the opportunity when it arrives
- Backing down when the opportunity requires sacrifice
- Forgetting to expand your skills to match your new vision
