# How to Use Good Debt to Generate Passive Income

## Overview

Robert Kiyosaki presents debt as a financial tool rather than something to fear or eliminate. Debt is classified as good or bad depending on how it is used — whether it is good or bad depends on cash flow direction. When borrowed money is used to acquire income-producing assets, it becomes "good debt" that builds wealth. When borrowed money funds consumption with no income return, it becomes "bad debt" that drains wealth.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When evaluating whether to take on student loan debt
- When deciding to purchase a house, car, or other large asset
- When considering using a credit card for a purchase
- When learning how to distinguish assets from liabilities
- When building financial intelligence and cash flow control
- When an advisor, teacher, or "guru" advises you to live debt-free without explaining why

## Steps

### Step 1: Learn the Five Words of Financial Intelligence

Memorize and internalize these five words: income, expense, asset, liability, cash flow.

### Step 2: Define Assets and Liabilities by Cash Flow

Ask one question for every item you own or are considering purchasing: does it put money in your pocket, or does it take money out of your pocket? If it puts money in your pocket, it is an asset. If it takes money out of your pocket, it is a liability. This definition applies regardless of whether the item is a house, car, boat, student loan, or credit card.

### Step 3: Classify Every Debt as Good or Bad

Good debt is borrowed money used to purchase income-producing assets. Bad debt is borrowed money used for consumption that produces no income. Student loan debt is bad debt — in the transcript, Kiyosaki calls it "the worst possible type of debt" because, unlike business debt, it cannot be discharged in bankruptcy and must still be repaid for the full obligation period. Other examples of bad debt include credit card spending on luxury items and personal consumer purchases.

### Step 4: Acquire Assets Using Good Debt

Borrow money specifically to buy income-generating assets such as rental properties, a business, or equipment that pays for itself. Structure each purchase so that the asset covers its own expenses and still delivers positive cash flow to you.

### Step 5: Control the Direction of Cash Flow

Monitor where your money goes every month. Employees have cash flow going out to taxes, mortgage payments, and living expenses. Entrepreneurs and investors structure their affairs so that cash flow moves toward them through asset income. If you want to be successful as an employee or entrepreneur, you must control the direction of your cash flow.

## Examples

### Example 1: Rental Property Purchased With a Credit Card

According to Robert Kiyosaki's broader body of work (not specific to this video), he bought his first investment property using a credit card. After taking real estate classes to learn how to evaluate deals, he identified an undervalued property, paid for it with his credit card, and rented it out. The rent covered the mortgage, operating costs, and left positive cash flow each month. That positive cash flow made the credit card debt "good debt." Kiyosaki has noted that he wishes he had never sold that first property.

### Example 2: Student Loan Debt With an Unwanted Career Outcome

A student changed her college major multiple times and finished a degree she no longer wanted. She is now stuck paying back student loan debt for a career she does not intend to pursue. Because student loan debt cannot be discharged in bankruptcy, it becomes a financial burden. This is bad debt because it produces no income stream and cannot be escaped through legal means.

### Example 3: A Boat That Generates Income

Most boats are liabilities because they typically cost money for docking, maintenance, fuel, and insurance without generating income. As a hypothetical example from Robert Kiyosaki's teachings, a boat placed in a charter program where other people pay to rent it becomes an asset. Because the boat generates rental income that exceeds its expenses, it becomes an asset and the debt used to acquire it becomes good debt.

### Example 4: Rental Properties Funded Entirely by Debt

According to Robert Kiyosaki's broader body of work, he and his wife have borrowed money to acquire rental properties. The properties generate positive cash flow each month. Tenants pay the mortgages, and the owners collect the surplus income. This demonstrates how good debt, used with proper knowledge and structure, can generate passive income.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Evaluate every debt decision by tracing the cash flow direction — into your pocket or out of your pocket
- ✅ Take real estate and financial education classes before using debt to acquire assets
- ✅ Use credit cards to purchase income-producing assets, not consumer goods
- ✅ Ensure you are absolutely certain you will commit to graduating before taking on student loan debt
- ✅ Structure investments so the asset pays for its own expenses and still delivers positive cash flow
- ✅ Never stop improving your financial intelligence by studying cash flow patterns
- ❌ Do not assume a house you live in is an asset — in Robert Kiyosaki's framework, it is a liability because it pulls money from your pocket through taxes, insurance, maintenance, and mortgage payments
- ❌ Do not take on student loan debt unless you are absolutely certain you will commit to graduating.
- ❌ Do not cut up credit cards unless you are unable to control your spending — credit cards are tools that can fund asset purchases

## Keep In Mind

- In this framework, the definition of asset and liability is based on cash flow: an asset puts money in your pocket; a liability takes money out of your pocket. This definition applies to houses, cars, boats, student loans, and people.
- Financial intelligence is your ability to control cash flow.
- As Alexandra observes in the video, traditional education often does not cover practical finance and business skills — what is taught in schools may not apply directly to how you run a business. Seek out teachers and mentors with practical experience in finance.
- Being an entrepreneur rather than an employee changes your cash flow pattern permanently. Employees have taxes flowing out before income; entrepreneurs do not.
- In the Robert Kiyosaki framework, a personal residence is classified as a liability rather than an asset under this cash-flow definition, regardless of whether you have a mortgage or own it outright.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Student loan debt cannot be discharged in bankruptcy. It cannot be eliminated through bankruptcy and must still be repaid. Only accept it if you are absolutely certain you will commit to graduating.
- Good debt requires targeted real estate knowledge. If you buy real estate without proper education, a single mistake can turn a potential asset into a financial liability.
- Using credit cards for asset purchases requires the discipline to repay the card from asset income, not from wages or savings.
- Entrepreneurship and real estate investing carry real risk. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Assuming your primary home is an asset because you have no mortgage.
  **Solution:** Within the Robert Kiyosaki framework, reclassify it as a liability. Even with no mortgage, the home costs money for taxes, insurance, repairs, and upkeep every month. Under this cash-flow definition, if it does not put cash in your pocket, it is a liability.
- **Problem:** Taking student loans for a degree without confirming your intended career outcome.
  **Solution:** Before signing for any student loan, verify that you will graduate and that you know what you are going to study.
- **Problem:** Using debt to buy consumer goods such as clothing, electronics, or vacations.
  **Solution:** Only use borrowed money for purchases that generate income. If the item does not pay for itself and produce surplus cash, pay for it with cash you have already earned.
- **Problem:** Flipping properties or making quick real estate deals without education.
  **Solution:** Study real estate thoroughly before entering the market. A single bad deal can turn a potential asset into a liability. Robert Kiyosaki advises against flipping because one mistake can reverse cash flow from positive to negative.
