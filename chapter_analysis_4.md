# Chapter 4 Analysis: The Six Mantras of Loving Speech

## 1. Chapter Title and Main Theme

**Title:** The Six Mantras of Loving Speech  
**PDF Pages:** 38–49  

**Main Theme:** This chapter teaches six specific, actionable sentences—called "mantras"—that embody loving speech and compassionate communication. These mantras help practitioners communicate presence, understanding, care, and humility in relationships. The foundational principle is that mindfulness must precede speech; true love is rooted in being truly present for oneself and others.

---

## 2. Actionable Lessons (Step-by-Step)

### Preparation for All Mantras
- **Step 1:** Begin with mindful breathing to bring about your true presence.
- **Step 2:** Breathe in and out three times before speaking any mantra.
- **Step 3:** Look into the other person's eyes.
- **Step 4:** Say the mantra in a calm, present state. Your calm will be communicated to the other person.

### Mantra 1: "I am here for you"
- Practice mindful breathing and mindful walking to bring body and mind together.
- Offer your true presence—fresh, solid, free, and calm—rather than expensive gifts.
- Use with yourself: say "I am here for you" to yourself, bringing your mind home to your body.
- Use over the phone: hold the phone, breathe mindfully, ask if the person has a moment, then say the mantra.
- Do not wait for the other person to reciprocate. Both parties benefit immediately.

### Mantra 2: "I know you are there, and I am very happy"
- Only practice after you have produced true presence via Mantra 1.
- Acknowledge the other person's presence as precious to your happiness.
- Imagine the person has moved away or passed on to remind yourself of the gift of their presence.
- Practice several times a day—before work, at dinner, over the phone, or by email.
- Note: Mantras feel awkward at first but become easier once you see results.

### Mantra 3: "I know you suffer, and that is why I am here for you"
- Use when you notice the other person is suffering.
- You do not need to fix the problem—just be present.
- True love is made of mindfulness.

### Mantra 4: "I suffer, please help"
- Use when you suffer and believe the other person caused your suffering.
- **Physical action:** Write the sentence on a piece of paper the size of a credit card and put it in your wallet.
- Do not sulk or pretend you do not suffer when asked.
- Do not punish the other person by saying "Leave me alone. I can survive very well without you."
- **Extended version:** "I suffer. I want you to know it. I don't understand why you did or said what you did. So please explain. I need your help."
- **Three-sentence breakdown:**
  1. "I suffer and I want you to know" — share your suffering.
  2. "I am doing my best" — practice mindfulness, look deeply into your suffering, and consider whether your perception is mistaken.
  3. "Please help" — acknowledge you cannot figure it out alone.
- **Plum Village rule:** You have the right to suffer 24 hours but not more.
- If not calm enough within 24 hours, write the mantra on paper and leave it on the other person's desk.

### Mantra 5: "This is a happy moment"
- Use when you are with someone you care about.
- Recognize existing conditions of happiness (easy breathing, each other, blue sky, earth supporting you).
- Mindfulness transforms the present moment into a wonderful moment.

### Mantra 6: "You are partly right"
- Use equally well when someone praises or criticizes you.
- Acknowledge what is true in their statement while recognizing your other qualities.
- Retain humility without falling into false humility.
- Can be said aloud or silently to yourself.
- Accept both your strengths and weaknesses; accept others the same way.

### General Relationship Building
- Listen compassionately to yourself first to come home to yourself.
- Help loved ones come home to themselves by giving them space.
- When both feel at home in yourselves, you find a home in each other.
- Use your eyes, ears, and mouth for compassionate communication.

## 3. Key Concepts and Principles

- **True Presence:** The best gift you can give a loved one. More precious than material things. Requires mindfulness to restore body and mind to the here and now.
- **Mindfulness as Foundation of Love:** You cannot love properly and deeply without mindfulness.
- **Support Without Words:** People communicate love in many ways without saying "I love you" (e.g., a tree providing oxygen and beauty).
- **Double Benefit:** When you produce a mantra, both you and the other person benefit. The effect is double.
- **Conditions of Happiness:** Happiness is available now if you are mindful enough to recognize existing conditions.
- **Looking Deeply:** Investigate the real cause of suffering rather than assuming the other person is at fault.
- **Home:** A true home in yourself is the base of all relationships. From this base, you can help build a compassionate community.
- **Loving Speech vs. Authority:** Using mantras replaces authority-based communication with love-based communication, keeping the door of communication open.

## 4. Examples Used in the Chapter

- **Tree outside the window:** Supporting us with beauty, freshness, and oxygen without saying "I love you."
- **Author's teacher (novice monk):** Loved the author deeply but never said "I love you," following traditional ways where saying it would seem to lose sacredness.
- **Elizabeth and her sister:** Elizabeth stopped lecturing and started using Mantra 2 ("I'm really happy that you're here") to express heartfelt appreciation.
- **Elizabeth and her husband:** When hurt, instead of punishing, Elizabeth slowly practiced Mantra 4 ("You said this thing to me that I really don't understand. What was that about?") and discovered his remarks were often about something else entirely, opening a door to his world.
- **Elizabeth and the gardener at Plum Village:** After being scolded for picking rose petals, Elizabeth practiced Mantra 3 ("I understand the situation better now, and I won't touch the flowers in the courtyard if you prefer") and Mantra 1 ("I'm here for you") by offering to water the roses and trim the rosehips during the gardener's absence.
- **Friend at retreat:** Practiced Mantra 4 with roommates by sharing that he didn't need to talk, just needed space. This helped roommates understand, not take it personally, and be more accepting.
- **Driver thinking of everything except the passenger:** Illustrates how ignoring presence makes the loved one feel unloved.

## 5. Best Practices Mentioned

- Begin every interaction with mindful breathing to cultivate presence.
- Breathe in and out three times before speaking any mantra.
- Look into the other person's eyes when delivering a mantra.
- Practice mindful breathing and mindful walking to restore true presence.
- Do not wait for reciprocation of mantras.
- Practice mantras consistently throughout the day.
- Use mantras before work, at dinner, over the phone, or by email.
- When suffering, practice looking deeply into the roots of your suffering.
- Consider that your perception of being hurt may be mistaken.
- Accept yourself with all weaknesses and strengths, and try to improve slowly at your own pace.
- Accept others without judgment, recognizing they also have positive qualities.
- Use loving speech instead of authority, especially with children.
- Give loved ones space to listen to themselves.
- Build a home base within yourself before trying to help others or society.

## 6. Common Pitfalls Mentioned

- **Punishing the other person:** When suffering, wanting to show you can survive without them. This is indirect punishment.
- **Pretending you don't suffer:** Responding "Suffer? Why should I be suffering?" when asked, which is untruthful and pushes people away.
- **Saying "Leave me alone. I can survive very well without you":** This is a mistake; when we suffer, we need others.
- **Trying to fix instead of being present:** When someone is suffering, the impulse is to fix it, but true love is simply being there.
- **Ignoring presence:** Not paying attention to the person you love, making them feel unloved.
- **Getting puffed up by praise:** Ignoring your own challenges when someone admires you.
- **Getting lost in criticism:** Letting negative feedback overshadow your positive qualities.
- **False humility:** Denying your strengths while acknowledging weaknesses.
- **Judging without looking deeper:** Telling someone they have no value without investigating their totality.
- **Thinking material gifts equal presence:** Believing expensive things are as precious as true presence.
- **Lecturing loved ones:** Telling them what to do instead of using loving speech.
- **Assuming you know the cause of suffering:** Being too sure the other person caused your suffering without examining your own perception.

---

## 7. Security / Safety Notes

- **24-Hour Suffering Rule (Plum Village practice):** You have the right to suffer for 24 hours but not more. There is a deadline. If you cannot calm down within 24 hours to practice the fourth mantra, write it down and leave it on the other person's desk so it is sure to be seen.
