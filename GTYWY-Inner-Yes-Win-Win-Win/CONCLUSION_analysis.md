# Getting to Yes with Yourself - Conclusion Analysis

## Overview

This concluding chapter of "Getting to Yes with Yourself" synthesizes the entire framework of the inner yes method and presents its three transformative outcomes: a win within, a win with others, and a win for the whole. It teaches readers that the most powerful change in life is shifting one's inner attitude from "no" to "yes," which makes all other external negotiations and relationships possible.

## When to Follow Book Teachings

- When you need to resolve conflicts in personal or professional relationships
- When preparing for a negotiation or difficult conversation
- When feeling stuck in resentment, blame, or anxiety about the future
- When seeking inner peace and contentment as a foundation for outward success
- When you want to shift from adversarial win-lose dynamics to cooperative win-win-win outcomes

## Lessons From Book

### Core Lesson: The Three Wins

The fundamental teaching is that getting to yes with yourself unlocks three kinds of wins. The inner yes serves as the prerequisite for all external success.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Morning Mirror Preparation

**Use Case:** Daily personal ritual for self-preparation.

Before facing daily challenges, the author looks in the mirror and reminds himself that the biggest opponent he will face that day is himself. He reviews the six inner yes steps mentally, asking himself guiding questions for each. This prepares him to get out of his own way.

##### Example 2: Nelson Mandela's Transformation

**Use Case:** Overcoming extreme adversity and injustice through inner work.

While imprisoned for 27 years, Mandela applied the inner yes principles: he went to the "balcony" to observe himself, learned to know himself deeply, refused to play the blame game, took full responsibility, reframed his perspective to see life on his side, let go of resentments, and forgave his enemies. Upon release, he welcomed all races and led South Africa to a win-win-win democratic agreement ending apartheid.

##### Example 3: Preventing Conflicts Before They Start

**Use Case:** Relationship and workplace harmony.

By staying calm and grounded through inner yes practice, you avoid being provoked, avoid taking attacks personally, and prevent saying or doing things you will later regret. With sincere respect and genuine willingness to address others' needs, you resolve matters before they escalate into serious disputes.

##### Example 4: Author's Professional Journey

**Use Case:** Large-scale peacemaking.

Ury spent 35 years working to prevent wars, initially believing practical negotiation strategies were the key. He realized that sustained world peace requires inner peace first—the difficulty in global cooperation stems from our inability to get to yes with ourselves.

#### Step 1: Put Yourself in Your Shoes

**Action:** Notice the inner critic and observe your thoughts and feelings without judgment.

Each morning, ask yourself: Can you notice the inner critic at work and simply observe your thoughts and feelings without judging? What underlying needs do your feelings point to? What do you really need? This step helps you separate your observing self from your reactive emotions.

#### Step 2: Develop Your Inner BATNA

**Action:** Stop blaming others and commit to taking care of your deepest needs no matter what.

Ask: Are you blaming anyone or anything for your needs not being met? What benefit does this blame provide you—and what are the costs? Can you commit to take care of your deepest needs no matter what? Your inner BATNA is knowing you can meet your own core needs independently.

#### Step 3: Reframe Your Picture

**Action:** See life as on your side rather than against you.

Ask: Do you feel like life is in some way against you? How can you make your own happiness today? If life is challenging, can you nonetheless choose to say yes to it, just the way it is? Reframe challenges as opportunities and choose to embrace life rather than resist it.

#### Step 4: Stay in the Zone

**Action:** Release past resentments and future anxieties to remain present and effective.

Ask: Are you carrying any resentments about the past or anxieties about the future? What will it take to let go and accept life as it is today? What is one small step you can take to stay in the zone, where you are at your best? The zone is the state of flow and optimal performance.

#### Step 5: Respect Them Even If

**Action:** Extend genuine respect to others regardless of their behavior toward you.

Ask: Are you feeling any antagonism toward anyone? What is it like to be in their shoes? Even if they are not showing you respect, can you still respect them? This breaks the cycle of mutual disrespect and opens the door to cooperation.

#### Step 6: Give and Receive

**Action:** Shift the game from taking to giving and from win-lose to win-win-win.

Ask: Do you feel a fear of scarcity in any situation you are currently facing? What will it take for you to change the game from taking to giving? This step transforms the fundamental dynamic from competition to collaboration.

#### Best Practices

- Practice the six steps daily, especially in the morning, to build mental muscles
- Adapt the method to your own needs and preferences (solitary walk, coffee with a friend, etc.)
- Prepare before difficult conversations by running through the six steps in advance
- Exercise all six steps together rather than relying on just one
- View the journey as lifelong, not a single trip
- When on the fly in conflict, rely on steady practice to stay on the balcony
- Treat each step like a muscle—the more you exercise it, the stronger it gets
- Make the method your own so it works best for you

#### Keep In Mind

- Understanding the six steps helps a lot, but in the end, no one can do the work but you
- As with any sport, you may never be perfect, but you will gradually get better with practice
- The work of arriving at an inner yes is some of the hardest work anyone can do, all the more so because it is invisible
- There is no bigger yes than the yes inside, no bigger win than the win within
- An inner yes brings a growing sense of calm, contentment, satisfaction, and sufficiency
- The key work in negotiation starts from within
- Getting to yes with yourself not only helps resolve conflicts but actually prevents them from arising in the first place

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Expecting perfection in the practice of inner yes.
  **Solution:** Accept that the journey is lifelong and gradual improvement is the goal, not perfection.

- **Problem:** Only practicing one or two steps of the six-step method.
  **Solution:** Exercise all six steps together, as each step makes the next easier and they collectively enable full transformation.

- **Problem:** Reacting impulsively in the middle of a difficult conversation without preparation.
  **Solution:** Run through the six steps in advance before any problematic conversation to bring your best ally instead of your worst opponent.

- **Problem:** Blaming others for unmet needs, which creates dependency and resentment.
  **Solution:** Recognize the costs of blame and commit to taking care of your deepest needs yourself by developing your inner BATNA.

- **Problem:** Treating life as an adversary and resisting challenges.
  **Solution:** Reframe your picture to see life as on your side, and choose to say yes to life even when it is challenging.

- **Problem:** Carrying past resentments and future anxieties that keep you from the zone.
  **Solution:** Practice letting go and accepting life as it is today, taking one small step at a time to stay grounded.
