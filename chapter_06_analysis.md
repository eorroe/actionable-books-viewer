Lecture 6: The Social Influence of Divergence & Lecture 7: Word of Mouth—Powerful and Persuasive

## Actionable Lessons with Step-by-Step Instructions

### Lesson 1: Understand When People Conform vs. Diverge
1. Identify the domain of the behavior or choice (functional vs. identity-relevant).
2. If the domain is functional (e.g., detergent, refrigerators), expect conformity—people follow the crowd because it signals quality.
3. If the domain is identity-relevant (e.g., music, clothing, cars), expect divergence—people avoid choices associated with groups they don't want to resemble.
4. Monitor who is adopting a product or idea; if it's people unlike your target audience, anticipate divergence.

### Lesson 2: Leverage Identity Signaling in Marketing
1. Determine what identity your product signals to consumers.
2. Identify which social groups currently use your product.
3. If those groups repel your target audience, reposition or adjust branding to shift the signal.
4. Use visible branding (logos, emblems) to make the signal explicit and widely recognizable when targeting mass markets.
5. Use subtle, less overt signals (color, detailing, craftsmanship) when targeting insiders or high-status consumers who want to distinguish themselves from wannabes.

### Lesson 3: Apply the Inverted-U Branding Strategy
1. For low-priced products, minimize or omit prominent branding—consumers may not want to signal cheap choices.
2. For mid-priced products, increase brand prominence—consumers want to signal they can afford more than generic options.
3. For high-priced luxury products, reduce brand prominence—wealthy insiders prefer subtle signals that only other insiders can recognize, avoiding poaching by counterfeiters and wannabes.
4. Test branding visibility across price points to confirm the inverted-U pattern holds for your product category.

### Lesson 4: Protect Premium Signals from Counterfeiting
1. Recognize that overt, logo-heavy products are more vulnerable to counterfeiting because counterfeiters target signal value, not quality.
2. Introduce subtle, harder-to-replicate design elements (specific colors, materials, craftsmanship details) that only knowledgeable insiders can identify.
3. Gradually reduce overt branding as the product moves upmarket to maintain exclusivity and reduce piracy.
4. Create a tiered signaling system where different subtleties communicate different levels of insider knowledge.

### Lesson 5: Harness Word of Mouth for Growth
1. Prioritize creating experiences, products, or content that people want to share with others.
2. Recognize that word of mouth drives 20–50% of all purchasing decisions—allocate marketing resources accordingly.
3. Encourage first-time customers to become advocates; a single word-of-mouth conversation from a first-time customer can increase restaurant sales by nearly $200.
4. Optimize for review quality: a 5-star review versus a 1-star review can drive approximately 20 additional book sales on Amazon.
5. Invest in interpersonal communication channels (social sharing, referral programs, community building) as they often outperform traditional advertising in aggregate impact.

### Lesson 6: Design for Social Transmission
1. Build built-in triggers or shareable moments into products and services.
2. Create social currency—make people look good when they share your product or idea.
3. Use emotion to increase sharing; stories and news that evoke strong feelings spread more widely.
4. Leverage online platforms (Facebook, Twitter, etc.) to amplify interpersonal communication, but focus on the underlying social dynamics rather than platform-specific tactics.

## Key Concepts and Definitions

- **Divergence**: The act of avoiding or abandoning a product, idea, or behavior to prevent being misidentified with an undesired social group or identity.
- **Conformity**: The act of doing the same thing as others, typically in functional domains where following the crowd signals quality or correctness.
- **Identity-Relevant Domains**: Areas of life where choices signal who we are or who we want to be (e.g., music, clothing, cars). In these domains, people care about what others think and who those others are.
- **Functional Domains**: Areas of life where choices are based primarily on practical utility (e.g., detergent, refrigerators). In these domains, social proof (others choosing it) signals quality.
- **Signal Value**: The meaning or identity a product, behavior, or choice communicates to others, separate from its functional utility.
- **Overt Signals**: Explicit, easily recognizable markers (large logos, brand names) that communicate identity to a broad audience.
- **Subtle Signals (Dog-Whistle Fashion)**: Discreet, less blatant markers (specific colors, detailing, craftsmanship) that fly beneath the radar of most observers but are recognized by insiders.
- **Insiders**: People with expertise or knowledge in a given domain who can decode subtle signals.
- **Wannabes**: People who desire to seem wealthy, high-status, or part of a group but lack the genuine credentials or knowledge.
- **Inverted-U Relationship**: The pattern where brand prominence increases with price up to a mid-point, then decreases as price increases further—reflecting shifting signaling strategies across price tiers.
- **Word of Mouth (WOM)**: Interpersonal communication about products, ideas, or behaviors—sharing stories, news, reviews, and recommendations with others.
- **Social Influence**: The effect that other people's choices, behaviors, and identities have on our own decisions.

## Examples from the Text

1. **Honda Element**: Marketed as a "dorm room on wheels" for college students, but became popular with 30- and 40-somethings and senior citizens. As a result, the signal shifted, and the original target audience (20-somethings) diverged to avoid looking like their parents or older generations.

2. **Naming Children "Junior"**: Professionals stopped naming their children "Junior" after the practice was adopted by the working class, to avoid being misidentified as working class.

3. **Candy Consumption**: People ate 35% less candy when they saw an obese person take a lot, diverging to avoid being associated with obesity.

4. **Teenagers, Democrats, Jocks**: Teenagers don't want to look like their parents; Democrats don't want to look like Republicans; jocks don't want to look like geeks—all examples of divergence in identity-relevant domains.

5. **Brand Prominence and Price (Inverted-U)**:
   - Cheap products: Almost no branding.
   - Mid-priced products: Branding becomes more prominent.
   - Expensive products: Branding becomes less prominent.
   - Mercedes: For every $5,000 increase in price, the emblem shrinks by around a centimeter.
   - Gucci handbags and Louis Vuitton shoes: Less prominent branding = higher price.

6. **Counterfeit Handbags**: Counterfeiters target products with prominent logos because customers buying counterfeits care about signal value (others seeing the brand), not quality.

7. **Restaurant Word of Mouth**: A single word-of-mouth conversation from a first-time customer leads to almost $200 more in restaurant sales.

8. **Amazon Book Reviews**: A 5-star review (vs. a 1-star review) leads to approximately 20 more books sold.

## Best Practices

1. **Segment by Identity Relevance**: Before applying social influence strategies, clearly categorize the product or behavior as functional or identity-relevant. Conformity tactics work in functional domains; divergence tactics work in identity-relevant domains.

2. **Map Your Signal**: Understand exactly what identity your product currently signals and to whom. Conduct audience research to identify which groups are currently associated with your brand.

3. **Design Tiered Branding**: Plan branding visibility across your product line to match the inverted-U pattern—minimal for entry-level, prominent for mid-tier, subtle for luxury.

4. **Create Insider Authentication**: For premium products, develop subtle, hard-to-replicate signals that only knowledgeable consumers can identify. This protects against counterfeiting and maintains exclusivity.

5. **Amplify Word of Mouth**: Invest in creating share-worthy experiences, exceptional quality, or emotional resonance. Word of mouth drives 20–50% of purchases and is more trusted than traditional advertising.

6. **Optimize for Review Quality**: Encourage honest, positive reviews. The difference between a 5-star and 1-star review can mean 20+ additional unit sales on platforms like Amazon.

7. **Monitor Signal Poaching**: Watch for when wannabes or counterfeiters begin adopting your signals. When this happens, plan to introduce new subtle signals or diverge to maintain insider status.

8. **Use Social Proof Strategically**: In functional domains, highlight how many people use or approve of your product. In identity-relevant domains, highlight that "people like you" use it, and emphasize which types of people use it.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Misreading Domain Type**: Applying conformity tactics (social proof, crowd appeals) to identity-relevant products can backfire if the crowd associated with the product is not the target audience.

2. **Ignoring Signal Shifts**: Failing to monitor who is adopting your product can lead to unintended signal drift, causing your original target audience to diverge (as with the Honda Element).

3. **Over-Branding Luxury Products**: Using large, prominent logos on high-end products invites counterfeiting and signals to insiders that the product is no longer exclusive. Wealthy consumers may diverge.

4. **Under-Branding Mid-Tier Products**: Failing to make mid-priced products identifiable misses the opportunity to signal that the consumer can afford more than generic alternatives.

5. **Assuming All Consumers Want Overt Signals**: Not recognizing that insiders prefer subtle signals and that overt signals are more vulnerable to being poached by outsiders.

6. **Neglecting Word of Mouth**: Over-investing in traditional advertising while under-investing in creating shareable experiences, referral programs, or community engagement. Word of mouth is more impactful in aggregate.

7. **Confusing Correlation with Causation in Reviews**: Assuming that more reviews always equal better sales, without considering review quality and star rating impact.

8. **Copycatting Without Understanding Signaling**: Attempting to replicate a competitor's branding or positioning without understanding the identity signals it communicates and to whom.

9. **Ignoring Platform Dynamics**: Treating all word-of-mouth channels (Facebook, Twitter, in-person) as identical. Each channel has different social dynamics and sharing motivations.

10. **Forgetting That Signals Are Relative**: A signal's meaning depends on context and who else is using it. What signals luxury in one context may signal something entirely different in another.
