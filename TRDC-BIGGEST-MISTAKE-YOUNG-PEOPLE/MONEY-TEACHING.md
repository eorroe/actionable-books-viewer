# Invest in What You Love Rather Than Do What You Love

## Overview

This Money Teaching guides young people away from the common mistake of prioritizing career passion over asset acquisition. Robert Kiyosaki explains that focusing solely on "doing what you love" keeps you trapped in the Employee (E) and Self-employed (S) quadrants, paying 40% in taxes as Employees and 60% in taxes as Self-employed according to Kiyosaki's Cashflow Quadrant framework (these are illustrative effective tax rates used within the framework, not current statutory rates for any specific year or jurisdiction), missing opportunities to build wealth through businesses, real estate, and commodities. The key is to invest in assets you love rather than pursue careers you love, accepting that you had to learn about taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits to build the assets you love.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are a young person deciding between a career path and building wealth
- When working on choosing investments or asset classes for long-term growth
- When the user asks about financial independence, passive income, or the Cashflow Quadrant

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Cashflow Quadrant

Learn the four quadrants: E (Employee), S (Small Business/Self-employed), B (Big Business—per Kiyosaki's framework, businesses with 500 or more employees are the threshold he uses to distinguish a scalable Big Business from a Small Business/Self-employed operation), and I (Investor). Recognize that E pays 40% in taxes (per Kiyosaki's Cashflow Quadrant framework, illustrative effective tax rate), S pays 60% in taxes (per Kiyosaki's Cashflow Quadrant framework, illustrative effective tax rate), B pays 20% (per Kiyosaki's Cashflow Quadrant framework, illustrative effective tax rate), and I pays 0% (per Kiyosaki's Cashflow Quadrant framework, illustrative effective tax rate), and both E and S require constant work. Aim to move toward B and I for financial freedom.

### Step 2: Shift Your Mindset from Doing to Investing

Stop focusing on "doing what you love" as a career. Start thinking about "investing in what you love" as asset acquisition. Accept that you have to learn about taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits to build assets you love. Passion is greedy—meaning inwardly focused, self-serving motivation centered on personal gain or satisfaction; purpose is for the people—meaning outwardly focused motivation centered on serving others.

### Step 3: Build Your First Asset - A Business

Start a business with 500 or more employees (per Kiyosaki's framework, the threshold he uses to distinguish a scalable Big Business from a Small Business/Self-employed operation) rather than staying self-employed. Learn business skills: taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits. Understand that businesses afford you the lifestyle you want and provide capital for other investments. To break the catch-22—where you need business income to fund real estate purchases, but you need real estate as collateral to secure business capital—start by learning about taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits through your business; use business profits to fund real estate purchases; and consider leveraging other people's money (OPM), meaning borrowed capital from lenders, investors, or partners.

### Step 4: Invest in Real Estate

Acquire properties you love such as land, buildings, or fixer-uppers. Use your business income to fund real estate purchases. Accept that real estate is illiquid—meaning it takes months to years to sell, requires finding a specific buyer, or involves substantial transaction costs—so you must have knowledge of taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits before purchasing.

### Step 5: Diversify into Commodities

Invest in assets you love such as food (avocado farms), oil, gold, and silver. Focus on assets you love rather than chasing shiny objects—high-visibility, high-hype investments that temporarily capture attention the way a fishing lure draws a fish (e.g., Bitcoin or the stock market), distracting from building durable assets such as businesses, real estate, or commodities.

### Step 6: Understand Liquidity and Risk

Paper assets (stocks, bonds, mutual funds, Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs), 401(k) retirement savings plan, Individual Retirement Account (IRA)) are liquid—meaning they can be sold quickly (typically within days to weeks) at market price; if you make a mistake, you can get out. Real estate, businesses, and commodities are illiquid—meaning they take months to years to sell, require finding a specific buyer, or involve substantial transaction costs. The less liquid the asset, the higher your financial IQ. "You've got to be so much smarter." Kiyosaki continues: "And most people, they have it. They have that ability to be that smart. But they'd rather do what they love rather than invest in what they love." You can make mistakes with liquid assets and get out; with illiquid assets, if you make a mistake, you can't get out.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Invest in assets, not just careers
- ✅ Learn about taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits to build assets you love
- ✅ Start with a business before investing in real estate
- ✅ Prioritize liquidity education before moving into illiquid assets
- ✅ Focus on purpose (serving people) over passion (greed)
- ❌ Don't chase shiny objects like Bitcoin—high-visibility, high-hype investments that temporarily capture attention the way a fishing lure draws a fish, distracting from building durable assets such as businesses, real estate, or commodities
- ❌ Don't stay trapped in the E or S quadrants
- ❌ Don't avoid learning about taxes, debt, insurance, and lawsuits
- ❌ Don't stay in paper assets, 401(k)s, and IRAs because you don't want to take the risk in the B and I quadrants (Business and Investing)

## Keep In Mind

- Real estate and business investing present a circular dependency: you need business income to fund real estate purchases, but you often need real estate as collateral to build business capital — break the cycle by starting a low-capital or service-based business first to generate cash flow, use those profits as a down payment to acquire property, then use the real estate equity as collateral for business expansion loans
- One of the most important things you have in your life is time. It's one of your greatest assets or your liability - when you're young you're just havin' a lot of fun and life's exciting, but being young can be a liability to you—young people waste their time (their greatest asset) on fun and novelty instead of building assets, and time is finite and cannot be recovered once spent
- Kiyosaki says: "the higher the risk, the higher the returns, but also the higher dedication, education, and study you have to go through"
- You can get rich in the E and S quadrants, but you pay 40% in taxes as Employees and 60% in taxes as Self-employed (per Kiyosaki's Cashflow Quadrant framework, illustrative effective tax rates used within the framework, not current statutory rates for any specific year or jurisdiction)
- Young people often think they can delay financial education, but eventually you get old and then you're not young again
- Passion is greedy—meaning inwardly focused, self-serving motivation centered on personal gain or satisfaction; purpose is for the people—meaning outwardly focused motivation centered on serving others

## Security & Safety Notes

- Real estate and business investments are illiquid—meaning they take months to years to sell, require finding a specific buyer, or involve substantial transaction costs—so you cannot easily exit if you make a mistake
- Higher-risk assets require more education; do not invest in assets you have not researched
- Always understand liquidity before investing in illiquid assets

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Focusing on "doing what you love" as a career instead of investing in assets
  **Solution:** Shift your focus from career passion to acquiring assets
- **Problem:** Chasing shiny objects like Bitcoin—high-visibility, high-hype investments that temporarily capture attention the way a fishing lure draws a fish, distracting from building durable assets such as businesses, real estate, or commodities
  **Solution:** Build assets through business and real estate
- **Problem:** Not learning skills like taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits
  **Solution:** Accept that learning about taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits is necessary to build the assets you love
- **Problem:** Ignoring that paper assets can be sold quickly if you make a mistake, while real estate and businesses cannot be easily exited
  **Solution:** Understand that paper assets are liquid and you can get out if you make a mistake, while real estate and businesses require you to be smarter going in
- **Problem:** Thinking you can delay financial education because you are young
  **Solution:** Treat time as a critical resource and start building financial education immediately
