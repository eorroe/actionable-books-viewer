# Invest in What You Love Rather Than Do What You Love

## Overview

This Money Teaching guides young people away from the common mistake of prioritizing career passion over asset acquisition. Robert Kiyosaki explains that focusing solely on "doing what you love" keeps you trapped in the Employee and Self-employed quadrants, paying the highest taxes and missing opportunities to build real wealth through businesses, real estate, and commodities. The key is to invest in assets you love rather than pursue careers you love, accepting that you may need to do things you hate in the short term to build the assets you love.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are a young person deciding between a career path and building wealth
- When working on choosing investments or asset classes for long-term growth
- When the user asks about financial independence, passive income, or the Cashflow Quadrant

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Cashflow Quadrant

Learn the four quadrants: E (Employee), S (Small Business/Self-employed), B (Big Business with 500+ employees), and I (Investor). Recognize that E and S pay the highest taxes and require constant work. Aim to move toward B and I for true financial freedom.

### Step 2: Shift Your Mindset from Doing to Investing

Stop focusing on "doing what you love" as a career. Start thinking about "investing in what you love" as asset acquisition. Accept that you may need to do tasks you find unpleasant in the short term to build assets you love. Passion is greedy; purpose is for the people.

### Step 3: Build Your First Asset - A Business

Start a business with 500+ employees rather than staying self-employed. Learn essential business skills: taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits. Understand that businesses afford you the lifestyle you want and provide capital for other investments.

### Step 4: Invest in Real Estate

Acquire properties you genuinely love such as land, buildings, or fixer-uppers. Use your business income and other people's money to fund real estate purchases. Accept that real estate is illiquid - you cannot easily exit, so you must be smarter going in.

### Step 5: Diversify into Commodities

Invest in tangible assets you love such as food (avocado farms), oil, gold, and silver. Focus on assets that provide real value rather than "shiny objects" like cryptocurrencies.

### Step 6: Understand Liquidity and Risk

Paper assets (stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs, 401(k), IRA) are liquid but lower risk/reward. Real estate, businesses, and commodities are less liquid but higher risk/reward. The less liquid the asset, the higher your financial IQ must be. You can make mistakes with liquid assets and recover; illiquid mistakes are harder to fix.

## Examples

### Example 1: Building a Business

Kiyosaki learned about taxes, debt, insurance, and lawsuits through his business. He now has employees and can afford to invest in real estate because of his business income. His business affords him the lifestyle he wants, not his career passion.

### Example 2: Investing in Real Estate

Kiyosaki invests only in properties he loves. He looks at numbers, houses, and land that genuinely interest him. He has never purchased a piece of land or building he did not love.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Invest in assets, not just careers
- ✅ Learn skills you hate to build assets you love
- ✅ Start with a business before investing in real estate
- ✅ Prioritize liquidity education before moving into illiquid assets
- ✅ Focus on purpose (serving people) over passion (greed)
- ❌ Don't chase shiny objects like Bitcoin or cryptocurrencies
- ❌ Don't stay trapped in the E or S quadrants
- ❌ Don't ignore taxes, debt, insurance, and lawsuits
- ❌ Don't invest in assets you have not studied just because peers, advisors, or social media influencers are investing in them

## Keep In Mind

- Time is either your greatest asset or your greatest liability - when you're young, time can be wasted on fun instead of learning
- The higher the risk, the higher the returns, but also the higher the dedication, education, and study required
- You can get rich in the E and S quadrants, but you'll pay the highest taxes
- Young people often think they have plenty of time, but time passes quickly
- Passion is greedy; purpose is for the people

## Security & Safety Notes

- Real estate and business investments are less liquid - you cannot easily exit if you make a mistake
- Higher-risk assets require deeper education; do not invest in assets you have not studied
- Always understand liquidity before investing in illiquid assets

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Focusing on "doing what you love" as a career instead of investing in assets
  **Solution:** Shift your focus from career passion to acquiring assets that generate passive income
- **Problem:** Chasing shiny objects like Bitcoin or trending investments
  **Solution:** Build real assets through business and real estate that you understand deeply
- **Problem:** Not learning essential skills like taxes, debt, and accounting because they're boring
  **Solution:** Accept that completing tasks you find unpleasant is necessary to build the assets you love
- **Problem:** Ignoring liquidity differences between asset classes
  **Solution:** Understand that paper assets are liquid and forgiving, while real estate and businesses require more expertise
- **Problem:** Thinking you have plenty of time because you're young
  **Solution:** Treat time as your most valuable asset and start building financial intelligence immediately
