**Main Theme:** Effective communication with others requires first establishing a connection with yourself through mindfulness, then applying two core practices: deep listening and loving speech (Right Speech). The chapter teaches that understanding suffering is the foundation of love, and provides practical tools including the Six Mantras of Loving Speech, the Four Elements of Right Speech, and the Four Criteria for skillful communication.

## Actionable Lessons

### 1. Establish Your Own Home Through Self-Communication
- Reserve time alone each day for communicating with yourself
- Practice nonthinking and nontalking to listen to yourself
- Practice mindful breathing and mindful walking
- Be aware of your in-breath and out-breath before communicating with others
- You cannot communicate well with another person if you don't communicate well with yourself

### 2. Begin Every Communication with the Buddha Nature Practice
- Remember at the beginning of every communication that there is a Buddha inside each person
- Join your palms and bow with respect (or look them in the eye and smile)
- Breathe in and out mindfully while bringing your hands together
- Form a lotus flower with your hands as an offering
- Silently say: "A lotus for you. A Buddha to be."
- Even if you shake hands, in your mind offer a lotus flower

### 3. Practice Deep Listening (First Key to Communication)
- Listen first, speak second — deep listening needs to come before expression
- Practice mindfulness of suffering — recognizing and embracing suffering in yourself and the other person
- Listen with only one intention: to help the other person suffer less
- Listen without interrupting, even if the person says wrong things
- If the quality of your listening is not good enough, pause and continue another day
- Practice mindful breathing and mindful walking until you're ready to listen
- Say to yourself: "I am listening to this person with only one purpose: to give this person a chance to suffer less."
- If the person is expressing bitterness, accusation, or blaming, continue to listen compassionately
- Don't correct the person's wrong perceptions during deep listening — do that later
- You can listen compassionately for thirty minutes and help the other person suffer much less
- After deep listening, you will have a chance later to give the person information to correct their perception

### 4. Practice Loving Speech (Second Key to Communication)
- Speak in a way that helps others recognize the suffering inside themselves and in you
- Be skillful with your words so the listener is not caught in wrong perceptions
- The speaker must be very mindful, and the listener must be careful not to get caught in words
- Words are nourishment — what you say should convey only compassion and understanding
- Practice Right Speech: the speech of compassion, tolerance, and forgiveness

### 5. Practice the Four Elements of Right Speech
1. **Tell the truth** — Don't lie or turn the truth upside down
2. **Don't exaggerate** — Refrain from inventing and exaggerating
3. **Be consistent** — No double-talk or speaking differently to different people for selfish reasons
4. **Use peaceful language** — Don't use insulting, violent, cruel, or judgmental words

### 6. Apply the Four Criteria to Your Speech
1. **Speak the language of the world** — Use terms people can understand based on their daily experience
2. **Speak according to the understanding of the listener** — Keep truthful content the same but adapt to the person's perspective and ability to receive
3. **Prescribe the right medicine for the disease** — Adapt your teaching to the other person's background, like a doctor prescribing the right medicine
4. **Reflect the absolute truth** — Be humble and look deeply to discover how to talk about difficult truths

### 7. Practice Deep Listening with Suffering
- Look deeply and see that not only on your side but also on the other side there is a lot of fear and suffering
- When you understand their suffering, you feel compassion for them
- Your way of looking at them changes when you see their suffering
- Compassionate listening doesn't mean you agree with wrong perceptions — it means you understand the suffering behind them

### 8. Check If You Understand the Other Person Enough
- Ask yourself: "Do I understand him enough?" "Do I understand her enough?"
- Ask the other person: "Do you think I understand you enough?"
- Don't wait until the other person is full of anger to ask this question
- Say: "Please tell me, please help me. Because I know very well that if I don't understand you, I will make a lot of mistakes."
- This question applies to friends, family, coworkers, and anyone you care about
- You may have lived with someone for years but still not know them deeply

### 9. Practice the Six Mantras of Loving Speech
Before saying any mantra, breathe in and out three times to become calm and present.

**Mantra 1: "I am here for you."**
- The best gift you can give a loved one is your presence
- Practice mindful breathing and mindful walking to bring body and mind together
- Use this mantra with yourself as well: "I am here for you" means you are there for yourself
- You can use this mantra over the phone — hold the phone, breathe, and say it
- Both you and the other person benefit from this mantra

**Mantra 2: "I know you are there, and I am very happy."**
- Only use this after practicing the first mantra and producing your presence
- Recognize the presence of the other person as precious
- Practice this several times a day — before work, at dinner, over the phone
- Imagine the other person is gone to feel the gift of their presence

**Mantra 3: "I know you suffer, and that is why I am here for you."**
- Use this when you notice the other person is suffering
- You don't need to do anything other than be there
- True love is made of mindfulness
- When your loved ones are suffering, just being present helps them suffer less right away

**Mantra 4: "I suffer. Please help."**
- Use this when you suffer and believe the other person has caused your suffering
- Write it down on a piece of paper the size of a credit card and put it in your wallet
- If you're not calm enough within 24 hours, write it and leave it where the other person will see it
- The extended version: "I suffer. I want you to know it. I don't understand why you did or said what you did. So please explain. I need your help."
- Breakdown: "I suffer and I want you to know" + "I am doing my best" + "Please help"

**Mantra 5: "This is a happy moment."**
- Use this when you are with someone you care about
- Recognize conditions of happiness available in the here and now
- You can breathe easily, have each other, have blue sky and the earth supporting you
- Mindfulness makes the present moment a wonderful moment

**Mantra 6: "You are partly right."**
- Use this when someone praises or criticizes you
- When praised: "You are partly right. You know that I have other things in me too" — retain humility
- When criticized: "Darling, you are only partly right, because I also have good things in me"
- This is the truth — you don't lie and don't fall into false humility
- Accept yourself with all weaknesses and strengths

### 10. Handle Anger Skillfully
- Don't communicate when you are angry — you aren't lucid
- Don't suppress anger — it will explode if ignored
- Don't express anger physically (shouting, hitting pillow) — this rehearses and nourishes anger
- Go home to yourself and take care of your anger
- Use mindful breathing and mindful walking
- Recognize, embrace, and look deeply at your strong emotions
- You are like a person whose house is on fire who goes chasing after the arsonist instead of putting out the fire
- Use the fourth mantra: "I suffer. Please help."
- Write down your suffering on paper if you cannot speak calmly within 24 hours
- Phone or email the other person only after calming your anger

### 11. Reconcile in Families
- Don't wait for the other person to change — change yourself first
- Stop judging and return to your own internal communication
- Don't try to force the other person to change
- Give yourself a deadline for making a start (e.g., last night of a retreat)
- Practice mindful breathing, mindful walking, and mindfulness in daily activities
- Cultivate mindfulness to recognize habit energy each time it arises
- Each time you embrace habit energy with mindfulness, it becomes weaker
- You can stop the cycle of suffering transmission to future generations

### 12. Communicate in Long-Term Relationships
- Don't immediately correct or reproach your partner
- Go back to yourself, back to your peace, until you know how to handle the situation in a loving way
- Only when you are calm, invite your partner to speak
- Say you are sorry for not having understood her or him better
- Listen deeply even if what she says is complaining or unkind
- Don't interrupt — let her speak out everything
- You may take days to look deeply before skillfully addressing wrong perceptions

### 13. Bring Compassionate Communication to Divided Groups
- First have both groups practice communicating with themselves (mindful breathing, nonthinking, deep listening to themselves)
- Only in the second week encourage compassionate communication between groups
- Each group tells the other about their suffering using loving speech, trying not to blame or accuse
- The listening group listens with compassion and doesn't interrupt to correct misperceptions
- When groups recognize each other's suffering, compassion arises and fear disappears

### 14. Transform Peace Negotiations
- Don't negotiate right away — first practice breathing, walking, sitting, and calming
- If atmosphere gets too heated, stop and breathe in and out together
- Government officials should organize peace talks using deep listening and loving speech

## Key Concepts and Principles

1. **Two Keys to Compassionate Communication**: Deep listening and loving speech are the best instruments for establishing and restoring communication and relieving suffering.

2. **Deep Listening**: Listening with the intention of helping the other person suffer less, without getting caught in judgment, without interrupting, and with only one purpose.

3. **Loving Speech (Right Speech)**: Speaking in a way that helps others recognize suffering inside themselves and in you, using words that nourish rather than poison.

4. **Right Speech vs. Wrong Speech**: Right Speech conveys insight, understanding, and compassion. Wrong Speech causes ill-being through unkind, untruthful, or violent words.

5. **Four Elements of Right Speech**: Tell the truth, don't exaggerate, be consistent (no double-talk), use peaceful language.

6. **Four Criteria for Skillful Speech**: Speak the language of the world, speak according to the listener's understanding, prescribe the right medicine for the disease, reflect the absolute truth.

7. **Understanding Suffering as Foundation of Love**: Love is based on understanding. Each of us is hungry for understanding. Compassion and love are born from understanding.

8. **Happiness as Capacity to Understand and Love**: Happiness is defined as the capacity to understand and to love. Without understanding and love, no happiness is possible.

9. **Self-Communication as Prerequisite**: Before connecting with others, ensure you can communicate with yourself. If you don't communicate well with yourself, you cannot communicate well with another person.

10. **Buddha Nature in Everyone**: There is a Buddha (most understanding and compassionate person) inside each of us. Bowing and joining palms is a practice of awakening this nature.

11. **Mindfulness of Compassion**: Listening with only one intention — to help the other person suffer less. This keeps compassion alive during listening.

12. **Suffering Transmission**: Suffering is passed down from ancestors and parents. Unless you understand and transform your own suffering, it continues to be passed to future generations.

13. **Wrong Perception as Source of Suffering**: A wrong perception can cause enormous suffering. We must ask ourselves, "Are you sure your perception is right?"

14. **The Home Metaphor**: Come home to yourself. Create a true home within yourself through mindful communication. Then you can help others come home to themselves.

15. **24-Hour Suffering Deadline**: In Plum Village practice, you have the right to suffer for 24 hours but not more. You must practice the fourth mantra before the deadline.

16. **Absolute Truth vs. Relative Truth**: Up and down are relative truths we use for common language. Absolute truths (like no-self, no-birth and no-death) must be conveyed skillfully based on the listener's capacity.

## Examples Used in the Chapter

1. **The Vietnamese Couple Tragedy (PDF page 55)**: A husband returns from war and his wife brings their son. The boy says the man is not his daddy because he saw another man visiting at night. The husband's pride prevents him from asking for help. The wife also suffers in silence. Both die by suicide (the wife drowns herself). If either had asked for help, the tragedy would have been averted.

2. **The Man Reconciling with His Father (PDF page 57)**: At a retreat in Oldenburg, Germany, a man was given a midnight deadline to reconcile with his father with whom he had been angry for years. When he called his father the next morning, he naturally used loving speech without trying. His father told him about his suffering for the first time.

3. **The Little Girl and the Mummy (PDF page 34)**: A little girl at a museum asks, "Am I going to be lying dead on a table one day?" The author answers "No" because it is not the right time or place to teach her about impermanence.

4. **The Buddha and the Milk/Butter/Cheese (PDF page 33)**: Someone gives a woman milk in the morning and comes back for it at night. The milk has turned into butter and cheese. The Buddha explains that the teaching given depends on the degree of wisdom and ability of the receiver.

5. **The Buddha and the Jain Man (PDF page 34)**: A Jain man asks the Buddha if human beings have a self. The Buddha remains silent because saying "there is no self" would cause the man to suffer. He teaches according to the listener's capacity.

6. **The Times of India Editors (PDF page 36)**: During the 2008 Mumbai terrorist attacks, the author advises journalists to report truthfully but in a way that doesn't water the seeds of fear and anger in readers. They should help people understand why things happen.

7. **The Israeli-Palestinian Retreat (PDF page 60)**: Groups of Israelis and Palestinians at Plum Village practice mindful communication. Initially they are full of fear, anger, and mistrust. Through deep listening and loving speech, they recognize each other's suffering and compassion arises.

8. **Elizabeth Using the Six Mantras (PDF pages 46-47)**: Elizabeth uses the second mantra ("I'm really happy that you're here") with her sister. She uses the fourth mantra with her husband to understand his hurtful remarks. She uses the third mantra with a gardener who scolded her, and the first mantra to offer care while he travels.

9. **The Man in the Retreat Room (PDF page 48)**: A retreat participant shares with his roommates using the fourth mantra ("I suffer; please help") that he needs space, helping them understand without taking it personally.

10. **The Woodstove Analogy (PDF page 51)**: Expressing anger physically (shouting, hitting a pillow) is like opening windows on a defective woodstove — the smoke just comes back. You have to fix the woodstove (understand the root of anger).

## Best Practices Mentioned

1. **Reserve time for self-communication daily** — Communicate with yourself before communicating with others

2. **Begin every encounter with mindful awareness** — Breathe before speaking, recognize the Buddha nature in others

3. **Deep listening first** — Listen before you speak. Deep listening needs to come first.

4. **Listen without interrupting** — Even if the person says wrong things, continue to listen. Correct later.

5. **Listen with one purpose** — To help the other person suffer less. Say this as a reminder to yourself.

6. **Pause if not ready to listen** — "I want to listen to you when I'm at my best. Would it be all right if we continued tomorrow?"

7. **Practice the 24-hour rule** — You have 24 hours to practice the fourth mantra after suffering

8. **Write the fourth mantra down** — Put "I suffer. Please help" on a credit card-sized paper in your wallet

9. **Breathe before saying mantras** — Breathe in and out three times before pronouncing any mantra

10. **Check your understanding regularly** — Ask "Do I understand you enough?" and "Do you think I understand you enough?"

11. **Adapt speech to the listener** — Speak according to the person's background, ability, and wisdom level

12. **Tell the truth skillfully** — There are many ways to tell the truth. Find a loving and protective way.

13. **Don't exaggerate** — Stick to the truth, don't make small things seem very big

14. **Be consistent** — Don't use forked tongue or double-talk

15. **Use peaceful language** — Refrain from violent, condemning, abusive, humiliating, or judgmental words

16. **Practice mindful breathing when angry** — Don't communicate when angry. Calm yourself first.

17. **Give yourself a deadline for reconciliation** — Set a deadline to begin reconciling with someone with whom you have difficulty

18. **Change yourself, not the other person** — Don't try to force the other person to change. Transform yourself first.

19. **Don't interrupt during conflict resolution** — Let the other person speak out everything before responding

20. **Use the "I suffer, please help" framework** — Share your suffering, acknowledge you're doing your best, ask for help

21. **Practice "You are partly right"** — Preserve humility whether being praised or criticized

22. **Look deeply into the other person's suffering** — Before judging, understand that their suffering is causing their behavior

23. **Practice with children** — The Six Mantras can be used by children to change tense situations right away

24. **Practice as a group for collective healing** — Create a collective energy of peace and mindfulness when working with divided groups

## Common Pitfalls Mentioned

1. **Communicating when angry** — You aren't lucid when angry. Acting while angry escalates suffering.

2. **Suppressing anger** — Suppressed anger will explode if ignored. You need to get genuinely in touch with it.

3. **Expressing anger physically** — Shouting or hitting a pillow rehearses and nourishes anger rather than transforming it.

4. **Interrupting during deep listening** — Interrupting transforms the session into a debate and ruins everything.

5. **Speaking before listening** — Talking first doesn't usually work. Deep listening needs to come first.

6. **Using double-talk or forked tongue** — Speaking differently to different people for selfish reasons causes division and suffering.

7. **Exaggerating** — Making small things seem very big takes you away from truth and destroys trust.

8. **Using violent or judgmental language** — Wrong speech (cruel, insulting, condemning) causes ill-being.

9. **Telling the truth violently** — Even telling the truth in a violent, attacking way can cause great suffering.

10. **Not looking at your own suffering first** — If you haven't understood your own suffering, you cannot understand another person's.

11. **Waiting for the other person to change** — If you wait for the other person to change, you may spend all your time waiting.

12. **Pride preventing reconciliation** — Pride can prevent you from asking for help, leading to tragedy (as in the Vietnamese couple story).

13. **Pretending you don't suffer** — Being untruthful about your suffering as a way of punishing the other person ("Leave me alone. I can survive very well without you.")

14. **Not asking for help** — When we suffer, we usually do the opposite of asking for help, which makes suffering worse.

15. **Misunderstanding the other person's suffering** — Assuming others are intentionally hurting you when they may not know another way

16. **Losing compassion during listening** — Without practicing mindfulness of compassion, you quickly lose ability to listen when faced with wrong perceptions or bitterness.

17. **Neglecting self-communication** — Without the first step of connecting with yourself, connecting deeply with others isn't possible.

18. **Repeating teachings word for word** — Giving someone exactly the teaching you heard might not be appropriate for that person

19. **Confusing connectivity with communication** — Technology cannot replace authentic communication

20. **Leaving suffering unacknowledged** — Pretending everything is okay when there is difficulty prevents understanding and compassion from arising
