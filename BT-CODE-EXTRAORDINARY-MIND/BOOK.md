# The Code of the Extraordinary Mind

## Overview

"The Code of the Extraordinary Mind" by Vishen Lakhiani is a groundbreaking guide to transcending the limiting rules and beliefs imposed by society, culture, and upbringing. Drawing from his own journey—from a disillusioned Microsoft intern to the founder of Mindvalley—Lakhiani introduces a framework called consciousness engineering, which teaches readers how to identify and upgrade their mental models of reality and daily systems for living. Through ten transformative laws, the book empowers readers to break free from "Brules" (bullshit rules), rewrite disempowering beliefs, master happiness through Blissipline, set meaningful end goals, and ultimately discover their personal quest to make a dent in the universe.

## When to Follow Book Teachings

- When you feel stuck in a career, relationship, or life path that feels dictated by societal expectations rather than personal passion
- When you want to understand why certain beliefs and rules hold you back and learn practical methods to question and replace them
- When you are ready to take control of your happiness, productivity, and sense of purpose using proven frameworks like the Twelve Areas of Balance and the Three Most Important Questions
- When you seek to build daily transcendent practices—such as gratitude, forgiveness, and meditation—that rewire your mind for extraordinary performance and well-being
- When you are ready to move beyond conventional goal-setting and discover a calling that aligns with your deepest values and desires

## Lessons From Book

### Lesson 1

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Steve Jobs' quote

Everything around you that you call life was made up by people no smarter than you, and you can change it.

##### Example 2: Vishen at Microsoft

At 22, Vishen landed his dream internship at Microsoft, working in Bill Gates' home, but felt too ashamed to shake Gates' hand because he hated his job. He deliberately got himself fired and later moved to New York City, working at a nonprofit for a salary below the poverty line.

##### Example 3: Elon Musk at Netscape

Musk walked into Netscape with a resume, waited in the lobby, and when no one spoke to him, said, "Fuck it! I'll just start my own company." That decision eventually led to SpaceX, Tesla, and PayPal.

##### Example 4: The Himba tribe

The Himba people of Namibia have many words for green but no word for blue. When shown a circle of green squares with one blue square, they couldn't easily identify the blue outlier—proving that language shapes what we can perceive.

#### Step 1: Recognize that you live in two worlds: the physical world of absolute truth (rocks are hard, water is wet) and the culturescape—the world of relative truth made of beliefs, rules, and constructs passed down through generations.

#### Step 2: Identify the rules you are living by that were installed by family, culture, education, or society without your conscious choice.

#### Step 3: Categorize rules as either physical/law-based (absolute) or social/cultural constructs (relative).

#### Step 4: Practice questioning: Ask yourself whether a rule serves your happiness and goals or is simply a "should" inherited from others.

#### Step 5: Accept that safety is often overrated. Embrace the idea that life is meant to be a beautiful joyride with ups and downs, not a slow, steady, safe linear progression.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Accept that the rules of the culturescape are not absolute and can be rewritten
- ✅ Look for disempowering rules that create boredom, stagnation, or unhappiness
- ✅ Study extraordinary people who questioned their culturescape (Elon Musk, Dean Kamen, Richard Branson)
- ✅ Welcome dips and failures as opportunities for growth and learning
- ❌ Don't blindly follow rules just because everyone else does
- ❌ Don't confuse safety with a good life—avoid letting fear keep you stuck

#### Keep In Mind

- The culturescape is real in its effects even though it exists in our heads. It governs how societies act, but very real does not mean very right.
- Language shapes perception. If you have no word for a concept, you may not be able to perceive it.
- A fish is the last to discover water because it has been swimming in it all its life. Few people realize how powerful the culturescape really is.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Questioning the culturescape can anger loved ones, cause relationship strain, or lead to social pushback.
- You may need to make difficult decisions about your career, religion, family expectations, or relationships.
- The author warns: You might decide to leave your current relationship, raise your kids with different beliefs, question your religion, or start a business.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Following rules just to avoid disappointing others.
  **Solution:** Remember that staying on a path that makes you miserable ultimately disappoints everyone, including yourself.
- **Problem:** Assuming that because something has always been done a certain way, it must be the right way.
  **Solution:** Apply the Brule Test from Lesson 2 to every major rule in your life.
- **Problem:** Thinking that safety and predictability are the keys to a good life.
  **Solution:** Reframe life as a non-linear journey where dips and rises are natural and necessary for growth.

### Lesson 2

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The beef Brule

At age nine, Vishen questioned the Hindu rule against eating beef. After researching, he reasoned that ancient Hindus may have avoided beef because cows were useful pets and working animals, not because of an absolute divine command. His mom agreed, and he ate his first beef burger (which was overrated).

##### Example 2: The college Brule

Google senior VP Laszlo Bock stated that college degrees aren't as important as they once were. Ernst & Young announced they would no longer consider grades as the main criteria for recruitment.

##### Example 3: The marriage Brule

Vishen's family expected him to marry within the Sindhi ethnicity. He married Kristina, who is Estonian. Their children learn multiple languages and participate in Russian Orthodox, Lutheran, and Hindu traditions without being locked into any one path.

##### Example 4: The hard work Brule

A Gallup study shows people who find meaning and joy in their work retire much later than those who don't. Working hard at something you love doesn't feel like work.

##### Example 5: Dean Kamen and North Dumpling Island

Kamen questioned the Brule of the nation-state and created his own country, North Dumpling Island, complete with its own constitution, anthem, and currency (the dumpling), backed by Ben & Jerry's ice cream.

#### Step 1: Learn the definition of a Brule: A bulls**t rule adopted to simplify understanding of the world, handed down by tribe, family, culture, or educational system.

#### Step 2: Understand the five ways Brules get installed: Childhood indoctrination, authority figures, need to belong, social proof, and internal insecurities.

#### Step 3: Apply the Five-Question Brule Test to any rule you suspect might be a Brule:
   - Question 1: Is it based on trust and hope in humanity?
   - Question 2: Does it violate the Golden Rule?
   - Question 3: Did I take it on from culture or religion?
   - Question 4: Is it based on rational choice or contagion?
   - Question 5: Does it serve my happiness?

#### Step 4: For each Brule identified, decide whether to question, modify, or discard it.

#### Step 5: Replace outdated Brules with rules that actually serve your goals and well-being.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Constantly question rules, especially those that cause guilt, shame, or limitation
- ✅ Apply the Brule Test to career, relationship, religious, and lifestyle rules
- ✅ Recognize that social contagion causes people to adopt beliefs without rational choice
- ✅ Embrace the idea that "My family/culture/people have always done it this way" is not an acceptable argument for continuing a rule
- ❌ Don't reject all rules indiscriminately—distinguish between moral/ethical standards and outdated social constructs
- ❌ Don't let the need to belong override your independent thinking

#### Keep In Mind

- We inherit and transmit behaviors, emotions, beliefs, and religions not through rational choice but through contagion.
- When we are unsure how to react, we actively look to others for guidance and consciously imitate them.
- Brules are powerful because they provide mental shortcuts, but they often remain long past their expiration dates.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Breaking Brules can lead to being labeled a misfit, rebel, or troublemaker.
- Family and friends may push back or make you feel guilty for choosing your own path.
- Certain Brules (such as those involving law or physical safety) should still be respected.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Rejecting all rules and falling into chaos.
  **Solution:** Focus on rules that limit your potential, happiness, or authenticity, not on fundamental ethical standards.
- **Problem:** Staying silent about your choices to avoid conflict.
  **Solution:** Communicate your choices with compassion and clarity. You can honor your relationships while living by your own code.
- **Problem:** Assuming that because something is cultural, it must be valuable.
  **Solution:** Culture is like water—most beautiful and useful when it's moving. Stagnant culture becomes poisonous.

### Lesson 3

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Vishen's sales transformation

After a two-day meditation class in Los Angeles, Vishen applied alpha-state meditation and intuition to his phone sales job. Instead of calling lawyers A to Z, he ran his finger down the listings and called those where he felt an impulse. His closing rate tripled, and within nine months he was promoted to director of sales.

##### Example 2: The Achuar tribe

In the Ecuadorian Amazon, the Achuar people drink chicha—a fermented beverage made from chewed yucca roots and saliva—instead of water because their pond water is full of bacteria. What seems "normal" to us is nothing more than what was programmed into us.

##### Example 3: Hotel maid study

Researchers told one group of hotel maids that their daily work met CDC exercise guidelines. A month later, those maids had lost weight, lowered blood pressure, and showed improved body fat and BMI—without changing their actions, only their belief about their work.

##### Example 4: Jon Butcher's Lifebook

Jon Butcher divided his life into twelve categories and mapped out beliefs, vision, strategy, and purpose for each. This inspired Vishen's Twelve Areas of Balance.

#### Step 1: Understand the two components of consciousness engineering:
   - **Models of Reality (Hardware)**: Your beliefs about the world. These can be swapped out like computer hardware when they become outdated.
   - **Systems for Living (Software)**: Your daily habits and processes for putting your beliefs into practice. These can be upgraded like apps.

#### Step 2: Rate your life in the Twelve Areas of Balance on a scale of 1 to 10:

1. Love Relationship
2. Friendships
3. Adventures
4. Environment
5. Health and Fitness
6. Intellectual Life
7. Skills
8. Spiritual Life
9. Career
10. Creative Life
11. Family Life
12. Community Life

#### Step 3: Identify the areas with the lowest scores—these are where you likely have disempowering models or obsolete systems.

#### Step 4: Treat yourself as a highly tuned operating system ready to install new hardware (beliefs) or new apps (habits) whenever needed.

#### Step 5: Never get attached to your current models or systems because newer, better ones are always being discovered.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ View every personal growth book or course as either a model upgrade (new belief) or a system upgrade (new habit)
- ✅ Use the Twelve Areas of Balance as a regular health check for your life
- ✅ Think of models as swappable hardware and systems as updatable software
- ✅ Embrace the idea that you are not your beliefs—you can change them at will
- ❌ Don't cling to outdated models just because they are familiar
- ❌ Don't assume that because a system worked in the past, it will work forever

#### Keep In Mind

- Our models of reality do more than create feelings—they influence the actual reality we experience every day.
- Most modern models and systems are lacking in conscious practices. We've evolved our physical systems rapidly but our spiritual and mental systems are stuck in the past.
- The Achuar wake at 4 a.m. to share dreams and drink wayusa tea, treating the dream world as equally important as the waking world.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Some practices (such as ayahuasca) involve plant-based ceremonial brews and should only be undertaken with proper guidance and in safe contexts.
- The Achuar's way of life is radically different and may not be replicable or advisable in modern contexts.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Thinking that models of reality are fixed and unchangeable.
  **Solution:** Adopt the single most effective model: your models of reality are swappable.
- **Problem:** Trying to change everything at once.
  **Solution:** Focus on one or two areas at a time, starting with the lowest-rated Twelve Areas.
- **Problem:** Believing that personal growth means abandoning your culture entirely.
  **Solution:** Appreciate your culture but let it flow and evolve. Don't buy into dogma that requires everything to stay the same as it was generations ago.

### Lesson 4

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The monk in the hot tub

In Fiji, a monk told Vishen he had low self-esteem. Vishen realized he was overly defensive in meetings, sensitive to criticism, and driven by a need to prove he was enough—a belief installed in childhood.

##### Example 2: Mary's kiss at the dance

In college, Vishen kissed the prettiest girl in the room while dancing. Instead of being offended, she said, "Are you kidding? You're f***ing hot." That single event shattered his model that he was unattractive and transformed his dating life.

##### Example 3: Marisa Peer's hypnotherapy

Vishen regressed to three childhood moments that installed disempowering beliefs: (1) "To be a great teacher, you have to suffer," (2) "Don't ask for too much because someone will get hurt if you do," (3) "Do not stand out. It's not safe to stand out." Within four months of removing these beliefs, his income doubled.

 ##### Example 4: The Pygmalion Effect

Teachers told that certain randomly selected students were high-performers caused those students to actually score higher—proving that others' beliefs about us influence our reality.

#### Step 1: Discover your hidden models of reality by reflecting on childhood experiences that caused you to form beliefs about yourself, others, and the world.

#### Step 2: Use the "What I Love about Myself" exercise and the Gratitude Exercise daily to root out disempowering models before they take hold.

#### Step 3: Ask yourself two questions when evaluating a model:
   - Question 1: Is my model of reality absolute truth or relative truth?
   - Question 2: Does this really mean what I think it means?

#### Step 4: Replace disempowering models with empowering ones by:
   - Identifying the old belief
   - Finding evidence that contradicts it
   - Creating a new, more empowering belief
   - Reinforcing the new belief through repetition and experience

#### Step 5: Use the Lefkoe belief process: Ask "What" questions instead of "Why" questions to get to the root of a problem without triggering defensiveness.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Practice the nightly ritual with your children (or yourself): Ask about one thing they were grateful for and one thing they loved about themselves
- ✅ Ask "What happened?" instead of "Why did you do that?" when dealing with children or yourself
- ✅ Adopt the four new external models: human intuition is real, mind-body healing works, happiness at work is the new productivity, and it is possible to be spiritual but not religious
- ✅ Use creative visualization to heal physical or emotional issues (Vishen healed his acne in four weeks using this technique)
- ❌ Don't assume that just because you have a belief, it is true or serves you
- ❌ Don't ignore the meaning-making machine—it runs constantly and creates meanings that may be distorted or damaging

#### Keep In Mind

- Our models of reality lie below the surface. Often we do not realize we have them until some intervention or contemplative practice makes us aware.
- The meaning-making machine never sleeps. It runs during childhood and adulthood, creating meanings about events that are often simplistic and distorted.
- A simple suggestion can change what we think about ourselves and even our bodies, inside and out.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Hypnotherapy and regression should be conducted with qualified professionals.
- When working with childhood memories, be gentle with yourself and consider professional support if trauma is involved.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that a disempowering belief is simply "the truth."
  **Solution:** Challenge every belief by asking: Is this absolute truth or relative truth? Can I find evidence that contradicts this?
- **Problem:** Replacing one belief with another without evidence.
  **Solution:** Use the Lefkoe process and ask "What" questions to understand the actual event, not the story you've built around it.
- **Problem:** Trying to change beliefs through willpower alone.
  **Solution:** Combine rational examination with practices like meditation, visualization, and hypnotherapy for faster results.

### Lesson 5

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Richard Branson's system

"It's all about finding and hiring people smarter than you, getting them to join your business and giving them good work, then getting out of the way and trusting them. You must make them see their work as a mission."

##### Example 2: Vishen's writing system

It took Vishen three years to figure out what to write about, one year to get a framework done, and three months to write the first chapter. By optimizing his systems (developing methods for titles, frameworks, and story styles), he eventually wrote an entire chapter in a single day.

##### Example 3: Patrick Grove

Grove solved massive problems by stepping out of the "do-do trap"—being so busy doing that he never stepped back to think about whether his systems were obsolete. In 2013, he acquired three car websites across Southeast Asia and built them into a $100 million valuation in one year.

##### Example 4: Mindvalley's Learn Day

On the first Friday of every month, nobody works unless it's crucial. Instead, everyone focuses on learning how to work better—researching, reading, experimenting with new tools.

#### Step 1: Identify the three stages of upgrading systems for living:
   - **Discovery Process**: Find new systems from books, conferences, courses, or experimentation. Treat it like browsing the App Store.
   - **Refresh Rate**: Decide how often you will upgrade your systems (Vishen experiments with a new exercise system every year).
   - **Set Points and Measurement**: Establish nonnegotiable minimums and measure whether your new system is actually better.

#### Step 2: Apply the three-step method to upgrade a system:
   - Step 1: Identify areas where you need changes (look at your lowest-scoring Twelve Areas of Balance).
   - Step 2: Determine your set points—specific, absolutely achievable minimums you will not go below.
   - Step 3: Test your set points weekly and immediately correct if you slip.
   - Step 4: When you recover, turn up the heat slightly—add one more unit (e.g., 50 push-ups becomes 51) to ensure continuous growth.

#### Step 3: For each Twelve Area of Balance, select one book to read that might give you a bold new perspective.

#### Step 4: Use Mindvalley's Learn Day concept: dedicate time each month to step back from doing and think about how to improve your systems.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Try to upgrade at least one system every week, not because things are broken but because it's energizing
- ✅ Use set points as detection methods—they tell you when you're slipping before it becomes a crisis
- ✅ Turn up the heat gradually (e.g., 50 push-ups to 51) rather than making unrealistic jumps
- ✅ Make set points nonnegotiable to prevent stagnation and ensure growth with age
- ✅ Read widely and browse the "App Store" of personal growth systems regularly
- ❌ Don't confuse set points with goals—goals pull you forward, set points establish minimum thresholds you will not drop below. Unlike goals that pull you forward, set points prevent backsliding. However, once you consistently meet a set point, you can raise it incrementally (e.g., 50 → 51 push-ups) to ensure continuous growth.
- ❌ Don't skip the measurement step—if you can't measure effectiveness, you can't improve it

#### Keep In Mind

- Even as we enter one of the most disruptive eras in human history, our systems and structures still live on past their expiration dates.
- Billions of people wake up with stress, anxiety, and fear and do nothing about it because they assume it's normal. It isn't.
- "Happy hour" shouldn't be the drinks you have on Friday to celebrate getting through another week.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- When experimenting with new health or fitness systems, consult professionals and listen to your body.
- Set points should be achievable—setting the bar too high is punishing and unrealistic.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Falling into the "do-do trap"—being so busy doing that you never step back to evaluate.
  **Solution:** Schedule regular Learn Days or reflection time to assess whether your systems are still optimal.
- **Problem:** Setting goals without measurement mechanisms.
  **Solution:** Pair every new goal or system with a measurable set point and a weekly check-in.
- **Problem:** Abandoning set points when life gets busy.
  **Solution:** Make set points nonnegotiable. If you slip, immediately initiate a correction procedure.
- **Problem:** Thinking that because a system worked once, it will work forever.
  **Solution:** Upgrade your refresh rate. Experiment with new systems regularly.

### Lesson 6

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Vishen's Starbucks business

In 2003, Vishen's side business (Mindvalley) earned $4.50 per day—enough for a Grande Café Mocha. It grew to $5.50 (Venti), then $6.50 (Venti with hazelnut), then a Subway sandwich. He gamified his life before he knew what gamification was.

##### Example 2: The 2008 turnaround

In May 2008, Mindvalley was losing $15,000 per month and Vishen was about to lay off employees. He shifted his model from stress-and-anxiety to "be happy now," wrote down a $300K revenue target, hit it, took the team to the beach, set a $500K target, and then a $1 million target. By December 2008, monthly revenue hit $1 million.

##### Example 3: Richard Branson

When asked, "What do you do when you're sad?" Branson replied, "I can't remember the bad times. I only remember the good things that happened in my life."

##### Example 4: John D. Rockefeller's poem

Written at age 86, "I was early taught to work as well as play, My life has been one long, happy holiday."

#### Step 1: Understand the Four States of Human Living:
   - **Negative Spiral**: Not happy in the now and no vision for the future. Avoid this state.
   - **Current Reality Trap**: Happy now but no vision. This brings temporary happiness but not long-term fulfillment.
   - **Stress and Anxiety**: Big goals but happiness tied to attaining them. This causes burnout.
   - **Bending Reality**: Happy in the now AND have an exciting vision for the future. This is the ideal state.

#### Step 2: Adopt the core model: "Have big goals—but don't tie your happiness to your goals. You must be happy before you attain them."

#### Step 3: Cultivate happiness in the now through:
   - Gratitude for what you already have (reverse gap thinking)
   - Focusing on the journey, not just the destination
   - Avoiding the "if/then" model of happiness

#### Step 4: Develop an exciting vision for your future:
   - Ensure goals are not Brule-based
   - Visualize what you don't yet know (don't limit yourself to what you can already imagine)
   - Avoid overestimating short-term and underestimating long-term capabilities

#### Step 5: Use the Eight Statements exercise to gauge where you are on the path to bending reality.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Be happy now, not when you reach some future milestone
- ✅ Keep the reverse gap in mind: measure backward from where you came, not forward to an ever-receding horizon
- ✅ Let happiness fuel your vision rather than being a reward for achieving it
- ✅ Remember that we should not do things so we can be happy—we should be happy so we can do things
- ❌ Don't tie your happiness to outcomes—this creates a swirling spiral of unhappy crises
- ❌ Don't confuse means goals with end goals when creating your vision

#### Keep In Mind

- Your thoughts and beliefs create your reality, but only when your present state is joyful.
- When you're bending reality, it feels as if the universe has your back and luck is on your side.
- The right model of reality to choose is one that serves you. Whether it's the law of attraction or the reticular activating system, what matters is that it works.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Being happy now does not mean being complacent or lacking ambition. It means enjoying the pursuit itself.
- Avoid using "being present" as an excuse to avoid planning for the future.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Tying happiness to future achievements ("I'll be happy when I get that promotion...").
  **Solution:** Practice the reverse gap daily—list things you're grateful for that you already have.
- **Problem:** Chasing goals that are really Brules in disguise.
  **Solution:** Ask yourself if the goal speaks to your soul or simply conforms to what others expect.
- **Problem:** Being happy now but having no vision (Current Reality Trap).
  **Solution:** Pair daily bliss practices with bold, soul-stirring end goals.
- **Problem:** Having big goals but postponing happiness (Stress and Anxiety).
  **Solution:** Reframe your daily work as part of the joy, not just a means to an end.

### Lesson 7

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Richard Branson dancing on the table

At dinner on Necker Island, Branson halted a serious business conversation, climbed onto the table, and danced with Kristina. He demonstrated that happiness is under our control and that life is not all business.

##### Example 2: Doctors and candy

In a study, doctors primed to feel happy (given candy they couldn't eat) were almost twice as speedy at making the right diagnosis and avoided anchoring bias better than the control group.

##### Example 3: Martin Seligman's optimism study

Among 15,000 newly hired MetLife salespeople, the top 10 percent of optimists performed 88 percent better than the top 10 percent of pessimists.

##### Example 4: 40 Years of Zen

Vishen participated in a biofeedback program where the secret to increasing alpha waves was forgiveness. Each time he forgave someone, his alpha waves spiked.

##### Example 5: The man who stole from Mindvalley

Vishen had to forgive an employee who stole over $100,000 and then threatened him. After the forgiveness exercise, his brain wave score hit its highest level ever.

#### Step 1: Understand the three types of happiness:
   - **Happiness from Special and Unique Experiences**: Short-term, chemically induced bliss (e.g., winning, sex, great food). In small doses, fantastic; in excess, addictive and destructive.
   - **Happiness from Growth and Awakening**: Enduring happiness from spiritual practices, meditation, and reaching higher states of consciousness.
   - **Happiness from Meaning**: The fulfillment that comes from contributing to something larger than yourself, even when it requires sacrifice.

#### Step 2: Practice the three Blissipline systems daily:
   - **System 1: The Power of Gratitude**: Every morning and evening, list 3-5 things you're grateful for in your personal life and 3-5 in your work life. Focus on the reverse gap—how far you've come, not how far you have to go.
   - **System 2: Forgiveness**: Forgive everyone who has wronged you, from minor annoyances to major betrayals. Use the Liberation Exercise:
     - Step 1: Set the scene—relive the moment with your eyes closed.
     - Step 2: Feel the anger and pain—but only for a few minutes.
     - Step 3: Forgive into love—feel compassion, ask what you learned, imagine the pain the other person may have been carrying.
   - **System 3: The Practice of Giving**: Do one nice thing a day for someone. Give time, love, understanding, skills, ideas, or physical help. Bring systems like Love Week to your workplace.

#### Step 3: Practice the reverse gap: When you feel discouraged, turn around and measure backward from your starting point rather than forward toward an ever-receding ideal.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Begin and end every day with gratitude—morning and night
- ✅ Focus on feelings, not just mechanical list-making
- ✅ Share gratitude practices with kids, partners, and coworkers
- ✅ Forgive into love, not just "let go"—criminal acts still need to be reported
- ✅ Give without expecting anything in return; the act of giving itself generates bliss
- ✅ Do the "What I Love about Myself" exercise daily
- ❌ Don't make gratitude a mechanical exercise—focus on the actual feelings
- ❌ Don't skip forgiveness because it feels uncomfortable—alpha waves (creativity, compassion) are suppressed by grudges
- ❌ Don't tie giving to outcomes—give for the joy of giving

#### Keep In Mind

- Our brains are hardwired to perform at their best when they are positive, not negative or neutral.
- Hedonic adaptation means we return to a baseline happiness level after events. But our happiness set point is hackable and can be raised.
- The Dalai Lama said: "But who can you help if you're unhappy?" Bliss is not selfish—it allows you to help others more effectively.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Forgiveness does not mean tolerating abuse or failing to report crimes. You can forgive someone while still taking protective action.
- Some forgiveness exercises can bring up intense emotions. Be patient with yourself and seek support if needed.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Making gratitude a mechanical checklist.
  **Solution:** For each item, spend 5-10 seconds letting the feeling well up. If it doesn't feel genuine, keep searching.
- **Problem:** Forcing yourself to forgive before you're ready.
  **Solution:** Be patient. For a serious offense, forgiveness can take hours or days. For a small offense, five minutes may be enough.
- **Problem:** Thinking that being happy means you don't care about problems.
  **Solution:** Blissipline means dealing with adversity from a positive place. You can be empathetic and feel for others while maintaining your own happiness.
- **Problem:** Giving to look good or expecting reciprocation.
  **Solution:** Give without attachment. The act of giving itself is what generates bliss.

### Lesson 8

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Vishen's end goals

Reaching the top of Mount Kinabalu at sunrise; honeymoon in Svalbard with Kristina; seeing his daughter dance for the first time; inviting employees into his dream office.

##### Example 2: A-Fest as an end goal

After writing down his true end goals in 2010 (travel, luxury, friends, fun, meeting legends, teaching), Vishen started A-Fest. It emerged organically and fulfilled every item on his bucket list.

##### Example 3: Amir's blueprint

Amir from Mindvalley wrote down goals to be a professional speaker and author. By 26, he had written "My Islam: How Fundamentalism Stole My Mind—And Doubt Freed My Soul," recommended by Foreign Policy as one of the 25 must-read books of 2013.

##### Example 4: Luminita Saviuc

She wrote "be a world-renowned author and speaker" on her blueprint. Her blog post "15 Things to Give Up to Be Happy" went viral on Facebook with 1.2 million shares, leading to a book deal.

#### Step 1: Distinguish between means goals and end goals:
   - **Means goals** have a "so" in them (e.g., "Get a good GPA so you can get into a good college"). They are stepping-stones to something else and often conform to Brules.
   - **End goals** are about experiences, growth, and contribution. They bring joy in and of themselves and are not undertaken for material reward.

#### Step 2: Ask yourself the Three Most Important Questions:
   - **Question 1**: What experiences do you want to have in this lifetime?
   - **Question 2**: How do you want to grow?
   - **Question 3**: How do you want to contribute?

#### Step 3: Do the exercise with a 3-minute timer per category to shut down your logical mind and let your intuition flow.

#### Step 4: Apply the five-step goal-check process (designed by Mia Koning):
   - Step 1: Identify a goal.
   - Step 2: Answer: "When I achieve this goal, I will be able to ___, ___, ___."
   - Step 3: Answer: "When I achieve all this, I will feel ___, ___, ___."
   - Step 4: Identify the true underlying objectives based on steps 2 and 3.
   - Step 5: Compare: Is the original goal the only or best way to achieve these objectives?

#### Step 5: Create your "Blueprint for the Soul" on a sheet of paper with three columns: Experiences, Growth, Contribution. Post it publicly and share it with others.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Focus on feelings—what feelings will a goal bring?
- ✅ Trust your intuition and don't overthink; use the three-minute timer
- ✅ Make sure goals are scary and exciting (per Joe Vitale: "A good goal should scare you a little and excite you a lot.")
- ✅ Distinguish end goals from means goals ruthlessly
- ✅ Share your blueprint with others to create accountability and inspiration
- ✅ Review your blueprint regularly and update it as you evolve
- ❌ Don't choose a career as an end goal—let your career find you based on your end goals
- ❌ Don't set goals solely for money, titles, or external validation

#### Keep In Mind

- When means goals become your focus, you miss the point. Money and objects only generate experiences.
- Your mind will find a way to bring you whatever goal you set—so make sure it's truly what your heart wants.
- Steve Jobs said: "You can't connect the dots looking forward; you can only connect them looking backward. So you have to trust that the dots will somehow connect in your future."

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Don't share your blueprint with people who will belittle your dreams. Share it with supportive, like-minded individuals.
- Be prepared for your goals to evolve as you grow. The blueprint is a living document.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Confusing means goals with end goals.
  **Solution:** Ask "why" five times. If the answer still involves "so I can..." it's probably a means goal.
- **Problem:** Setting goals based on what you think you should want.
  **Solution:** Use the timer method to bypass the logical mind and access what you truly desire.
- **Problem:** Setting goals that depend on other people's actions.
  **Solution:** Make sure your goals are within your control. For example, "To be consistently surrounded by love" is better than "To be in a loving relationship with a specific person."
- **Problem:** Forgetting to review and update the blueprint.
  **Solution:** Post it where you see it daily and revisit it monthly or quarterly.

   - Question 1: Recall a time when you experienced Heaven on Earth. What was happening?
   - Question 2: Imagine you have a magic wand and can create Heaven on Earth. What is Heaven on Earth for you?

   - All of us are connected to all life
   - We are capable of intuitive insights from this connection
   - There is a higher collective mind that picks individual Godicles to work on visions
   - We are endowed with God-like powers

#### Step 5: Take a baby step. Even registering a company name (as Vishen did with Mindvalley) signals intention to the universe. Don't wait to know the full path—just take the first step.
