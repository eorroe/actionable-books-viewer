# Chapter 6 Analysis: Bend Reality

## 1. Actionable Lessons

### Lesson 1: Adopt the Bending Reality Core Principle

**Description:** The foundational insight from Vishen Lakhiani's personal crisis: stop postponing happiness. Have big, bold goals for your future, but do NOT tie your happiness to achieving them. Be happy now, in the present moment, and let that happiness fuel your pursuit of your vision.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Write down your big, bold goals for the future (your vision).
2. Separately, write down what makes you happy right now (independent of those goals).
3. Check: Are you tying your happiness to future goal attainment? ("I'll be happy when...")
4. Reframe: "I am happy now AND I am pursuing my vision."
5. Each day, begin with a gratitude practice for what you already have before working toward goals.
6. When you notice anxiety about outcomes, return to present-moment happiness as the baseline.

**Best Practices:**
- Keep the vision bold—this is what pulls you forward.
- Separate "happiness" from "attainment of goals" in your mental model.
- Use past small wins as evidence that happiness can exist now regardless of scale.
- Let happiness be the fuel, not the reward.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Tying happiness to external outcomes (a deal, promotion, relationship status).
- Abandoning goals entirely in favor of present happiness only (that's the Current Reality Trap).
- Using "I should be happy now" to suppress legitimate negative emotions rather than transform them.
- Setting tiny, uninspiring goals because you're afraid to fail.

**Keep in Mind Notes:**
- This is NOT the "Current Reality Trap"—it requires BOTH present happiness AND future vision.
- The author's business only turned around after he made happiness a daily practice, not after new strategies.
- Happiness is a choice available now, not a reward for future achievement.

### Lesson 2: Understand and Identify the Four States of Human Living

**Description:** Depending on the balance between "happiness in the now" and "vision for the future," you can be in one of four distinct states. Only one—Bending Reality—is optimal for extraordinary performance.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Assess your current state by answering two questions:
   - Am I happy right now, independent of future outcomes?
   - Do I have a compelling vision of the future pulling me forward?
2. Map your current state:
   - **Not happy now + No vision** = Negative Spiral (depression zone)
   - **Happy now + No vision** = Current Reality Trap (temporary pleasure, no fulfillment)
   - **Not happy now + Have vision (tied to goals)** = Stress and Anxiety (burnout zone)
   - **Happy now + Have vision** = Bending Reality (optimal state)
3. If you're in the Stress and Anxiety state, practice Lesson 1 to decouple happiness from goals.
4. If you're in the Current Reality Trap, develop an exciting vision for your future (see Lesson 4).
5. If you're in the Negative Spiral, first build small daily happiness practices, then add vision work.

**Best Practices:**
- Honestly assess which state you spend the most time in.
- Recognize that the Stress and Anxiety state feels productive but actually destroys performance.
- Understand that "balance" is the key—not maximizing one at the expense of the other.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Mistaking the Stress and Anxiety state for ambition or dedication.
- Thinking the Current Reality Trap is the goal (vacation-mode permanently).
- Believing the Negative Spiral is permanent or reflective of reality rather than a mental state.
- Trying to skip the "happy now" foundation and jump straight to big visions.

### Lesson 3: Practice Being Happy in the Now

**Description:** Happiness in the present moment is the first essential ingredient of Bending Reality. It stems from the pursuit of your vision combined with gratitude for what you already have—not from achieving future milestones.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Identify what currently drains your joy (e.g., tying self-worth to revenue numbers).
2. Build daily practices that generate present-moment happiness:
   - Morning gratitude list (3-5 things)
   - Celebrate small wins (as the author did with $4.50/day profits)
   - Infuse fun into your workday (games, social connection, playful challenges)
3. When you achieve a goal, celebrate immediately rather than immediately moving to the next goal.
4. Notice the difference between happiness as a by-product of the journey versus a reward for the destination.
5. Study extraordinary people you admire—notice whether they seem perpetually happy despite challenges.

**Best Practices:**
- Use gamification: make progress feel like playing a game you love.
- Create micro-rewards that are accessible daily (e.g., the author's Starbucks progression).
- Share wins with your team to amplify collective happiness.
- Remember Richard Branson's approach: "I can't remember the bad times. I only remember the good things."

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Using happiness practices to avoid dealing with real problems—happiness doesn't mean ignoring issues.
- Waiting for "enough" success before allowing yourself to be happy.
- Assuming that being happy means being unproductive or unserious.
- Depending on others for your happiness (make it an internal practice).

**Security/Safety Notes:**
- Happiness practices should not be used to justify staying in toxic situations (abusive relationships, dangerous jobs) under the guise of "being positive."
- Mental health conditions (clinical depression, anxiety disorders) may require professional intervention alongside any happiness practices.

### Lesson 4: Develop an Exciting Vision for Your Future

**Description:** The second essential ingredient of Bending Reality is a bold, soul-speaking vision for the future. Extraordinary people live partly in the future, and this vision pulls them forward with energy and purpose.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Identify areas in your life where you feel a pull to contribute, create, or explore.
2. Ask: "If money were no object, what would I spend my time doing?"
3. Ensure your vision is NOT a Brule (a social/cultural construct you absorbed)—ask whether it truly speaks to your soul.
4. Write your vision as a compelling, specific future scenario (not just a metric).
5. Keep the vision visible (wall, phone wallpaper, team display) to maintain the pull.
6. Set stretch goals aligned with the vision, but decouple your happiness from hitting them.

**Best Practices:**
- Focus on end goals (the vision) rather than means goals (societal milestones like job titles).
- Recognize that extraordinary people have visions in all domains: art, service, products, family, adventure.
- Balance being present (happiness now) with living in the future (vision)—both are necessary.
- Review and refine your vision periodically, but avoid constant rewriting.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Confusing Brules with genuine visions (e.g., "I want a corner office" may be a Brule, not a soul-vision).
- Setting goals so small they don't pull you forward.
- Visualizing only what you already know—limiting yourself to familiar possibilities.
- Overestimating short-term results and underestimating long-term potential.
- Letting others define your vision for you.

### Lesson 5: Break the "If/Then" Happiness Model

**Description:** Most people operate on a flawed "if/then" model: "If I get X (job, partner, house), then I'll be happy." This places happiness outside your control and creates a vicious cycle of postponement. Flip the model.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. List all your "if/then" happiness beliefs (e.g., "If I hit $1M revenue, THEN I'll be happy").
2. For each one, ask: "Is this actually true? Have I ever achieved something similar and found the happiness lasted?"
3. Reframe each: "Happiness is available to me NOW, regardless of X."
4. Choose one "if/then" belief to dismantle this week.
5. Practice being happy about the process, not just the outcome.
6. When you notice yourself saying "I'll be happy when...," interrupt and reframe to "I choose to be happy now as I pursue..."

**Best Practices:**
- Recognize that happiness accelerates goal attainment—it is a performance enhancer, not a distraction.
- Use evidence from your own past: when were you happiest? Was it tied to achievements?
- Separate the feeling of happiness from the condition that supposedly creates it.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Using this lesson to justify never setting goals or pursuing ambitious outcomes.
- Assuming that because you CAN be happy now, you should never feel disappointment or sadness.
- Thinking that choosing happiness means suppressing negative emotions.
- Abandoning the pursuit of goals because "I can be happy without them."

**Keep in Mind Notes:**
- The author was in a "swirling spiral of unhappy crises" precisely because he tied his happiness to business success.
- Happiness should not be the reward—it should be the fuel.
- When happy, you perform better, attract more opportunities, and "kick ass in life."

### Lesson 6: Avoid the Three Vision-Hacking Traps

**Description:** Modern goal-setting often leads us astray in three specific ways. Recognizing these traps helps you set visions that are truly extraordinary rather than culturally mandated.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Trap 1: Confusing Brules with Goals**
   - Review each of your goals and ask: "Is this MY vision, or something society told me I should want?"
   - Discard or reframe Brule-based goals.
2. **Trap 2: Only Visualizing What You Know**
   - Ask: "What visions might exist that I haven't even imagined yet?"
   - Seek inspiration outside your usual field (cross-pollination).
   - Explore "unseen, unknown visions" that may be unique gifts only you can give.
3. **Trap 3: Bad Timeframe Predictions**
   - For short-term goals (next year): undercommit—don't bite off more than you can chew.
   - For long-term goals (3+ years): overcommit—expect far more of yourself than you currently imagine possible.
   - Calibrate your planning accordingly.

**Best Practices:**
- Regularly audit your goals for Brule contamination.
- Expose yourself to diverse ideas and role models to expand your vision vocabulary.
- Use the "3-year rule": if you can't see how to get there in 3 years, don't abandon it—just begin.
- Remember: extraordinary minds create their own goals rather than adopting the "infectious wants" of the culturescape.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Believing a goal is authentic simply because it feels important or is widely pursued.
- Assuming your current imagination limits what's actually possible for you.
- Getting discouraged because you're not progressing as fast as you expected (timeframe miscalculation).

### Lesson 7: Apply Law 6: Bend Reality

**Description:** Law 6 formalizes the chapter's thesis. Extraordinary minds are able to bend reality—they have bold, exciting visions for the future while remaining happy in the present. This balance allows them to move toward their visions faster while having fun. To outsiders, they appear lucky.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Explicitly state your vision in writing (bold, specific, future-oriented).
2. Assess your present happiness level independent of that vision.
3. If present happiness is low, apply Lessons 1 and 3 before pursuing the vision.
4. If the vision is weak or Brule-based, apply Lesson 4 before proceeding.
5. Each morning, remind yourself: "I am happy now AND I am moving toward my vision."
6. Track progress weekly, but measure journey joy and milestone achievement separately.
7. Notice when opportunities, people, and coincidences begin to gravitate toward you—this is a sign you are bending reality.

**Best Practices:**
- Treat this as a practice (Blissipline) rather than a one-time insight.
- Share the concept with your team or community—collective bending reality amplifies results.
- Use the Eight Statements exercise (below) to regularly self-assess.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Assuming "bending reality" means passive wishing without action.
- Expecting immediate results before the state is fully internalized.
- Using "the universe has my back" as an excuse for lack of planning or effort.
- Trying to bend reality for trivial or harmful ends (the model serves you best when aligned with genuine contribution).

### Lesson 8: Use the Eight Statements Exercise to Self-Assess

**Description:** A simple self-assessment tool to gauge where you stand on the path to bending reality. Statements 1-4 measure happiness in the now; statements 5-8 measure vision for the future.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Read all eight statements below.
2. For each, rate yourself: NOT AT ALL TRUE / SOMETIMES TRUE / VERY TRUE.
3. Tally results:
   - If Statements 1-4 are "Very True": You are likely happy in the now.
   - If Statements 5-8 are "Very True": You likely have a good vision for the future.
   - If ALL EIGHT are "Very True": You are likely bending reality.
4. Most people score high on one dimension but not both. Identify your gap.
5. If low on happiness: focus on Lesson 3 practices.
6. If low on vision: focus on Lesson 4 practices.
7. Retake monthly to track progress.

**The Eight Statements:**
1. I love my current job to the point where it does not feel like work.
2. My work is meaningful to me.
3. There are often moments at work that make me so happy the time just flies by.
4. When things go wrong, I don't worry at all. I just know something good is on the horizon.
5. I feel excited about my future, knowing even better things are always on their way.
6. Stress and anxiety don't seem to faze me much. I trust in my ability to attain my goals.
7. I look forward to the future because I have unique and bold goals on the horizon.
8. I spend a good amount of time thinking excitedly about my visions for the future.

**Best Practices:**
- Answer honestly—this is a diagnostic tool, not a test to pass.
- Retake after implementing changes to measure growth.
- Share with a coach, mentor, or accountability partner for deeper insight.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Rating based on how you think you SHOULD feel rather than how you actually feel.
- Using the assessment as a one-time check rather than a regular practice.
- Ignoring the gap and trying to power through with willpower alone.

## 3. Summary

Chapter 6, "Bend Reality," documents Vishen Lakhiani's transformation from a stressed, near-bankrupt entrepreneur to a wildly successful business owner who worked 21 days a month from beaches around the world—and the mental model shift that made it possible. After his business reached $250,000 in monthly revenue but was hemorrhaging $15,000 per month in 2008, the author had a profound insight: **stop postponing your happiness. Be happy now.** He realized his anxiety about revenue goals was creating a negative feedback loop—his unhappiness was sabotaging his business performance. By setting bold goals while making daily happiness non-negotiable, he flipped his state of mind. Revenue grew 400% in eight months, culminating in Mindvalley's first million-dollar month in December 2008.

The chapter formalizes this experience into the philosophy of **Bending Reality**: the optimal state of human existence achieved by balancing two ingredients—**(1) being happy in the now** and **(2) holding an exciting vision for the future**. Four mental states are mapped along these two axes: the Negative Spiral (no happiness, no vision), the Current Reality Trap (happiness without vision), Stress and Anxiety (vision without present happiness), and Bending Reality (both). The author argues that the Stress and Anxiety state, while common among entrepreneurs, is actually counterproductive because happiness is the fuel for performance, not the reward for it. This is expressed in the reframed model: **"We shouldn't do things so we can be happy. We should be happy so we can do things."**

The chapter also addresses how to build each element. For happiness, the "if/then" model is exposed as flawed because it places happiness outside personal power. For vision, three traps are identified: confusing Brules with goals, only visualizing what is already known, and miscalculating timeframes (overestimating short-term, underestimating long-term). The chapter introduces **Law 6: Bend Reality** as a formal law of the extraordinary mind, and provides the **Eight Statements Exercise** as a practical self-assessment tool. The chapter concludes by introducing **Blissipline**—the discipline of sustaining the bending reality state—as the focus of the next chapter, positioning Bending Reality as the gateway to ultimate productivity, "luck," and effortless achievement.
