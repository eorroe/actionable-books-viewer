# Chapter 17 Analysis: The Divisions of Faith

## Lesson 1: Faith is determined by the modes of material nature

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Understand that one's faith (sraddha) is shaped by the mode of material nature one is situated in.
2. Recognize the three modes: goodness (sattva), passion (rajas), and ignorance (tamas).
3. Realize that faith evolves based on one's existence under these modes.
4. Acknowledge that one's faith determines what one worships and how one lives.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Those in goodness worship the demigods for specific purposes.
- Those in passion worship demons or powerful people.
- Those in ignorance worship ghosts and spirits.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- The living entity is originally transcendental to all modes but becomes conditioned through association.
- One's heart position determines one's faith position.
- Faith can be changed by associating with a bona fide spiritual master and following scriptural rules.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Blind faith without intelligence.
- Believing that faith alone is enough without following scriptural principles.
- Not recognizing that one's faith is conditioned by material association.

---

## Lesson 2: Austerities and penances not prescribed by scripture are demoniac

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Avoid undertaking severe austerities or penances that are not mentioned in scriptural injunctions.
2. Do not perform austerities out of pride, egoism, lust, or attachment.
3. Do not torture the bodily organs or disturb the Supersoul dwelling within the body.
4. Follow only those austerities that are directed by the scriptures.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Fasting for spiritual advancement is recommended; fasting for political ends or social purposes is not.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Such unauthorized austerities disturb both the material elements of the body and the Supreme Personality of Godhead within.
- Those who engage in such acts are considered demons (asuras).

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Performing austerities for some ulterior purpose like political gain.
- Undertaking self-torture for impossible objectives.
- Disturbing others through unauthorized fasting or austerities.

---

## Lesson 3: Food, sacrifice, austerity, and charity are all threefold according to the modes of nature

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Understand that all actions—eating, sacrificing, performing austerities, and giving charity—are influenced by the three modes.
2. Discern analytically what kind of performances belong to which mode.
3. Do not consider all sacrifices, foods, or charities to be the same.
4. Avoid the foolish notion that one can do whatever one likes and attain perfection.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- One who can analytically distinguish between modes is wise.
- One who cannot discriminate is foolish.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Not all forms of sacrifice or charity are equally beneficial.
- Misleading teachers who say one can do whatever one likes are not acting according to scripture.

---

## Lesson 4: Foods in the mode of goodness

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Choose foods that increase the duration of life, purify existence, and give strength, health, happiness, and satisfaction.
2. Prefer foods that are sweet, juicy, fatty (from milk products, not animal fat obtained by slaughter), and palatable.
3. Include milk products, sugar, rice, wheat, fruits, and vegetables in your diet.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Milk, butter, cheese give animal fat without killing innocent creatures.
- Foods that are pure by nature and aid health are in the mode of goodness.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Animal fat obtained by slaughter is not recommended; the civilized method is through milk.

---

## Lesson 5: Foods in the mode of passion

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Avoid foods that are too bitter, too sour, too salty, too pungent, too dry, or too hot.
2. Avoid foods overly mixed with red pepper.
3. Recognize that such foods cause pain, distress, and disease.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Foods that produce mucus in the stomach and lead to disease are in the mode of passion.

---

## Lesson 6: Foods in the mode of ignorance

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Avoid food cooked more than three hours before being eaten (except prasadam, food offered to the Lord).
2. Avoid tasteless, stale, putrid, decomposed, or unclean food.
3. Do not eat remnants of food left by others unless that food was first offered to the Supreme Lord or eaten by saintly persons, especially the spiritual master.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Such foods give a bad odor and attract those in ignorance but repulse those in goodness.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Remnants of food not offered to the Lord increase infection or disease.

---

## Lesson 7: Sacrifice in the mode of goodness

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Perform sacrifice according to scriptural rules and one's prescribed duty.
2. Perform sacrifice without any expectation of material reward.
3. Offer respect to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, flowers, and eatables as a matter of duty.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Going to a temple or church simply to offer respect to the Deity, not for economic benefit.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Sacrifice for economic benefit or material benefit is not in the mode of goodness.

---

## Lesson 8: Sacrifice in the mode of passion

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Avoid performing sacrifice for some material end or benefit.
2. Avoid performing sacrifice ostentatiously, out of pride, to gain respect, honor, and worship.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Sacrifices performed to elevate oneself to the heavenly kingdom or for material benefits are in the mode of passion.
- Results are temporary and not permanent.

---

## Lesson 9: Sacrifice in the mode of ignorance

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Do not perform sacrifice in defiance of scriptural injunctions.
2. Do not perform sacrifice without distribution of spiritual food (prasadam).
3. Do not perform sacrifice without chanting Vedic hymns.
4. Do not perform sacrifice without remunerations to the priests.
5. Do not perform sacrifice without faith.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Ceremonial shows of religiosity performed to make money and then spending for recreation are in the mode of darkness.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Such sacrifices produce a demoniac mentality and do not benefit human society.

---

## Lesson 10: Austerity of the body in the mode of goodness

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Offer respect and worship to the Supreme Lord.
2. Offer respect to the brahmanas (qualified priests), the spiritual master, and superiors like father and mother.
3. Practice cleanliness, both external and internal.
4. Cultivate simplicity in behavior.
5. Follow only those actions sanctioned by scriptural injunctions.
6. Practice celibacy—sex is sanctioned only in married life.
7. Practice nonviolence.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- These are the austerities and penances of the body.

---

## Lesson 11: Austerity of speech in the mode of goodness

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Speak truthfully and beneficially.
2. Avoid speech that agitates the minds of others.
3. Do not speak nonsense; all statements should be backed by scripture.
4. Ensure speech is very pleasurable to the ear.
5. Recite the Vedas regularly and study Vedic literature.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- When speaking in spiritual circles, one's statements must be upheld by the scriptures.
- One should at once quote scriptural authority to back up what one is saying.
- Limitless stock of Vedic literature is available for study.

---

## Lesson 12: Austerity of mind in the mode of goodness

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Cultivate satisfaction of the mind by diverting it away from thoughts of sense enjoyment.
2. Be simple and without duplicity towards others.
3. Maintain gravity in thought—do not deviate from Krsna consciousness.
4. Practice self-control by detaching the mind from sense enjoyment.
5. Purify one's nature by becoming Krsna conscious.
6. Think of the welfare of all.
7. Observe silence in the sense of always thinking of self-realization.
8. Be straightforward in dealings.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Divert the mind to Vedic literature, which is full of satisfying stories in the Puranas and the Mahabharata.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- The more one thinks of sense enjoyment, the more the mind becomes dissatisfied.
- Silence means one is always thinking of self-realization.

---

## Lesson 13: Threefold austerity practiced to please the Supreme

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Practice austerity of body, speech, and mind.
2. Practice with transcendental faith (paraya sraddha).
3. Have no desire for material benefit or fruitive results.
4. Direct all austerities toward pleasing the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- This threefold austerity is in the mode of goodness when practiced without material desire.

---

## Lesson 14: Avoid ostentatious penances performed for respect and honor

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Do not arrange to be worshiped by subordinates.
2. Do not let others wash your feet or offer riches to you artificially.
3. Avoid performing penances merely to attract people and receive honor.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Such penances are in the mode of passion.
- Results are temporary, neither stable nor permanent.

---

## Lesson 15: Avoid foolish penances of self-torture or destruction of others

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Do not undergo penances out of obstinate self-torture.
2. Do not perform austerities to destroy or injure others.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- The demon Hiranyakasipu performed severe austerities to become immortal and kill the demigods, but was ultimately killed by the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- To undergo penances for something impossible is in the mode of ignorance.

---

## Lesson 16: Charity in the mode of goodness

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Give charity out of duty, not out of desire for reward.
2. Give at the proper time and place.
3. Give to a worthy person—a qualified brahmana, a Vaisnava (devotee), or in temples.
4. Give without any consideration of return.
5. Prefer places of pilgrimage, lunar or solar eclipses, end of the month, or to qualified persons.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Charity to the poor is sometimes given out of compassion, but if a poor man is not spiritually worth receiving charity, there is no spiritual advancement.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Indiscriminate charity is not recommended in Vedic literature.

---

## Lesson 17: Charity in the mode of passion

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Avoid giving charity with the expectation of getting something in return.
2. Avoid giving charity for fruitive results, such as elevation to the heavenly kingdom.
3. Avoid giving charity grudgingly or with repentance afterwards.
4. Avoid giving charity under obligation at the request of a superior.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- Charitable foundations that offer gifts to institutions where sense gratification goes on are not recommended.

---

## Lesson 18: Charity in the mode of ignorance

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Do not contribute to indulgence in intoxication and gambling.
2. Do not give charity at an unpurified place or time.
3. Do not give to unworthy persons.
4. Do not give charity without respect or proper attention.
5. Do not give charity with contempt.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Such charity encourages sinful persons and is not beneficial.
- Giving to a suitable person without respect is also in the mode of darkness.

---

## Lesson 19: Use the sacred syllables "om tat sat" in all spiritual activities

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Use "om tat sat" to indicate the Supreme Absolute Truth in all sacrifices, charities, and penances.
2. Utter these three words in conjunction with the holy name of the Supreme Lord (e.g., om tad visnoh).
3. Begin all transcendental activities with "om tat sat" to attain the Supreme.
4. Perform sacrifice, penance, and charity with the word "tat" to get free from material entanglement.
5. Use the word "sat" to indicate the Absolute Truth and devotional activities meant to please the Supreme Person.

**Examples/Best Practices:**
- When initiating a person or offering the sacred thread, vibrate "om tat sat."
- In all kinds of yajna performances, invoke "om tat sat."
- Work done for "om tat sat" means acting in Krsna consciousness.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- These three words have been used from the beginning of creation by brahmanas chanting Vedic hymns and during sacrifices.
- One who acts without following scriptural regulations will not attain the Absolute Truth.

---

## Lesson 20: Activities without transcendental faith are useless

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Perform all sacrifices, austerities, and charities with transcendental faith in the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
2. Avoid performing any spiritual activity without faith.
3. Begin from the very beginning in Krsna consciousness under the guidance of a bona fide spiritual master.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Sacrifices, austerities, and charities performed without faith are called "asat" (nonpermanent).
- They are useless both in this life and the next.
- The ultimate goal of all Vedic instructions is the understanding of Krsna.
- No one can obtain success without following this principle.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Being attracted to worship demigods, ghosts, or Yaksas like Kuvera in the conditional state.
- Thinking that the mode of goodness alone is sufficient without directly taking to Krsna consciousness.

---

## Lesson 21: Transcendence to all three modes through Krsna consciousness

**Step-by-step instructions:**
1. Recognize that the mode of goodness is better than passion and ignorance, but it is still material.
2. Understand that one who takes directly to Krsna consciousness is transcendental to all three modes.
3. Follow the process of gradual elevation by associating with a bona fide spiritual master and following scriptural injunctions.

**Keep-in-mind:**
- Although there is a process of gradual elevation, direct Krsna consciousness is the transcendental path.
- The real principle of religious faith is situated in pure goodness, where one can understand the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
