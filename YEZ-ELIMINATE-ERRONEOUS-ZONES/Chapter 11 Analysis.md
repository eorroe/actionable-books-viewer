# Chapter XI: Farewell to Anger

## Overview

This chapter teaches that anger is a choice rather than a human necessity, and that it can be eliminated entirely through conscious control of your thoughts and reactions. It provides practical strategies for postponing anger, using accountability partners, keeping an anger journal, and defusing hostility in the first ten seconds before it takes hold. The chapter also addresses deeper psychological patterns such as expectations for others, the payoff system that reinforces anger, and the use of humor as a remedy. By learning to think new thoughts when frustrated, you can replace immobilizing anger with more fulfilling emotions.

## When to Follow These Book Teachings

- When you find yourself reacting with anger to minor frustrations or other people's behavior
- When anger is damaging your relationships and you want practical strategies to gain control
- When you need to break the habit of using anger as a tool for manipulation or control

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize That Anger Is a Choice, Not a Human Necessity

1. Recognize that anger is an erroneous zone, a kind of psychological influenza that incapacitates you.
2. Stop accepting anger as a part of your life by eliminating the internal sentence: "If only you were more like me."
3. Begin to think of yourself as someone who can learn to think new thoughts when frustrated so that immobilizing anger can be replaced by more fulfilling emotions.
4. Accept that annoyance, irritation, and disappointment are feelings you will continue to experience, but anger can be eliminated.

The book notes that you may have justified short-fuse behavior by saying "It's only human" or "If I don't express it, then I'll store it up and have an ulcer," but these are faulty justifications.

### Step 2: Use Controlled (Fake) Anger as a Strategy for Teaching

1. When trying to use anger constructively to teach a child something, fake the anger.
2. Raise your voice and look stern, but do not experience the physical and psychological pain that goes with real anger.
3. Think like this: "This behavior she is exhibiting is dangerous to her. I want to make her see that playing in the street will not be tolerated. I'll raise my voice to demonstrate my strong feelings about it. But I won't go around being mad."

A mother who cannot master controlled display of anger punishes her children, sends them to their room, screams constantly, and is almost always in a state of pique. The book explains that the irony of anger is that it never works in changing others; it only intensifies the other person's desire to control the angry person.

### Step 3: Allow Others the Right to Be Different

1. Remind yourself at the moment of anger that everyone has a right to be what he chooses.
2. Stop demanding that anyone be different, as this will simply prolong your anger.
3. Work at allowing others to choose just as you insist on your own right to do the same.

Instead of choosing anger when you run into someone or something you don't like, you can begin to think of others as having a right to be different from what you'd prefer. You may not like it, but you don't have to be angry about it.

### Step 4: Postpone Anger to Gain Control

1. If you typically react with anger in a particular circumstance, postpone the anger for fifteen seconds, and then explode in your typical fashion.
2. Next try thirty seconds, and keep lengthening the intervals.
3. Once you begin to see that you can put anger off, you will have learned control.
4. With lots of practice, you will eventually eliminate it totally.

Postponing is control; the book presents this as a graduated exercise in lengthening the intervals between provocation and angry response.

### Step 5: Get in Touch with Your Thoughts at the Time of Anger

1. First and most important, get in touch with your thoughts at the time of your anger.
2. Remind yourself that you don't have to think that way simply because you've always done so in the past.
3. Remember that awareness is paramount.

The book emphasizes that anger is the result of thinking, not something that simply happens to you. When faced with circumstances that are not going the way you would like, you tell yourself that things shouldn't be that way, and then you select a familiar angry response.

### Step 6: Use an Accountability Partner

1. Ask someone that you trust to help.
2. Have them tell you when they see your anger either verbally or with an agreed signal.
3. When you get the signal, think about what you're doing and then try the postponing strategy.

This is presented as a concrete strategy for lengthening your fuse by leveraging external feedback.

### Step 7: Keep an Anger Journal

1. Keep an anger journal and record the exact time, place, and incident in which you chose to be angry.
2. Be religious about the entries; force yourself to record all angry behavior.
3. You will soon find, if you are persistent, that the very act of having to write the incident down will persuade you to choose anger less often.

The journal is designed to make you accountable to yourself by creating a written record of every angry choice.

### Step 8: Announce After an Angry Outburst That You Have Slipped

1. After you have had an angry outburst, announce that you've just slipped.
2. State that one of your goals is to think differently so that you don't experience this anger.
3. The verbal announcement will put you in touch with what you've done, and will demonstrate that you are truly working on yourself.

This verbal acknowledgment serves as a self-monitoring tool to reinforce your commitment to eliminating anger.

### Step 9: Use Physical Closeness to Neutralize Hostility

1. Try being physically close to someone that you love at the moment of your anger.
2. One way to neutralize your hostility is to hold hands, despite your inclination not to.
3. Keep holding hands until you've expressed how you feel and dissipated your anger.

This is presented as a direct physical intervention to break the cycle of angry reaction in a loving relationship.

### Step 10: Discuss Anger-Provoking Patterns When You Are Not Angry

1. Talk with those who are the most frequent recipients of your anger at a time when you are not angry.
2. Share with each other the most anger-provoking activities in the other.
3. Devise a way of communicating your feelings without debilitating anger.
4. Agree on a written note, a messenger, or a cooling-off walk, so that you don't continue abusing each other with senseless anger.
5. After several cooling-off walks, you will begin to see the folly of striking out.

This exercise is designed to create mutual understanding and agreed-upon protocols for handling conflict before it escalates.

### Step 11: Defuse Anger in the First Ten Seconds

1. Defuse your anger for the first few seconds by labeling how you feel.
2. Also label how you believe your partner feels as well.
3. Focus on the first ten seconds as the most crucial; once you've passed these, your anger will often have subsided.

The book identifies the first ten seconds as the most crucial window for intervention before anger takes hold.

### Step 12: Expect Disagreement from Others

1. Keep in mind all the things you believe will be met with disapproval by fifty percent of the folks, fifty percent of the time.
2. Once you expect others to disagree, you will not choose to feel angry.
3. Instead, say to yourself that the world is straight since people aren't agreeing with everything I say, think, feel, and do.

This reframing exercise helps you accept disagreement as normal rather than as a trigger for anger.

### Step 13: Get Rid of Expectations for Others

1. Get rid of the expectations you have for others.
2. When the expectations go away, so will the anger.

The book states that much of the time people and things will not go the way you would like them to go, and the likelihood of it changing is zero. Every time you choose anger when you run into someone or something you don't like, you are deciding to be hurt or immobilized because of reality.

### Step 14: Remind Yourself About the Nature of Children

1. Remind yourself that children will always be active and loud.
2. Recognize that getting angry about it won't do any good.
3. While you can help children to make constructive choices in other areas, accept that you will never be able to alter their basic nature.

This is a specific application of the expectation-elimination principle to parenting.

### Step 15: Love Yourself to Eliminate Anger

1. Love yourself.
2. If you do, you won't ever burden yourself with that self-destructive anger.

The book frames self-love as the ultimate protection against choosing anger, because when you think highly of yourself and refuse to let others control you, you won't hurt yourself with present-moment anger.

### Step 16: Develop a Sense of Humor as a Remedy for Anger

1. Help others choose to laugh.
2. Learn to stand back and observe the incongruity of almost every life situation.
3. Laugh just for the sake of laughing. It is its own justification. You don't have to have a reason to laugh. Just do it.
4. Observe yourself and others in this nutty world, and then decide whether to carry around anger or to develop a sense of humor.

"It is impossible for you to be angry and laugh at the same time. Anger and laughter are mutually exclusive and you have the power to choose either." The book quotes Winston Churchill: "It is my belief, you cannot deal with the most serious things in the world unless you understand the most amusing."

### Step 17: Use a Traffic Jam as a Control Exercise

1. In a traffic jam, time yourself. See how long you can go without exploding.
2. Work at the control aspect.
3. Instead of hollering at a passenger, ask him a civil question.
4. Use the time creatively to write a letter, or a song, or to devise ways out of the traffic jam, or relive the most exciting sexual experience of your life, or better yet, plan to improve on it.

The traffic jam is presented as a specific opportunity to practice postponing and redirecting anger.

### Step 18: Use Frustration as a Challenge, Not an Excuse for Anger

1. Instead of being an emotional slave to every frustrating circumstance, use the situation as a challenge to change it.
2. By focusing on changing the situation, you will have no present moment time for the anger.

The book contrasts being an emotional slave to circumstances with using them as active challenges, noting that anger gets in the way and is good for nothing.

### Step 19: Reframe Others' Behavior as Their Ownership, Not Yours

1. Think new thoughts such as: "If he wants to be a fool, I'm not going to choose to be upset. He, not me, owns his dumb behavior."
2. Or think: "Things aren't going the way I think they should. While I don't like it, I'm not going to immobilize myself."
3. Refuse to give other people's behavior and ideas the power to upset you.
4. Think highly of yourself and refuse to let others control you.

The book presents these exact internal sentences as replacements for angry thinking, shifting from the external to the internal side of the mental health ledger.

### Step 20: Recognize the Payoff System That Reinforces Anger

1. Understand the psychological motives that keep your fuse short, including:
   - Directing responsibility for how you feel to the person or event rather than taking charge of your own feelings
   - Manipulating those who fear you
   - Drawing attention and feeling important and powerful
   - Using anger as an excuse to go temporarily insane and then exonerate yourself
   - Getting your way because others would rather placate you than deal with your anger
   - Avoiding intimacy or love by getting angry over something
   - Manipulating others with guilt
   - Breaking down communication in which you feel threatened
   - Avoiding having to work on yourself
   - Indulging in self-pity
   - Avoiding clear thinking
   - Excusing losing or poor performance
   - Justifying anger as "human"
2. Once you see these payoffs, you can begin to dismantle the reasons for keeping anger.

The book lists these specific psychological rewards to help you understand why you may unconsciously choose anger, so you can begin to eliminate the internal logic that sustains it.

## Security & Safety Notes

- If you are in a relationship with someone who uses threatening behavior, the silent treatment, or violence as control strategies, seek professional help and safety planning immediately
- When using a nonverbal signal with a dominant partner, ensure the signal does not escalate conflict or put you at risk

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You react with anger to every frustration, letting circumstances control your emotions and damage your relationships.
  **Solution:** Postpone your anger for fifteen seconds, then thirty, and keep lengthening the intervals. Once you begin to see that you can put anger off, you will have learned control.

- **Problem:** You have expectations for how others should behave, and get angry when they don't meet them.
  **Solution:** Get rid of the expectations you have for others. When the expectations go away, so will the anger. Remind yourself that much of the time people and things will not go the way you would like them to go.

- **Problem:** You think anger is just "human nature" and that you can't change it.
  **Solution:** Recognize that anger is a choice, not a human necessity. Accept that annoyance and disappointment are feelings you will continue to experience, but anger can be eliminated.

- **Problem:** You want to use anger to teach or control others, but it only intensifies their desire to control you.
  **Solution:** When trying to use anger constructively to teach, fake the anger. Raise your voice and look stern, but do not experience the physical and psychological pain that goes with real anger.
