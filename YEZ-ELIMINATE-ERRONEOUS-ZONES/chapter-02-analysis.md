## Chapter II: First Love

### Lesson 1: Self-Worth Cannot Be Verified by Others
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Recognize that your worth is determined by you alone, not by others' opinions or behavior toward you.
  2. Stop depending on others for your value; if you do, it becomes "other-worth" rather than self-worth.
  3. If you are secure in yourself, you neither want nor need others to conform to your dictates.
  4. When you feel loved and worthy, you can love others genuinely—not for thanks or payoffs, but because of the genuine pleasure of being a helper or lover.
  5. Giving love is directly related to how much love you have for yourself. If the self you hold is unworthy or unloved, giving becomes impossible.
- **Examples from book:** Noah, a middle-aged man who could not say "I love you" to his wife and children because he needed affirmation of his self-worth in return. Once he recognized his worth was intact before he started, saying those words would become possible regardless of the response.

### Lesson 2: Never Confuse Your Self-Worth With Your Behavior or Others' Behavior Toward You
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Separate your self-worth (which is a given) from your behavior or the behavior of others toward you.
  2. Reframe messages: instead of "I am bad because I am bad," think "She doesn't like me. That's her decision, and while I don't like it, I'm still important."
  3. Even if you have behaved in a way you dislike, do not loathe yourself. Learn from the error and resolve not to repeat it, but do not associate it with your self-worth.
- **Examples from book:** The R. D. Laing "Knots" passage showing how a child internalizes "My mother does not love me / I am bad because she does not love me / She does not love me because I am bad." The book teaches replacing this with the understanding that a parent's disapproval is about behavior, not about the core self.

### Lesson 3: Tuning Into Self-Acceptance
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Destroy the myth that you have one single self-concept that is either entirely positive or entirely negative. You have many self-images that vary from moment to moment.
  2. Do not lump all negative self-thoughts into a collective "No" when asked "Do you like yourself?"
  3. Break down the areas of dislike into specifics. This gives you definite goals to work on.
  4. Acknowledge that your self-worth—the ever-present shadow, your consultant for personal happiness and mastery—must be unrelated to your self-assessments. You exist. You are human. That is all you need.
  5. Choose to be worthy to yourself forever, then work on your self-images separately from that baseline worth.
- **Examples from book:** The chapter notes you have feelings about yourself physically, intellectually, socially, and emotionally, and that your self-portraits are as numerous as your activities. Through all behaviors there is always YOU—the person you either accept or reject.

### Lesson 4: Loving Your Body
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Make a very long list of every part of your body—hair, forehead, eyes, eyelids, cheeks, mouth, nose, teeth, neck, arms, fingers, breasts, stomach, kidneys, spleen, arteries, femurs, Rolandic fissure, cochlea, pinna, adrenal glands, uvula, and all other components.
  2. Identify which parts you find objectionable.
  3. For parts that can be changed (e.g., stomach too big, hair wrong color), view them as choices made in an earlier present moment and make new present-moment decisions about them. Make changing them a goal if you wish.
  4. For parts that cannot be changed (legs too long, eyes too narrow, breasts too small or large), reject society's definition of beauty and decide to like the physical you. Nothing is too anything, and long legs are no better or worse than hair or non-hair.
  5. Decide to like your physical self and declare it worthy and attractive to you, thereby rejecting the comparisons and opinions of others.
  6. Accept your natural human body without shame. Do not be embarrassed or apologetic when your body behaves naturally.
  7. If you choose to use cosmetic aids, do so only for novelty or personal fulfillment—not from disliking what you are covering up.
- **Examples from book:** Frank, a thirty-two-year-old man who compulsively cleans himself after sweating and cannot tolerate his own normal body odors. He sprays his bathroom, uses many cosmetic products, and showers before and after sex. The lesson teaches that self-love means accepting natural odors without shame, even if you choose not to share them.

### Lesson 5: Choosing More Positive Self-Images
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Choose to think of yourself as intelligent by applying your very own standards to yourself.
  2. Understand that aptitude is really a function of time rather than some inborn quality. With enough time and effort you could master almost any academic skill.
  3. If you dislike how smart you've chosen to be, recognize this as mere self-contempt, not a fixed limitation.
  4. Apply the same logic to all self-pictures: you are as socially adept, artistic, mechanical, athletic, and musical as you choose to be.
  5. Do not confuse your abilities with your worthiness.
  6. Decide now to base self-acceptance on what you believe is appropriate for you. Repairing things that don't measure up can be a delightful undertaking.
- **Examples from book:** The chapter cites grade norms for standardized achievement tests: scores achieved by top students at one grade level are achieved by the majority at a later grade level. John Carroll's "A Model for School Learning" states: "Aptitude is the amount of time required by the learner to attain mastery of a learning task. Implicit in this formulation is the assumption that, given enough time, all students can conceivably attain mastery of a learning task."

### Lesson 6: Recognize and Eliminate Self-Veto (Self-Put-Down) Behaviors
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Become aware of when you engage in self-put-down behavior. Every time you do, you reinforce old self-doubt and reduce opportunities for love.
  2. Stop rejecting compliments. Accept them with a simple "thank-you."
  3. Stop making up excuses for why you look nice. Own your appearance.
  4. Stop giving credit to others when it rightfully belongs to you.
  5. Stop using other-directed references when speaking ("My husband says..."). Speak for yourself.
  6. Stop having your opinions verified by others. Trust your own judgment.
  7. Stop refusing to order something you want because you don't feel you're worth it.
  8. Stop withholding orgasms or sexual fulfillment from yourself.
  9. Stop not buying yourself things because you think you have to buy them for someone else instead.
  10. Stop avoiding indulgences (flowers, wine, etc.) because you feel wasteful.
  11. Stop turning when someone yells "Hey, Stupid" in a crowd.
  12. Stop using pet names for yourself (and having others use them) that are really put-downs: Dum-dum, Silly, Sweetie-pie, Baby (for an adult), Funny-face, Shorty, Fatty, Baldy.
  13. Stop dismissing gifts or compliments with negative thoughts like "You must have a drawer full of jewelry at home for other girls" or "You're practically blind or trying to make me feel good."
  14. Stop thinking relationships will end once someone discovers the real you.
  15. Stop believing others accept you only out of charity.
- **Examples from book:** Shirley, a young woman who sabotaged every romantic relationship. When a man told her he cared, her head contradicted: "He's only saying that because he knows it's what I want to hear." She searched for sentences that repudiated her worth because she didn't believe she was worth being loved. The cycle of renunciation reinforced her lack of worth.

### Lesson 7: Accept Yourself Without Complaint
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Accept yourself as a worthy person because you choose to do so.
  2. Eliminate complaint, especially about things over which you can do nothing (rough rocks, cloudy sky, cold ice).
  3. Stop complaining to others about your own dissatisfaction; they are almost always powerless to do anything about it.
  4. Stop permitting others to abuse you with their self-pity and misery. Ask them: "Why are you telling me this?" or "Is there anything I can do to help you with this?"
  5. Ask yourself the same questions when you catch yourself complaining: "Why am I telling myself this?" and "Is there anything I can do to help me with this?"
  6. Do not complain about being tired or not feeling well. If you are tired, exercise options other than complaining. It abuses the listener and won't make you less tired.
  7. If you are truly working on self-love and experience pain or discomfort, work on it yourself rather than choosing someone to lean on.
  8. If you notice things in yourself or others that you dislike, actively set about taking corrective steps rather than complaining.
  9. Practice silent self-praise or helping someone else achieve fulfillment instead of complaining.
- **Examples from book:** The book gives an exercise: at a social gathering of four or more couples, chronicle how much conversation is actually spent in complaining. After the party, ask yourself how much of the complaining accomplished anything or who really cares about all the things bemoaned. The next time you are about to complain, keep the uselessness of that night in mind.

### Lesson 8: Understand the Difference Between Self-Love and Conceit
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Understand that self-love has nothing to do with boastful, chest-thumping behavior designed to win attention and approval from others.
  2. Recognize that boastful behavior is neurotic and motivated by how others see you. It means the individual is evaluating himself on the basis of external views.
  3. True self-love requires no convincing of others. Internal acceptance is sufficient.
  4. Do not demand the love of others as proof of your worth. Self-love means you love yourself regardless of external validation.
- **Examples from book:** The chapter contrasts self-love with telling everyone how wonderful you are. The latter is an attempt to win approval; the former is internal acceptance that makes external approval unnecessary.

### Lesson 9: Examine the Rewards for Not Loving Yourself (The Self-Scorn Maintenance System)
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Understand that all behavior is caused. To eliminate self-destructive behavior, you must first understand why you engage in it and what maintenance system keeps it in place.
  2. Identify which of the following dividends (payoffs) you receive from self-denunciation:
     - A built-in excuse for why you can't get love: you simply aren't worth being loved back.
     - Avoidance of risks that come with love relationships, eliminating any possibility of rejection or disapproval.
     - Easier to stay the same; if you're not worthy, there is no point in trying to grow or be better, so you remain unchanged.
     - Gain pity, attention, and even approval from others, which substitutes for the risky business of a real love relationship.
     - Convenient scapegoats to blame for your own misery, so you don't have to do anything about it yourself.
     - Present-moment mini-depressions that allow you to avoid behavior that would help you be different; self-pity serves as an escape route.
     - Regression to being a good little boy or girl, calling upon leftover child responses to please the "big-people" you learned to regard as superior; regression feels safer than risk.
     - Reinforcing leaning-on-others behavior by making others more significant than yourself.
     - Inability to take charge of your own life because you won't feel you are worth the happiness you covet.
  3. Recognize that these rewards, while unhealthy, are real and explain why you hang on to old thinking.
  4. Remember that refusing to grow into a self-loving person is a deathlike choice; the only evidence for life is growth.
- **Examples from book:** The chapter presents these as the components of the self-scorn maintenance system and explains that without understanding these motives, old self-destructive actions will continue to recur.

### Lesson 10: Some Easy-to-Master Self-Love Exercises
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. **Correct self-loathing thoughts out loud:** When you catch yourself saying something like "I'm really not that smart, I guess I was just lucky," recognize that you have just behaved in a self-loathing way. Correct yourself immediately out loud with a statement such as: "I just said I was lucky, but luck really had nothing to do with it. I got the grade because I deserved it." This is one small step toward self-love—a recognition of the present-moment put-down and a decision to act differently.
  2. **Accept love and acceptance with gratitude:** When others reach you with love or acceptance, select new responses. Instead of instantly being skeptical, accept them with "thank-you" or "I'm happy that you feel that way."
  3. **Say "I love you" directly:** If there is someone you feel genuine love toward, say it right out front: "I love you." While checking out the reactions you receive in return, pat yourself on the back for taking the risk.
  4. **Order what you really want:** In a restaurant, order something you really enjoy no matter what it costs. Give yourself a treat because you are worth it. Begin to select items that you would prefer in all situations, including the grocery store. Indulge yourself with a favorite product because you are worth it. Outlaw self-denial unless absolutely necessary—and it rarely is.
  5. **Rest and recharge:** After a tiring day and a large meal, take a brief nap or a jog in the park even if you have too many things to do. It will help you feel one hundred percent better.
  6. **Join an enjoyable organization or activity:** Perhaps you've been putting this off because of responsibilities and lack of time. By choosing to love yourself and partake of the slices of life you want, those you serve will begin to learn self-reliance of their own. You will serve them out of choice rather than obligation, and you will experience an absence of resentment toward them.
  7. **Eliminate jealousy:** Recognize that jealousy is a put-down of yourself. By comparing yourself to another and imagining you are loved less, you make others more important than you. Remind yourself that (1) someone can always choose another without it being a reflection on you, and (2) whether or not you are chosen by any significant other is not how you validate your own self-worth. With practice at self-love, circumstances that previously caused jealousy will be reversed. You will believe so much in yourself that you won't need the love or approval of others to give you value.
  8. **Treat your body well:** Select good nutritional foods, eliminate excess weight (a health risk as well as an indication of self-rejection), take regular walks or bicycle rides, choose plenty of healthy exercise, get outdoors to enjoy fresh air because it feels good. Keep your body healthy and attractive because you are important and are going to treat yourself that way. Any total day spent cooped up or inactive in boring routine activities is a vote for self-enmity.
  9. **Practice sexual self-love:** Stand naked in front of a mirror and tell yourself how attractive you are. Get in touch with your body. Explore yourself sensually, give yourself goosebumps of shivery pleasure. With others, choose sexual fulfillment for yourself rather than making your partner's pleasure more important than your own. Slow down the process of sex, teaching your lover what you like with words and actions. Choose orgasm for yourself. Make yourself achieve the ultimate physical experience by believing that you are worth it, and then getting lost in the excitement of verifying it for you.
  10. **Stop equating performance with self-worth:** You may lose your job or fail a project. You may not like how you performed a task. But that doesn't mean you are without worth. You must know for yourself that you are worth something regardless of your achievements. Once you eliminate this confusion, you will be able to undertake all kinds of projects, and your final score—while it may be interesting to you—will in no way determine how valuable you are as a person.
- **Examples from book:** The chapter frames these as behaviors of those who love themselves. It emphasizes that as a child you knew instinctively you were worthy; these exercises help you return to that natural state of self-love.
