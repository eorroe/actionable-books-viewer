# Chapter IX: Putting an End to the Critic

## Overview

This chapter provides a comprehensive framework for eliminating procrastination and the critical, observing behavior that sustains it. It teaches that a non-doer is very often a critic who sits back and watches others while philosophizing about their efforts, and that becoming a doer requires effort, risk, and change. The chapter offers practical strategies including living five minutes at a time, immediately beginning postponed tasks, and using creative mental exercises to eliminate boredom. It also addresses deeper psychological barriers such as fear of failure, need for approval, and the use of anxiety to avoid living effectively.

## When to Follow These Book Teachings

- When you find yourself criticizing others instead of taking action yourself
- When you use boredom, anxiety, or fatigue as excuses to postpone tasks
- When you need practical strategies to begin tackling long-avoided responsibilities

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognizing That Procrastinators Are Often Critics

1. Recognize in yourself the behavior of being an observer rather than a doer.
2. Resolve to eliminate critical and observing behaviors entirely so that you can be a doer rather than a procrastinating critic.

"A non-doer is very often a critic, that is, someone who sits back and watches doers, and then waxes philosophically about how the doers are doing. It is easy to be a critic, but being a doer requires effort, risk and change." Real champions such as Henry Aarons, Johnny Carsons, Bobby Fishers, Katharine Hepburns, and Joe Louises "have no time for criticizing others. They're too busy doing. They work. They help others who are not as talented, rather than serve as their critics."

### Step 2: Eliminating Boredom as a Spin-Off of Procrastination

1. Recognize that boredom is a choice and not caused by the environment.
2. Eliminate boredom by doing something else with your mind or energy at that moment.
3. Use your mind creatively in previously boring circumstances.

"Life is never boring but some people choose to be bored. The concept of boredom entails an inability to use up present moments in a personally fulfilling way. Boredom is a choice; something you visit upon yourself." Example: "At a meeting, change the dull tempo with a pertinent question, or make your mind go off in exciting ways such as writing a poem, or memorizing twenty-five numbers backwards, just for the sheer drill of memory training. Decide to never be bored again."

### Step 3: Identifying Your Typical Procrastinating Behaviors

1. Review the list of typical procrastinating behaviors and identify which ones apply to you.

Staying in a job where you are stuck; hanging onto a sour relationship and hoping it will get better; refusing to work on relationship difficulties; not tackling addictions; putting off arduous or menial tasks; avoiding confrontations; being afraid to change locations geographically; putting off spending time with children; deciding to start your diet tomorrow; using sleep or tiredness as a reason for putting it off; getting sick when faced with a troublesome task; using the "I don't have time to do it" ruse; constantly looking forward to a vacation; being a critic and using criticism to camouflage your own refusal to do; refusing to get a physical checkup; being afraid to make a move toward someone you're fond of; being bored; planning but never putting in action a regular exercise program; living your entire life for your children and always putting off your own happiness.

### Step 4: Understanding the Reasons You Continue to Procrastinate

1. Review the list of reasons for procrastination to gain insight into why you procrastinate.

Procrastination allows escape from unpleasant activities; you can feel comfortable with your self-delusional system; you can stay exactly as you are forever; by being bored you have someone or something else to blame; by being a critic you can feel important at the expense of others; by waiting for things to get better you can blame the world for your unhappiness; you can avoid ever having to fail; wishing for things to happen allows you to return to a safe and protected childhood; you can win sympathy from others; you can justify sloppy performance; by putting it off you might be able to get someone else to do it for you; procrastination enables you to delude yourself into believing that you are something other than what you really are; by avoiding a task you can escape success.

### Step 5: Making a Decision to Live Five Minutes at a Time

1. Make a decision to live five minutes at a time.
2. Instead of thinking of tasks in long-range terms, think about now.
3. Try to use up a five-minute period doing what you want.
4. Refuse to put off anything that would bring about satisfaction.

### Step 6: Simply Beginning the Task You Have Been Postponing

1. Sit down and get started on something you have been postponing.
2. Begin a letter or a book.
3. Recognize that much of your putting it off is unnecessary since you will very likely find the job enjoyable once you give up the procrastination.
4. Use beginning to help eliminate anxiety about the whole project.

"Begin a letter or a book."

### Step 7: Assessing Your Fear by Asking What Is the Worst That Could Happen

1. Ask yourself: "What is the worst thing that could happen to me if I did what I'm putting off right now?"
2. Recognize that the answer is usually so insignificant that it may jar you into action.
3. Assess your fear and you will have no reason to hang onto it.

### Step 8: Giving Yourself a Designated Time Slot for the Postponed Task

1. Give yourself a designated time slot (for example, Wednesday from 10:00 to 10:15 P.M.) which you will devote exclusively to the task you have been putting off.
2. Use the fifteen minutes of devoted effort to see yourself over the hump of procrastination.

"say Wednesday from 10:00 to 10:15 P.M."

### Step 9: Thinking of Yourself as Too Significant to Live with Postponement Anxiety

1. Think of yourself as too significant to live with anxiety about the things you have to do.
2. When you know you are uncomfortable with postponement anxiety, remember that people who love themselves don't hurt themselves that way.

### Step 10: Tackling the Fear of Living Effectively by Looking at Your Now

1. Look carefully at your now.
2. Decide what you are avoiding in your current moments.
3. Begin to tackle the fear of living effectively.
4. Recognize that procrastination is substituting the now with anxiety about a future event.
5. Make the event become the now, so that the anxiety must go.

### Step 11: Taking Immediate Action on Addictive or Avoided Behaviors

1. Quit smoking now.
2. Begin your diet this moment.
3. Give up alcohol this second.
4. Put the book down and do one push-up as your beginning exercise project.
5. Tackle problems with action now.
6. Remind yourself that doing it is far more important than performing well.
7. Recognize that the only thing holding you back is you and the neurotic choices you have made because you don't believe you're as strong as you really are.

"Quit smoking…now! Begin your diet…this moment! Give up booze…this second. Put this book down and do one push-up as your beginning exercise project. That's how you tackle problems…with action now! Do it! The only thing holding you back is you, and the neurotic choices you've made because you don't believe you're as strong as you really are. How simple…just do it!"

### Step 12: Using Your Mind Creatively to Eliminate Boredom

1. Start using your mind creatively in what were previously boring circumstances.
2. At a meeting, change the dull tempo with a pertinent question.
3. Make your mind go off in exciting ways such as writing a poem, or memorizing twenty-five numbers backwards, just for the sheer drill of memory training.
4. Decide to never be bored again.

"At a meeting, change the dull tempo with a pertinent question, or make your mind go off in exciting ways such as writing a poem, or memorizing twenty-five numbers backwards, just for the sheer drill of memory training. Decide to never be bored again."

### Step 13: Moving from Critic to Doer When Facing Criticism

1. When someone begins to criticize you, ask: "Do you think I need a critic now?"
2. When you find yourself being a critic, ask the person in your company if he wants to hear your criticism, and if so, why?
3. Use these questions to help move from the critic to the doer column.

### Step 14: Evaluating Your Life as If You Had Six Months to Live

1. Look hard at your life.
2. Ask: Are you doing what you would choose to be doing if you knew you had six months to live?
3. If not, begin doing it because, relatively speaking, that's all you have.
4. Recognize that given the eternity of time, thirty years or six months make no difference. Your total lifetime is a mere speck. Delaying anything makes no sense.

### Step 15: Being Courageous About Undertaking an Activity You Have Been Avoiding

1. Be courageous about undertaking an activity that you have been avoiding.
2. Recognize that one act of courage can eliminate all that fear.
3. Stop telling yourself that you must perform well.
4. Remind yourself that doing it is far more important.

### Step 16: Deciding Not to Be Tired Until the Moment Before You Get Into Bed

1. Decide not to be tired until the moment before you get into bed.
2. Do not allow yourself to use fatigue or illness as an escape or to put off doing anything.
3. Recognize that when you take away the reason for the illness or exhaustion—that is, avoidance of a task—physical problems "magically" disappear.

### Step 17: Eliminating the Words "Hope," "Wish" and "Maybe" from Your Vocabulary

1. Eliminate the words "hope," "wish" and "maybe" from your vocabulary because they are the tools of putting it off.
2. If you see these words creeping in, substitute new sentences.
3. Change "I hope things will work out" to "I will make it happen."
4. Change "I wish things were better" to "I am going to do the following things to ensure that I feel better."
5. Change "Maybe it will be okay" to "I will make it okay."

The three neurotic phrases of the procrastinator: "I hope things will work out." "I wish things were better." "Maybe it'll be okay." The substitutions: "I will make it happen." "I am going to do the following things to ensure that I feel better." "I will make it okay."

### Step 18: Keeping a Journal of Your Own Complaining or Critical Behavior

1. Keep a journal of your own complaining or critical behavior.
2. By writing these actions down, you will accomplish two things:
   a. You will see how your critical behavior surfaces in your life—the frequency, patterns, events and people that are related to your being a critic.
   b. You will stop yourself from criticizing because it will be such a pain to have to write in the journal.

### Step 19: Having a Conference with All Involved When Procrastination Affects Others

1. If you are putting something off which involves others (for example, a move, a sex problem, a new job), have a conference with all involved and ask their opinions.
2. Be courageous about talking of your own fears.
3. See if you are delaying for reasons that are only in your head.
4. By enlisting the aid of a confidant to help you with your procrastination, you will have made it a joint effort.
5. Soon you will dissipate much of the anxiousness that goes along with procrastination by sharing that as well.

"a move, a sex problem, a new job"

### Step 20: Writing a Contract with Your Loved Ones to Deliver Postponed Goods

1. Write a contract with your loved ones in which you will deliver the goods you want to but which you may have been postponing.
2. Have each party keep a copy of the contract.
3. Build in penalties for defaulting.
4. Examples of contract items: a ball game, dinner-out, vacation, or theater visit.

"Whether it's a ball game, dinner-out, vacation, or theater visit, you'll find this strategy helpful and personally rewarding, since you'll be participating in events that you also find enjoyable."

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You criticize others to camouflage your own refusal to act, feeling important at the expense of others while remaining stuck.
  **Solution:** When you catch yourself being a critic, ask "Do you think I need a critic now?" and move from the critic to the doer column.

- **Problem:** You delay starting tasks because they feel overwhelming, using "I don't have time" or fatigue as excuses.
  **Solution:** Give yourself a designated time slot (for example, Wednesday from 10:00 to 10:15 P.M.) and use those fifteen minutes to begin. Much of your putting it off is unnecessary since you will likely find the job enjoyable once you start.

- **Problem:** You allow anxiety about a future event to immobilize you in the present moment, substituting the now with worry.
  **Solution:** Make the event become the now. Look carefully at your current moment, decide what you are avoiding, and begin tackling the fear of living effectively.

- **Problem:** You get sick or tired when faced with a troublesome task, using illness as an escape.
  **Solution:** Decide not to be tired until the moment before you get into bed. When you take away the reason for the illness—avoidance of a task—physical problems "magically" disappear.
