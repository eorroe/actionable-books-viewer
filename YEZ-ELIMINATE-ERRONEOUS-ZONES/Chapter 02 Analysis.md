# Chapter II: First Love

## Overview

This chapter teaches that self-worth is a baseline given—not something to be earned, verified, or borrowed from others. It provides the foundational understanding that your worth is intact regardless of your behavior, others' opinions, or external achievements, along with practical exercises for building and maintaining genuine self-love. The chapter also exposes the hidden payoffs of self-scorn and offers a structured toolkit for eliminating self-put-down behaviors and replacing them with self-acceptance.

## When to Follow These Book Teachings

- When you find yourself depending on others' approval, compliments, or reactions to feel worthy or valuable
- When you engage in self-put-down behaviors—rejecting compliments, making excuses for your appearance, or withholding enjoyment from yourself
- When you want to build a stable, internal sense of worth that persists regardless of circumstances, achievements, or what others think

## Steps

### Step 1: Claim Your Self-Worth Independently of Others

Recognize that your worth is determined by you alone, not by others' opinions or behavior toward you. Stop depending on others for your value; if you do, it becomes "other-worth" rather than self-worth. If you are secure in yourself, you neither want nor need others to conform to your dictates. When you feel loved and worthy, you can love others genuinely—not for thanks or payoffs, but because of the genuine pleasure of being a helper or lover. Giving love is directly related to how much love you have for yourself. If the self you hold is unworthy or unloved, giving becomes impossible.

### Step 2: Separate Your Self-Worth From Your Behavior

Separate your self-worth (which is a given) from your behavior or the behavior of others toward you. Reframe messages: instead of "I am bad because I am bad," think "She doesn't like me. That's her decision, and while I don't like it, I'm still important." Even if you have behaved in a way you dislike, do not loathe yourself. Learn from the error and resolve not to repeat it, but do not associate it with your self-worth.

### Step 3: Practice Self-Acceptance Across All Dimensions

Destroy the myth that you have one single self-concept that is either entirely positive or entirely negative. You have many self-images that vary from moment to moment. Do not lump all negative self-thoughts into a collective "No" when asked "Do you like yourself?" Break down the areas of dislike into specifics. This gives you definite goals to work on. Acknowledge that your self-worth—the ever-present shadow, your consultant for personal happiness and mastery—must be unrelated to your self-assessments. You exist. You are human. That is all you need. Choose to be worthy to yourself forever, then work on your self-images separately from that baseline worth.

### Step 4: Develop Loving Acceptance of Your Body

Make a very long list of every part of your body—hair, forehead, eyes, eyelids, cheeks, mouth, nose, teeth, neck, arms, fingers, breasts, stomach, kidneys, spleen, arteries, femurs, Rolandic fissure, cochlea, pinna, adrenal glands, uvula, and all other components. Identify which parts you find objectionable. For parts that can be changed (e.g., stomach too big, hair wrong color), view them as choices made in an earlier present moment and make new present-moment decisions about them. Make changing them a goal if you wish. For parts that cannot be changed (legs too long, eyes too narrow, breasts too small or large), reject society's definition of beauty and decide to like the physical you. Nothing is too anything, and long legs are no better or worse than hair or non-hair. Decide to like your physical self and declare it worthy and attractive to you, thereby rejecting the comparisons and opinions of others. Accept your natural human body without shame. Do not be embarrassed or apologetic when your body behaves naturally. If you choose to use cosmetic aids, do so only for novelty or personal fulfillment—not from disliking what you are covering up.

### Step 5: Choose Positive Self-Images Deliberately

Choose to think of yourself as intelligent by applying your very own standards to yourself. Understand that aptitude is really a function of time rather than some inborn quality. With enough time and effort you could master almost any academic skill. If you dislike how smart you've chosen to be, recognize this as mere self-contempt, not a fixed limitation. Apply the same logic to all self-pictures: you are as socially adept, artistic, mechanical, athletic, and musical as you choose to be. Do not confuse your abilities with your worthiness. Decide now to base self-acceptance on what you believe is appropriate for you. Repairing things that don't measure up can be a delightful undertaking.

### Step 6: Identify and Eliminate Self-Veto (Self-Put-Down) Behaviors

Become aware of when you engage in self-put-down behavior. Every time you do, you reinforce old self-doubt and reduce opportunities for love. Stop rejecting compliments. Accept them with a simple "thank-you." Stop making up excuses for why you look nice. Own your appearance. Stop giving credit to others when it rightfully belongs to you. Stop using other-directed references when speaking ("My husband says..."). Speak for yourself. Stop having your opinions verified by others. Trust your own judgment. Stop refusing to order something you want because you don't feel you're worth it. Stop withholding orgasms or sexual fulfillment from yourself. Stop not buying yourself things because you think you have to buy them for someone else instead. Stop avoiding indulgences (flowers, wine, etc.) because you feel wasteful. Stop turning when someone yells "Hey, Stupid" in a crowd. Stop using pet names for yourself (and having others use them) that are really put-downs: Dum-dum, Silly, Sweetie-pie, Baby (for an adult), Funny-face, Shorty, Fatty, Baldy. Stop dismissing gifts or compliments with negative thoughts like "You must have a drawer full of jewelry at home for other girls" or "You're practically blind or trying to make me feel good." Stop thinking relationships will end once someone discovers the real you. Stop believing others accept you only out of charity.

### Step 7: Practice Acceptance Without Complaint

Accept yourself as a worthy person because you choose to do so. Eliminate complaint, especially about things over which you can do nothing (rough rocks, cloudy sky, cold ice). Stop complaining to others about your own dissatisfaction; they are almost always powerless to do anything about it. Stop permitting others to abuse you with their self-pity and misery. Ask them: "Why are you telling me this?" or "Is there anything I can do to help you with this?" Ask yourself the same questions when you catch yourself complaining: "Why am I telling myself this?" and "Is there anything I can do to help me with this?" Do not complain about being tired or not feeling well. If you are tired, exercise options other than complaining. It abuses the listener and won't make you less tired. If you are truly working on self-love and experience pain or discomfort, work on it yourself rather than choosing someone to lean on. If you notice things in yourself or others that you dislike, actively set about taking corrective steps rather than complaining. Practice silent self-praise or helping someone else achieve fulfillment instead of complaining.

### Step 8: Distinguish True Self-Love From Conceit

Understand that self-love has nothing to do with boastful, chest-thumping behavior designed to win attention and approval from others. Recognize that boastful behavior is neurotic and motivated by how others see you. It means the individual is evaluating himself on the basis of external views. True self-love requires no convincing of others. Internal acceptance is sufficient. Do not demand the love of others as proof of your worth. Self-love means you love yourself regardless of external validation.

### Step 9: Examine the Rewards for Not Loving Yourself (The Self-Scorn Maintenance System)

Understand that all behavior is caused. To eliminate self-destructive behavior, you must first understand why you engage in it and what maintenance system keeps it in place. Identify which of the following dividends (payoffs) you receive from self-denunciation:

- A built-in excuse for why you can't get love: you simply aren't worth being loved back.
- Avoidance of risks that come with love relationships, eliminating any possibility of rejection or disapproval.
- Easier to stay the same; if you're not worthy, there is no point in trying to grow or be better, so you remain unchanged.
- Gain pity, attention, and even approval from others, which substitutes for the risky business of a real love relationship.
- Convenient scapegoats to blame for your own misery, so you don't have to do anything about it yourself.
- Present-moment mini-depressions that allow you to avoid behavior that would help you be different; self-pity serves as an escape route.
- Regression to being a good little boy or girl, calling upon leftover child responses to please the "big-people" you learned to regard as superior; regression feels safer than risk.
- Reinforcing leaning-on-others behavior by making others more significant than yourself.
- Inability to take charge of your own life because you won't feel you are worth the happiness you covet.

Recognize that these rewards, while unhealthy, are real and explain why you hang on to old thinking. Remember that refusing to grow into a self-loving person is a deathlike choice; the only evidence for life is growth.

### Step 10: Master Self-Love Through Practical Exercises

**Correct self-loathing thoughts out loud:** When you catch yourself saying something like "I'm really not that smart, I guess I was just lucky," recognize that you have just behaved in a self-loathing way. Correct yourself immediately out loud with a statement such as: "I just said I was lucky, but luck really had nothing to do with it. I got the grade because I deserved it." This is one small step toward self-love—a recognition of the present-moment put-down and a decision to act differently.

**Accept love and acceptance with gratitude:** When others reach you with love or acceptance, select new responses. Instead of instantly being skeptical, accept them with "thank-you" or "I'm happy that you feel that way."

**Say "I love you" directly:** If there is someone you feel genuine love toward, say it right out front: "I love you." While checking out the reactions you receive in return, pat yourself on the back for taking the risk.

**Order what you really want:** In a restaurant, order something you really enjoy no matter what it costs. Give yourself a treat because you are worth it. Begin to select items that you would prefer in all situations, including the grocery store. Indulge yourself with a favorite product because you are worth it. Outlaw self-denial unless absolutely necessary—and it rarely is.

**Rest and recharge:** After a tiring day and a large meal, take a brief nap or a jog in the park even if you have too many things to do. It will help you feel one hundred percent better.

**Join an enjoyable organization or activity:** Perhaps you've been putting this off because of responsibilities and lack of time. By choosing to love yourself and partake of the slices of life you want, those you serve will begin to learn self-reliance of their own. You will serve them out of choice rather than obligation, and you will experience an absence of resentment toward them.

**Eliminate jealousy:** Recognize that jealousy is a put-down of yourself. By comparing yourself to another and imagining you are loved less, you make others more important than you. Remind yourself that (1) someone can always choose another without it being a reflection on you, and (2) whether or not you are chosen by any significant other is not how you validate your own self-worth. With practice at self-love, circumstances that previously caused jealousy will be reversed. You will believe so much in yourself that you won't need the love or approval of others to give you value.

**Treat your body well:** Select good nutritional foods, eliminate excess weight (a health risk as well as an indication of self-rejection), take regular walks or bicycle rides, choose plenty of healthy exercise, get outdoors to enjoy fresh air because it feels good. Keep your body healthy and attractive because you are important and are going to treat yourself that way. Any total day spent cooped up or inactive in boring routine activities is a vote for self-enmity.

**Practice sexual self-love:** Stand naked in front of a mirror and tell yourself how attractive you are. Get in touch with your body. Explore yourself sensually, give yourself goosebumps of shivery pleasure. With others, choose sexual fulfillment for yourself rather than making your partner's pleasure more important than your own. Slow down the process of sex, teaching your lover what you like with words and actions. Choose orgasm for yourself. Make yourself achieve the ultimate physical experience by believing that you are worth it, and then getting lost in the excitement of verifying it for you.

**Stop equating performance with self-worth:** You may lose your job or fail a project. You may not like how you performed a task. But that doesn't mean you are without worth. You must know for yourself that you are worth something regardless of your achievements. Once you eliminate this confusion, you will be able to undertake all kinds of projects, and your final score—while it may be interesting to you—will in no way determine how valuable you are as a person.

## Examples

### Example 1: Noah — Needing Approval Before Saying "I Love You"

Noah, a middle-aged man, could not say "I love you" to his wife and children because he needed affirmation of his self-worth in return. Once he recognized his worth was intact before he started, saying those words would become possible regardless of the response. His withholding of love was itself a self-veto behavior rooted in the belief that he needed external validation to be worthy.

### Example 2: Shirley — Sabotaging Relationships Through Self-Scorn

Shirley, a young woman, sabotaged every romantic relationship. When a man told her he cared, her head contradicted: "He's only saying that because he knows it's what I want to hear." She searched for sentences that repudiated her worth because she didn't believe she was worth being loved. The cycle of renunciation reinforced her lack of worth. Her behavior exemplifies how self-put-downs reduce opportunities for genuine love.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Claim your self-worth as a given, independent of others' opinions or your achievements
- ✅ Separate your self-worth from your behavior and from how others treat you
- ✅ Break down negative self-thoughts into specific areas rather than lumping them all together
- ✅ Reject society's definition of beauty and decide for yourself what is attractive
- ✅ Apply your own standards to judge your intelligence and abilities, not external benchmarks
- ✅ Accept compliments with a simple "thank-you" rather than deflecting or dismissing them
- ✅ Speak for yourself instead of referencing others' opinions
- ✅ Stop withholding enjoyment, treats, or fulfillment from yourself
- ✅ Correct self-loathing thoughts out loud the moment you catch them
- ✅ Accept love and expressions of affection with gratitude
- ✅ Treat your body with care because you are worth it
- ✅ Practice sexual self-love and prioritize your own fulfillment
- ✅ Know that your final score in life does not determine your value as a person
- ❌ Don't depend on others to verify your self-worth or make you feel valuable
- ❌ Don't confuse your abilities or performance with your fundamental worthiness
- ❌ Don't reject compliments or make excuses for positive things said about you
- ❌ Don't use self-put-down pet names or let others use them for you
- ❌ Don't believe that others accept you only out of charity or pity
- ❌ Don't let jealousy of others diminish your own sense of worth
- ❌ Don't complain about things you cannot change; take corrective action instead
- ❌ Don't practice self-denial as a rule—indulge because you are worth it

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You depend on others to affirm your self-worth, and feel empty or devastated when their response is negative or absent.
  **Solution:** Recognize that your worth is intact before you start. Others' reactions reflect their own state, not yours. You neither need nor want others to conform to your dictates in order to feel worthy.

- **Problem:** You engage in self-veto behaviors—rejecting compliments, dismissing achievements, or withholding enjoyment—that reinforce old self-doubt and reduce opportunities for love.
  **Solution:** Become aware of each self-put-down in the moment it occurs. Correct it out loud: "I just said I was lucky, but luck had nothing to do with it. I got the grade because I deserved it." Accept love and compliments with "thank-you."

- **Problem:** You confuse self-love with conceit, and avoid claiming your worth because you don't want to appear boastful or arrogant.
  **Solution:** Understand that self-love is internal acceptance that requires no convincing of others. Conceit is neurotic behavior designed to win external approval. True self-love is silent and sufficient on its own.

- **Problem:** You don't understand why you continue to engage in self-destructive behavior even when you know it hurts you.
  **Solution:** Identify the specific neurotic dividends you receive from self-denunciation—such as avoiding risk, gaining pity, or having a ready-made excuse. These payoffs, while unhealthy, are real and explain why you hang on to old thinking. Only by seeing them clearly can you choose differently.
