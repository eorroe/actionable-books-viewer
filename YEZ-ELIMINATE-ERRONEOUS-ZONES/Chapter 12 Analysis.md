# Chapter XII: Portrait of a Person Who Has Eliminated All Erroneous Zones

## Overview

This chapter paints a comprehensive portrait of a person who has eliminated all erroneous zones, describing 24 distinct characteristics of someone who lives fully in the present moment without fear, anger, dependency, or approval-seeking. It covers how such a person likes virtually everything about life, is free from guilt and worry, lives without approval-seeking, and maintains high energy and aggressive curiosity. The chapter shows that such individuals are independent, honest, non-blaming, and creatively engaged with life, serving as a complete model for personal transformation.

## When to Follow These Book Teachings

- When you want to understand what a fully realized person who has eliminated all erroneous zones looks like
- When you need a comprehensive portrait of the attitudes and behaviors of someone living without fear, anger, or dependency
- When you're looking for a holistic model to guide your own personal development across all areas of life

## Steps

### Step 1: Like Virtually Everything About Life

1. Like life itself—picnics, movies, books, sports, concerts, cities, farms, animals, mountains, and just about everything.
2. If it rains, like it. If it's hot, dig it rather than complain about it. If in a traffic jam, at a party, or all alone, simply deal with what is there.
3. Develop a sensible acceptance of what is, and an outlandish ability to delight in that reality.
4. Do not pretend to enjoy; be genuine in your enjoyment.
5. When asked what you don't like, be hard pressed to come up with an honest answer.
6. See rain as beautiful, thrilling, and something to experience.
7. Observe slush, splash around in it, and accept it as part of what it means to be alive.
8. Like cats, bears, worms, and all of life.
9. When situations such as disease, droughts, mosquitoes, floods arise, do not spend present moments complaining about them or wishing they weren't so.
10. If situations need to be eradicated, work at eradicating them—and enjoy the work.

The book describes someone who doesn't have sense enough to come in out of the rain because they see rain as beautiful and thrilling. They observe slush and splash around in it rather than sending them into a fury. Try as you might, you'll have a tough time coming up with something they'll dislike doing. Truly they are likers of life, and they wallow in all of it, getting out of it all that is possible for them.

### Step 2: Be Free From Guilt

1. Admit to making mistakes and vow to avoid repeating certain behavior that is counterproductive.
2. Do not waste time wishing that you hadn't done something.
3. Do not be upset because you dislike something that you did at an earlier moment in life.
4. Do not lament the past.
5. Do not make others choose guilt by asking inane questions such as "Why didn't you do it differently?" or "Aren't you ashamed of yourself?"
6. Recognize that life lived through is just that, and no amount of feeling bad will alter the past.
7. See that feeling bad in the present moment only reinforces a poor self-image.
8. Learn from the past rather than remonstrating about the past.
9. Never manipulate others by telling them how bad they've been.
10. When someone tries to manipulate you with guilt tactics, do not get angry at them; simply ignore them, go away, or change the subject.
11. When guilt comes along, unceremoniously pass it by rather than make yourself and others miserable with it.

The book notes that healthy fulfilled people are free from guilt and all the attendant anxiety that goes with using any present moments in being immobilized over past events. The strategies that work so beautifully on most people fail with these healthy individuals. They are free from guilt themselves without any effort because it is natural; they never help others to choose guilt.

### Step 3: Be a Nonworrier

1. Refuse to worry.
2. Keep yourself free from the anxiety that accompanies worry.
3. Recognize that worrying must take place in the present moment and that it is a foolish way to go about living your current life.
4. Do not spend present moments agonizing about things in the future over which you have no control.

These individuals don't know how to worry. It is not a part of their makeup. They are not necessarily calm at all moments, but they are unwilling to spend present moments agonizing about things in the future over which they have no control. They have an internal signal that seems to remind them that all worrying must take place in the present moment, and that it is a foolish way to go about living one's current life.

### Step 4: Live in the Present Moment

1. Live now rather than in the past or the future.
2. Do not be threatened by the unknown; seek out experiences that are new and unfamiliar.
3. Love ambiguity.
4. Savor the now at all times, aware that this is all you have.
5. Do not plan for a future event and let long periods of inactivity elapse as you await that event.
6. Treat the moments between events as just as livable as those taken up by the events themselves.
7. Get every pleasure out of your daily life.
8. Do not be a postponer, saving for a rainy day.
9. Gather in your happiness now, and when a future now arrives, gather in that one as well.
10. See the folly of waiting to enjoy.
11. Be busy grabbing present-moment fulfillment rather than waiting for payoffs and never being able to seize them.

They are not postponers, saving for a rainy day, and while our culture disapproves their behavior, they are unthreatened by self-reproach. These individuals are always enjoying simply because they see the folly of waiting to enjoy. It is a natural way of living, very much like that of a child or an animal. They are busy grabbing present-moment fulfillment, while most people spend their lives waiting for payoffs, and never being able to seize them.

### Step 5: Be Strikingly Independent

1. See independence as superior to dependence in all relationships.
2. Treasure your own freedom from expectations.
3. Build relationships upon mutual respect for the right of an individual to make decisions for himself.
4. Do not impose your values on the loved one.
5. Put a high premium on privacy.
6. Like to be alone at times and go to great lengths to ensure your privacy is protected.
7. Be selective about your love but be deeply and sensitively loving.
8. Want those you love to be independent, to make their own choices, and to live their lives for themselves.
9. Want even more for others to make it without crutches or leaning.
10. When someone starts leaning on you, disappear first emotionally and then physically.
11. Refuse to be dependent, or depended upon, in a mature relationship.
12. With children, provide a model of a caring person but encourage self-reliance almost from the very beginning with a great amount of love offered at every turn.

They are out of the nest, and while they may have a strong love for and devotion to family, they see independence as superior to dependence in all relationships. If someone needs them, they reject such a need as hurtful to the other person as well as to themselves. Thus, the moment you start leaning on these people, you'll find them disappearing, first emotionally, and then physically as well. They refuse to be dependent, or depended upon, in a mature relationship.

### Step 6: Live Without Approval-Seeking

1. Function without approval and applause from others.
2. Do not seek out honors as most people do.
3. Be unusually free from the opinion of others, almost uncaring about whether someone else likes what you've said or done.
4. Do not attempt to shock others or to gain their approval.
5. Be internally directed and literally unconcerned about others' evaluations of your behavior.
6. Do not couch your messages in carefully worded phrases designed to please; if you want someone to know what you think, say exactly what you think.
7. When someone says something about you, do not be destroyed or immobilized; take the data they provide, filter it through your own values, and use it for growth.
8. Do not need to be loved by everyone.
9. Do not harbor the inordinate wish to be approved by all for everything that you do.
10. Recognize that you will always incur some disapproval.
11. Function as you, rather than some external other, dictates.

They are able to function without approval and applause from others. They are unusual in that they are able to function as they, rather than some external other, dictates. They can be almost blunt in their honesty since they do not couch their messages in carefully worded phrases designed to please. If you want to know what they think, that is exactly what you'll hear. They do not need to be loved by everyone, nor do they harbor the inordinate wish to be approved by all for everything that they do.

### Step 7: Resist Enculturation

1. Make your own choices even if those choices conflict with what everybody else does.
2. Ignore petty rules if they make no sense.
3. Quietly shrug at the little conventions that are such an important part of so many lives.
4. Do not engage in small talk because it's the polite thing to do.
5. Refuse to be ruled by society or to become a slave to it.
6. Do not rebelliously attack society; know internally when to ignore and function in a clear-headed and sensible way.

They are not rebels, but they do make their own choices even if those choices conflict with what everybody else does. They can ignore petty rules if they make no sense, and quietly shrug at the little conventions that are such an important part of so many lives. They're not cocktail partiers, nor do they engage in small talk because it's the polite thing to do. They are their own people and while they see society as an important part of their lives, they refuse to be ruled by it or to become a slave to it. They don't rebelliously attack but they do know internally when to ignore and function in a clear-headed and sensible way.

### Step 8: Know How to Laugh and Create Laughter

1. Find humor in virtually all situations.
2. Laugh at the most absurd as well as the most solemn of occasions.
3. Love to help others to laugh and be facile at creating humor.
4. Do not use ridicule to create laughter.
5. Do not laugh at people; laugh with them.
6. Laugh at life and see the whole thing as fun, even though you are deliberate in your own pursuits.
7. Recognize that there is really no such thing as the right thing in the right place.
8. Create an atmosphere in which others can choose joy for themselves.

They find humor in virtually all situations, and they can laugh at the most absurd as well as the most solemn of occasions. They love to help others to laugh, and they are facile at creating humor. They do not have good timing for they know that there is really no such thing as the right thing in the right place. They love the incongruous, yet they are unhostile in their humor; never, but never using ridicule to create laughter. They don't laugh at people, they laugh with them. They do laugh at life, and they see the whole thing as fun, even though they are deliberate in their own pursuits.

### Step 9: Accept Yourself Without Complaint

1. Know that you are a human being and that being so involves certain human attributes.
2. Know that you look a certain way and accept it.
3. Accept being tall or short, bald or with lots of hair.
4. Be able to live with sweat.
5. Do not be phony about your physical humanity.
6. Accept what you are.
7. Do not hide behind artificialities or apologize for what you are.
8. Do not know how to be offended by anything that is human.
9. Like yourself and accept what you are.
10. Accept all of nature for what it is rather than wishing it were otherwise.
11. Do not complain about things that won't change such as heat waves, rainstorms, or cold water.
12. Accept yourself and the world as it is.
13. Do not engage in self-deprecation or wishing.

They know that they look a certain way, and they accept it. If they're tall, that's okay, but so is being short. Bald is fine, and so is lots of hair. They can live with sweat! They are not phony about their physical humanity. They've accepted themselves, and therefore they are the most natural of people. No hiding behind artificialities, no apologizing for what they are. They don't know how to be offended by anything that is human. They like themselves and accept what they are. Similarly, they accept all of nature for what it is, rather than wishing it were otherwise. They never complain about things that won't change, such as heat waves, rainstorms, or cold water. They accept themselves and the world as it is. No pretenses, no groaning, simply acceptance. Follow them around for years, and you'll never hear self-deprecation or wishing. You'll see doers doing. You'll see them observing the world the way it is, like a child who accepts the natural world and enjoys it for all it's worth.

### Step 10: Appreciate the Natural World

1. Love being outdoors in nature.
2. Trip around in all that is unspoiled and original.
3. Especially love mountains, sunsets, rivers, flowers, trees, animals and virtually all flora and fauna.
4. Be unceremonious and unpretentious.
5. Love the naturalness of the universe.
6. Be at peace with nature.
7. Be capable of functioning in a man-made world.
8. Appreciate what has become stale for others.
9. Never tire of a sunset or a trip in the woods.
10. Spontaneously appreciate over and over again—a bird in flight, a caterpillar, a cat giving birth to kittens.

They love being outdoors in nature, and tripping around in all that is unspoiled and original. They especially love things like mountains, sunsets, rivers, flowers, trees, animals and virtually all flora and fauna. They are naturalists as people, unceremonious and unpretentious, and they love the naturalness of the universe. They never tire of a sunset or a trip in the woods. A bird in flight is a magnificent sight over and over again. A caterpillar never grows tiresome, nor does a cat giving birth to kittens. Over and over again they spontaneously appreciate.

### Step 11: Have Insight Into Others and Yourself

1. See what may seem complex and indecipherable to others as clear and understandable.
2. View the problems that immobilize so many others as only minor annoyances.
3. Remain uninvolved emotionally in problems so that you can surmount barriers that remain insurmountable to others.
4. Recognize immediately what others are attempting to do to you.
5. Shrug and ignore while others are angered and immobilized.
6. Never be perplexed or stumped; view what seems confusing or insoluble to most others as a simple condition with a ready resolution.
7. See a problem as really only an obstacle to be overcome rather than a reflection of what you are or aren't as a person.
8. Locate your self-worth within so that any external concerns can be viewed objectively rather than as a threat to your value.

They have insight into the behavior of others, and what may seem complex and indecipherable to others, they see as clear and understandable. The problems that immobilize so many others are often viewed as only minor annoyances by these people. This lack of emotional involvement in problems makes them able to surmount barriers that remain insurmountable to others. They have insight into themselves too, and they recognize immediately what others are attempting to do to them. They can shrug and ignore while others are angered and immobilized. They are never perplexed or stumped and what may seem to be confusing or insoluble to most others is often viewed by them as a simple condition with a ready resolution.

### Step 12: Never Engage in Useless Fighting

1. If fighting will help bring about change, then fight.
2. Never find it necessary to fight uselessly.
3. Do not be a martyr.
4. Be a doer.
5. Be a helper.
6. Be almost always engaged in work that will make other people's lives more pleasant or tolerable.
7. Do not take your struggles to bed with you every night as a breeding ground for ulcers, heart disease and other physical disorders.
8. Be incapable of stereotyping—often don't even notice physical differences in people including racial, ethnic, size and sexual.
9. Do not judge others by their looks.
10. Spend vast amounts of time in the service of others—because you like it that way.

They are not band-wagoners, jumping on various causes as a way of bringing importance to themselves. If fighting will help bring about change, then they will fight but never will they find it necessary to fight uselessly. They are not martyrs. They are doers. They are also helpers. They are almost always engaged in work that will make other people's lives more pleasant or tolerable. They are warriors on the forefront of social change, and yet they don't take their struggles to bed with them every night as a breeding ground for ulcers, heart disease and other physical disorders. They are incapable of stereotyping. They often don't even notice the physical differences in people, including racial, ethnic, size and sexual.

### Step 13: Treat Your Body Well

1. Do not believe in being immobilized by colds and headaches.
2. Believe in your ability to rid yourself of such maladies.
3. Do not go around telling others how bad you feel, how tired you are, or what diseases are currently infecting your body.
4. Treat your body well.
5. Eat well.
6. Exercise regularly as a way of living.
7. Refuse to experience most of the infirmities that render many people helpless for various periods of time.
8. Like to live well, and do.

These are not sickly people. They don't believe in being immobilized by colds and headaches. They believe in their ability to rid themselves of such maladies, and they never go around telling others how bad they feel, how tired they are, or what diseases are currently infecting their bodies. They treat their bodies well. They like themselves, and consequently they eat well, exercise regularly (as a way of living) and refuse to experience most of the infirmities that render many people helpless for various periods of time. They like to live well, and they do.

### Step 14: Be Honest

1. Do not be evasive with your responses.
2. Do not pretend or lie about anything.
3. See lying as a distortion of your own reality and refuse to participate in self-delusional behavior.
4. Avoid having to distort to protect others.
5. Behave in ways that allow others to make their own decisions rather than protecting them from reality.
6. Deal effectively with what is rather than what you would like it to be.

They are not evasive with their responses, nor do they pretend or lie about anything. They see lying as a distortion of their own reality, and they will not participate in self-delusional behavior. While they are private people they will also avoid having to distort to protect others. They know that they are in charge of their own world, and that others are as well. Thus, they will behave in ways that will often be perceived as cruel but in fact they are simply allowing others to make their own decisions. They deal effectively with what is, rather than what they would like to be.

### Step 15: Do Not Blame

1. Be internal in your personality orientation.
2. Refuse to ascribe responsibility to others for what you are.
3. Do not spend a great deal of time talking about others and focusing on what someone else has done or failed to do.
4. Do not talk about people; talk with them.
5. Help others and yourself to assign responsibility where it belongs.
6. Do not gossip or spread evil information.
7. Be so busy being effective in your own life that you have no time for the petty connivances that occupy many people's lives.

These people don't blame. They are internal in their personality orientation, and they refuse to ascribe responsibility to others for what they are. Similarly, they will not spend a great deal of time talking about others and focusing on what someone else has done or failed to do. They do not talk about people, they talk with them. They do not blame others, they help others and themselves to assign responsibility where it belongs. They are not gossips or spreaders of evil information. They are so busy being effective in their own lives that they have no time for the petty connivances that occupy many people's lives. Doers do. Critics blame and complain.

### Step 16: Have Little Concern With Order, Organization, or Systems

1. Have self-discipline but no need to have things and people fit into your own perceptions of how everything ought to be.
2. Have no oughts for others.
3. See everyone as having choices and recognize that petty things that drive others insane are simply the results of someone else's decision.
4. Do not see the world as having to be any special way.
5. Have no preoccupation with cleanliness or orderliness.
6. Exist in a functional way, and if everything isn't fitting in as you would prefer, find that all right too.
7. Use organization as a useful means rather than an end in itself.
8. Attack any concern in your own unique way—making a bowl of soup, writing a report, or mowing the grass.
9. Apply your own imagination to the act.
10. Do not have to do it a certain way.
11. Do not consult manuals or ask experts; simply attack the problem as you see fit.

These individuals have little concern with order, organization, or systems in their lives. They have self-discipline but no need to have things and people fit into their own perceptions of how everything ought to be. They have no oughts for others. They see everyone as having choices, and those petty things that drive others insane are simply the results of someone else's decision. They do not see the world as having to be any special way. They have no preoccupation with cleanliness or orderliness. They exist in a functional way, and if everything isn't fitting in as they would prefer, they find that all right too. Organization then, for these people, is simply a useful means rather than an end in itself. Because of this lack of organizational neurosis, they are creative. They attack any concern in their own unique way, be it making a bowl of soup, writing a report, or mowing the grass. They apply their own imagination to the act, and the result is a creative approach to everything. They don't have to do it a certain way. They don't consult manuals or ask experts; they simply attack the problem as they see fit. This is creativity, and without exception, they have it.

### Step 17: Maintain High Energy Levels

1. Do not know how to be bored.
2. See all life events as opportunities for doing, thinking, feeling and living.
3. Apply your energy in virtually all life circumstances.
4. Were you to be imprisoned, use your mind in creative ways to avoid the paralysis of loss of interest.
5. Channel the same energy that others have in ways productive for yourself.

These are people with exceptionally high energy levels. They seem to require less sleep, and yet they are excited about living. They do, and they are healthy. They can muster tremendous surges of energy for completing a task because they choose to be involved in it as a fulfilling present-moment activity. Their energy is not supernatural; it is simply the result of loving life and all the activities in it. They don't know how to be bored. All life events present opportunities for doing, thinking, feeling and living, and they know how to apply their energy in virtually all life circumstances. Were they to be imprisoned, they would use their minds in creative ways to avoid the paralysis of loss of interest. Boredom is not in their lives because they are channeling the same energy that others have in ways productive for themselves.

### Step 18: Be Aggressively Curious

1. Never know enough; search for more and want to learn each and every present moment of your life.
2. Be not concerned with having to do it right or having done it wrong.
3. If something doesn't work, or doesn't accomplish the greatest amount of good, discard it rather than mull it over in regret.
4. Be a truth seeker in the learning sense, always excited about learning more.
5. Never believe you are a finished product.
6. Learn from children and stock brokers and animals.
7. Want to know more about what it means to be any kind of person—a welder, a cook, or a corporate vice-president.
8. Be a learner, not a teacher.
9. Never know how to act snobbish or superior, since you never feel that way.
10. See every person, every object, every event as an opportunity for knowing more.
11. Be aggressive in your interests, not waiting for information to come along, but going after it.
12. Do not be afraid to talk to a waitress, ask a dentist what it feels like to have your hands in someone's mouth all day, or inquire of the poet what is meant by this or that line.

They are aggressively curious. They never know enough. They search for more and want to learn each and every present moment of their lives. They are not concerned with having to do it right or having done it wrong. If it doesn't work, or it doesn't accomplish the greatest amount of good, then it is discarded, rather than mulled over in regret. They are truth seekers in the learning sense, always excited about learning more and never believing they are a finished product. If they are around a barber, they want to learn about barbering. They never feel or act superior, showing off their merit badges for others to applaud. They learn from children and stock brokers and animals. They want to know more about what it means to be a welder, a cook, a hooker, or a corporate vice-president. They are learners not teachers. They never know enough, and they don't know how to act snobbish or superior, since they never feel that way. Every person, every object, every event represents an opportunity for knowing more. And they are aggressive in their interests, not waiting for information to come along, but going after it.

### Step 19: Do Not Fear Failure

1. Welcome failure.
2. Do not equate being successful in any enterprise with being successful as a human being.
3. Keep your self-worth from within so that any external event can be viewed objectively as simply effective or ineffective.
4. Know that failing is merely somebody else's editorial opinion and not to be feared since it cannot affect self-worth.
5. Try anything.
6. Participate just because it's fun.
7. Never fear having to explain yourself.

They are not afraid to fail. In fact, they often welcome it. They do not equate being successful in any enterprise with being successful as a human being. Since their self-worth comes from within, any external event can be viewed objectively as simply effective or ineffective. They know that failing is merely somebody else's editorial opinion and not to be feared since it cannot affect self-worth. Thus, they will try anything, participate just because it's fun, and never fear having to explain themselves.

### Step 20: Do Not Choose Anger in Any Immobilizing Way

1. Do not say to yourself that other people should behave differently and that events should be otherwise.
2. Accept others as they are.
3. Work at changing events that you dislike.
4. Eliminate emotions that are in any way self-destructive and facilitate those which are self-enhancing.

Similarly, they never choose anger in any immobilizing way. Using the same logic (and not having to think it through each time since it has become a way of life) they don't say to themselves that other people should behave differently and that events should be otherwise. They accept others as they are, and they work at changing events that they dislike. Thus, anger is impossible because the expectancies are not there. These are people who are capable of eliminating emotions that are in any way self-destructive and facilitating those which are self-enhancing.

### Step 21: Be Without Defensiveness

1. Do not play games and try to impress others.
2. Do not dress for others' approval.
3. Do not go through the motions of explaining yourself.
4. Have simplicity and naturalness.
5. Do not get seduced into making issues of small or big things.
6. Do not be an arguer or hot-headed debater.
7. State your views, listen to others and recognize the futility of trying to convince someone else to be as they are.
8. Say "That's all right; we're just different. We don't have to agree."
9. Let it go at that without any need to win an argument or persuade the opponent of the wrongness of his position.
10. Be unafraid of giving a bad impression but do not strive to do so.

These happy individuals display an admirable lack of defensiveness. They won't play games and try to impress others. They don't dress for others' approval, nor do they go through the motions of explaining themselves. They have a simplicity and naturalness, and they won't get seduced into making issues of small or big things. They aren't arguers or hot-headed debaters; they simply state their views, listen to others and recognize the futility of trying to convince someone else to be as they are. They'll simply say, "That's all right; we're just different. We don't have to agree." They let it go at that without any need to win an argument or persuade the opponent of the wrongness of his position. They are unafraid of giving a bad impression but they don't strive to do so.

### Step 22: Have Universal, Not Local, Values

1. Do not identify with the family, neighborhood, community, city, state, or country.
2. See yourself as belonging to the human race.
3. Do not be patriotic to a special boundary; see yourself as a part of the whole of humanity.
4. Take no glee in having more enemy dead, since the enemy is as human as the ally.
5. Do not subscribe to the lines drawn by men to describe how one should be affiliated.
6. Transcend traditional boundaries.

Their values are not local. They do not identify with the family, neighborhood, community, city, state, or country. They see themselves as belonging to the human race, and an unemployed Austrian is no better or worse than an unemployed Californian. They are not patriotic to a special boundary; rather they see themselves as a part of the whole of humanity. They take no glee in having more enemy dead, since the enemy is as human as the ally. The lines drawn by men to describe how one should be affiliated are not subscribed to. They transcend traditional boundaries, which often causes others to label them as rebels or even traitors.

### Step 23: Have No Heroes or Idols

1. View all people as human and place no one above yourself in importance.
2. Do not demand justice at every turn.
3. When someone else has more privileges, see that as a benefit to that person rather than as a reason for being unhappy.
4. When playing an opponent, want them to do well rather than wishing a poor performance in order to win by default.
5. Want to be victorious and effective on your own rather than gaining through the shortcomings of others.
6. Do not insist that everyone be equally endowed, but look inward for your happiness.
7. Do not be critics, nor take pleasure in other people's misfortunes.
8. Be too busy being to notice what your neighbors are doing.

They have no heroes or idols. They view all people as human, and they place no one above themselves in importance. They do not demand justice at every turn. When someone else has more privileges, they see that as a benefit to that person, rather than as a reason for being unhappy. When playing an opponent they want him to do well, rather than wishing a poor performance in order to win by default. They want to be victorious and effective on their own, rather than gaining through the shortcomings of others. They do not insist that everyone be equally endowed, but look inward for their happiness. They are not critics, nor do they take pleasure in other people's misfortunes. They are too busy being, to notice what their neighbors are doing.

### Step 24: Love Yourself

1. Be motivated by a desire to grow.
2. Treat yourself well when given the option.
3. Have no room for self-pity, self-rejection, or self-hate.
4. When asked "Do you like yourself?" be able to give a resounding "Of course I do!"
5. Make each day a delight.
6. Live all of your present moments.
7. Do not be problem free, but be free from emotional immobility as a result of the problems.
8. Measure your mental health not in whether you slip but in what you do when you slip.
9. When you slip, do not lie there and whine about having fallen; get up, dust yourself off, and get on with the business of living.
10. Do not chase after happiness; live and let happiness be your payoff.

Most significantly, these are individuals who love themselves. They are motivated by a desire to grow, and they always treat themselves well when given the option. They have no room for self-pity, self-rejection, or self-hate. If you ask them, "Do you like yourself?" you'll get a resounding, "Of course I do!" They are rare birds indeed. Each day is a delight. They have it together and they live all of their present moments. They are not problem free, but free from emotional immobility as a result of the problems. The measure of their mental health is not in whether they slip but in what they do when they slip. Do they lie there and whine about having fallen? No, they get up, dust themselves off, and get on with the business of living. People who are free from erroneous zones don't chase after happiness; they live and happiness is their payoff.

## Examples

### Example 1: Liking Life in All Circumstances

The book describes someone who doesn't have sense enough to come in out of the rain because they see rain as beautiful and thrilling. They observe slush and splash around in it rather than sending them into a fury. Try as you might, you'll have a tough time coming up with something they'll dislike doing. Truly they are likers of life, and they wallow in all of it, getting out of it all that is possible for them.

### Example 2: Freedom from Guilt

Healthy fulfilled people are free from guilt and all the attendant anxiety that goes with using any present moments in being immobilized over past events. The strategies that work so beautifully on most people fail with these healthy individuals. They are free from guilt themselves without any effort because it is natural; they never help others to choose guilt.

### Example 3: Living Without Approval-Seeking

They are able to function without approval and applause from others. They are unusual in that they are able to function as they, rather than some external other, dictates. They can be almost blunt in their honesty since they do not couch their messages in carefully worded phrases designed to please. If you want to know what they think, that is exactly what you'll hear. They do not need to be loved by everyone, nor do they harbor the inordinate wish to be approved by all for everything that they do.

### Example 4: Aggressive Curiosity

If they are around a barber, they want to learn about barbering. They never feel or act superior, showing off their merit badges for others to applaud. They learn from children and stock brokers and animals. They want to know more about what it means to be a welder, a cook, a hooker, or a corporate vice-president. They are learners not teachers. Every person, every object, every event represents an opportunity for knowing more. And they are aggressive in their interests, not waiting for information to come along, but going after it.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You waste present moments worrying about the future or agonizing over the past, immobilized by anxiety.
  **Solution:** Live now rather than in the past or the future. Worrying must take place in the present moment and it is a foolish way to go about living your current life. Savor the now at all times, aware that this is all you have.

- **Problem:** You seek approval and applause from others, feeling destroyed or immobilized by disapproval.
  **Solution:** Function without approval and applause from others. Be internally directed and literally unconcerned about others' evaluations of your behavior. When someone says something about you, take the data they provide, filter it through your own values, and use it for growth.

- **Problem:** You blame others for your problems and spend time talking about what someone else has done or failed to do.
  **Solution:** Be internal in your personality orientation. Refuse to ascribe responsibility to others for what you are. Do not talk about people; talk with them. Be so busy being effective in your own life that you have no time for the petty connivances that occupy many people's lives.

- **Problem:** You are immobilized by guilt over past actions, feeling bad in the present moment and making others feel guilty as well.
  **Solution:** Admit to making mistakes and vow to avoid repeating certain behavior, but do not waste time wishing that you hadn't done something. Learn from the past rather than remonstrating about it. When guilt comes along, unceremoniously pass it by.

- **Problem:** You fear failure and avoid trying new things, equating failure in an enterprise with failure as a human being.
  **Solution:** Welcome failure. Keep your self-worth from within so that any external event can be viewed objectively as simply effective or ineffective. Failing is merely somebody else's editorial opinion and cannot affect self-worth. Try anything, participate just because it's fun.
