## Chapter X: Declare Your Independence

### Lesson 1: Understand Psychological Independence vs. Psychological Dependence
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Understand that psychological independence means total freedom from all obligatory relationships and an absence of other-directed behavior.
  2. Recognize that psychological independence means being free from having to do something you would not otherwise choose, were the relationship not to exist.
  3. Identify that the obligation in a relationship is the problem, rather than the relationship itself.
  4. Note that obligation breeds guilt and dependency, while choice fosters love and independence.
- **Examples from book:** 
  - "If you want some kind of relationship, then it is not unhealthy. But if you need it, or are forced into it, and subsequently feel resentment, then you are in a self-defeating area. Thus, it is the obligation that is the problem, rather than the relationship itself. Obligation breeds guilt and dependency, while choice fosters love and independence. There is no choice in a psychologically dependent relationship, consequently there will always be indignation and ill-feelings in any such alliance."

### Lesson 2: Recognize That Dependency Is a Choice
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Understand that dependency is not something that just happens because of association with domineering people; it is a choice.
  2. Recognize that you teach people to dominate you and treat you the way you've always been treated.
  3. Acknowledge that domineering strategies are repeated only if they work—they work if they keep you in line and in a dependent position.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Dependency is not something that just happens because of association with domineering people. It is, like all erroneous zone behavior, a choice. You teach people to dominate you, and to treat you the way you've always been treated."
  - "They work if they keep you in line and in a dependent position in the relationship."

### Lesson 3: Identify Common Dependency-Fostering Behaviors
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Review the list of common dependency behaviors to identify which apply to you.
  2. Note behaviors such as feeling unable to leave the nest, asking permission of a spouse, invasions of privacy, feeling required to visit/telephone/entertain, falling into depression after a loved one's death, feeling committed to a job and never venturing out, having expectations for how others have to be, being hurt by what others say/feel/think/do, only feeling happy if your mate is happy, taking orders, allowing someone else to make decisions for you, and not doing things in front of a dominant person because they wouldn't approve.
- **Examples from book:** The book lists these specific dependency behaviors:
  - "Feeling unable to leave the nest or leaving it with bad feelings on both sides."
  - "Feeling required or obliged to visit, telephone, entertain, chauffeur and the like."
  - "Asking permission of a spouse for anything, including spending money, authority to speak, or use of the car."
  - "Invasions of privacy, such as looking through drawers and private records of children."
  - "Falling into depression and immobilization after the death of a loved one."
  - "Feeling committed to a particular job and never venturing out on your own."
  - "Having expectations for how a spouse, parent, or child has to be."
  - "Only feeling happy or successful if your mate is feeling that way."
  - "Taking orders from someone."
  - "Allowing someone else to make decisions for you, or always asking advice before deciding."
  - "Not doing something in front of a parent or dominant person because they wouldn't approve."

### Lesson 4: Recognize Common Strategies for Maintaining Dominance and Control in Relationships
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Identify which of these strategies are being used against you or by you in your relationships.
  2. Recognize that these strategies are used only if they work, and they stop when one partner refuses to be manipulated.
- **Examples from book:** The book lists these specific strategies:
  - "Yelling, screaming and voice-raising of any kind."
  - "Threatening behavior such as, 'I'll leave, I'll get a divorce.'"
  - "Inducement of guilt. 'You had no right to—' 'I don't understand how you could have done such a thing.'"
  - "The use of anger and explosive behavior, such as throwing things, swearing, hitting objects."
  - "The physical ailment ploy. Having a heart attack, headache, back problem, or whatever when one partner doesn't act the way the other wants him to."
  - "The silent treatment. No talking and deliberate sulking are superlative strategies."
  - "The tears routine in which you cry in order to help the other person to feel guilty."
  - "The leaving scene. Simply getting up and walking out is a good way to manipulate a partner."
  - "The 'You don't love me' or 'You don't understand me' contrivance for getting one's way."
  - "The suicide scheme. 'If you don't do what I want, then I'll kill myself' or 'If you leave me, I'll end it all.'"

### Lesson 5: Reframe Your Self-Talk About Treatment
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. When you feel you are receiving shabby treatment, do not ask, "Why don't you treat me better?"
  2. Instead ask, "What am I doing to teach others to treat me this way?"
  3. Put the focus on yourself and begin changing your reactions.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Rather than saying, 'Why don't you treat me better?' begin to say, 'What am I doing to teach others to treat me this way?' Put the focus on you, and begin changing those reactions."
  - "If you give the proper submissive reactions, you are teaching the other what you will tolerate."

### Lesson 6: Model Independence for Your Children (Parenting Lesson)
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Be the person you want your children to become by modeling independence, self-confidence, and self-esteem yourself.
  2. Do not make your children more important than yourself; do not sacrifice yourself for your children.
  3. Recognize that you cannot hand your children self-confidence; they must acquire it by seeing you living the same way yourself.
  4. Foster independence rather than dependence by not using guilt or threats.
  5. Treat your child as a person with dignity, similar to how you would treat a friend.
  6. Allow your child to have their own space and make decisions about it (e.g., "A made bed is not any more psychologically sound than an unmade bed").
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Children learn from the behavior of their models. If you are full of guilt and unfulfilled in your life, but telling your children not to be, then you are selling a tainted product."
  - "Only by treating yourself as the most important person and not always sacrificing yourself for your children will you teach them to have their own self-confidence and belief in themselves."
  - "If you are a sacrificer, you are modeling sacrificing behavior. And what does sacrificing behavior mean? Putting others before yourself, not liking yourself, seeking approval and other erroneous behavior."
  - "If a child spills the catsup on the table, it's not a 'Why don't you watch what you're doing; you're so clumsy' routine. Instead, you'll see the same kind of a response that would be given to a friend were they to spill something. 'Can I help you?' No abuse because they are owned, but a respect for the child's dignity."
  - "A mother is not a person to lean on, but a person to make leaning unnecessary."

### Lesson 7: Recognize the Difference Between Healthy Family Dynamics and Dependent Family Dynamics
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Understand that in families that focus on independence, movement toward being one's own person is seen as normal, not as a challenge to authority.
  2. Recognize that family members should want to be together rather than feel obligated to be together.
  3. Value privacy over demands to share everything.
  4. Ensure that parents maintain their own happiness and fulfillment independent of their children.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "In families that focus on independence, movement toward being one's own person is seen as normal, rather than as a challenge to anyone's authority. Clinging and needing are not emphasized. Similarly, there are no demands that a child have allegiance forever, simply because of membership in a family."
  - "The parents feel that their own happiness is paramount, because without it there can be no family harmony. Hence the parents can go off alone occasionally, and not feel obliged to always be there for their children."
  - "The mother isn't a slave, because she doesn't want her own children (especially the girls) to become one."

### Lesson 8: Accept That Others Will Disapprove of Your Independent Behavior
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Remind yourself that parents, spouses, friends, bosses, children, and others will often disapprove of your behavior, and that has nothing to do with who or what you are.
  2. Expect disapproval as a given fact in any relationship.
  3. If you expect it, you will not be thrown by it, and you can break dependency ties that enslave you emotionally.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Remind yourself that parents, spouses, friends, bosses, children and others will often disapprove of your behavior, and that has nothing to do with who or what you are. It is a given fact in any relationship that you will incur some disapproval. If you expect it, then you won't be thrown by it. In this way you can break many of the dependency-ties that enslave you emotionally."

### Lesson 9: Avoid the Trap of Avoiding Dominant People as a Form of Control
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Recognize that even if you are deliberately avoiding dominant people (parent, spouse, boss, child), you are still being controlled by them in their absence if you are experiencing emotional immobilization because of them.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Even if you are deliberately avoiding dominant people (parent, spouse, boss, child), you are still being controlled by them in their absence if you are experiencing emotional immobilization because of them."

### Lesson 10: Practice the Five-Minute Goal for Dealing with Dominant People
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Give yourself five-minute goals for how you are going to deal with dominant people in your life.
  2. Try a one-shot "No, I don't want to," and test the reaction of the other person.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Give yourself five-minute goals for how you're going to deal with dominant people in your life. Try a one-shot 'No, I don't want to,' and test the reaction of your reaction in the other person's."

### Lesson 11: Use a Nonverbal Signal with Your Dominant Partner
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Arrange a planning session with your dominant partner at a time when you are feeling unthreatened.
  2. During this session, explain that you sometimes feel manipulated and submissive.
  3. Agree on a nonverbal signal (such as a simple tugging of the ear or a thumb in the mouth) to let the other person know how you are feeling when it crops up.
  4. Explain that you don't want to discuss it at the time the signal is used.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Arrange a planning session with your dominant partner at a time when you are feeling unthreatened. During this session, explain that you sometimes feel manipulated and submissive and that you would like to arrange a nonverbal signal to let the other person know how you are feeling when it crops up, but that you don't want to discuss it at that time. A simple tugging of the ear or a thumb in the mouth to signal you're feeling submissive at a particular time."

### Lesson 12: State Your Feelings and Act Out the Behavior You Desire
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. At the moment you are feeling shoved-around psychologically, state how you feel to the person.
  2. Then act out the way you would like to behave.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "At the moment you are feeling shoved-around psychologically, state how you feel to the person, and then act out the way you'd like to behave."

### Lesson 13: Reverse the Logic of Obligation
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. If you are feeling obligated to visit certain people, ask yourself if you would want others to visit you simply because they felt required to do so.
  2. If the answer is no, extend a similar courtesy to those you are treating in this manner, and talk it out with them.
  3. Reverse the logic and see how undignified a relationship of obligation really is.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "If you are feeling obligated to visit certain people, ask yourself if you would want others to visit you simply because they felt required to do so. If not, extend a similar courtesy to those you are treating in this manner, and talk it out with them. That is, reverse the logic and see how undignified a relationship of obligation really is."

### Lesson 14: Take Action to Get Out of Your Dependency Role
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Make a decision to get out of your dependency role by doing volunteer work, reading, getting a baby-sitter (even if you can't afford it), or getting a job that doesn't necessarily even pay well.
  2. Do this because the remuneration of your own high self-esteem is worth whatever the price it costs in money or time.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Make a decision to get out of your dependency role by doing volunteer work, reading, getting a baby-sitter (even if you can't afford it), getting a job that doesn't necessarily even pay well. Why? Simply because the remuneration of your own high self-esteem is worth whatever the price it costs in money or time."

### Lesson 15: Insist on Financial Independence
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Insist on financial independence with no strings and no accounting to anyone.
  2. Recognize that you are a slave if you must ask for money.
  3. If total financial independence is impossible, arrange to make your own money in any creative way that you can devise.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Insist on financial independence with no strings, and no accounting to anyone. You are a slave if you must ask for money. If that is impossible, arrange to make your own money in any creative way that you can devise."

### Lesson 16: Stop Giving and Taking Orders
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Stop giving orders to others.
  2. Stop taking orders from others.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Let them go! Let yourself go! Stop giving orders! Stop taking orders!"

### Lesson 17: Recognize Your Need for Privacy and Separateness
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Recognize your desire for privacy and not having to share everything you feel and experience with someone.
  2. Understand that you are unique and private.
  3. If you feel you must share everything, then you are without choice, and of course, a dependent.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Recognize your desire for privacy and not having to share everything you feel and experience with someone. You are unique and private. If you feel you must share everything then you are without choice, and of course, a dependent."

### Lesson 18: Give Children Their Own Private Space and Control
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Let a child's room be his.
  2. Give him an area that he can control.
  3. As long as it's not unhealthy, allow him to make decisions about how it will be organized.
  4. Understand that a made bed is not any more psychologically sound than an unmade bed.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Let a child's room be his. Give him an area that he can control, and, as long as it's not unhealthy, allow him to make decisions about how it will be organized. A made bed is not any more psychologically sound than an unmade bed, even though you may have learned the opposite."

### Lesson 19: Practice Separate Activities and Autonomy in Your Relationship
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. At a party, congregate with people away from your partner. Don't feel responsible to be with that person at all times. Split up and then join forces when it is over.
  2. If you want to go to a movie and your partner wants to play tennis, do it that way! Allow yourself more separateness.
  3. Take mini-trips alone, or with friends without having to feel attached to your partner.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "At a party, congregate with people away from your partner. Don't feel responsible to be with that person at all times. Split up and then join forces when it is over. You'll double your learning and experience."
  - "If you want to go to a movie and your partner wants to play tennis, do it that way! Allow yourself more separateness, and your moments of togetherness will be happier, and more exciting."
  - "Take mini-trips alone, or with friends without having to feel attached to your partner. You'll feel more strongly toward your partner when you come back, and you'll treasure your independent functioning as well."

### Lesson 20: Release Yourself From the Responsibility of Making Others Happy
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Keep in mind that you have no responsibility to make others happy. Others make themselves happy.
  2. If you feel it is your mission to make others happy, you are a dependent who will also feel gloom when the other person is down, or even worse, feel as though you let him down.
  3. Recognize that you are responsible for your own emotions and so is everyone else. No one has control over your feelings, except you.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Keep in mind that you have no responsibility to make others happy. Others make themselves happy. Thus, you may truly enjoy the company of another, but if you feel it is your mission to make them happy, then you are a dependent who will also feel gloom when the other person is down. Or even worse, you'll feel as though you let him down. You are responsible for your own emotions and so is everyone else. No one has control over your feelings, except you."

### Lesson 21: Recognize That Habit Is Not a Reason to Continue Submissive Behavior
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Remember that habit is no reason to do anything.
  2. Just because you've always been submissive to others is not sufficient justification for allowing it to continue.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Remember that habit is no reason to do anything. Just because you've always been submissive to others is not sufficient justification for allowing it to continue."

### Lesson 22: Write Your Own Declaration of Independence
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Write your own Declaration of Independence in which you spell out for yourself how you want to function in all relationships.
  2. Do not eliminate compromise, but wipe out any manipulation without representation.
  3. Use the phrase: "I, the person, in order to have a more perfect union, etc."
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Write your own Declaration of Independence in which you spell out for yourself how you want to function in all relationships, not eliminating compromise, but wiping out any manipulation without representation. 'I, the person, in order to have a more perfect union, etc.'"

### Lesson 23: Talk Openly With Those You Feel Dependent Upon
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Talk with each person that you feel you are psychologically dependent upon.
  2. Declare your aims of functioning independently.
  3. Explain how you feel when you do things out of a sense of obligation.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Talk with each person that you feel you are psychologically dependent upon. Declare your aims of functioning independently, explain how you feel when you do things out of a sense of obligation. This is an excellent strategy for getting started, because the other may not even be aware of how you feel as a dependent."

### Lesson 24: Understand the Psychological Payoffs of Dependency (So You Can See Their Cost)
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Recognize that dependency can keep you in the protective custody of others and give you the little child benefits of not being responsible for your own behavior.
  2. See that by staying dependent, you can blame your shortcomings on others.
  3. Understand that if you are dependent on others, you don't have to undertake the hard work and risk of change.
  4. See that dependency allows you to avoid guilt and avoid making choices or decisions for yourself.
  5. Recognize that it is just plain easier to be a follower than a leader, but this makes you into less than a whole, independently functioning person.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "By staying dependent, you can blame your shortcomings on others."
  - "You can avoid guilt which you choose when you behave assertively. It is easier to behave than to learn to eliminate guilt."
  - "When everything else is boiled away, it is just plain easier to be a follower than a leader. You can do what you're told and avoid trouble, even though you may not like the way you feel as a follower. It's still simpler than taking all those risks that go with being your own person."
  - "Dependency is distasteful because it makes you into less than a whole, independently functioning person. But it is easier, that's for sure."

### Lesson 25: Recognize That Nest-Leaving Is the Goal of Effective Parenting and Living
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Understand that throughout the animal kingdom, parenting means to teach the offspring the skills necessary to be independent and then to leave.
  2. See that in humans, the neurotic need to own and live one's life through one's children subverts the goal of raising a child to be independent into the idea of raising a child to hold onto him.
  3. Make nest-leaving a natural event rather than one loaded with trauma.
  4. Support your children's desire for autonomy and emotional independence.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Throughout the animal kingdom, parenting means to teach the offspring the skills necessary to be independent and then to leave. With us humans, the instinct is still the same, that is, to be independent, but the neurotic need to own and live one's life through one's children seems to take over, and the goal of raising a child to be independent is subverted into the idea of raising a child to hold onto him."
  - "So be it. You can make nest-leaving a natural event, or one which is loaded with trauma and which will haunt the child and the relationship forever."

### Lesson 26: Build Marriage on Independence Rather Than Dependence
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Understand that a relationship based on love is one in which each partner allows the other to be what he chooses, with no expectations and no demands.
  2. See that it is a simple association of two people who love each other so much that each would never expect the other to be something that he wouldn't choose for himself.
  3. Build a union based on independence rather than dependence.
  4. Put spaces in togetherness—this ironically solidifies marriages.
  5. Do not confuse dependency with love.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "A relationship based on love, as was said earlier, is one in which each partner allows the other to be what he chooses, with no expectations and no demands. It is a simple association of two people who love each other so much that each would never expect the other to be something that he wouldn't choose for himself. It is a union based on independence, rather than dependence."
  - "Longevity is not an indication of success in marriage. Many people stay married out of fear of the unknown, or because of inertia, or simply because it is the thing to do. In a successful marriage, a marriage where both partners feel genuine love, each is willing to let the other person choose for himself rather than to dominate. There is not the constant hassling that involves thinking and speaking for the other partner, and demanding that he does what he's supposed to."
  - "Dependency is the serpent in the paradise of a happy marriage. It creates patterns of dominance and submission and ultimately destroys relationships."
  - "Most important, dependency should not be confused with love. Putting some spaces in togetherness ironically solidifies marriages."

### Lesson 27: Recognize That Dependency Is the Enemy of Happiness and Fulfillment
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Understand that dependency may appear harmless, but it is the enemy of all happiness and fulfillment.
  2. See that the moment you need another person, you become vulnerable and a slave.
  3. Recognize that if the one you need leaves, changes his mind, or dies, you are forced into immobilization, collapse, or even death.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Being psychologically independent involves not needing others. I didn't say wanting others, I said needing. The moment you need, you become vulnerable, a slave. If the one you need leaves, or changes his mind, or dies, you are then forced into immobilization, collapse, or even death."
  - "The reasons for hanging on to this self-thwarting behavior are not very complicated. You may know the payoffs for being a dependent, but do you know how destructive they are? Dependency may appear harmless, but it is the enemy of all happiness and fulfillment."

### Lesson 28: Recognize That Leaving the Nest Means Becoming Your Own Person
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Understand that leaving the nest means becoming your own person, living and choosing the behaviors that you want.
  2. Recognize that it does not mean breaking off in any sense of the word.
  3. If you enjoy your way of interacting with anyone and it doesn't interfere with your own goals, then it is something you can cherish rather than change.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "Leaving the nest means becoming your own person, living and choosing the behaviors that you want. It does not mean breaking off in any sense of the word. If you enjoy your way of interacting with anyone and it doesn't interfere with your own goals, then it is something you can cherish rather than change."

### Lesson 29: Understand That You Get the Most Respect for Being Independent
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. If you asked those upon whom you are emotionally dependent, you would surprisingly discover that they most admire those who think and act for themselves.
  2. You get the most respect for being independent, particularly from those who try the hardest to keep you subordinate.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "More irony. You get the most respect for being independent, particularly from those who try the hardest to keep you subordinate."
  - "And while you may feel real fear about breaking away from dependent relationships, if you asked those upon whom you are emotionally dependent, you would surprisingly discover that they most admire those who think and act for themselves."

### Lesson 30: Evaluate Whether Your Relationship Is Based on Love or Dependency
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Ask yourself: "Do I want this relationship, or do I need it?"
  2. If you need it, or are forced into it, and subsequently feel resentment, recognize that you are in a self-defeating area.
  3. If you want the relationship and it doesn't interfere with your own goals, then it is something you can cherish rather than change.
- **Examples from book:**
  - "If you want some kind of relationship, then it is not unhealthy. But if you need it, or are forced into it, and subsequently feel resentment, then you are in a self-defeating area."
  - "As long as you feel that you have to do anything because it is expected of you in a particular relationship, and your doing it creates any resentment or your not doing it any guilt, you can count yourself as having work to do in this erroneous zone."
