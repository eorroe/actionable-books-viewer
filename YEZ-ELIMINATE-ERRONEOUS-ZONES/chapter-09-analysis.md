## Chapter IX: Putting an End to the Critic

### Lesson 1: Recognizing That Procrastinators Are Often Critics
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Recognize in yourself the behavior of being an observer rather than a doer.
  2. Resolve to eliminate critical and observing behaviors entirely so that you can be a doer rather than a procrastinating critic.
- **Examples from book:** "A non-doer is very often a critic, that is, someone who sits back and watches doers, and then waxes philosophically about how the doers are doing. It is easy to be a critic, but being a doer requires effort, risk and change." Real champions such as Henry Aarons, Johnny Carsons, Bobby Fishers, Katharine Hepburns, and Joe Louises "have no time for criticizing others. They're too busy doing. They work. They help others who are not as talented, rather than serve as their critics."

### Lesson 2: Eliminating Boredom as a Spin-Off of Procrastination
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Recognize that boredom is a choice and not caused by the environment.
  2. Eliminate boredom by doing something else with your mind or energy at that moment.
  3. Use your mind creatively in previously boring circumstances.
- **Examples from book:** "Life is never boring but some people choose to be bored. The concept of boredom entails an inability to use up present moments in a personally fulfilling way. Boredom is a choice; something you visit upon yourself." Example: "At a meeting, change the dull tempo with a pertinent question, or make your mind go off in exciting ways such as writing a poem, or memorizing twenty-five numbers backwards, just for the sheer drill of memory training. Decide to never be bored again."

### Lesson 3: Identifying Your Typical Procrastinating Behaviors
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Review the list of typical procrastinating behaviors and identify which ones apply to you.
- **Examples from book:** Staying in a job where you are stuck; hanging onto a sour relationship and hoping it will get better; refusing to work on relationship difficulties; not tackling addictions; putting off arduous or menial tasks; avoiding confrontations; being afraid to change locations geographically; putting off spending time with children; deciding to start your diet tomorrow; using sleep or tiredness as a reason for putting it off; getting sick when faced with a troublesome task; using the "I don't have time to do it" ruse; constantly looking forward to a vacation; being a critic and using criticism to camouflage your own refusal to do; refusing to get a physical checkup; being afraid to make a move toward someone you're fond of; being bored; planning but never putting in action a regular exercise program; living your entire life for your children and always putting off your own happiness.

### Lesson 4: Understanding the Reasons You Continue to Procrastinate
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Review the list of reasons for procrastination to gain insight into why you procrastinate.
- **Examples from book:** Procrastination allows escape from unpleasant activities; you can feel comfortable with your self-delusional system; you can stay exactly as you are forever; by being bored you have someone or something else to blame; by being a critic you can feel important at the expense of others; by waiting for things to get better you can blame the world for your unhappiness; you can avoid ever having to fail; wishing for things to happen allows you to return to a safe and protected childhood; you can win sympathy from others; you can justify sloppy performance; by putting it off you might be able to get someone else to do it for you; procrastination enables you to delude yourself into believing that you are something other than what you really are; by avoiding a task you can escape success.

### Lesson 5: Making a Decision to Live Five Minutes at a Time
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Make a decision to live five minutes at a time.
  2. Instead of thinking of tasks in long-range terms, think about now.
  3. Try to use up a five-minute period doing what you want.
  4. Refuse to put off anything that would bring about satisfaction.
- **Examples from book:** None specific beyond the instruction itself.

### Lesson 6: Simply Beginning the Task You Have Been Postponing
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Sit down and get started on something you have been postponing.
  2. Begin a letter or a book.
  3. Recognize that much of your putting it off is unnecessary since you will very likely find the job enjoyable once you give up the procrastination.
  4. Use beginning to help eliminate anxiety about the whole project.
- **Examples from book:** "Begin a letter or a book."

### Lesson 7: Assessing Your Fear by Asking What Is the Worst That Could Happen
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Ask yourself: "What is the worst thing that could happen to me if I did what I'm putting off right now?"
  2. Recognize that the answer is usually so insignificant that it may jar you into action.
  3. Assess your fear and you will have no reason to hang onto it.
- **Examples from book:** None specific beyond the instruction itself.

### Lesson 8: Giving Yourself a Designated Time Slot for the Postponed Task
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Give yourself a designated time slot (for example, Wednesday from 10:00 to 10:15 P.M.) which you will devote exclusively to the task you have been putting off.
  2. Use the fifteen minutes of devoted effort to see yourself over the hump of procrastination.
- **Examples from book:** "say Wednesday from 10:00 to 10:15 P.M."

### Lesson 9: Thinking of Yourself as Too Significant to Live with Postponement Anxiety
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Think of yourself as too significant to live with anxiety about the things you have to do.
  2. When you know you are uncomfortable with postponement anxiety, remember that people who love themselves don't hurt themselves that way.
- **Examples from book:** None specific beyond the instruction itself.

### Lesson 10: Tackling the Fear of Living Effectively by Looking at Your Now
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Look carefully at your now.
  2. Decide what you are avoiding in your current moments.
  3. Begin to tackle the fear of living effectively.
  4. Recognize that procrastination is substituting the now with anxiety about a future event.
  5. Make the event become the now, so that the anxiety must go.
- **Examples from book:** None specific beyond the instruction itself.

### Lesson 11: Taking Immediate Action on Addictive or Avoided Behaviors
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Quit smoking now.
  2. Begin your diet this moment.
  3. Give up alcohol this second.
  4. Put the book down and do one push-up as your beginning exercise project.
  5. Tackle problems with action now.
  6. Remind yourself that doing it is far more important than performing well.
  7. Recognize that the only thing holding you back is you and the neurotic choices you have made because you don't believe you're as strong as you really are.
- **Examples from book:** "Quit smoking…now! Begin your diet…this moment! Give up booze…this second. Put this book down and do one push-up as your beginning exercise project. That's how you tackle problems…with action now! Do it! The only thing holding you back is you, and the neurotic choices you've made because you don't believe you're as strong as you really are. How simple…just do it!"

### Lesson 12: Using Your Mind Creatively to Eliminate Boredom
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Start using your mind creatively in what were previously boring circumstances.
  2. At a meeting, change the dull tempo with a pertinent question.
  3. Make your mind go off in exciting ways such as writing a poem, or memorizing twenty-five numbers backwards, just for the sheer drill of memory training.
  4. Decide to never be bored again.
- **Examples from book:** "At a meeting, change the dull tempo with a pertinent question, or make your mind go off in exciting ways such as writing a poem, or memorizing twenty-five numbers backwards, just for the sheer drill of memory training. Decide to never be bored again."

### Lesson 13: Moving from Critic to Doer When Facing Criticism
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. When someone begins to criticize you, ask: "Do you think I need a critic now?"
  2. When you find yourself being a critic, ask the person in your company if he wants to hear your criticism, and if so, why?
  3. Use these questions to help move from the critic to the doer column.
- **Examples from book:** None specific beyond the instruction itself.

### Lesson 14: Evaluating Your Life as If You Had Six Months to Live
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Look hard at your life.
  2. Ask: Are you doing what you would choose to be doing if you knew you had six months to live?
  3. If not, begin doing it because, relatively speaking, that's all you have.
  4. Recognize that given the eternity of time, thirty years or six months make no difference. Your total lifetime is a mere speck. Delaying anything makes no sense.
- **Examples from book:** None specific beyond the instruction itself.

### Lesson 15: Being Courageous About Undertaking an Activity You Have Been Avoiding
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Be courageous about undertaking an activity that you have been avoiding.
  2. Recognize that one act of courage can eliminate all that fear.
  3. Stop telling yourself that you must perform well.
  4. Remind yourself that doing it is far more important.
- **Examples from book:** None specific beyond the instruction itself.

### Lesson 16: Deciding Not to Be Tired Until the Moment Before You Get Into Bed
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Decide not to be tired until the moment before you get into bed.
  2. Do not allow yourself to use fatigue or illness as an escape or to put off doing anything.
  3. Recognize that when you take away the reason for the illness or exhaustion—that is, avoidance of a task—physical problems "magically" disappear.
- **Examples from book:** None specific beyond the instruction itself.

### Lesson 17: Eliminating the Words "Hope," "Wish" and "Maybe" from Your Vocabulary
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Eliminate the words "hope," "wish" and "maybe" from your vocabulary because they are the tools of putting it off.
  2. If you see these words creeping in, substitute new sentences.
  3. Change "I hope things will work out" to "I will make it happen."
  4. Change "I wish things were better" to "I am going to do the following things to ensure that I feel better."
  5. Change "Maybe it will be okay" to "I will make it okay."
- **Examples from book:** The three neurotic phrases of the procrastinator: "I hope things will work out." "I wish things were better." "Maybe it'll be okay." The substitutions: "I will make it happen." "I am going to do the following things to ensure that I feel better." "I will make it okay."

### Lesson 18: Keeping a Journal of Your Own Complaining or Critical Behavior
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Keep a journal of your own complaining or critical behavior.
  2. By writing these actions down, you will accomplish two things:
     a. You will see how your critical behavior surfaces in your life—the frequency, patterns, events and people that are related to your being a critic.
     b. You will stop yourself from criticizing because it will be such a pain to have to write in the journal.
- **Examples from book:** None specific beyond the instruction itself.

### Lesson 19: Having a Conference with All Involved When Procrastination Affects Others
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. If you are putting something off which involves others (for example, a move, a sex problem, a new job), have a conference with all involved and ask their opinions.
  2. Be courageous about talking of your own fears.
  3. See if you are delaying for reasons that are only in your head.
  4. By enlisting the aid of a confidant to help you with your procrastination, you will have made it a joint effort.
  5. Soon you will dissipate much of the anxiousness that goes along with procrastination by sharing that as well.
- **Examples from book:** "a move, a sex problem, a new job"

### Lesson 20: Writing a Contract with Your Loved Ones to Deliver Postponed Goods
- **Actionable Steps:**
  1. Write a contract with your loved ones in which you will deliver the goods you want to but which you may have been postponing.
  2. Have each party keep a copy of the contract.
  3. Build in penalties for defaulting.
  4. Examples of contract items: a ball game, dinner-out, vacation, or theater visit.
- **Examples from book:** "Whether it's a ball game, dinner-out, vacation, or theater visit, you'll find this strategy helpful and personally rewarding, since you'll be participating in events that you also find enjoyable."
