# Chapter 9 Analysis: Practices for Compassionate Communication

## 1. Chapter Title and Main Theme

- **Title:** Practices for Compassionate Communication
- **Author:** Thich Nhat Hanh
- **Main Theme:** Practical mindfulness-based practices for cultivating compassionate communication within oneself and in relationships, including tools for healing conflict, restoring harmony, and building deeper connection through presence, loving speech, and deep listening.

---

## 2. Actionable Lessons (Step-by-Step)

### The Computer Bell
1. Program a mindfulness bell on your computer to sound every quarter of an hour (or as often as desired).
2. When the bell sounds, stop what you are doing and return to yourself.
3. Breathe in and out three times to release tension in the body and smile.
4. Resume work.

### Drinking Tea in Mindfulness
1. Set aside time to drink tea mindfully.
2. Stop all thinking while drinking tea.
3. Focus attention fully on the tea.
4. Breathe in, aware of your in-breath, your body, and the tea.
5. Establish yourself in the here and now, body and mind united.

### Listening to Your Inner Child
1. Take time to go back and tenderly embrace the wounded child within you.
2. Talk to the child with the language of love.
3. Use phrases such as: "Dear one, in the past, I left you alone. I've gone away from you for so long. I'm sorry. Now I have come back to take care of you, to embrace you. I know you suffer so much, and I have neglected you. But now I've learned the way to take care of you. I am here now."
4. Cry with the child if needed.
5. When sitting, spend time sitting and breathing with the child: "Breathing in, I go back to my wounded child; breathing out, I take good care of my wounded child."
6. When walking, take the hand of your little child.
7. Talk to the child several times a day for healing to take place.
8. Go back to the inner child every day and listen for five or ten minutes.

### Writing a Love Letter
1. Spend a couple of hours alone writing a real letter to someone with whom you have difficulties.
2. Write to someone you see every day or someone you haven't seen for years, or even someone no longer living.
3. Practice looking deeply into the nature of the relationship while writing.
4. Use loving speech throughout the letter.
5. Admit your own responsibility for suffering: "Now I realize that I have been responsible for my own suffering."
6. Promise to refrain from saying or doing things that cause suffering.
7. Ask the other person to tell you what is in their heart: "You need to help me; otherwise it is not possible for me to do it. I can't do it alone."
8. Decide later whether to send the letter — sending is optional.

### Peace Treaty
1. As the angry person:
   - Refrain from saying or doing anything that might cause further damage or escalate anger.
   - Do not suppress anger.
   - Practice breathing and taking refuge in the island of yourself.
   - Calmly tell the one who made you angry about your anger and suffering within 24 hours (verbally or by delivering a peace note).
   - Ask for an appointment later in the week (e.g., Friday evening) to discuss more thoroughly.
   - Do not deny anger ("I am not angry. It's okay. There is nothing to be angry about.").
   - Practice breathing and looking deeply into daily life (while sitting, lying down, standing, and walking) to see:
     - a. Ways you yourself have been unskillful.
     - b. How you have hurt the other person because of habit energy.
     - c. How the strong seed of anger in you is the primary cause of your anger.
     - d. How the other person's suffering, which waters the seed of your anger, is the secondary cause.
     - e. How the other person is only seeking relief from their own suffering.
     - f. That as long as the other person suffers, you cannot be truly happy.
   - Apologize immediately once you realize your unskillfulness and lack of mindfulness.
   - Postpone the meeting if you do not feel calm enough.
2. As the person who made the other angry:
   - Respect the other person's feelings; do not ridicule them.
   - Allow enough time for them to calm down.
   - Do not press for immediate discussion.
   - Confirm the other person's request for a meeting and assure them you will be there.
   - Practice breathing and taking refuge in the island of yourself to see:
     - a. Seeds of unkindness and anger as well as habit energy to make the other person unhappy.
     - b. How you mistakenly thought making the other person suffer would relieve your own suffering.
     - c. How by making them suffer, you make yourself suffer.
   - Apologize as soon as you realize your unskillfulness and lack of mindfulness, without justifying yourself.
3. Sign the treaty with date and location.

### Peace Note
1. Copy the peace note template and keep blank copies available wherever needed.
2. Use in place of the fourth mantra note kept in your wallet.
3. When someone does something that brings suffering, say: "What you just said hurt me. I would like to look deeply into it, and I would like you to also look deeply into it. Let's make an appointment for sometime later in the week to look at it together."
4. Wait a few days before discussing the issue — pick any night (suggest Friday evening or early Saturday).
5. While waiting, practice looking deeply into the nature of your suffering; the other person can do the same.
6. When discussing:
   - Have one person express themselves while the other listens deeply.
   - Use loving speech of the deepest kind — the kind the other person can understand and accept.
   - Listen with good quality to relieve the other person's suffering.
   - Have tea together afterward.
7. If by the evening the suffering has not been transformed, begin with one person expressing themselves while the other listens deeply.

### Beginning Anew
1. When difficulty arises and one person feels resentment or hurt, practice "beginning anew."
2. Look deeply and honestly at yourself — past actions, speech, and thoughts.
3. Create a fresh beginning within yourself and in relationships.
4. Practice daily by:
   - Expressing appreciation to people you care about.
   - Apologizing right away when you do or say something that hurts others.
   - Politely letting others know when you have been hurt.
5. Formal weekly practice in families or work settings — three-part process:
   - **Part 1: Flower watering**
     - Acknowledge the wholesome, wonderful qualities of others.
     - Speak one at a time, waiting until moved to speak.
     - Hold a vase of flowers or an object as you speak.
     - Others listen without response; no interruptions allowed.
     - Speak the truth; avoid flattery.
     - Return the vase to the center when finished.
   - **Part 2: Expressing regrets**
     - Recall and express any regrets from earlier in the week.
     - Undo small resentments before they grow.
   - **Part 3: Expressing hurts and difficulties**
     - Speak frankly but not destructively, using loving speech.
     - Never blame or argue.
     - Listen compassionately with willingness to relieve suffering, not to judge or argue.
     - If something untrue is heard, continue to listen deeply; correct privately and calmly a few days later.
     - End with a moment of silence.
6. Spend most time on flower watering, especially when the practice is new.
7. Slowly add the second and third parts as trust is built; never skip flower watering.

### The Cake in the Refrigerator
1. When the atmosphere becomes heavy and unpleasant and one person is losing temper:
   - Breathe in and out three times to give yourself courage.
   - Turn to the upset person and say you just remembered something.
   - When they ask what, say: "I remember that we have a cake in the refrigerator."
2. The meaning is: "Please, let's not make each other suffer anymore."
3. The upset person can withdraw from the fight without causing more tension by saying "I'll go and get the cake."
4. The person goes to the kitchen, opens the refrigerator, boils water for tea, all while following their breathing.
5. Substitute something else (fruit, toast, etc.) if no real cake is present.
6. The other person practices mindful breathing while sitting alone.
7. After tea and cake are placed on the table, join for a tea party.
8. Coax hesitant participants: "Please come and have some tea and cake with me."

### Hugging Meditation
1. Practice hugging meditation with a friend, family member, partner, or even a tree.
2. First bow and recognize the presence of the other.
3. Close your eyes, take a deep breath, and visualize yourself and your beloved three hundred years from now.
4. Enjoy three deep, conscious breaths to bring yourself fully there.
5. Say to yourself: "Breathing in, I know that life is precious in this moment. Breathing out, I cherish this moment of life."
6. Smile at the person, expressing your desire to hold them.
7. Open your arms and hug.
8. Hold for three in-and-out breaths:
   - First breath: aware that you are present in this moment and happy.
   - Second breath: aware that the other is present in this moment and they are happy.
   - Third breath: aware that you are here together right now on this earth, feeling deep gratitude and happiness for your togetherness.
9. Release and bow to each other to show thanks.
10. Alternative method:
    - First breath: become aware that you and your beloved are both alive.
    - Second breath: think of where you will both be three hundred years from now.
    - Third breath: return to the insight that you are both alive.

## 3. Key Concepts and Principles

- **Mindfulness bell:** Using an external auditory reminder to return to the present moment and check in with oneself.
- **Island of self:** A refuge within oneself accessed through breathing; a place of calm and self-awareness.
- **Habit energy:** Automatic patterns of thought, speech, and action that cause suffering without mindfulness.
- **Loving speech:** Communicating truthfully, kindly, and in a way the other person can understand and accept.
- **Deep listening:** Listening with full attention and the willingness to relieve the other's suffering, without judgment, interruption, or correction.
- **Primary and secondary causes of anger:** The primary cause is the strong seed of anger within; the secondary cause is the other person's suffering that waters that seed.
- **Wounded inner child:** The wounded child within each adult who needs care, attention, and loving recognition.
- **Generational suffering:** Suffering passed down through ancestors when wounded children are not cared for; healing oneself breaks the cycle for descendants.
- **Flower watering:** The practice of sincerely acknowledging the beautiful qualities of others.
- **Beginning anew:** Creating a fresh beginning by recognizing positives, expressing regret, and honestly communicating hurts.
- **The cake in the refrigerator:** A symbolic, nonjudgmental invitation to stop conflict and restore harmony.
- **Hugging meditation:** A physical practice of presence, gratitude, and reconciliation through mindful hugging.
- **Peace treaty:** A preventive tool for committing to mindful, non-escalating behavior in relationships.
- **Peace note:** A healing tool for communicating hurt and requesting a calm, future discussion.
- **Drink your tea!** (Zen commandment): A reminder to be fully present, body and mind united, in the here and now.

- Speaking to an inner wounded child: "Dear one, in the past, I left you alone. I've gone away from you for so long. I'm sorry."
- Writing a love letter admitting: "Now I realize that I have been responsible for my own suffering."

- The cake in the refrigerator scenario: one person remembers there is a cake, inviting the upset person to step away from the conflict without losing face.
- Hugging meditation with three conscious breaths, visualizing being together three hundred years from now.
- A family or workplace holding a weekly "beginning anew" ceremony with flower watering, regrets, and expressing hurts.

## 5. Best Practices Mentioned

- Practice looking deeply into the nature of your own suffering and the other person's suffering.
- Use the peace treaty as a preventive tool and the peace note as a healing tool.
- Do not suppress anger; express it calmly and skillfully within 24 hours.
- Apologize immediately as soon as you realize unskillfulness.
- Wait a few days before discussing hurtful incidents to allow cooling down and deeper reflection.
- Use loving speech and the kind of speech the other person can understand and accept.
- When beginning anew, focus primarily on flower watering, especially at first.
- Spend time on the second and third parts of beginning anew only as trust is built.
- Never interrupt the person holding the flowers during flower watering.
- When someone expresses hurt, continue to listen deeply even if you hear something untrue; correct privately and calmly later.
- Inhale and exhale three times before responding in conflict situations.
- Practice breathing and taking refuge in the island of yourself.
- Use the cake in the refrigerator as a nonjudgmental way out of a dangerous situation.
- Hug with total presence, body and mind united.
- Talk to your inner child several times a day.
- End practices with a moment of silence.
- Schedule difficult conversations for Friday evenings or early Saturdays so the weekend remains available for enjoying being together.

---

## 6. Common Pitfalls Mentioned

- Suppressing anger instead of expressing it calmly.
- Denying anger: saying "I am not angry. It's okay. There is nothing to be angry about."
- Pressuring someone for an immediate discussion when they are hurt or angry.
- Ridiculing another person's feelings.
- Arguing or blaming when expressing hurts.
- Interrupting the speaker during flower watering or deep listening.
- Judging or trying to argue while another person is expressing their pain.
- Correcting or replying while the other person is still expressing hurt, which causes the practice not to bear fruit.
- Using flattery instead of genuine appreciation during flower watering.
- Skipping flower watering when adding new parts to the beginning anew practice.
- Making the situation worse by speaking too soon after being hurt.
- Forgetting to breathe and return to oneself during conflict.
