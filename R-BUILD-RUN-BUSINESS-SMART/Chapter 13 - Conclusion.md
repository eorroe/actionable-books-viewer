# Chapter 13: CONCLUSION

## Lessons

### Lesson 1: Inspiration Is Perishable — Act On It Immediately
**Context:** The chapter opens with the central thesis that ideas are immortal, but inspiration is not. Inspiration behaves like fresh fruit or milk — it has an expiration date. This is the core actionable lesson of the conclusion.

**Examples:**
- "Inspiration is like fresh fruit or milk: It has an expiration date."
- "If you want to do something, you've got to do it now. You can't put it on a shelf and wait two months to get around to it."
- "Later, you won't be pumped up about it anymore."
- "If you're inspired on a Friday, swear off the weekend and dive into the project."
- "When you're high on inspiration, you can get two weeks of work done in twenty-four hours. Inspiration is a time machine in that way."

**Steps:**
1. Recognize when inspiration strikes — treat it as a signal to act immediately.
2. Drop everything non-essential and begin working on the inspired idea right away.
3. Do not defer the work to a later date; commit to starting within the current moment or same day.
4. Ride the momentum — use the heightened energy to accomplish in hours what might otherwise take weeks.

**Best Practices:**
- ✅ Treat inspiration as a perishable resource that must be used immediately.
- ✅ When inspired, protect your time and dive into the project without delay.
- ✅ Use high-inspiration periods to crush large amounts of work quickly.

**Keep In Mind:**
- Inspiration is a productivity multiplier and a motivator, but only while it lasts.
- Inspiration is a "now thing" — it will not wait for you to be ready.
- Ideas themselves last forever, but the drive to execute them does not.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- **Problem:** Putting an inspired idea on hold because you feel too busy or unprepared.
  **Solution:** Start anyway, even in a small way. Action cements inspiration and builds momentum.
- **Problem:** Waiting for the "perfect time" to begin a project.
  **Solution:** There is no perfect time. The inspired moment IS the perfect time.

**Key Terms:**
- **Inspiration:** A perishable, time-sensitive burst of motivation that dramatically increases productivity.
- **Perishable:** Describes something that loses value or effectiveness over time if not used immediately.
- **Productivity multiplier:** A state (like inspiration) that allows you to accomplish far more work in the same amount of time.

### Lesson 2: Gratitude And Community Engagement
**Context:** The closing section thanks readers and invites them to share their experiences with the book's principles, reinforcing the idea of a community of practitioners.

**Examples:**
- "Thank you for reading our book"
- "We hope it inspires you to rework how you do things."
- "If so, drop a line to rework@37signals.com and let us know how it's going."

**Steps:**
1. After reading and applying the book's lessons, reflect on what has changed in your work.
2. Share your story with the authors via rework@37signals.com.
3. Continue the conversation with others who are also reworking their approach to business and productivity.

**Best Practices:**
- ✅ Apply what you learn and then share feedback with the creators.
- ✅ Engage with the community of practitioners to reinforce your own learning.

**Keep In Mind:**
- Learning is reinforced when you teach or discuss it with others.
- The authors value real-world application and want to hear success stories.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- **Problem:** Reading the book but never implementing any of the lessons.
  **Solution:** Pick one lesson from each chapter and apply it immediately — start small, but start.
- **Problem:** Consuming content passively without reflecting or acting.
  **Solution:** After each chapter, write down one actionable change you will make.

**Key Terms:**
- **Rework:** The act of challenging conventional business wisdom and doing things differently — simpler, faster, and with less overhead.
- **Community of practitioners:** A group of people who apply the book's principles in real-world settings and share experiences.
