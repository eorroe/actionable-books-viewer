# Chapter 4: Drifting with the Devil

## Key Lessons

- Lesson 1: Understanding the Habit of Drifting
  - Action: Recognize and stop drifting behavior in daily life
  - Step-by-step:
    1. Define drifting: permitting yourself to be influenced and controlled by circumstances outside your own mind
    2. Identify areas where you drift (health, marriage, occupation, savings, environment, dominating thoughts)
    3. Stop accepting whatever life throws at you without making a protest
    4. Stop letting others do your thinking for you
    5. Develop the habit of thinking for yourself on all subjects
  - Best practices:
    - Think independently on every subject that affects your life
    - Make your own decisions rather than following the crowd
    - Question beliefs supplied by others rather than accepting them passively
  - Common pitfalls:
    - Problem: Letting parents, teachers, or religious instructors do your thinking
      Solution: Develop your own independent thought and question inherited beliefs
    - Problem: Drifting through school without knowing what occupation you wish to follow
      Solution: Define your major purpose early and align education with it
    - Problem: Accepting opinions that are not your own
      Solution: Form your own opinions based on your own reasoning

- Lesson 2: How the Devil Controls Minds Through Drifting
  - Action: Close the nine doors through which the Devil enters your mind
  - Step-by-step:
    1. Identify the nine entry points: fear, superstition, avarice, greed, lust, revenge, anger, vanity, and laziness
    2. Recognize that these habits allow the Devil to silently enter your mind
    3. Stop each habit before it becomes permanent
    4. Establish positive habits in their place
    5. Remember that the mind is the sum total of one's habits
  - Best practices:
    - Address negative habits while young before they become permanent
    - Replace fear-based thinking with faith, courage, hope, and definiteness of purpose
    - Understand that accurate thought is the Devil's opposition
  - Common pitfalls:
    - Problem: Allowing children to be influenced by fear-based religious instruction
      Solution: Teach faith-based principles that encourage independent thought
    - Problem: Letting laziness and indifference lead to procrastination
      Solution: Take immediate action on decisions rather than putting them off

- Lesson 3: The Two Approaches to Mind Control
  - Action: Recognize and resist physical heredity and environmental manipulation
  - Step-by-step:
    1. Understand physical heredity: weaknesses passed from ancestors that create weak brains
    2. Recognize environmental manipulation: the principle of habit used to control your mind
    3. One by one, identify habits that have been established in your mind
    4. Replace each bad habit with a good one
    5. Take control of your own mind rather than letting circumstances control you
  - Best practices:
    - Be aware that environment is used to control people through the principle of habit
    - Actively choose your environment rather than drifting into inharmonious surroundings
    - Build strong habits of independent thought to resist manipulation
  - Common pitfalls:
    - Problem: Drifting into inharmonious environments and remaining there
      Solution: Actively change your environment when it becomes negative
    - Problem: Letting physical heredity excuse lack of mental development
      Solution: Use your prerogative right to think for yourself regardless of heredity

- Lesson 4: Recognizing the Drifter and the Non-Drifter
  - Action: Evaluate yourself against the characteristics of drifters and non-drifters
  - Step-by-step:
    1. Review the complete list of drifter characteristics
    2. Honestly assess which characteristics apply to you
    3. Study the characteristics of non-drifters
    4. Identify the gap between where you are and where you want to be
    5. Develop a plan to transform from drifter to non-drifter
  - Best practices:
    - Be brutally honest with yourself in your self-assessment
    - Focus on one characteristic at a time for transformation
    - Surround yourself with non-drifters as models
  - Common pitfalls:
    - Problem: Having opinions on everything but accurate knowledge of nothing
      Solution: Seek accurate knowledge before forming opinions
    - Problem: Beginning many things but completing nothing
      Solution: Focus on one major purpose and see it through to completion

- Lesson 5: The Drifter's Impact on Key Life Areas
  - Action: Stop drifting in health, marriage, occupation, savings, and dominating thoughts
  - Step-by-step:
    1. Health: Stop eating too much and the wrong sort of food; develop proper eating habits
    2. Marriage: Do not drift into marriage without plan or purpose; design the relationship for harmony
    3. Occupation: Do not drift out of school into the first job without a definite aim or purpose
    4. Savings: Spend wisely and save consistently; do not let fear of poverty control you
    5. Environment: Do not remain in inharmonious environments; actively choose positive surroundings
    6. Dominating thoughts: Stop thinking negative thoughts; think in constructive terms
  - Best practices:
    - Make definite plans for each major area of life before entering them
    - Take control of your health rather than drifting into poor habits
    - Choose occupations based on purpose, not just necessity
    - Cultivate positive dominating thoughts through faith, courage, and hope
  - Common pitfalls:
    - Problem: Drifting into marriage without plan, then bickering over money and children
      Solution: Establish clear purpose and harmony before and during marriage
    - Problem: Drifting into first job with no aim except making a living
      Solution: Define your occupation purpose before accepting any job
    - Problem: Spending freely and saving sparingly, leading to fear of poverty
      Solution: Create a budget and savings plan that gives you control

- Lesson 6: The Path to Becoming a Non-Drifter
  - Action: Transform from a drifter to a non-drifter through service and definite purpose
  - Step-by-step:
    1. Wake up and recognize your drifting habits
    2. Identify a form of service useful to as many people as possible
    3. Give before you get - provide value first
    4. Develop a major goal in life toward which you are always working
    5. Create many minor goals that all lead toward your central scheme
    6. Make quick decisions and stick to them
    7. Give direct answers and never fall back on evasions
    8. Accept favors sparingly or not at all
    9. Admit when you do not know the answer
    10. Never blame others for your mistakes
    11. Use your mind for all purposes
  - Best practices:
    - Focus on giving service to others as the path to success
    - Maintain definiteness of purpose in all actions
    - Practice making decisions quickly and standing by them
    - Be an inspiration to all who come into contact with your mind
  - Common pitfalls:
    - Problem: Waiting to receive before giving
      Solution: Always give first, then receive
    - Problem: Lacking a major purpose in life
      Solution: Define your central scheme and align all goals with it

- Lesson 7: The Role of Parents, Teachers, and Religious Instructors
  - Action: Ensure adults encourage independent thought in children rather than destroying it
  - Step-by-step:
    1. Recognize that parents, teachers, and religious instructors unknowingly serve the Devil's purpose when they destroy children's power to think for themselves
    2. Do not complete children's sentences or do their homework for them
    3. Allow children to be truly in charge of their own tasks
    4. Do not teach children to believe exactly as you do in religion, politics, and marriage
    5. Encourage children to develop their own reasoning abilities
    6. Support educational methods that develop independent thought rather than just cramming information
  - Best practices:
    - Let children solve their own problems rather than providing all the answers
    - Applaud teachers and schools who encourage independent thought
    - Use faith-based rather than fear-based instruction with children
    - Allow children to make errors and learn from them without ridicule
  - Common pitfalls:
    - Problem: Parents helping children too much, destroying self-confidence
      Solution: Allow children to do things on their own even if imperfectly
    - Problem: Religious instructors using fear of hell to control children's thinking
      Solution: Teach faith, courage, and hope rather than fear and punishment
    - Problem: Schools teaching everything except how to use one's own mind
      Solution: Supplement education with independent thinking exercises

- Lesson 8: The Devil's Tactics for Controlling Nations
  - Action: Recognize that drifting leads to dictatorship and loss of freedom
  - Step-by-step:
    1. Understand that drifting is the most common cause of failure in every walk of life
    2. Recognize that dictators are appointed and manipulated by the Devil
    3. See that dictators do not drift - that is why they rule over drifters
    4. Understand that poverty and physical illness are contagious diseases found among drifters
    5. Recognize that non-drifters possess most of the wealth of the world
    6. Defend freedom of speech and independent thought in your nation
  - Best practices:
    - Be vigilant against leaders who drift on important issues
    - Support education that develops independent thought
    - Recognize that accurate thought is the only power that can stop the Devil
  - Common pitfalls:
    - Problem: Allowing leaders to drift on important agreements and policies
      Solution: Demand definiteness of purpose and action from leaders
    - Problem: Believing that poverty is inevitable for most people
      Solution: Understand that poverty is found only among drifters, not non-drifters

## Actionable Principles
- People who think for themselves never drift; those who do little or no thinking for themselves are drifters
- A drifter is one who permits himself to be influenced and controlled by circumstances outside of his own mind
- The mind is nothing more than the sum total of one's habits
- Drifting destroys the power of individual initiative
- The Devil cannot exist in the minds of those who think accurately
- Accurate thought is death to the Devil
- Difters acquire nothing except that which no one else wants
- Non-drifters possess most of the wealth of the world
- The major distinguishing feature of the non-drifter is that he has a mind of his own and uses it for all purposes
- Laziness + Indifference = Procrastination = Drifting
- The non-drifter's message to drifters: wake up and give some form of service useful to as many people as possible
- Give before you get
- Men who know how to get material things generally know how to keep out of the hands of the Devil
- Poverty, like physical illness, is a contagious disease found among drifters
- Difters work harder to get out of thinking than most others work in earning a good living
- All other creatures move in response to definite laws of nature; man alone drifts when he wills
- The Devil controls the weak, not those who think for themselves
