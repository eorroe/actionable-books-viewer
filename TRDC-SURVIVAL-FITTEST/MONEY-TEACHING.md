# Survival of the Fittest Requires Financial Literacy, Not Academic Compliance

## Overview

This money teaching explains how going to school actually diminishes your ability to get rich.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you acknowledge that your formal education did not prepare you for financial success
- When you are students, parents, or educators questioning the value of traditional schooling for getting rich
- When you want to understand why conventional advice like "go to school, get a job, you'll make money" no longer works
- When you are preparing to start a business or invest and need to develop adaptability, cooperation, and a willingness to learn from mistakes

## Steps

### Step 1: Reject the School's Definition of "Cheating" and Embrace Cooperation

In school, asking a smart person for help is labeled cheating. In business and investing, this same behavior is called cooperation. Ask the smartest person for help. Kiyosaki's rich dad called this cooperation; his poor dad called it cheating.

### Step 2: Recognize That School Punishes the Very Behaviors That Build Wealth

Schools punish mistakes, but in business and investing, mistakes are the foundation of success. Kiyosaki says each failed investment or business deal teaches you something new. Accept that you will make errors, analyze them, and move forward. Schools teach fear of failure.

### Step 3: Prioritize Financial Literacy Over Academic Credentials

A college degree does not guarantee financial security. Robert Kiyosaki has a college degree but learned nothing about money in school. Financial literacy—understanding cash flow, assets, liabilities, and how money works—is the education Kiyosaki says you need. Study real estate, stocks, businesses, and tax law through mentors like a "rich dad."

### Step 4: Redefine "Survival of the Fittest" for the Modern Economy

In school, the "fittest" is the A student who follows rules and avoids mistakes. In the real economy, the fittest are those who can adapt, cooperate, learn continuously, and take risks. Kiyosaki argues that being a C student who learns from others and embraces failure can produce greater wealth than being an A student who fears mistakes and depends on a paycheck.

### Step 5: Seek a Rich Dad or Mentor Who Teaches Money

Formal schooling is unlikely to provide the financial education you need. Find a mentor—like Kiyosaki's "rich dad"—who understands how money works. Learn about cash flow, balance sheets, and how to use debt as a tool. Play the CASHFLOW game to practice investing in a safe environment. Your financial future depends on the quality of your financial teachers.

### Example 1: The Cheating Mindset Shift

Teachers threw Robert Kiyosaki out of class for asking the smartest girl in class for help. His teachers called it cheating. His rich dad called it cooperation. In business, asking the smartest person for help is not cheating—it is a strategy Kiyosaki used to prepare for business.

### Example 2: Depression and Suicide Rates Linked to School Pressure

Kiyosaki references Shawn Achor's research showing that the age of suicide is going down and depression went from age 28 to age 14. The way schools teach kids to compete for grades rather than cooperate and learn from failure makes it harder for young people to take the risks necessary to get rich.

### Example 3: The A Student vs. The C Student

Kiyosaki was a C student who learned by cooperating with classmates and making mistakes. He found that by asking smart people for help, he was preparing himself to do well in business. C students can end up in better positions because they are not afraid of failure and are willing to learn from others. A students can end up working for C students who started businesses and built assets.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that good grades and a stable job will make you rich.
  **Solution:** Shift your focus from earning a high salary to building assets that generate income.
- **Problem:** Fear of failure caused by school conditioning.
  **Solution:** Reframe every mistake as data. Analyze what went wrong, adjust your strategy, and try again.
- **Problem:** Waiting for a teacher or institution to teach you about money.
  **Solution:** Take ownership of your financial education. Read books, find mentors, and practice with simulations.
- **Problem:** Trying to do everything alone because asking for help feels like cheating.
  **Solution:** Build a team. Ask the smartest people for help.
