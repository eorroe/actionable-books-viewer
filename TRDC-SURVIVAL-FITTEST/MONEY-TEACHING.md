# Survival of the Fittest Requires Financial Literacy, Not Academic Compliance

## Overview

This money teaching exposes how traditional schooling undermines financial success and redefines the skills needed to thrive in business and investing. Robert Kiyosaki explains that school teaches obedience and individual competition rather than cooperation, financial literacy, and learning from mistakes—all of which are essential for building wealth. The core message is that the "survival of the fittest" mindset taught in schools must be unlearned and replaced with cooperation, continuous learning, and real-world financial education.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you feel that your formal education did not prepare you for financial success
- When you are a student, parent, or educator questioning the value of traditional schooling for wealth building
- When you want to understand why conventional advice like "go to school, get a job, work hard" may no longer work
- When you are preparing to start a business or invest and need the right mindset for cooperation and risk-taking

## Steps

### Step 1: Reject the School's Definition of "Cheating" and Embrace Cooperation

In school, asking a smart person for help is labeled cheating. In business and investing, this same behavior is called cooperation or networking. Seek out knowledgeable people, study their strategies, and build relationships that accelerate your learning. Your rich dad would call this smart; your poor dad might call it cheating. Choose the perspective that builds wealth.

### Step 2: Recognize That School Punishes the Very Behaviors That Build Wealth

Schools punish mistakes, but in business and investing, mistakes are the foundation of success. Every failed investment or business deal teaches you something new. Accept that you will make errors, analyze them, and move forward. The fear of failure taught by schools keeps countless individuals trapped in safe but unfulfilling jobs.

### Step 3: Prioritize Financial Literacy Over Academic Credentials

A college degree does not guarantee financial security. Robert Kiyosaki has a college degree but learned nothing about money in school. Financial literacy—understanding cash flow, assets, liabilities, and how money works—is the real education you need. Study real estate, stocks, businesses, and tax law on your own or through mentors like a "rich dad."

### Step 4: Redefine "Survival of the Fittest" for the Modern Economy

In school, the "fittest" is the A student who follows rules and avoids mistakes. In the real economy, the fittest are those who can adapt, cooperate, learn continuously, and take calculated risks. Being a C student who learns from others and embraces failure can make you far wealthier than an A student who fears mistakes and depends on a paycheck.

### Step 5: Seek a Rich Dad or Mentor Who Teaches Money

Formal schooling is unlikely to provide the financial education you need. Find a mentor—like Kiyosaki's "rich dad"—who understands how money works. Learn about cash flow, balance sheets, and how to use debt as a tool. Play games like CASHFLOW to practice investing in a safe environment. Your financial future depends on the quality of your financial teachers.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Cheating Mindset Shift

In school, Robert Kiyosaki was thrown out for asking the smartest girl in class for help. His teachers called it cheating. His rich dad called it cooperation. In business, sitting next to the smartest person in the room and learning from them is not cheating—it is a survival strategy. This reframing allowed Kiyosaki to see that collaboration, not isolated competition, builds wealth.

### Example 2: Depression and Suicide Rates Linked to School Pressure

Kiyosaki references Shawn Achor's research showing that the age of suicide is going down and depression rates among kids have skyrocketed from age 28 to 14. The root cause is the way schools teach kids to compete for grades rather than cooperate and learn from failure. This pressure-cooker environment makes it harder for young people to take the risks necessary to get rich.

### Example 3: The A Student vs. The C Student

Kiyosaki was a C student who learned by cooperating with classmates and making mistakes. He argues that C students routinely end up running the world because they are not afraid of failure and are willing to learn from knowledgeable peers. A students, conditioned to avoid mistakes and follow rules, routinely end up working for the C students who started businesses and built assets.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Sit next to the smartest person in the room and learn from them
- ✅ Redefine mistakes as tuition for success, not as failures
- ✅ Study financial statements, cash flow, and tax law on your own
- ✅ Find a rich dad or mentor who has already achieved what you want
- ✅ Play money simulation games to practice investing without real risk
- ✅ Question everything you were taught about money in school

## Keep In Mind

- Academic credentials do not equal financial intelligence.
- The school system was designed to produce employees, not business owners or investors.
- Your poor dad's advice may keep you poor; your rich dad's advice may make you rich.
- Survival of the fittest in the modern economy means being adaptable, cooperative, and financially literate.
- Financial education is ultimately your responsibility, not your school's.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not confuse illegal activity with cooperation. The reframing of "cheating" in this teaching refers to ethical collaboration and knowledge-sharing, not fraud or deception.
- Always comply with applicable laws and regulations when seeking financial advice or mentors.
- Be cautious of anyone promising guaranteed wealth; real investing involves risk and requires due diligence.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that good grades and a stable job will make you rich.
  **Solution:** Shift your focus from earning a high salary to building assets that generate passive income.
- **Problem:** Fear of failure caused by school conditioning.
  **Solution:** Reframe every mistake as data. Analyze what went wrong, adjust your strategy, and try again.
- **Problem:** Waiting for a teacher or institution to teach you about money.
  **Solution:** Take ownership of your financial education. Read books, find mentors, and practice with simulations.
- **Problem:** Trying to do everything alone because asking for help feels like cheating.
  **Solution:** Build a team. Business is a team sport. Surround yourself with an accountant, attorney, banker, and other advisors who have your best interests at heart.
