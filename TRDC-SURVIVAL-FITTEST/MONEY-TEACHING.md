# Survival of the Fittest Requires Financial Literacy, Not Academic Compliance

## Overview

This money teaching presents Robert Kiyosaki's view that, for many people, going to school tends to limit—rather than build—their ability to get rich.

## Core Message

Robert Kiyosaki's central claim in this teaching is that the skills and mindset rewarded by traditional schooling—avoiding mistakes, competing individually for grades, and relying on credentials—often work against the skills needed to build wealth. He proposes that cooperation, learning from errors, and seeking mentors who understand how money works are more reliable paths to financial success.

## Key Concepts

- **Cooperation vs. Cheating:** Kiyosaki's "rich dad" reframed asking knowledgeable people for help as cooperation; his "poor dad" called it cheating. The same behavior produces opposite outcomes depending on the frame.
- **Fear of Failure:** Many schools condition students to avoid mistakes, which Kiyosaki argues undermines the risk-taking required in business.
- **Financial Literacy vs. Academic Credentials:** Kiyosaki asserts that a college degree does not teach how money works; practical mentorship does.
- **Survival of the Fittest (Kiyosaki's Definition):** In business, the "fittest" are those who learn from mistakes and cooperate with experts, not those with the highest grades.

## Prerequisites

- An openness to questioning the assumption that formal education alone leads to financial success.
- Familiarity with Robert Kiyosaki's "rich dad / poor dad" framework (optional but helpful for full context).

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this teaching, you will be able to:
- Distinguish between cooperation and cheating as defined by Kiyosaki's two fathers.
- Identify at least two school-conditioned behaviors that conflict with wealth-building mindsets.
- Name one concrete action to replace a fear-of-failure response with a learning-from-mistakes practice.

## Key Takeaways

- **Perspective:** This teaching presents Robert Kiyosaki's viewpoint that formal schooling tends to limit, rather than build, wealth-building capacity for many people.
- **Core distinction:** Kiyosaki contrasts his "poor dad's" view of asking for help as cheating with his "rich dad's" view of the same behavior as cooperation.
- **Actionable shift:** Replace the fear of failure often reinforced by school with a practice of analyzing mistakes and learning from them.
- **Mentorship over credentials:** Kiyosaki asserts that financial literacy is more reliably gained from mentors than from academic institutions.

## Context

This money teaching is drawn from Robert Kiyosaki's discussion with Alexandra on why he disliked school and how that experience shaped his views on financial education. Kiyosaki draws on his own upbringing—his "poor dad," who valued conventional credentials, and his "rich dad," who valued practical financial intelligence—to argue that the behaviors many schools reward (avoiding mistakes, competing for grades, individual achievement) are often the opposite of the behaviors that build wealth (cooperation, learning from errors, seeking expert guidance). He references Shawn Achor's book *Big Potential* (2018) to support his claim that school systems that prioritize individual competition over cooperation may contribute to the age of suicide going down, kids committing suicide at a younger age, and depression went from age 28 to 14, and that this dynamic also means you make it harder to get rich.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are students, parents, or educators questioning the value of traditional schooling for getting rich
- When you want to understand why conventional advice like "go to school, get a job, you'll make money" no longer works
- When you are preparing to start a business and need to develop cooperation and a willingness to learn from mistakes

## Steps

### Step 1: Reject the School's Definition of "Cheating" and Embrace Cooperation

In school, asking a smart person for help is labeled cheating. In business and investing, this same behavior is called cooperation. Ask the smartest person for help. Kiyosaki's rich dad called this cooperation; his poor dad called it cheating.

### Step 2: Recognize That School Punishes the Very Behaviors That Build Wealth

Kiyosaki argues that, in many school systems, mistakes are penalized rather than treated as learning opportunities. He says, "I was a C student. And I found out that by cheating, I was actually preparing myself to do well in business." Accept that you will make errors, analyze them, and move forward. According to Kiyosaki, many schools teach fear of failure.

### Step 3: Prioritize Financial Literacy Over Academic Credentials

A college degree does not guarantee financial security. Robert Kiyosaki says, "I have a college degree, but I learned nothing about money." And "what I've learned, I had to learn on my own from my rich dad." Find a mentor—like Kiyosaki's "rich dad"—who understands how money works.

### Step 4: Redefine "Survival of the Fittest" for the Modern Economy

In school, the "fittest" is the A student who follows rules and avoids mistakes. Kiyosaki says: "I was a C student. And I found out that by cheating, I was actually preparing myself to do well in business."

### Step 5: Seek a Rich Dad or Mentor Who Teaches Money

Formal schooling is unlikely to provide the financial education you need. Find a mentor—like Kiyosaki's "rich dad"—who understands how money works.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Cheating Mindset Shift

Teachers threw Robert Kiyosaki out of class for asking the smartest girl in class for help. His poor dad called that cheating. His rich dad reframed the same behavior as cooperation, saying, "that's not cheating, that's cooperation." Kiyosaki learned from his rich dad: asking the smartest person for help is not cheating—it is a strategy he used to prepare for business.

### Example 2: Depression and Suicide Rates Linked to School Pressure

Kiyosaki references Shawn Achor's research—presented in *Big Potential* (2018)—showing that the age of suicide is going down and depression went from age 28 to age 14. According to Kiyosaki, the way many schools teach kids to compete for grades rather than cooperate and learn from failure means you make it harder to get rich.

### Example 3: The A Student vs. The C Student

Kiyosaki says he was a "D, F, and C student" who learned by asking smart people for help—behavior his poor dad called cheating but his rich dad called cooperation. By asking smart people for help—that's not cheating, that's cooperation—he was preparing himself to do well in business.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Ask the smartest person for help — applicable whenever you face a problem you cannot solve alone and a knowledgeable person is available, especially before making a high-stakes financial decision.
- ✅ Analyze each mistake — applicable after any outcome that falls short of your goal, ideally within 24 hours while the context is still fresh.
- ✅ Learn about money through mentors — applicable when you lack experience in a specific financial domain; prioritize mentors with a verifiable track record in that domain.

## Security & Safety Notes

- The reframing of "cheating" in this teaching refers to collaboration and knowledge-sharing, not fraud or deception.
- Always comply with applicable laws and regulations when seeking financial advice or mentors.
- Be cautious of anyone promising guaranteed wealth; real investing involves risk and requires due diligence.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that good grades and a stable job will make you rich.
  **Solution:** Shift your focus from earning a high salary to doing well in business.
- **Problem:** Fear of failure caused by school conditioning.
  **Solution:** Accept that you will make errors, analyze them, and move forward.
- **Problem:** Waiting for a teacher or institution to teach you about money.
  **Solution:** Don't wait for school to teach you about money. Kiyosaki had to learn on his own from his rich dad.
- **Problem:** Trying to do everything alone because asking for help feels like cheating.
  **Solution:** Ask the smartest people for help instead of trying to do everything alone.
