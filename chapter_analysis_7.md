# Chapter 7 Analysis: Creating Community in the World

## 1. Chapter Title and Main Theme

**Chapter Title:** Creating Community in the World
**PDF Pages:** 69–73

**Main Theme:** This chapter teaches that compassionate communication reaches its full power only when extended beyond individual relationships into communities — neighborhoods, workplaces, and societies. The central thesis is that mindful speech and deep listening are the foundation of authentic community, and that collective mindfulness energy far exceeds individual practice. Community is not just a context for practice; it *is* the practice. True transformation of the world requires building mindful, compassionate communities that model reconciliation, deep listening, and loving speech at every level of society.

---

## 2. Actionable Lessons (Step-by-Step)

### 1. Extend Compassionate Communication Beyond Personal Relationships
- Recognize that the power of mindful communication is magnified when brought into your community, workplace, and neighborhood
- Do not limit loving speech and deep listening only to close friends and family
- Actively create an inclusive, compassionate foundation as the basis for interacting with everyone

### 2. Commit Your Community to Mindful Speech and Deep Listening
- A community committed to mindful speech and deep listening can be very effective at making society better
- These two practices can serve as part of a global ethic available to people of any culture or religious tradition
- Use them to reduce conflict and restore communication in divided groups

### 3. Generate and Harness Collective Mindfulness Energy
- Understand that mindfulness is a kind of energy
- When individual mindfulness energies are brought together, they are increased a thousandfold
- The whole is much, much greater than the sum of its parts
- Practice sitting together in community to create collective energy

### 4. Practice Communal Meditation and Silence as Communication
- Consider shared meditative silence or communal chanting as a form of communication — not just prayer or a religious rite
- When the community sits and concentrates together, it creates a collective energy containing compassion and awakened understanding
- Sitting together in silence is a practice of listening to your own suffering and the suffering of the world

### 5. Allow the Community to Support Your Individual Practice
- When you see other people who are in good communication with themselves and others, let them inspire you
- If suffering is hidden behind too many layers for you to penetrate alone, open your heart to the collective energy of the community
- Trust that the collective energy can penetrate suffering inside you that you could not reach by yourself
- Mindful listening and speaking will make it easier to build a stronger community

### 6. Practice Patience with Those Who Cannot Receive Love (Hungry Ghosts)
- Recognize the "hungry ghost" condition: a person who has a big, empty stomach (desperately needs love) but a tiny throat (cannot absorb it)
- Be very patient and give such a person a lot of time and space
- Do NOT be too eager to help — being overly eager can have the opposite effect and produce the opposite response
- Remain fresh, loving, compassionate, and spacious
- Look deeply to find a skillful mantra to help enlarge their capacity to receive
- Understand that community building takes time

### 7. Practice Walking, Breathing, and Speaking for the Community
- When you walk, don't walk for yourself alone; walk for your ancestors and your community
- When you breathe, allow the larger world to breathe for you
- When you are angry, allow your anger to be released and embraced by the larger community
- When you speak, allow the insight of collective humanity to speak through you

### 8. Transform Your Identity Through Community (Be the Community)
- Practice the insight: "Be your community and let your community be you"
- If you can do this for one day, you are already transformed
- See yourself in the community and see the community in you
- Be like the river arriving at the ocean; be like bees and birds flying together
- This transformation of seeing will transform how, and how effectively, you communicate

### 9. Build Community Through Loving Speech and Deep Listening in Groups
- Loving speech and deep listening are the key tools for community building
- Learn to speak in a way that does not cause suffering in yourself or your community
- Even if you have suffering and anger, train yourself to speak in a way that helps others understand what is happening in you
- If your community doesn't practice loving speech and deep listening, it is not an authentic community

### 10. Engage in Political and Social Action Through Mindful Community
- Find better ways to communicate in work environments and political environments
- Work to transform governments into mindful, compassionate places of deep listening and loving speech
- Contribute as a citizen and as a member of the human family
- Community building is the foundation for transforming and healing the world

## 3. Key Concepts and Principles

1. **Communicare (Latin Root):** Both "communication" and "community" derive from the same Latin root — *communicare* — meaning to impart, share, or make common. This reveals that communication is the very fabric of community.

2. **Mindfulness as Energy:** Mindfulness is described as a kind of energy. When individual mindfulness energies are combined in community, they multiply exponentially — "increased a thousandfold." Collective mindfulness far surpasses individual practice.

3. **The Whole Is Greater Than the Sum of Its Parts:** Systematic change cannot be achieved without the energy of community. The combined power of mindful individuals working together exceeds what any single person can accomplish alone.

4. **Collective Mindfulness Supports Individual Practice:** Seeing others practicing mindfulness inspires and supports your own practice. The community's collective energy can penetrate and help release suffering that you cannot reach alone.

5. **The Hungry Ghost (Preta):** A Buddhist image of someone with a large, empty stomach but a tiny throat — unable to absorb nourishment even when it is plentiful. Used to describe people who desperately need love and understanding but lack the capacity to receive it due to past suffering.

6. **Communal Silence as Communication:** Shared meditative silence or communal chanting is a form of communication — not prayer or ritual, but a powerful form of action that creates collective compassionate energy.

7. **Listening to World Suffering:** Sitting in silent community is a practice of listening not only to one's own suffering but to the suffering of the world itself.

8. **Community Training:** Communities committed to mindfulness help members learn to speak, breathe, and walk mindfully. The community trains you, and you train yourself. Community accelerates the development of mindful habits.

9. **Altruism Is Nurtured in Community:** Scientists have observed that exposure to altruistic behavior within a community "waters the seed" of altruism in others. When a community stays together, generosity grows and is passed to descendants; when dispersed, it dies quickly.

10. **Sacrifice for the Community (Stickleback Fish):** Social animals — fish, birds, ants, bees — demonstrate that some members will explore danger and even sacrifice themselves so the community can be safe. This behavior nourishes the altruism of the whole group.

11. **"Be Your Community and Let Your Community Be You":** The fundamental insight of community practice — dissolving the boundary between self and community. When you walk, breathe, and speak for the community, the community walks, breathes, and speaks through you.

12. **Community as the Basis for World Transformation:** Personal and community transformation are inseparable from world transformation. Healing the community heals the world. Community building is not separate from saving the planet or creating a just society — it is the necessary foundation.

13. **Technology Is Not Enough:** Technology without mindfulness can be more destructive than constructive. A connected, mindful community is the essential element for creating a sustainable environment or just society — physical action and technology alone cannot achieve these goals.

14. **Authentic Community Requires Practice:** If a community does not practice loving speech and deep listening, it is not an authentic community. Authenticity is defined by practice, not by membership or labels.

## 4. Examples Used in the Chapter

1. **Stickleback Fish (PDF page 72):** A species of fish that swims in bands of thousands. When a predator shadow is detected, a few dozen fish detach and explore. If there's real danger, some stay to be eaten while the rest warn the band. This self-sacrifice nourishes the altruism of the entire community. Scientists found that communities staying together develop growing generosity passed to descendants; dispersed communities lose it quickly.

2. **Ants, Bees, and Birds (PDF page 72):** These social animals demonstrate the same altruistic behavior as stickleback fish. Humans also demonstrate heroism — sacrificing themselves for their communities in disasters and crises.

3. **The Hungry Ghost (Pretas) (PDF pages 70–71):** In Buddhism, a hungry ghost has a huge empty stomach and is desperately hungry, but has a tiny throat. Even with plenty of food available, it cannot swallow or absorb anything. This is a metaphor for people who have been deeply wounded — even when offered understanding and love, they cannot receive it because their capacity to trust and receive has been damaged.

4. **Walking Without Awareness (PDF page 72):** "We walk without any awareness or enjoyment of our steps. We walk as if we have to run. We speak but don't know what we are saying; we create a lot of suffering while speaking." This illustrates the habitual, unmindful state that communities committed to mindfulness can help members transform.

5. **The River and the Ocean (PDF page 73):** "Be like the river when it arrives at the ocean." This image illustrates dissolving the boundary between self and community — the river loses its separate identity when it reaches the ocean, just as the practitioner dissolves into the community.

6. **Bees and Birds Flying Together (PDF page 73):** "Be like the bees and birds that fly together." This illustrates living in community — flying in formation, moving as one, seeing yourself in the community and the community in you.

7. **Heroes Sacrificing Themselves (PDF page 72):** News reports of people sacrificing their lives for their communities nourish the seed of altruism in everyone who hears about them. Scientists confirm that exposure to such behavior waters the seed of altruism in observers.

8. **Seeing the Insight of Collective Humanity Speak Through You (PDF page 72):** The idea that when you speak from a mindful community, you are not just expressing your own limited views — you are allowing the accumulated wisdom and compassion of the entire human community to speak through you.

## 5. Best Practices Mentioned

1. **Extend mindful communication to all levels of society** — apply loving speech and deep listening not just with family but in neighborhoods, workplaces, and political environments.

2. **Build community as a global ethic** — mindful speech and deep listening should be practices available to people of any culture or religious tradition.

3. **Practice communal meditation** — sit together in silence as a community; this creates collective compassionate energy and is itself a form of communication.

4. **Allow community energy to penetrate your suffering** — when you cannot release deep suffering alone, open your heart to the collective mindfulness energy of the community.

5. **Practice patience with those who cannot receive love** — recognize the "hungry ghost" condition; don't push too hard. Remain spacious and compassionate.

6. **Use skillful mantras to help wounded people open** — look deeply for the right words that can help someone enlarge their capacity to receive love and understanding.

7. **Walk, breathe, and speak for the community** — extend your practice beyond yourself to include ancestors, community, and all beings.

8. **Practice "being your community"** — dissolve the boundary between self and community; let the community be you and be the community.

9. **Build community through loving speech and deep listening** — these are the essential tools. Without them, a group is not an authentic community.

10. **Transform workplaces and governments** — work toward making all institutions places of mindful speech and deep listening.

11. **Participate in community building as a citizen and human family member** — each person has a role to play in building mindful communities.

12. **Let the insight of collective humanity speak through you** — when you speak from a mindful community, draw on the wisdom of the whole rather than just your individual perspective.

13. **Stay together to grow generosity** — communities that stay physically and practice together develop and pass on altruistic values across generations.

14. **Use suffering as a gateway to community** — sitting together in silence can be a practice of listening to your own suffering and the suffering of the world.

## 6. Common Pitfalls Mentioned

1. **Limiting mindful communication to personal relationships only** — failing to extend loving speech and deep listening to neighborhoods, workplaces, and political environments.

2. **Believing technology alone can create community or change the world** — without mindfulness, technology can be more destructive than constructive. A connected community is the essential foundation for sustainability and justice.

3. **Being too eager to help the wounded** — pushing too hard or being overly eager to help someone who cannot yet receive love can backfire and produce the opposite response.

4. **Living unmindfully within a community** — walking without awareness, speaking without knowing what you are saying, creating suffering while speaking even when part of a group.

5. **Calling a group a community without practicing loving speech and deep listening** — if a community doesn't practice these, it is not an authentic community regardless of its name or membership.

6. **Trying to penetrate deep suffering alone** — attempting to release suffering hidden by many layers by yourself, even when practicing diligently, can fail. The community's collective energy is needed.

7. **Frustration and anger in community practice** — when practicing in community, more people are available to support you, but there are also more opportunities for frustration and anger. Failing to use loving speech and deep listening to address this breaks community.

8. **Being impatient with the "hungry ghost" condition** — expecting someone to be able to receive love and understanding when their capacity is currently blocked causes frustration for both parties.

9. **Speaking only from your individual perspective** — failing to let the insight of collective humanity speak through you limits the power and scope of your communication.

10. **Walking and breathing only for yourself** — keeping your practice individual and self-focused rather than extending it to ancestors, community, and the world.
